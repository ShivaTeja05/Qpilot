# 09 — Genetics and therapy applications of qPCR beyond mRNA expression

Researcher notes for the qPilot niche-discovery effort. Date: 17 October 2026. Scope: copy-number variation (CNV), miRNA and small-RNA qPCR, ChIP-qPCR, telomere length (T/S ratio), mitochondrial DNA copy number (mtDNA-CN), vector copy number (VCN), AAV titration and biodistribution for gene and cell therapy, minimal/measurable residual disease (MRD) in research, CRISPR editing validation, SNP genotyping by allelic discrimination, high-throughput and single-cell qPCR, circulating nucleic acids.

**Method note (read first).** The shared WebSearch budget for this session was exhausted before my first query ran, so this file was built entirely from direct fetches of primary sources I already knew (regulator PDFs, standards bodies, journal articles via DOI/Europe PMC, vendor pages, GitHub/CRAN/Bioconductor) plus text extracted locally from the PDFs. Consequences: (1) I could not collect forum/Reddit/ResearchGate complaint threads, so "evidence of pain" leans on peer-reviewed method-comparison studies, reporting audits and tool states rather than practitioner quotes; (2) a few paywalled or bot-walled papers are cited from abstracts only (marked); (3) community-size numbers exist for cell and gene therapy, telomere and MRD but not for SNP genotyping, ChIP-qPCR or mtDNA-CN. Everything below is cited inline as [n] to the numbered source list in section 6. Numbers given without a citation are labelled as estimates or inferences.

Vocabulary used throughout (for the two developers): **Cq/Ct** = quantification cycle, the cycle at which a well's fluorescence crosses a threshold; lower means more starting template. **Standard curve** = a dilution series of a known material; sample Cq is converted to copies by interpolating on it. **ΔCq / ΔΔCq** = Cq differences between a target and a reference assay, and between a sample and a calibrator; 2^-ΔΔCq is the usual fold-change estimate. **Efficiency (E)** = per-cycle amplification factor (1.0 = perfect doubling); LinRegPCR estimates it per well from the raw curve. **ICC** = intraclass correlation coefficient, the fraction of total variance that is between-sample rather than measurement error (the "repeatability"). **CV** = coefficient of variation (SD/mean). **LOD/LOQ** = limit of detection / quantification. **gDNA** = genomic DNA. **GLP** = Good Laboratory Practice (21 CFR Part 58), the quality regime for regulatory nonclinical studies.

## 1. How this corner of the niche works

### 1.1 Telomere length by qPCR (T/S ratio)

#### Takeaway
Telomere qPCR is a very large, mostly epidemiological community (the founding 2002 paper alone had 839 PubMed citations by 2018 [6]) that runs thousands of DNA samples in triplicate on 384-well plates and reports a relative ratio (T/S) whose repeatability, plate effects and pre-analytical sensitivity are now openly treated as a reproducibility crisis by the NIH-funded Telomere Research Network (TRN). The field's own recommendations (report ICC not CV; one extraction method per cohort; model plate and well-position effects; Z-score before analysis; a 35-item reporting checklist) are mostly unimplemented in any software; the only public pipeline is an NCI R package that "emulates the current series of tools and manual steps used by lab personnel" [20].

#### Cited findings
- The qPCR method: telomere repeat signal (T) is normalised by a single-copy gene signal (S) to give a T/S ratio, a relative average telomere length per genome; the 2009 monochrome multiplex version (MMqPCR) reads T at 74 °C and S at 88 °C in the same tube, with an 84 °C melt step each cycle, and runs triplicate sample wells with duplicate standards; T and S are read off standard curves of serially diluted reference DNA (linear over an 81-fold range, both efficiencies > 90 %); intra-assay geometric-mean CV 5.22 %, inter-assay 3.13 %; correlation with Southern-blot TRF R² = 0.844 [18].
- An "absolute" variant (aTL) uses synthetic oligonucleotide standards (an 84-mer telomere oligo and a 75-bp 36B4 oligo) to express results in kb per diploid genome; triplicates are accepted only if the Cq SD is below 1 Cq, about 90 % of samples pass, the 1301 control should show inter-assay CV < 7 % and intra-assay CV < 2 %, TRF agreement r² = 0.75, and values run about 7 kb below TRF [19].
- Community size and reporting quality: a systematic appraisal found 5,427 records, 767 after a PCR filter, 70 full texts assessed, 25 method-comparison studies included; average reporting completeness was 51 % across the two reporting guidelines (52 % TRN, 50 % Morinha); only about 10 % of papers reported storage, PCR efficiencies or excluded samples; only 14 of 25 said whether replicates were on the same or different plates; only 2 of 25 used ICC for repeatability; weighted average qPCR–TRF correlation about 0.75 [7].
- Inter-laboratory reproducibility (10 labs, 7 using qPCR): median inter-lab CV 24.17 % overall, 20.70 % for qPCR labs versus about 9.20 % for Southern/STELA labs; intra-batch CVs 1.86 % (Southern), 2.83 % (STELA), 4.57 % (qPCR); rank correlations between labs 0.63–0.99; the authors attribute most inter-lab variation to "apparently random variation" between qPCR labs, not to reference-gene choice; qPCR's wider dynamic range (about 7.8-fold vs 2.5-fold) partly offsets this after z-scoring [12].
- Precision in replicate terms: Southern blot duplicate ICC 0.93 (95 % CI 0.87–0.96) versus qPCR T/S ICC 0.81 (0.70–0.89); for longitudinal change, a qPCR study of 1,000 people has less power than a Southern blot study of 250; recommended fixes include more technical replicates, correcting for plate and well position, consistent extraction and storage, and controlling variable amplification efficiency; raising cross-sectional correlation to r = 0.95 "has a quite dramatic effect" on qPCR power [11].
- Simulation of measurement error: Cq error is magnified in the T/S ratio and grows with telomere length; simulated ICC about 0.80 at Cq error SD 0.05, 0.51 at 0.10, 0.28 at 0.15; CVs computed on raw Cq are "misleadingly low"; recommendations: report Cq SD per sample for both T and S, re-run a substantial subset and report the ICC of T/S, target an effective Cq error SD of about 0.075 (raw SD about 0.13 with triplicates), check skew/kurtosis, and do not control for baseline when analysing change [9]. Code and the two empirical datasets (dataset1.csv 81.4 kB, dataset2.csv 2.1 kB) are on Zenodo under CC BY 4.0 [10].
- The CV is argued to be invalid for comparing telomere methods because the zero point of a T/S ratio differs between qPCR and Southern blot and often between labs within a method [13]; the TRN's own ICC how-to repeats that "the coefficient of variation (CV) is an invalid statistic for TL measurement and should not be used", provides an R script using lme4 and rptR with data in long format (sample id, TL, batch), and notes the ICC can be computed with batch ignored, as random effect or as fixed effect, "where 'batches' can be thought of as different plates, or gels, or measurement sessions" [5]. rptR is MIT-licensed [21].
- TRN Recommendation 1 (Dec 2021): assay precision (ICC) must enter power and sample-size calculations; ICC "should be based on replicates with the greatest possible independence (ideally repeated samples from the same individual with independent DNA extractions)" and "should be determined for each cohort because they are assay and lab specific" [3]. Recommendation 2: all samples of a cohort extracted with one method and protocol; where that is impossible, estimate the protocol effect by re-extracting 20–30 samples under each protocol and adjust, or include protocol as a fixed effect with sample as random effect, or Z-transform within protocol [4].
- Extraction effects are large: median T/S 0.58 (QIAamp), 0.53 (QIAsymphony), 0.74 (ReliaPrep) on the same buffy coats; storage at 1 ng/µL destroyed correlation (R² 0.11 at 4 °C); a single QC sample run on twelve 384-well plates on three instruments gave overall T/S CV 2.20 %; no well-position or instrument effect was found in that lab; raw data shared as S1–S4 XLSX [14]. In another cohort, a magnetic-bead method gave about 40 % lower T/S than a column method, and the age-adjusted association with cardiovascular disease was significant with one method (OR 1.22, p = 0.0005) and not the other (OR 1.07, p = 0.469); that lab used LinRegPCR v12.5 but applied the mean efficiency per gene per plate, ran quadruplicates in 384-well format, deleted a replicate when its CV > 5 % and it was "clearly the outlier", otherwise excluded the sample; intra-assay CV 7.6 %, inter-plate CV 10.8 %; no ICC reported [16]. A method-specific calibrator (one large sample extracted by each method and run on every plate) removed detectable method differences in cattle and sheep; plate-to-plate CV about 8 % [15].
- The 2019 critical-factors review (TRN-endorsed): labs adopting LinRegPCR "have routinely calculated the average PCR efficiency of all samples from each assay plate and applied this average efficiency to individual samples"; assays are "typically run in triplicate wells, with some labs running duplicate runs of triplicate wells"; studies should report rules for excluding outliers within triplicates, samples outside the standard-curve range and criteria for repeat runs; all samples of one individual should be on the same plate; validation should cover specificity, linearity, precision, intra- and inter-assay CV, LLOQ/ULOQ and at least three QC samples (short, medium, long); the essential reporting items include "Analysis program", "Method of data analysis", "Reference standard", "Normalization and adjustment", "Acceptance and rejection criteria", "Intra and inter-assay cv" and "Number of replicates and +/- duplicate plates and change in well position" [6].
- The TRN reporting checklist (S1 of the appraisal) lists, among 35 items, "Method of accounting for well position effects", "Method of accounting for between-plate effects", "Level of independence of the replicates (plate vs day vs extraction)", "% of samples repeated due to failed QC", "% of samples excluded from analysis due to failed QC", "ICCs of sample/study groups" and "T/S ratio transformed to Z score prior to analysis" [8].
- Comparability across studies: T/S values "are not comparable across assays, laboratories, or sometimes runs within a lab"; percentage differences are meaningless; the proposed fix is Z-scoring after methodological corrections (e.g. plate position) and log transformation; two studies' raw sex differences differed 3.5-fold (0.18 vs 0.052) but were 0.24 vs 0.37 in SD units [17].
- Software today: the NCI Cancer Genomics Research Laboratory published `cgrtelomeres` (R, MIT) to "emulate the current series of tools and manual steps used by lab personnel to analyze their telomere qPCR data"; inputs are two Excel sheets (PlateContentReport, PlateList); the version history says "Plots and testing not implemented"; it has 1 star and was pushed in June 2026 [20]. A GitHub search for "telomere qPCR" returns only three repositories [64].
- The TRN itself: an NIA/NIEHS-sponsored network established in 2019 through one U24 and four U01 awards, running cross-method comparison studies and publishing recommendations [1][7]; it hosts an example dataset (example.xls) and sample-size figures keyed to ICC [2]. I found no statement of member or lab counts [1].

#### Inferences
- The analysis decisions that change telomere conclusions are (a) how T/S is computed (ΔΔCq vs standard curve vs per-well efficiency; and whether per-plate mean efficiency is substituted), (b) replicate outlier rules (CV > 5 % and "clearly the outlier"), (c) which plate/well-position/batch model is used, (d) how extraction-method or lot differences are calibrated, and (e) whether results are Z-scored. These are exactly the "judgement calls that could change a conclusion" qPilot's Decision Cards are built for, and none of the tools above exposes them.
- Because the community reports ~51 % of required items and only 8 % use ICC, a tool that computes ICC per cohort from the record and auto-fills the TRN checklist would be adopted first by the TRN-adjacent labs, which are the ones setting the norms.

#### Gaps
- No practitioner forum evidence (search budget). No count of TRN member labs. The Eisenberg 2015 well-position paper (PMC4478151) and Morinha 2020 ecology guidelines were not retrievable in full (author manuscript / paywall); well-position modelling is therefore cited via Nettle 2021 and the TRN checklist rather than the original.

### 1.2 miRNA and small-RNA qPCR

#### Takeaway
miRNA qPCR has no agreed reference genes; normalisation choice (global mean, NormFinder-selected endogenous set, single miRNA, spike-ins) changes which miRNAs come out differentially expressed, and in biofluids hemolysis can raise individual miRNAs up to 13-fold. Vendor panels embed spike-ins and a hemolysis indicator, but analysis lives in web tools or a 2019 desktop app, and the only broad open-source package (HTqPCR) is Artistic-2.0 and Cq-based.

#### Cited findings
- Global mean normalisation: for each sample take the mean expression of all miRNAs with Cq ≤ 35, in log space; panels of about 366–430 miRNAs; it outranked the three previously proposed reference miRNAs (miR-17-5p, miR-191, miR-103) in most sample sets; with small-RNA controls 89.1 % of differentially expressed miRNAs appeared down-regulated in MYCN-amplified tumours versus 57.6 % with the global mean; the method "is only valid when many miRNAs are measured"; for small panels, pick miRNAs that track the global mean in a pilot (ranked by geNorm V, excluding pairs within 2 kb) and use their geometric mean [22].
- Normaliser choice changes conclusions: in plasma from 10 sedentary and 14 trained men (179 miRNAs), the number of miRNAs with ≥ 5-fold change was 43 up/11 down (global mean), 43/13 (mean of 33 NormFinder-selected endogenous miRNAs), 41/15 (miR-21a-5p), 40/17 (miR-320d), 55/8 (mean of spike-ins) and 74/9 (cel-miR-39 alone); spike-in normalisation produced an "inverted" trend relative to endogenous methods; technical CV was lowest for miR-320d (34.62 %) and highest for cel-39 (44.40 %) and UniSp6 (48.88 %) [23].
- Hemolysis: 88 of 136 detectable plasma miRNAs were elevated in at least one hemolysed/non-hemolysed pair; miR-16, -451, -15b, -486-3p, -532-3p, -886-5p, -636, -1255B and RNU48 rose by up to 13-fold; proposed biomarkers miR-106a, -17, -92a and -210 were significantly affected; spike-ins do not correct for hemolysis; samples were called hemolysed at A414 > 0.2 [24]. Qiagen's serum/plasma panel includes miR-451a and miR-23a-3p and states that a ΔCq (miR-23a-3p − miR-451a) greater than 7 may indicate contamination; the panel carries cel-miR-39-3p, UniSp2, UniSp4, UniSp5, UniSp6 spike-ins and an inter-plate calibrator in triplicate (UniSp3 on other panels); recommended normalisation is "across the entire panel", with miR-93-5p and miR-425-5p named as usually stable references [26]. The miRCURY chemistry is poly(A) tailing, a universal poly(T) RT primer and two LNA-enhanced miRNA-specific primers with SYBR Green from as little as 1 pg total RNA; nine small-RNA controls are offered and users are told to validate them with geNorm or NormFinder [27].
- Chemistry matters: in a head-to-head test the authors' two-tailed RT-qPCR showed < 1 % unspecific cross-reaction, the stem-loop TaqMan design up to 22.48 %, a poly(A)-tailing design up to 50.71 % and a linker-ligation design up to 122.47 % (let-7 family); TaqMan and linker-ligation underestimated isomiRs; dynamic ranges 6–7 logs except 5 logs for linker ligation [25].
- Thermo's TaqMan Advanced miRNA workflow is a universal RT, a "miR-Amp" universal pre-amplification and then per-target MGB probe assays, detecting from 60 copies of input miRNA or 2 µL of plasma; array cards hold up to 380 targets, OpenArray up to 818 assays per sample; analysis is referred to "Applied Biosystems qPCR Analysis Modules" [28]. ExpressionSuite v1.3 (files dated late 2019, "PC" only) does ΔΔCq across many genes and samples with endogenous-control selection, box and correlation plots and multi-plate analysis; the page does not mention outlier removal or maintenance status [29].
- The miRQC study compared 12 commercial platforms on 20 standardized samples and found each has "different strengths and weaknesses" (abstract only) [30c]. The Blondal 2013 methods paper that defined the hemolysis indicator is cited from its abstract only [30f]. I found no formal "MIQE for miRNA" document; miRNA papers cite MIQE itself (e.g. [24]).
- Open tools: HTqPCR (Bioconductor, Artistic-2.0, v1.66.0) reads TaqMan Low Density Array/OpenArray, LightCycler, CFX and Fluidigm BioMark Cq exports and implements quantile, rank-invariant (two variants), deltaCt and geometric-mean normalisation, with "Undetermined"/"Unreliable" categories and a 40-cycle placeholder [61]. NormqPCR (LGPL-3) selects reference genes [70]. On GitHub, "miRNA qPCR" tools are small and mostly unlicensed or GPL (MirStat archived GPL-2; miRQPCRnorm "global fit normalization", MIT, 0 stars) [64].

#### Inferences
- The decision space for a miRNA study is: expressed-call threshold (Cq ≤ 35), normaliser family (global mean / selected endogenous / spike-in / single), hemolysis exclusion, pre-amplification uniformity, inter-plate calibrator use. A robustness grid over these is the natural qPilot extension, and the Faraldi table shows a conclusion (74 vs 43 "up" miRNAs) can double depending on one choice.
- Because panels are 96/384-well with fixed layouts and embedded controls, "panel presets" (which wells are spike-ins, IPC, hemolysis pair, candidate references) are cheap to encode and immediately useful.

#### Gaps
- No full text for Schwarzenbach 2015 (Clin Chem review) or Mestdagh 2014; no public dataset with raw miRNA panel curves identified; GeneGlobe's analysis features could not be read (JavaScript app).

### 1.3 Vector copy number, AAV titration and biodistribution (gene and cell therapy)

#### Takeaway
This is the fastest-growing qPCR workload with the most explicit, numeric acceptance rules of any research application: FDA expects a biodistribution qPCR LOQ of < 50 copies/µg gDNA demonstrated with 95 % confidence, triplicates with a spiked replicate and individual-animal reporting; EMA wants VCN per genome for integrating vectors and copies/µg for episomal; FDA's CAR-T guidance requires VCN per CAR-positive cell on the certificate of analysis with a risk-justified limit. Industry working groups have written validation criteria (efficiency 90–110 %, R² ≥ 0.980, QC %CV ≤ 25 %, spike recovery < 55 % = inhibition) that labs implement in instrument software plus Excel. There are 2,217 gene therapies (including CAR-T) in development and every one needs these assays; no open tool implements the rules.

#### Cited findings
- FDA Long Term Follow-Up guidance (2020): "Use a quantitative, sensitive assay such as quantitative PCR (qPCR)"; "The assay should have a demonstrated limit of quantitation of <50 copies/μg genomic DNA, so that your assay can detect this limit with 95% confidence"; "The DNA samples should be run in triplicate for each tissue... one replicate of each tissue sample should include a spike of control DNA"; "individual animal data should be provided. The method for how values below the Limit of Quantitation of the assay are categorized and calculation of the median or mean value should be specified"; persistence is indicated by signal above the < 50 copies/µg threshold [31].
- FDA CAR-T guidance (2024): "the average number of integrations per CAR-positive cell, generally referred to as VCN, should be determined and reported on the Certificate of Analysis (COA) for each lot"; VCN per total cells "lowers the reported vector integration rate"; "We recommend that the VCN release criterion be justified based on a risk assessment"; for products without extended culture an interim VCN at release followed by VCN on cultured cells may be needed because of "persistence of episomal copies of non-integrated vectors" [32]. FDA CMC guidance lists "vector genome titer by quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR)" among dose-determining assays that "should be qualified as suitable for use prior to initiating clinical studies" [33].
- EMA gene therapy guideline: "When performing NAT-based assays to measure vector copy number for integrating vectors, the limits of detection and quantification should be expressed preferably as vector copy number/genome. For episomal vectors ... as copy number/µg host cell DNA analysed"; integration studies must cover copy number, localisation, structural integrity and "persistency of the average vector copy number in the cells" [35].
- ICH S12 (2023): quantitation of vector/transgene nucleic acid "relative to input genomic DNA by established nucleic acid amplification methods (e.g., qPCR, digital PCR, etc.) is considered the standard"; "Spike and recovery experiments ... should be performed"; non-GLP biodistribution studies "are acceptable" and "Sample analysis for BD can be conducted in non-GLP manner" even inside a GLP toxicology study [34].
- Regulatory points-to-consider (CRO authors): LOD is the lowest standard positive in all replicates across five validation runs and cannot be below 3 copies (Poisson); LLOQ requires duplicate Cq %CV ≤ 2.0 %, back-calculated quantity %RE within ± 45 % and inter-assay Cq %CV ≤ 3.0 %; efficiency 90–110 % (slope about −3.1 to −3.6), R² ≥ 0.980; validation standards 10^8 down to 50, 25, 10, 5 copies and blank, at least eight non-zero levels in duplicate, at least 75 % of levels and a minimum of seven must pass per run (six in sample analysis); four QC sets (10^2, 10^3, 10^5, 10^7) with ≥ 50 % per level and ≥ 67 % overall meeting %CV ≤ 25 % and %RE ± 25 % (± 45 % for the lowest); samples in triplicate with one replicate spiked (e.g. 200 copies), spike recovery < 55 % indicates inhibition and the sample is re-run at 1/4 or 1/10 input; matrix DNA added to 1,000 ng per 50 µL reaction; naive DNA from at least three individuals must be below LOD; five runs over at least three days, two operators, two reagent lots; values below LOD reported as BLOD and below LLOQ as BLOQ; a failed standard curve or QC means the run is repeated and a second consecutive failure is escalated to the Study Director; supercoiled plasmid standards shift Cq by 2.65–4.38 cycles; FDA has not required qPCR validation while EMA has [36]. The 2024 AAPS working-group recommendations (37 experts, 24 organisations) cover biodistribution, transgene expression, shedding and cell persistence assays (abstract only; full text bot-walled) [30a]; the Global CRO Council white paper proposes consensus validation parameters and notes "regulatory guidance for ddPCR is lacking" (abstract) [30b].
- VCN methods: in colony-forming cells a duplex qPCR of vector (WPRE or HIV) and albumin (two copies per diploid cell) gives VCN = ratio × 2 against a plasmid standard carrying both targets; calibrator clones with 1, 3 and 8 insertions; standard-curve R² > 0.99, duplicate Cq within 0.5, < 0.1 copy per cell scored negative; proteinase-K lysates underestimated VCN by about 30–40 % [39]. The planned WHO lentiviral genomic-DNA standard is a three-vial panel (0, 1 and 5 integrated copies per genome) from a single-cell-derived 293T clone, to be value-assigned by at least 15 laboratories using TaqMan, SYBR and digital PCR; within one lab, plasmid calibrators and integrated-gDNA calibrators gave significantly different copy numbers in bulk cells [38]. In clinical CAR/TCR products ddPCR VCN precision was CV 0.02–0.04 across technicians, lower LOD 0.13 copies/µL, upper limit about 1 × 10^4 copies/µL; the measured VCN is a population average and the authors suggest dividing by flow-cytometry transduction efficiency for VCN per transduced cell; no qPCR-vs-ddPCR comparison was run [37].
- AAV titration: ITR2 qPCR gives "systematically higher titers (up to eightfold)" than other targets; the plasmid standard should be "linearized just outside the ITR region on each end"; at 10^8 copies the Cq was 12.42 for PvuII-digested, 15.26 for single-cut and 16.80 for uncut plasmid; reference standards ATCC VR-1616 (AAV2) and VR-1816 (AAV8); earlier inter-lab studies showed PCR titres varying by "almost 2-log"; report titres relative to the RSM and per MIQE [40]. In a qPCR-vs-ddPCR comparison, qPCR titres were on average 64× higher than ddPCR in crude samples (5.5× after re-calibrating the standard with ddPCR), qPCR was inhibited at high potassium phosphate, ddPCR CVs were significantly lower (p < 0.001), DNase at about 2 U/reaction removes free DNA, and the authors recommend calibrating qPCR standards against ddPCR-characterised material [41]. Another comparison found circular standards adequate for AAV8, CMV primers least variable, that the standard-curve buffer should match the sample buffer, and used efficiency 0.9–1.1 and R² > 0.99 as internal criteria [42].
- Market: 4,487 gene, cell and RNA therapies are in development, including 2,217 gene therapies (which includes genetically modified cell therapies such as CAR-T) and 940 non-genetically modified cell therapies; the gene-therapy pipeline is 1,332 preclinical, 427 Phase I, 387 Phase II, 59 Phase III and 12 pre-registration as of Q2 2026 (up from 2,155 total in Q2 2025); CAR-T is 66 % of the genetically modified cell pipeline; 46 gene therapies are approved worldwide; eight new approvals in the quarter [43]. The FDA's approved cellular and gene therapy list has 54 entries, including 7 CAR-T and about 10 AAV products [44].

#### Inferences
- Every preclinical biodistribution study is dozens of tissues × animals × triplicates × standard curves across many plates, with run-acceptance decisions (curve pass/fail, QC pass/fail, spike recovery, BLOQ handling) that today are made in instrument software plus spreadsheets. A deterministic rule pack with a record is a direct fit for qPilot's whole-study, multi-plate architecture; the "research use only" constraint matches ICH S12's statement that sample analysis can be non-GLP.
- VCN and AAV titre are "small plate, high stakes" assays where the standard material and its topology dominate error; a Decision Card that shows how the conclusion moves under alternative standards (plasmid vs gDNA calibrator; single-cut vs free-ITR) is more valuable than any new maths.

#### Gaps
- No public dataset with raw biodistribution or VCN curves found; the NIBSC site did not resolve (DNS) so the current status/code of the WHO lentiviral standard is unverified; Hays 2024 full text unavailable; no count of GLP CRO labs doing this work.

### 1.4 CNV, mtDNA copy number, ChIP-qPCR, CRISPR validation, SNP genotyping, high-throughput and single-cell qPCR

#### Takeaway
These are mature, mid-sized applications where the decisive step is a discrete call (copy number 2 or 3; genotype AA/AB/BB; editing %; cell "expressed/not") made from continuous data by vendor desktop tools (CopyCaller, TaqMan Genotyper, CFX Maestro, KlusterCaller) that are Windows-only, closed about their algorithms and expose little confidence information. Published open methods exist for each (qBase error propagation; BCGA full-curve genotype clustering; Haring %input; Livak Fano-factor LoD; McDavid hurdle model) but are unimplemented or GPL/Artistic-licensed.

#### Cited findings
- **CNV.** CopyCaller v2.1 (Windows .exe, 264 MB) performs relative quantitation for TaqMan Copy Number Assays with or without a calibrator, reports "confidence in copy number calls", imports QuantStudio 6/7/12K Flex results and supports Crt and Ct methods; the page says nothing about maintenance or a successor [47]. The qBase framework normalises with the geometric mean of up to five reference genes, propagates error with the delta method, calibrates between runs with the geometric mean of inter-run calibrators (about 70 % added uncertainty with one IRC, about 40 % with three) and checks IRC stability with an M value [46]. The D'haene 2010 objective CNV-calling paper is Elsevier-paywalled and could not be read (gap).
- **mtDNA-CN.** In a 1,000+-participant comparison, a multiplexed ND1/RPPH1 qPCR in triplicate (excluding ND1 Ct > 28 and ΔCt outliers, correcting a linear pipetting-order effect and modelling plate as a random effect) produced associations about 5.6 orders of magnitude less significant and effect sizes 5.8× less extreme than WGS-derived mtDNA-CN (incident CVD HR 0.63 by WGS vs 0.93 by qPCR); column kits optimised for ≥ 50 kb fragments may bias mtDNA; qPCR will remain common at under $2 per sample and "absolute quantification ... through the use of standard curves may improve upon the performance of qPCR" [45].
- **ChIP-qPCR.** The reference methods paper defines %input (ChIP signal / input signal), fold enrichment over no-antibody control (which the authors "strongly recommend not to use" because a 10× difference in background alone changes the value 10×), background subtraction (only safe when background is very low) and normalisation to a control locus; best practice is %input or relative-to-control with at least four replicates, presenting background alongside; PCR efficiency should be "as close as possible to two" [48]. Open tools: WARPqpcr (R; five-column samplesheet Well/Plate/Sample/Detector/Cq; %input via inputPercent, fold enrichment via mockName; identical sample names treated as technical replicates; no licence shown) [49]; a 2026 "Codex skill" for "auditable ChIP-qPCR percent-input analysis" that takes Excel/CSV Cq exports with `--input-percent`, `--additional-dilution` and `--exclude-flag` options and writes an Excel audit workbook, a Prism-ready table and a QC audit (no licence shown, 4 stars) [50].
- **CRISPR.** A qPCR "drop-off" assay with an LNA probe over the Cas9 cut site reports the unedited fraction by ΔΔCq; agreement with Sanger r² = 0.968 (Pearson 0.984); precision drops below 15 % and above 85 % editing; detects deletions > 3 nt; cis-sequencing is still needed to define alleles [51]. The widely cited validation survey compared T7E1, TIDE, IDAA and NGS and evaluated no qPCR method; TIDE/IDAA overestimate wild type by 10–20 % in pools and miscall clones [52].
- **SNP genotyping.** The BCGA algorithm uses the full real-time trace: it computes the VIC−FAM difference at every cycle, picks the "best cycle" with the largest between-sample variance (34–44 across 13 SNPs), clusters with partitioning around medoids choosing 2 or 3 clusters by silhouette width, anchors the heterozygote cluster on the no-DNA blanks, flags samples with silhouette below about 0.65, and was correct in 84/84 sequenced calls versus 72/84 for operator-adjusted endpoint analysis; R code was posted on a university site without a stated licence [53]. Vendor tools: TaqMan Genotyper v1.7.1 (Windows 10) autocalls with an "improved algorithmic approach", lets users set linear boundaries for homozygote/heterozygote/undetermined regions and omit outliers [54]; CFX Maestro "automatically assigns a genotype ... based on the positions of the NTCs and the angle and distance of the unknown data points from the NTCs", offers polar coordinates, and records whether each call was Auto or Manual [57]; QuantStudio Design & Analysis 2 has a built-in Genotyping module that is applied automatically to OpenArray genotyping files [58]; LGC's SNPviewer only displays cluster plots ("Genotyping calls cannot be changed within SNPviewer") [55] and KlusterCaller classifies points as homozygous, heterozygous or "inconclusive" with "unconfirmed" points in pink and no documented quality metric [56]. A GitHub search for allelic-discrimination genotyping tools returned one 0-star GPL-2 notebook [64].
- **High-throughput and single-cell.** Biomark exports one CSV per chip with 2,304 (48.48) or 9,216 (96.96) rows and a "Call" column (Pass/Fail) that HTqPCR maps to OK/Undetermined [61]. Single-cell practice: 20 pre-amplification cycles, 96 cells × 96 assays, LoD chosen as the lowest integer Cq (24) at which the Fano factor is ≥ 1 for all assays (Poisson noise), Log2Ex = LoD − Cq with negatives set to "ND", cells culled when a detection-failure score exceeds 3× the median (1.4 % removed), melt curves flagged 5.0 % of detection failures as non-specific with ten assays responsible, technical SD 0.15–0.25 cycles versus observed median SD about 1.4 [59]; the hurdle model (point mass at zero plus log-normal) and robust z-score filters on expression and expressed fraction were released as SingleCellAssay, now MAST (GPL ≥ 2) [58][62]; technical noise is "insignificant for initial copy numbers > 100" and rises steeply below about 20 cDNA copies, and splitting cDNA into replicate qPCRs adds noise rather than removing it [60]. Standard BioTools still sells Biomark X9 with Windows-only Real-Time PCR Analysis and SNP Genotyping Analysis software and describes the Singular Analysis Toolset as open source for the older Biomark HD/C1 [63]; GitHub single-cell qPCR repositories are mostly unlicensed lab scripts from 2014–2016 plus a 2026 "agent-friendly R workflow for Fluidigm HSC qPCR analysis" [64].

#### Inferences
- For CNV, mtDNA-CN and SNP genotyping the subjective moment is the rounding of a continuous estimate (2.6 copies; a point between clusters) into a discrete call; vendor tools give a confidence number but not the alternatives. These map onto Decision Cards with "what would flip this call" evidence.
- Single-cell qPCR is a shrinking community (inference from the age of the GitHub repositories and the move to scRNA-seq); the Biomark importer and LoD/Fano logic are cheap but should be lower priority than telomere, miRNA and VCN.

#### Gaps
- No community-size numbers for SNP genotyping, ChIP-qPCR or CNV; BCGA code availability unverified (site likely offline); the CopyCaller user guide (403) and the D'haene CNV calling criteria were not retrievable; no public raw-curve datasets found for any of these.

### 1.5 Research MRD and rare-target quantification (ASO-qPCR, BCR-ABL1)

#### Takeaway
The EuroMRD consortium (69 laboratories in 26 countries, twice-yearly quality rounds) has the most fully specified deterministic interpretation rules in qPCR: quantitative range, sensitivity and "positive below quantitative range" are defined by replicate agreement, distance from background and standard-curve limits, and a 2024 update subdivides the grey zone using criteria validated on 1,262 samples. These rule packs are published, numeric and directly implementable, and they generalise to any rare-target assay in research.

#### Cited findings
- EuroMRD 2007 rules: the lowest dilution within the quantitative range must give "reproducible amplification: ΔCT of all replicates ≤ 1.5", "all CT values ≥ 3.0 lower than the lowest CT value of the background (= amplification observed in normal MNC DNA)", and a mean CT 2.6–4.0 cycles from the previous 10-fold dilution (0.5–1.5 between 2-fold dilutions); the standard curve must have at least three dilution points, a minimum range of two logs, a slope between −3.1 and −3.9 and a correlation coefficient ≥ 0.98; sensitivity is the lowest dilution with at least one positive replicate, lowest CT ≥ 1.0 below background and within 20 cycles of the intercept; the ranges were set after "analysis of over 1400 RQ-PCR assays" [66]. The original group was 30 laboratories (abstract) [30d].
- 2024 update: "MRD low positive, <QR" requires three replicates with CT at least 1.0 below the lowest background CT and at least one replicate at least 3.0 below; the estimated level is reported in brackets, e.g. "MRD low positive, <10−4 (3 × 10−5)"; other non-quantifiable positives are "MRD of uncertain significance"; data are reported to one significant figure; QA rounds run twice a year with a fivefold acceptance window (reference/√5 to reference×√5), a 75 % pass criterion, and about 71 laboratories in 27 countries [67]. The companion study tested 11 scenarios on 1,262 samples; the chosen scenario (all three replicates positive and lowest CT ≥ 3 below background) raised true positivity from 77.5 % to 94.6 % while covering 42.8 % of such samples; RQ-PCR estimates agreed with second-marker or ddPCR values within 0.5 log in 76 % and within 1 log in 92 % of 278 samples [68].
- CML deep molecular response: results on the International Scale = (ΣBCR-ABL1 copies / Σreference copies) × CF × 100; MR4/MR4.5/MR5 require ≥ 10,000/32,000/100,000 ABL1 (or 24,000/77,000/240,000 GUSB) reference transcripts when undetectable; positivity cut-off Cq = intercept + 1 (about 41–42); any positive replicate under 3 copies is scored as 3 (Poisson LoD); replicates are summed before the ratio; each replicate needs ≥ 10,000 ABL1 to be evaluable [69].
- EuroMRD runs biannual QA for ALL (IG/TR, KMT2A), NHL (BCL1/2, IGH) and Ph+ ALL (BCR-ABL) targets; in December 2025 ESLHO formally adopted the EuroMRD EQA schemes [65].

#### Inferences
- The EuroMRD rules are an existence proof that a qPCR community can agree on fully deterministic, numeric, replicate-level decision logic. The same logic (replicate ΔCq, distance to background, curve limits, Poisson floor) applies to research assays for rare alleles, ctDNA research, residual plasmid/host-cell DNA and vector persistence. qPilot must stay clearly research-use (no patient reporting), but the rule packs and the robustness over their thresholds are reusable.

#### Gaps
- The 2024 supplementary tables with the updated curve criteria were not read; no evidence collected on how many research (non-diagnostic) labs run ASO-qPCR.

### 1.6 Which applications are growing, and where analysis is subjective today

#### Takeaway
Cell and gene therapy is the growth engine (pipeline +62 gene therapies year-on-year to 2,217; 59 in Phase III; eight approvals in Q2 2026) and its qPCR work is rule-bound but spreadsheet-executed. Telomere and circulating-miRNA epidemiology are large and stable, with subjectivity concentrated in normalisation, calibration and outlier handling. Single-cell qPCR is shrinking.

#### Cited findings
- Pipeline growth figures as in 1.3 [43]; 54 FDA-approved cellular/gene therapy products [44].
- Telomere: "thousands of papers" (appraisal) and 839 citations of the 2002 method by 2018 [6][7]; MRD: 69–71 laboratories in EuroMRD [65][67].
- Subjective steps documented above: replicate outlier deletion "if one replicate was clearly the outlier" [16]; plate-mean efficiency substitution [6]; normaliser choice changing differential-expression counts [23]; manual genotype overrides recorded as "Manual" [57]; "inconclusive"/"unconfirmed" genotype points [56]; BLOQ handling must be "specified" by the study [31]; VCN release criterion "justified based on a risk assessment" [32].

#### Inferences
- Growth ranking for qPilot's roadmap (estimate): gene/cell therapy VCN-titre-biodistribution > circulating miRNA/cfDNA research > telomere epidemiology (large, flat) > CNV/mtDNA-CN (flat) > SNP genotyping (flat, partly migrating to arrays/sequencing) > single-cell qPCR (declining).

#### Gaps
- No liquid-biopsy market numbers collected; cfDNA pre-analytical guideline (Meddeb 2019) available only as an abstract stating that "no standard operating procedure has yet been set" [74].

Permissive libraries are rare: most relevant R packages are GPL (qpcR, MAST), LGPL (NormqPCR) or Artistic-2.0 (HTqPCR); tidyqpcr (Apache-2.0) and rptR (MIT) are the exceptions; the CRAN package `pcr` was archived on 2025-11-07 [72].

## 3. Discoveries (ranked by impact × feasibility × fit)

### D1. "Telomere Study Mode": cohort-scale T/S with repeatability, plate models and the TRN checklist built in

1. **Persona and situation.** A technician or postdoc in a population-health or ageing lab (e.g. a TRN member lab) measuring T/S on 500–5,000 DNA samples per study, triplicates on 384-well plates (MMqPCR or monoplex), with 1–3 QC DNAs and a reference-DNA standard curve per plate, re-running a subset for repeatability, then handing a T/S table to an epidemiologist. Weekly to monthly runs; every study ends in a methods section judged against the TRN checklist.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** 51 % reporting completeness and only 2/25 studies using ICC [7]; inter-lab qPCR CV 20.7 % [12]; ICC 0.81 vs 0.93 and the 1,000-vs-250 power comparison [11]; extraction method shifting T/S by 40 % and deleting a CVD association [16]; TRN Recommendations 1–2 requiring per-cohort ICC and protocol-effect adjustment [3][4]; the TRN ICC script requiring users to reshape data to long format and run lme4/rptR in RStudio [5]; the NCI pipeline that emulates manual steps with no tests or plots [20]. Cost estimate: a re-run of 10 % of samples for ICC on a 2,000-sample cohort is about 200 samples × triplicate × 2 targets, i.e. several plates and days of bench time, which labs currently skip (inference from the 8 % ICC reporting rate).
3. **What exists today.** Vendor software gives Cq; LinRegPCR gives per-well efficiency which labs then average per plate [6]; Excel templates compute T/S; R scripts compute ICC; cgrtelomeres (MIT) packages one lab's manual steps [20]. Nothing computes ICC from the study record, models plate/well/extraction effects as the TRN asks, or shows which analysis choices change the T/S ranking.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: RDML (or vendor export) for all plates of a study, plus a sample sheet with sample id, individual id, extraction method/lot, plate, well, replicate run, role (sample/QC/standard/NTC), and for MMqPCR the two acquisition steps as two targets. Deterministic methods: per-well efficiency (LinRegPCR via rdmlpython, already in qPilot) with the option of per-plate mean efficiency as a robustness alternative [6][16]; T/S by (a) standard-curve interpolation with per-plate curves [18], (b) efficiency-corrected ratio (Pfaffl) [16], (c) ΔΔCq; replicate rules (Cq SD < 1 per aTL [19]; CV > 5 % with single-outlier deletion [16]); plate QC via the QC DNAs (reject plate if QC T/S deviates beyond a user limit; cite Lin's three QC levels [6]); well-position and plate effects as fixed/random terms in a linear mixed model, as the TRN how-to describes [5]; method-specific calibrator correction for extraction-method mixes [15] or protocol-as-fixed-effect with Z-scoring within protocol [4]; ICC (one-way and batch-adjusted) from the re-run subset [5]; final Z-scored T/S [17]. Decision Cards: "Replicate C of sample 1143 is 0.9 Cq off; drop replicate or sample? (ranking change: 14 samples cross the tertile boundary)"; "Plate 7 QC long-telomere control is 12 % high; re-run or model plate?"; "Two extraction kits detected; calibrate (23 bridging samples found) or Z-score within kit?"; "Per-well vs per-plate efficiency changes 31 T/S by > 5 %". Record: ICC with CI, plate/well model, outlier log, percent repeated and excluded (TRN items), the auto-filled TRN checklist text [8]. MCP: `qpcr_study` with a `telomere` method card; `records summarise` for the methods paragraph.
5. **Feasibility.** Datasets with licences: Nettle 2019 dataset1.csv/dataset2.csv (Cq replicates for T and S) and simulation code, CC BY 4.0 [10]; Dagnall 2017 S1–S4 XLSX under the PLOS CC BY licence [14]; Seeker 2016 data repository (no licence file; ask) [64]; TRN example.xls [2]. Methods with DOIs: Cawthon 2009 [18], O'Callaghan 2011 [19], Nettle 2019/2021 [9][11], TRN recommendations [3][4][5]. Libraries: ICC via one-way ANOVA/mixed model in statsmodels (BSD) rather than rptR/lme4 (R); no GPL needed. Effort: 2–3 weeks for the engine and cards, plus 1 week for the MMqPCR acquisition-step mapping. Hardest part: representing two-temperature acquisition in RDML consistently across instruments, and validating ICC and mixed-model results against the TRN R script on the example dataset.
6. **Why qPilot specifically.** Raw curves give per-well efficiency, which the field knows it should use and does not [6]; the robustness engine answers "would my cohort ranking change under the other reasonable analysis" which is the exact question the comparability papers raise [12][17]; the record carries the ICC and QC percentages the checklist demands [8]; MCP lets a cohort pipeline call the analysis unattended.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Labs are attached to Excel templates and in-house R; the TRN may publish its own tooling; aTL and MMqPCR variants multiply formats. Confidence: high that the pain is real and unserved; medium on adoption speed. Validation against Nettle's data and the TRN script is the acceptance test.

### D2. "Run-acceptance rule packs" for standard-curve absolute quantification in gene/cell therapy research (biodistribution, VCN, AAV titre)

1. **Persona and situation.** A bioanalytical scientist in an academic vector core, a biotech process-development group or a CRO method-development team, running 384-well plates of tissue DNA (or product gDNA) against a 8–9-point standard curve with QC sets, NTCs and spiked replicates, several plates per study, every week. Results go into a study report or a certificate of analysis.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** FDA's explicit LOQ, triplicate, spike and BLOQ-reporting expectations [31]; the industry points-to-consider with dozens of numeric criteria and a "repeat run, escalate on second failure" rule [36]; EMA's unit conventions [35]; ICH S12's spike-and-recovery and non-GLP sample analysis allowance [34]; FDA's VCN per CAR-positive cell requirement [32]; AAV titre bias up to 8× from the standard's topology [40] and 64× qPCR-vs-ddPCR discrepancies in crude samples [41]; 2,217 gene therapies in development [43]. Cost estimate: a failed plate (curve or QC) costs a day plus reagents; an undetected inhibited well changes a tissue's positivity call; neither is caught early by instrument software.
3. **What exists today.** Instrument software (QuantStudio DA2 "Standard curve" module [58], CFX Maestro) computes quantities; acceptance checks, spike recovery, BLOQ policy and per-animal tables are done in Excel under SOPs; ddPCR vendors give their own software; no open tool encodes the rules.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: RDML/vendor export per plate plus a study sheet (animal/lot, tissue/matrix, DNA ng loaded, replicate role incl. spike copies, standard copies, QC nominal). Deterministic rule pack (versioned "method card") implementing: curve acceptance (≥ 6–7 passing non-zero standards, slope/efficiency 90–110 %, R² ≥ 0.980), QC acceptance (%CV ≤ 25 %, %RE ± 25 %, ± 45 % at the lowest level), spike recovery with the < 55 % inhibition rule and the 1/4–1/10 re-run suggestion, LOD/LLOQ logic, BLOD/BLOQ labelling, copies per µg gDNA (or per genome / per diploid cell for VCN with the ×2 albumin convention [39]), and per-animal/per-tissue tables with the chosen below-LOQ policy stated [31][36]. Robustness grid: standards dropped at the ends vs kept; Cq cut-off 38/40; BLOQ as 0 vs LOQ/2 vs censored; per-plate vs pooled curve; plasmid vs gDNA calibrator when both present [38]. Decision Cards: "Standard 25 copies failed in one replicate: drop level (curve still ≥ 7 points) or repeat plate?"; "Spike recovery 48 % in liver #3: declare inhibition and re-run at 1/4"; "Tissue positivity for kidney flips if BLOQ is treated as LOQ/2". Record: rule-pack version, every pass/fail with evidence, escalation events, final tables; a `validation summary` export (five-run precision/accuracy tables) for method qualification write-ups. MCP: `qpcr_study` with `rulepack=fda_ltfu_2020` etc.
5. **Feasibility.** Rules are public [31][34][35][36]; no raw-curve public dataset exists (gap), so build synthetic plates plus the RDML benchmark's standard-curve plates; AAV reference materials ATCC VR-1616/VR-1816 are purchasable for a demo [40]. Effort: 2 weeks for the rule engine and tables, 1 week per additional rule pack (EMA units, EuroMRD-style sensitivity, AAV free-ITR standard checks). Hardest part: expressing rule packs declaratively so they are auditable and versioned, and avoiding any claim of GLP/Part 11 compliance.
6. **Why qPilot specifically.** Multi-plate study analysis with a hash-chained record is precisely what a bioanalyst's audit trail needs even outside GLP; the domain-agnostic decision core means the same rule-pack machinery serves ELISA later; the robustness engine turns "BLOQ policy" from a footnote into a tested sensitivity.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Regulated users will ask for Part 11 features qPilot refuses by design; CROs have LIMS; ddPCR is displacing qPCR for VCN and titre [41][37]. Positioning as the method-development and academic-core tool, and as the "second opinion" next to ddPCR, limits the risk. Confidence: medium-high.

### D3. Full-curve allelic discrimination with cluster-confidence cards (TaqMan, rhAmp, KASP endpoint)

1. **Persona and situation.** A genetics core or plant/animal breeding lab calling SNP genotypes on 96/384-well plates or OpenArray, dozens of plates a week, with a technician eyeballing scatter plots and overriding calls near cluster edges.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Endpoint analysis with operator thresholds was wrong in 12 of 84 sequenced calls versus 0 for the full-curve algorithm [53]; vendor tools call by angle/distance from NTCs [57], by user-drawn linear boundaries [54] or by proprietary rules that leave "inconclusive" and "unconfirmed" points [56]; calls can be flipped manually and the override is recorded only as "Manual" [57]; the single open repository found is a 0-star GPL notebook [64].
3. **What exists today.** TaqMan Genotyper (Windows 10), CFX Maestro, DA2 Genotyping module, KlusterCaller/SNPviewer; all closed, Windows-centric, no API, no cross-method agreement, no confidence per sample beyond a quality flag.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: two-dye raw curves per well from RDML (or endpoint RFU pairs for KASP). Deterministic methods: BCGA best-cycle difference + PAM clustering + silhouette [53] and the endpoint polar-coordinate method [57] as two members of the robustness grid; optional control-anchored calling when reference genotypes are on the plate. Output per sample: call, silhouette/confidence, agreement between methods, distance to the nearest alternative cluster. Decision Cards only for disagreements or low-silhouette samples ("sample B7 silhouette 0.30; endpoint says AB, full-curve says AA; re-run or accept?"), impact-ranked by downstream effect (e.g. Hardy–Weinberg deviation, allele frequency). Record: calls with method provenance, manual overrides with reasons. MCP: `qpcr_genotype`.
5. **Feasibility.** BCGA is fully described [53]; PAM/silhouette are in scikit-learn (BSD); no public multi-dye raw datasets found (gap; generate from a public TaqMan SNP plate run on the team's instrument or ask a core). Effort: 1–2 weeks. Hardest part: dye cross-talk and ROX normalisation differences across instruments in RDML.
6. **Why qPilot specifically.** It is the only open tool with per-well raw curves across the whole study; "agreement between reasonable methods" is the natural confidence statement for a call; the record logs human overrides as decisions.
7. **Risks and confidence.** SNP genotyping by qPCR is flat and partly migrating to arrays; KASP users have endpoint data only. Confidence: medium.

### D4. miRNA panel normalisation robustness plus biofluid QC cards

1. **Persona and situation.** A biomarker researcher running miRCURY or TaqMan Advanced panels (96/384 wells, 84–179 miRNAs) on plasma/serum from 20–200 subjects, with spike-ins, an inter-plate calibrator and the hemolysis pair on every plate; one study per month.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** 43 vs 74 "up" miRNAs depending on normaliser, with spike-ins inverting the trend [23]; global mean valid only for large panels [22]; hemolysis effects up to 13-fold with spike-ins unable to correct them [24]; vendor ΔCq > 7 rule applied by hand [26]; chemistry-dependent cross-reactivity up to 51 % [25]; analysis tools are a 2019 Windows app [29], a web portal [26] or an Artistic-licensed R package [61].
3. **What exists today.** GeneGlobe, ExpressionSuite, HTqPCR, Excel.
4. **The concrete feature.** Panel presets (plate maps with spike-in, IPC, hemolysis and candidate-reference wells for the Qiagen serum/plasma, cancer and exosome panels and TaqMan Advanced cards [26][28]). Deterministic methods: expressed-call threshold (Cq ≤ 35), global mean in log space [22], NormFinder/geNorm-selected endogenous sets (geNorm already in qPilot), spike-in normalisation, IPC inter-plate calibration (geometric mean of IPCs [46]), hemolysis flag ΔCq(miR-23a-3p − miR-451a) > 7 [26]. Robustness grid over normaliser family × expressed threshold × hemolysis exclusion × IPC on/off; per-miRNA "stable under n of m analyses" badge. Decision Cards: "Sample 17 hemolysis ΔCq 8.4: exclude (changes 6 miRNAs' significance) or keep"; "Spike-in and endogenous normalisation disagree on direction for 12 miRNAs". Record: normaliser choice with reason, exclusions, the robustness table.
5. **Feasibility.** Methods published [22][23][24][26]; Faraldi's data in supplements (licence to check); no raw-curve public set. Effort: 1–2 weeks on top of existing geNorm code. Hardest part: importing 384-well TLDA/OpenArray exports (roadmap item already named) and pre-amplification uniformity checks.
6. **Why qPilot specifically.** The robustness engine was built for "which normaliser" questions; miRNA is the case where that question has no agreed answer and the biggest effect.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Clarida may cover miRNA normalisation; vendor web tools are free. Confidence: medium-high on pain, medium on differentiation.

### D5. Objective copy-number calls with propagated error (CNV and mtDNA-CN)

1. **Persona and situation.** A human-genetics or mitochondrial lab calling gene copy number (TaqMan Copy Number Assays with RNase P reference) or mtDNA-CN (ND1/RPPH1) on hundreds of samples, in quadruplicate, several plates per study.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** CopyCaller is a Windows executable whose algorithm is described only as "confidence in copy number calls" [47]; qPCR mtDNA-CN associations are orders of magnitude weaker than sequencing-based estimates, with plate random effects and pipetting-order corrections needed [45]; error propagation and inter-run calibration are published but only in qBase (non-commercial licence) [46].
3. **What exists today.** CopyCaller, qbase+, Excel.
4. **The concrete feature.** ΔΔCq with multiple references (geometric mean), delta-method standard errors and inter-run calibrators [46]; copy number = 2 × ratio with a confidence interval; plate as random effect and pipetting-order covariate option [45]; calls with "distance to the nearest integer" and a Decision Card only when the CI spans two integers; robustness across reference assay choice and calibrator. Record: per-sample CI and call provenance.
5. **Feasibility.** Formulas public [46]; D'haene's calling criteria paywalled (gap: reimplement from qBase error model and state the rule); no public dataset (gap). Effort: 1 week. Hardest part: duplex (two-dye) wells in RDML and Crt-vs-Ct import from QuantStudio.
6. **Why qPilot specifically.** Error propagation plus robustness is the honest version of "confidence"; the record keeps the rounding decision.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Niche and flat; ddPCR dominates new CNV work. Confidence: medium.

### D6. ChIP-qPCR percent-input module with background shown, not subtracted

1. **Persona and situation.** A chromatin lab running ChIP-qPCR on 5–20 loci with input dilutions and IgG controls, several times a month, reporting bar charts.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** The reference methods paper recommends %input or relative-to-control with ≥ 4 replicates and advises against fold enrichment, which remains common [48]; open tools are unlicensed scripts with a five-column samplesheet [49] and a 2026 agent "skill" producing an Excel audit workbook with exclude flags [50].
3. **What exists today.** Excel; WARPqpcr; vendor calculators.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: samplesheet (well, plate, sample, locus, role: ChIP/IgG/input, input percent and extra dilution). Methods: %input with dilution correction, relative-to-control locus, background displayed; efficiency check; replicate CV. Cards: "IgG Cq within 1 cycle of ChIP at locus X: enrichment not supported"; "input dilution factor missing". Record and MCP as usual.
5. **Feasibility.** Days of work; formulas in [48]. Datasets: none public found (gap).
6. **Why qPilot.** Small, generalises the samplesheet/role model; the 2026 "skill" shows agent-era demand for auditable ChIP-qPCR.
7. **Risks.** Low impact; confidence medium.

### D7. Research "rare-target interpretation" rule pack (EuroMRD-style quantitative range, sensitivity, positive-below-QR)

1. **Persona and situation.** A research lab quantifying a rare target (patient-specific ASO-qPCR in research protocols, residual plasmid, persistence of vector sequences, ctDNA research assays) against a dilution series, in triplicate, with background controls.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Replicate-level rules exist and were validated on > 1,400 assays [66] and 1,262 samples [68], but are applied by hand under external QA [67]; FDA asks that persistence be judged against a demonstrated LOQ with triplicates and spikes [31]; CML scoring uses a Poisson 3-copy floor and reference-copy thresholds [69].
3. **What exists today.** Spreadsheets and instrument software; EuroMRD QA rounds.
4. **The concrete feature.** A rule pack implementing QR and sensitivity definitions (ΔCq ≤ 1.5, ≥ 3 Cq from background, 2.6–4.0 Cq per 10-fold step, slope −3.1 to −3.9, R² ≥ 0.98, ≥ 3 points, ≥ 2 logs), the 2024 "low positive <QR" criteria, Poisson floor and the one-significant-figure reporting; robustness over thresholds; cards for samples in the grey zone. 5. **Feasibility.** All rules public [66][67][68][69]; effort 1 week; no public dataset (gap).
6. **Why qPilot.** Rules are deterministic and record-friendly; robustness over thresholds exposes grey-zone fragility.
7. **Risks.** Clinical adjacency; must never be presented as diagnostic. Confidence: medium.

### D8. Biomark/single-cell import with Fano-factor LoD and detection-failure culling (lower priority)

1. **Persona.** Remaining Biomark HD/X9 users (stem-cell and immunology labs).
2. **Evidence.** LoD by Fano factor, culling at 3× median detection-failure score, melt-curve non-specific flags [59]; hurdle-model QC in a GPL package [58][62]; Biomark CSV with Pass/Fail calls [61]; vendor software Windows-only [63]; GitHub scripts unlicensed and old [64].
3. **Today.** Real-Time PCR Analysis software, HTqPCR, MAST, lab scripts.
4. **Feature.** Biomark CSV importer (2,304/9,216 rows, Call column), LoD/Fano selection, Log2Ex transform, culling, melt flags, robustness over LoD; no hurdle model (GPL) unless reimplemented from the paper [58].
5. **Feasibility.** Days; HTqPCR example CSV documents the format [61].
6. **Why qPilot.** Cheap; demonstrates domain-agnostic import. 
7. **Risks.** Shrinking community. Confidence: low-medium.

## 5. Open questions and what I could not verify

1. Practitioner complaint evidence (Reddit, ResearchGate, Biostars, vendor forums) was not collected because the web-search budget was exhausted; the pain inventory relies on method-comparison papers, reporting audits and tool states.
2. TRN membership/lab count: not stated on the site [1]; EuroMRD counts are explicit (69–71 labs) [65][67].
3. Hays et al. 2024 (AAPS J) full text was bot-walled; only the abstract is cited [30a]. The Global CRO Council 2022 paper likewise [30b].
4. The WHO lentiviral integration copy-number standard: the 2017 paper describes a plan [38]; NIBSC's site did not resolve, so current availability, code and assigned units are unverified.
5. D'haene 2010 CNV calling criteria, Eisenberg 2015 well-position model, Picard 2021 mtDNA-CN pitfalls, Morinha 2020 ecology guidelines, Schwarzenbach 2015 and Mestdagh 2014: paywalled or author-manuscript only; cited from abstracts or via other papers.
6. No public datasets with raw curves were found for VCN, biodistribution, AAV titre, CNV, SNP genotyping, ChIP-qPCR or miRNA panels; Cq-level public data exist only for telomere and single-cell examples. Supplementary data of Faraldi 2019, Lu 2020 and Dobnik 2019 may contain Cq tables (not checked).
7. Whether a formal "MIQE for miRNA" exists: I found none; miRNA papers cite MIQE 2009 and vendor QC schemes.
8. BCGA code availability and licence (2006 university URL) unverified.
9. GeneGlobe and Thermo Connect analysis-module capabilities could not be read (JavaScript apps).
10. The FDA "≤ 5 copies per cell" VCN figure often quoted in the field does not appear in the 2024 CAR-T guidance text I read, which instead asks for a risk-justified criterion [32]; treat the number as folklore unless a primary FDA source is found.
11. Community sizes for SNP genotyping, ChIP-qPCR, CNV and mtDNA-CN by qPCR: no numbers found.
