## 1. How this corner of the niche works

The four communities covered here share one thing that the gene-expression world does not: the number that matters is **absolute** (copies per reaction, per litre, per gram, or a copy-number ratio) or **binary** (detected / not detected), and it is used to make a decision outside the lab: close a beach, declare a river invaded by carp, label a food lot as GM, report a virus trend to a health department. Standard curves, limits of detection (LOD) and quantification (LOQ), controls for inhibition and contamination, and multi-laboratory agreement are therefore first-class concerns, and they are mostly handled today in R scripts, Excel workbooks and instrument software.

### 1.1 Environmental DNA (eDNA): targeted species detection

**Takeaway.** eDNA qPCR is run by federal and state agencies (USGS, USFWS, USDA-APHIS, US Army Corps, Canadian ministries), university labs and a growing consultancy sector. The published standard for LOD/LOQ is Klymus et al. 2020 (a 7-lab, 36-assay study with a public-domain R script), the published standard for assay readiness is the Thalinger et al. 2021 five-level scale, and the only tooling is a 5-input R script, a static website and a few R occupancy packages. Reporting is poor: 30% of 546 published assays were "incomplete" and none reached the top level [6].

**Who runs it and at what volume**
- The USFWS Whitney Genetics Laboratory (La Crosse, WI) processes roughly 9,000–10,000 eDNA samples per year for invasive bighead and silver carp monitoring in the Upper Mississippi and Great Lakes watersheds, April–December, by qPCR, under a Quality Assurance Project Plan (QAPP) and SOPs (2025 edition dated 25 March 2025) [9][10].
- The Klymus 2020 inter-laboratory study drew on seven labs: USGS (three centres), US Army ERDC, USDA-APHIS National Wildlife Research Center, USFWS Whitney Genetics Lab, University of Victoria, Washington State University and the Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry [1].
- Commercial providers: NatureMetrics (UK) claims 600+ client organisations in over 126 countries and 4.38 million validated species detections; its page does not give sample counts or accreditation status [22]. The authors of the validation-scale paper include staff from NatureMetrics, RSK ADAS and Sinsoma GmbH (all for-profit eDNA service companies), and the scale's website is hosted by NatureMetrics [6][7].
- UK great crested newt (GCN) monitoring is the canonical "operational" eDNA assay: validated against conventional surveys, used for management since 2014, and UK laboratories participating in GCN monitoring take part in proficiency tests [6]. (I could not reach the Natural England protocol text; see gaps.)
- Thalinger et al. counted 546 single-species assays in 327 papers to April 2019: ~60% designed for qPCR, ~35% endpoint PCR; 227 target fish, 74 amphibians; >40% use COI, ~23% cytb; >80% of assays appear in only one paper [6].

**The workflow, step by step (targeted eDNA)**
1. Assay design and validation "in silico" (sequence databases), "in vitro" (tissue of target and non-target species, dilution series for LOD/LOQ), "in situ" (field samples where the species is known present/absent) [6][8].
2. Field sampling: multiple field replicates per site, field blanks, immediate preservation, metadata [8].
3. Lab: dedicated clean room, unidirectional workflow, extraction with negative controls, qPCR with several technical replicates per sample, an internal positive control (IPC) to detect inhibition, no-template controls (NTC), positive controls [6][8][10].
4. Calling: decide which samples count as detections, apply contamination rules, then (ideally) occupancy modelling to estimate detection probability [6][8].
5. Reporting to managers/regulators: the USFWS QAPP routes lab results to an "eDNA Data Steward" and "eDNA Program Coordinator", who report to state partners and the public; samples are archived at −80 °C for up to five years [10].

**Standard-curve and LOD/LOQ practice (Klymus et al. 2020, Environmental DNA 2:271–282, CC BY)**
- Each of 7 labs ran replicate standard curves totalling 20–96 replicates per standard concentration for each of 36 assays; standards were synthetic double-stranded DNA of the target region (one non-linearised plasmid) [1].
- Definitions proposed: LOD = lowest standard concentration with ≥95% positive replicates; LOQ = lowest standard concentration whose coefficient of variation (CV) of back-calculated copies is <35%, where CV is computed from efficiency E and SD(Cq) with Forootan's formula; LOQ cannot be lower than LOD [1].
- Two methods: a **discrete threshold** method (LOD/LOQ restricted to tested standards) and a **curve-fitting** method: binary detection results fitted with all 15 log-dose sigmoid functions in the R package `drc`, best model chosen by log-likelihood, AIC, lack-of-fit and residual variance; LOQ modelled from CVs with exponential-decay, linear and polynomial models choosing the lowest residual standard error [1].
- Results: discrete LODs 4–250 copies/reaction; modelled LODs 2.19–260; discrete LOQs 10–1,920; modelled LOQs 6–839 copies/reaction [1].
- **Effective LOD**: with single-reaction detection probability p, n replicates give 1 − (1 − p)^n; for a 95% target, p = 1 − 0.05^(1/n); moving from 1 to 8 replicates lowered the effective LOD by a mean of 11.1-fold (SD 9.24), with three assays dropping 32–41-fold [1].
- **Model-choice sensitivity**: the 15 functions produced LODs differing by 1.07- to 8.72-fold on the same data; Weibull type II and 3-parameter asymptotic regression fitted best and solved 35 of 36 datasets, log-logistic and Michaelis–Menten solved 24, Weibull type I 21; more parameters gave lower LODs (p = 0.0186) [1].
- Outliers: the script flags Cq values <90% or >110% of the standard's median Cq but removes nothing; the user must decide and rerun [1].
- Standard stability: two assays with high LOD/LOQ dropped to typical values after the lab added TE buffer and tRNA to the standard dilutions; the authors recommend stabilisers in all standard dilutions, low-retention plastics and fresh dilutions at least weekly [1].
- Interpretation: detections below the LOD are not "noise" and should not be censored; detections should be ≤40 cycles with uniform curve morphology and clean NTCs; concentrations below the LOQ are to be treated qualitatively [1].
- Reporting minimum: (1) concentration range and number of replicate standards per concentration, (2) the determination approach, (3) the LOD probability (e.g., 95%) and LOQ precision (e.g., 35% CV) criteria, plus the definitions used; when an assay is transferred to a new lab its LOD "should be quantified to confirm similarity to the assay's published LOD before use" [1].
- The paper states that "eDNA studies rarely report these parameters and the methods used to measure them" and cites an interlaboratory microbial source-tracking study in which differing LOD/LOQ definitions made labs report the same raw result as detection vs non-detection [1].

**Tooling and its state**
- `cmerkes/qPCR_LOD_Calc` (USGS, public domain, DOI 10.5066/P9GT00GB): an R script with "5 lines for user input"; input CSV with Target, Cq, SQ (expected standard concentration) columns; blank/NA/"Undetermined" = negative; outputs three plots and four files (Analysis Log, Assay Summary CSV, LOD confidence intervals, Potential Outliers). README marks it "preliminary or provisional"; 26 stars, 12 forks, 14 commits; one open issue (January 2025, legends cut off in the LOD plot) and no closed issues [2][4]. A caveat in the README: if the SQ column contains calibration-curve copy numbers instead of nominal standard concentrations, the models break [2]. The companion `LOD_Analysis` repo (1 star) reproduces the paper's figures [3].
- Data: USGS release 10.5066/P9AKHU1R, public domain, published 2019-07-23, metadata updated 2022-04-28: `GEDWG_LOD_DATA3.csv` (raw multi-lab Cq data for standards of known concentration) and `Comparison-Data.csv` (calculator output under different model settings) [5].
- `edna-validation.com`: static site with the 122-variable checklist, 546-assay database and submission by email; no computation, "All Rights Reserved", last dated material September 2021 [7].
- Occupancy modelling: `eDNAoccupancy` (R, USGS public domain, v0.2.7, 6 stars, needs mvtnorm/pROC/mcmcse; site → sample → replicate Bayesian model) [16]; `msocc` (R, Bayesian multi-scale occupancy built for Yellowstone River eDNA monitoring, 5 stars, no licence stated, Shiny app) [17]; `occumb` (R, GPL-3.0, CRAN, JAGS/NIMBLE, for metabarcoding) [18]. The USGS toolbox page notes that occupancy models should account for false positives [8].
- Reporting/data standards: GBIF's guide "Publishing DNA-derived data" defines a Category III (targeted species detection by qPCR/ddPCR) and recommends DNA-derived-data extension fields including `quantificationCycle` (Cq preferred term per RDML), `thresholdQuantificationCycle`, `baselineValue`, `automaticThresholdQuantificationCycle`, `automaticBaselineValue`, `pcr_primer_lod` (example value 51), `pcr_primer_loq` (184), `estimatedNumberOfCopies`, `amplificationReactionVolume`, `probeReporter`, `probeQuencher`, `pcr_analysis_software`, `contaminationAssessment`, `experimentalVariance`, `concentration`; absence records "are highly dependent on the detection limit of the specific assay" and should carry enough information to judge false negatives and positives; licence CC BY-SA 4.0 [19][20]. Canada's CSA W214:21 sets minimum reporting requirements for eDNA studies in a QA/QC context for qPCR and metabarcoding, including assessing the probability of Type I/II errors (per the SCC notice and search summary; the standard text itself is paywalled and was not read) [21].
- USGS "eDNA Best Practices" toolbox (September 2022): for targeted studies "you should define the limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ)"; defines effective LOD as above and notes LOQ thresholds "usually ranging from 25% to 35%" CV, with others defining LOQ as ≥90% replicate detection; "You must test for PCR inhibition … an internal positive control (IPC) must be added"; "engage with lead management authorities … to decide on … the definition of a positive detection"; recommends MIQE plus Goldberg et al. 2016 tables; points to Lesperance et al. 2021 for LOD/LOQ statistics [8].

**What the USFWS QAPP actually does with controls (2025, 61 pages)**
- Field blanks run on the carp "IC combo" assay: if a field blank amplifies, all samples between the adjacent clean field blanks are "negated" (example: blanks 11, 22, 33 with 22 positive → samples 12–32 negated) [10].
- Hood controls (three per range): any target amplification negates all associated samples; negative extraction controls: any product negates the batch; positive extraction control passes if ≥1 of 8 replicates amplifies; PCR positive control passes at 1 of 2 replicates [10].
- Standard curves: minimum E = 80–120% and R² ≥ 0.95; "errant standard curve points may be dropped to recover curve statistics" as long as a 3-point curve remains, because the curves are "for assessing assay performance only, and not for quantification of starting copy number"; failing curves force re-amplification of the plate [10].

**Contamination and replicate rules in the literature**
- Sepulveda et al. 2020 reviewed 155 papers: 91% of targeted studies (100/110) reported at least one negative control; PCR controls 71%, field controls 51%, extraction controls 36%; 49% of targeted studies had lab-only controls, so field contamination "could have gone undetected"; only ~6% of targeted studies per year reported negative-control amplification, most did not report control results, and the ways of handling amplified controls "have been criticized" as subjective. For Asian carp and dreissenid mussels, agencies require agreement between two independent assays and resampling before scoring positive [11].
- Serrao et al. 2018: "threshold rationales are rarely reported in eDNA studies"; they propose ROC curves to set defensible Cq thresholds separating detection from non-detection [13].
- Mauvisseau et al. 2019 (freshwater pearl mussel): two assays with 10 replicates per standard concentration; COI assay LOD 0.78 pg and LOQ 7.8 pg tissue DNA; 16S assay detected 0.78 pg in only 1 of 10 replicates; lab sensitivity "can differ largely" from natural conditions; natural replicates from the same mesocosm differed significantly in 2 of 5 cases; bootstrap analysis of false-negative probability versus number of natural and technical replicates. Note their LOD definition (last standard dilution detected with Ct <45) predates Klymus and illustrates the definitional churn [12].
- Thalinger et al. 2021 interpretation rule: with Level 1–2 assays it is "impossible to tell" presence or absence from a PCR result; Level 3 positives mean "likely present" only if field negative controls are negative, the lab is eDNA-appropriate and positives are sequenced; Level 4–5 non-detections mean "likely absent" given appropriate timing and replication; the LOD is the Level 4 minimum criterion and detection-probability modelling is a Level 5 criterion [6].

**Generic LOD/LOQ methodology that the eDNA papers lean on**
- Forootan et al. 2017: LoD = ≥95% positive replicates, fitted with a sigmoidal (logistic) curve; LoQ = CV ≤35% of back-calculated concentrations; Poisson sampling alone gives a 3-molecule floor at 95%; resampling CIs need ~20+ replicates per concentration; non-detects bias the SD low if dropped; LoQ cannot be below LoD [14].
- Kralik & Ricchi 2017 (microbial diagnostics): LOD at 95% replicates for the whole method including extraction; LOQ as CV <25%; practical efficiency 90–105%; ≥6 log10 dynamic range; R² >0.98; ≥6 replicates for LOD (10–15 better); repeatability ≥15 repeats at four spike levels; reproducibility ≥8 labs; DNA recovery often ~30% or lower must be included [15].

**Inferences (eDNA)**
- The field has converged on definitions (95% LOD, 25–35% CV LOQ, effective LOD for n replicates) but not on tooling: the reference implementation is a provisional R script that most practitioners run once per assay, cannot see raw curves, and leaves outlier and model choices to a human with no record of the choice.
- The replicate-rule and contamination-negation logic is procedural, documented in agency QAPPs, and conclusion-flipping; it is exactly the kind of judgment call qPilot's Decision Cards are designed to capture and record.
- The GBIF extension gives a ready-made, licence-clean target schema for exporting per-well and per-assay facts (Cq, threshold, baseline, LOD, LOQ, software).

### 1.2 Wastewater-based epidemiology (WBE), post-2020

**Takeaway.** Hundreds of labs (academic, commercial, municipal, state) quantify SARS-CoV-2 N1/N2 and now a dozen other pathogens in sewage; CDC's NWSS prescribes what to report (a data dictionary of ~100 fields incl. LOD, inhibition, recovery, normalisation marker) but not how to analyse plates; inter-lab variability is large (7 log10 range in recovery and LOD across 36 SOPs) and the only open analysis packages are either archived (WES) or downstream of qPCR (wwinference, PHES-ODM).

**Scale and actors**
- CDC's NWSS receives data from state/territorial/local health departments (funded through the ELC cooperative agreement), a national testing contract (currently Verily Life Sciences; previously Biobot Analytics) and the academic WastewaterSCAN partnership (Stanford, Emory, Verily); it publishes influenza A, COVID-19, RSV, measles, H5 and mpox data, updated every Friday [26].
- WastewaterSCAN samples "nearly 150 locations across 40 states" serving >39 million people, tracks "more than a dozen" diseases, and displays results "PMMoV Adjusted (× 1M)"; its data licence is CC BY-NC 4.0 and research use requires contacting the team [26][28].
- The NWSS public SARS-CoV-2 concentration dataset holds 211,035 rows (columns: key_plot_id, date, pcr_conc_lin, normalization) and is archived as of 12 September 2025 with no new data added [27].
- Pecson et al. 2021 (Water Research Foundation study): 32 labs (17 academic, 6 commercial, 4 non-municipal government, 3 municipalities, 2 test manufacturers) ran 36 SOPs in eight method groups [29].

**Methods and rules**
- CDC testing guidance (archived page): RT-qPCR or RT-ddPCR; targets N1, N2 (CDC) and E_sarbeco; "You must include quantitative measurement controls for all SARS-CoV-2 RNA quantification methods" (calibration curve from a control of known concentration for RT-qPCR); use RNA not DNA controls, aliquoted, stored ≤−70 °C; NTCs each run, extraction blanks per batch, matrix recovery controls (murine or bovine coronavirus, BRSV); inhibition assessed by dilution scaling or spiked RNA versus spiked NTC; normalisation markers PMMoV, crAssphage, HF183, Lachno3 [25].
- CDC reporting guidance: labs convert copies/reaction to copies per volume of unconcentrated sample accounting for template volume, extraction concentration factor and sample concentration; presence = Ct <40 in exponential amplification (qPCR) or ≥3 positive droplets (dPCR), any assay or replicate; recovery fraction; inhibition measurement required; submissions via the DCIPHER portal using the NWSS Reporting Data Dictionary v5.2.3 (28 June 2024, Excel or four CSVs); trends by linear regression on log10 concentrations with ≥3 points, non-detects set to half the detection limit, optional weighted least squares; percent daily change = (10^slope − 1) × 100; CDC says point estimates of community infection "should not currently be used" [24].
- Pecson 2021 QAPP rules: a standard curve required on every plate with environmental samples; plasmid standards must be linearised; inhibition control = exogenous target in two wells with extract versus two with water, ΔCt >1.0 → dilute; qPCR results more than 1 Ct below the lowest quantifiable standard = non-detect; dPCR LOD typically ≤2 positive partitions of 10,000–20,000; two-thirds of SOPs had at least one replicate marked non-detect for falling outside the standard-curve range; standard curves built only from standards with quantifiable results; recovery <0.01% excluded [29].
- Pecson 2021 results: 80% of recovery-corrected results fell within ±1.15 log10 genome copies/L across 36 SOPs; within a single SOP SD 0.13 log10; recovery efficiency and LOD spanned 7 log10 across methods; platform (RT-qPCR vs RT-dPCR), primer set and pasteurisation "generally resulted in small differences compared to other sources of variability"; "the same SOP or laboratory should be selected to track SARS-CoV-2 trends at a given facility" [29].
- Reference material: NIST RGTM 10169 (synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA fragments, value-assigned by RT-dPCR, "compatible with RT-qPCR") shipped to 227 requesters in 33 countries over ~30 months; the programme ended May 2023 [34].

**Tools**
- WES (R, "Wastewater and Environmental Sampling", standard-curve and amplification-efficiency helpers, CC BY 4.0 per r-universe listing) was removed from CRAN on 2024-08-19 because "issues were not corrected in time" [32].
- PHES-ODM (Public Health Environmental Surveillance Open Data Model): v3.0.1, 15 report tables + 6 lookup tables, used or planned in 23 countries (EU DEEP platform, Canada's NML, Ontario), licence stated as CC BY 4.0 in the README but CC-BY-SA-4.0 in the repository sidebar; the project entered "long-term maintenance" in September 2026; QA/QC measures "being developed through a working group"; the docs do not show per-replicate Cq or standard-curve storage [31].
- CDC `wwinference` (R, Apache-2.0, Stan/EpiNow2) estimates infections from wastewater concentrations plus counts; it consumes concentrations, not plates [33].
- Lab-level qPCR→copies/L work is done in spreadsheets and instrument software; I found no maintained open tool that starts from raw amplification curves for WBE (gap; see section 5).

**Inferences (WBE)**
- The analytical bottleneck is upstream of every public tool: per-plate standard curves (required by QAPPs), drift across months of weekly runs, inhibition dilution decisions, recovery correction, non-detect rules and unit conversion, all feeding a 100-field submission file. That is a multi-plate, time-series, decision-heavy problem with an audit requirement.
- The programme's funding and platform picture is shifting (public concentration dataset archived in 2025; dPCR adoption by major providers), which raises the risk for a qPCR-only feature; the EPA recreational-water qPCR methods (next section) are a steadier anchor for the same mathematics.

### 1.3 Environmental microbiology absolute quantification (EPA water methods, 16S load) and the EMMI guidelines

**Takeaway.** EPA's Clean Water Act qPCR methods are the most prescriptive public specification of absolute-quantification QC anywhere: numeric acceptance criteria for R², efficiency, IAC inhibition offsets, control Cq SDs, lower limits of quantification, composite standard curves pooled across runs with ANCOVA, all implemented in an official Excel workbook. The EMMI survey of 100 recent papers shows how rarely the broader field reports the basics.

- EPA lists its qPCR methods for ambient water: 1609/1609.1 (enterococci with internal amplification control, IAC), 1611/1611.1 (enterococci), 1696/1696.1 (human-associated HF183/BacR287), 1697/1697.2 (HumM2), Method B (Bacteroidales); none are approved under 40 CFR 136, their presence "does not imply a regulatory requirement" [35].
- Method 1696.1 (2022 draft) Table 2 acceptance criteria: R² ≥ 0.98 and amplification efficiency 0.90–1.10 from a single-run calibration curve of ≥5 concentrations in at least triplicate; NTC (6 per run) and method blanks (3 per batch, triplicate) must be "Undetermined"; IAC proficiency: multiplex VIC Cq SD ≤ 1.16; sample-processing-control (salmon DNA, Sketa22) SD ≤ 0.62 Cq; the LLOQ is defined as "the 95% prediction upper limit at the 1 log10 copy DNA standard dilution"; results reported as log10 copies; control Cq values >LLOQ are tolerated for quantification applications only if reported [36].
- Method 1609.1 (2015) quantifies "calibrator cell equivalents" (CCE) by a ΔΔCT comparison against calibrator samples of known cell number, with Sketa22 as sample-processing control (test-sample SPC Ct must be within 3 Ct of the calibrator mean, else inhibition or poor recovery) and an IAC co-amplified in every reaction (1.5 Ct offset criterion; IAC working stock chosen to give Ct 30–34); initial and ongoing precision-and-recovery (IPR/OPR) criteria; calculations live in the "Method 1609.1/1611.1 calculation spreadsheet" [37].
- Draft Method C (E. coli) uses an official Excel workbook that "automatically screens for poor-quality data using a set of previously proposed acceptance criteria, generates weighted linear regression (WLR) composite standard curves" pooling Ct data from ≥4 individual curves, with intercept 36.66–39.25 and slope −3.23 to −3.74 acceptance ranges plus an ANCOVA test before pooling; an earlier Bayesian "master standard curve" (MSC) was dropped because it "requires specialized statistical software and expertise"; 21 labs contributed 91 curves in 2016; WLR and MSC gave comparable intercepts/slopes and test-sample estimates [38].
- Sivaganesan et al. 2008 introduced the Bayesian master calibration curve: pooled runs with run-to-run variation in slope and intercept in a hierarchical model (WinBUGS), propagating experiment-to-experiment, replicate and plasmid-versus-genomic uncertainty into unknown concentrations [39].
- Public EPA datasets: Ct measurements of reference materials and blinded test samples from the multi-lab Method C study (Aw et al. 2019, Water Research 156:465–474), xlsx, DOI 10.23719/1502439, "EPA Science Hub license" [40]; standard-curve data and example test samples for Lane et al. 2020, xlsx, same licence pointer [41].
- EMMI guidelines (Borchardt et al. 2021, ES&T 55:10210): of the 100 most recent health-related environmental microbiology qPCR/dPCR papers, 33% assessed sampling recovery, 10% ran a sampling-equipment negative control, 20 and 38 reported extraction negative/positive controls (about half reported results), ~40% tested RT/PCR inhibition, 46 reported an NTC and only 13 reported its result, and "only about a quarter of the papers that used qPCR reported methods for determining … Cq values, standard curve slopes, and R² values"; they give a formula for the number of replicate negative controls (3 for 95% confidence of excluding 1 copy/reaction contamination, 5 for 99%) [30].
- Efficiency and standards: Ruijter et al. 2021 argue standard-curve efficiency can be biased by dilution error (actual dilutions 4–9% off intended in their test), that assuming E = 2 "often implies completely wrong treatment effects", and recommend per-reaction efficiency from amplification curves (LinRegPCR) and one-point calibration with ~6 replicates of a 1000-copy standard, reported per MIQE [42]. Gray/Hubert et al. found in 5 of 6 16S assays that pure clone standards amplified less efficiently than environmental DNA, inflating abundance estimates, fixed by adding background non-target 16S genes to the standards [44]; Oldham & Duncan 2012 report circular plasmid standards overestimating copies by up to 8-fold in a eukaryotic system [45]; Jian et al. 2020 (absolute 16S load, 10-fold series 10²–10⁷ copies, rrnDB copy-number correction, efficiencies 91–98%) note that the reference organism used for the standard curve changes quantification and limits cross-study comparability (code in R package `mare`, article CC BY) [43].
- ISO 20395:2019 (Biotechnology — requirements for evaluating the performance of quantification methods for nucleic acid target sequences — qPCR and dPCR) covers calibration-curve and molecular-counting designs, threshold setting, acceptance criteria, validation (precision, linearity, LOQ, LOD, trueness, robustness) and measurement uncertainty; 50 pages, ISO/TC 276/SC 1, under revision as ISO/CD 20395.2 [46].

**Inferences.** Everything a composite-standard-curve engine needs is already specified in public documents with numbers (EPA 1696.1 Table 2, Method C workbook ranges, ENGL criteria below) and backed by public multi-lab Ct datasets (EPA data.gov, USGS release). The field's habit is Excel.

### 1.4 GMO quantification (and food, plant-health and veterinary validation frameworks)

**Takeaway.** GMO testing is the most codified absolute-ratio application of qPCR: GM% = event copies / taxon copies × 100 from two standard curves, validated by 12-lab collaborative trials against ENGL numeric criteria, converted to mass fraction with published conversion factors, and reported with expanded uncertainty (k = 2) for a legal compliance decision at 0.9% or 0.1%. The maths is public (CC BY 4.0 JRC documents); the method texts (ISO 21570) and reference materials are paid; the computations happen in Excel.

- ISO 21570:2005 provides "an overall framework of quantitative methods" for GMO detection by PCR, 103 pages, for food and also feed and environmental plant samples [47]. GMOMETHODS, the EU database of validated reference methods, lists 228 methods (updated 7 October 2026) by event, element, construct and species; records cite ISO 21569:2005 and others; JRC-produced validation data are CC BY 4.0 while applicant methods are restricted [56]. The EURL GMFF "method validations" page lists 195 dossiers at various steps [57].
- ENGL minimum performance requirements (2015, applied by the EURL): slope of the standard curve −3.1 to −3.6 (E 90–110%) averaged over ≥5 runs; each R² ≥ 0.98; dynamic range at least 50–2,520 copies for the GM module and 50–56,000 copies for the taxon module, covering "at least 0.09 % and 4.5 % in GM mass fraction" for combined modules (2023 text); trueness within ±25% over the whole dynamic range; RSDr ≤25% from ≥15 results (also at 0.1% mass fraction for Reg. 619/2011 methods); LOQ ≤ lowest point of the dynamic range (0.09% or 50 copies), from ≥15 results; LOD <0.045% (combined) or <25 copies (individual module) at 95% confidence, e.g., 60 replicates with ≥59 positive at ~20, 10, 5 and 1 copies (1 copy expected to give ~36% negatives, CI 24–49%); robustness: RSDr and trueness under combined small changes ≤30%, tested at the LOQ; RSDR <35% over the dynamic range, <50% below 0.2% or 100 copies [50][51].
- The 2023 Part 2 extends the criteria to digital PCR: efficiency and R² "not applicable"; inhibition checked with ≥2 dilution levels in duplicate, diluted result × dilution factor within 25% of the undiluted; linearity by regression of expected versus observed GM content [51].
- Verification in a new lab (ENGL WG-MV v2, 2018): verification must be documented in the quality system (design, method description, acceptance criteria, test records, fitness statement); extraction performed at least twice (three recommended) on two test portions, different days/operators; defines practical LOD/LOQ in copies or mass fraction given a known number of taxon copies; usually on a certified reference material (CRM) [52].
- A worked example, EURL validation report VL-01/22 (maize MON 95275, 40 pages, CC BY 4.0): calibration from a 10% (copy-number-ratio) genomic DNA sample S1 diluted 1:4 to S2–S4 and 1:5 to S5, giving 250, 63, 16, 3.9 and 0.78 ng maize DNA per reaction; copies computed by dividing DNA mass by the published maize 1C value 2.73 pg; Cq plotted against log10 copies, regression per target, interpolation, GM% = event copies / haploid genome copies × 100; 12 national reference laboratories (NRLs) selected from 23 volunteers; participants "reported the raw data to the EURL GMFF on an Excel sheet that was designed, validated and distributed by the EURL"; one lab could not fill the template; ANOVA with Cochran and Grubbs outlier tests per ISO 5725-2 (0–3 outliers per level); efficiency 87–100% (event) and 91–105% (hmg), mean R² 1.00; RSDr 5.4–16%, RSDR 7.7–17%, bias 3.8–9.1% at 0.06–10% GM; absolute LOD <5 copies; a measured zygosity ratio of 0.58 means 0.058% copy ratio corresponds to 0.1% mass fraction; the report notes that calculations "were done over not rounded data … might create small inconsistencies in the numerical values reported" [55].
- Copy ratio vs mass fraction: EU law prescribes mass fraction; qPCR results depend on the CRM used for calibration and dPCR yields copy ratios, so the EURL publishes harmonised conversion factors per CRM with the formula CF_CRM = cp_T × (m_GM + m_nonGM) / (cp_E × m_GM); hemizygous maize gives CFs that "could already fluctuate from 0.34 to 0.66"; the result in copy ratio is divided by CF and uncertainties combined as u_comb = √(u_m² + u_CF²); example: 10 ± 3% cp/cp of DAS-40278-9 maize with CF 0.36 becomes 28 ± 10% m/m, while soya with CF 0.981 is unchanged [54].
- Measurement uncertainty (ENGL MU guidance, 3rd ed.): top-down approach from collaborative-trial RSDr (usable only if the lab's own RSDr is ≤ that value) plus an extraction component; coverage factor k = 2 permitted with N = 15 routine duplicates or 5 days × 5 replicates; report C ± U (k = 2) above the LOQ; compliance is assessed on C − U: only if that exceeds the threshold (0.9% labelling under Reg. 1829/2003; 0.1% under Reg. 619/2011) is non-compliance assumed "beyond reasonable doubt" at 95% [53]. Regulation (EU) 619/2011 defines the minimum required performance limit, requires EURL/ENGL-validated quantitative methods able to analyse at 0.1%, and reports x ± U with non-compliance when x − U ≥ 0.1% mass fraction per feed material [58].
- Food microbiology: ISO 16140-2:2016 is the protocol for validating alternative (proprietary) methods, including PCR kits, against a reference method across food, feed, environmental and primary-production samples [48]; MicroVal certifies methods to ISO 16140-2/-6/-7 through eight expert laboratories, with "80+" certificates valid four years (page figures inconsistent: 50+ elsewhere) [49]. I did not obtain the ISO 16140-2 statistical details (gap).
- Plant health: EPPO PM 7 standards relevant to validation include PM 7/98 (laboratory accreditation requirements, new version), PM 7/76, PM 7/122 (interlaboratory comparisons), PM 7/147 (reference material) [60]; the EPPO validation database holds roughly 335 validation sheets contributed by labs, with disclaimers and no Cq-level data or LOD columns in the listing [60].
- Veterinary: WOAH Terrestrial Manual chapter 1.1.6 defines LOD as the analyte amount in a matrix "that would produce a positive result at least a specified percent of the time", recommends two-stage dilution designs narrowing the 100%–0% interval, requires repeatability from independently extracted replicates, and Stage 3 reproducibility with at least three laboratories testing the same blinded panel of ≥20 samples [59].

**Inferences.** A GM% module is small to implement and completely specified, but its users are accredited official-control labs whose instruments and LIMS are locked down; the realistic qPilot users are method developers, NRL researchers, food-science academics and dPCR/qPCR comparison studies, not routine compliance testing.

### 1.5 Vocabulary (quick glossary for the developers)
- **Cq/Ct**: quantification cycle. **Standard (calibration) curve**: Cq versus log10 of known copies; slope −3.32 means 100% efficiency; E = 10^(−1/slope) − 1 [50].
- **LOD / LOQ / effective LOD / LLOQ**: see 1.1 and 1.3; LLOQ in EPA 1696.1 is a prediction-interval definition, different from Klymus's 95%-detection definition [1][36].
- **IPC / IAC / SPC**: internal positive control (exogenous template added to the sample well to detect inhibition), internal amplification control (co-amplified non-target sequence), sample processing control (DNA spiked before extraction to track recovery) [8][36][37].
- **Matrix recovery control**: a surrogate virus spiked before concentration (OC43, BCoV, BRSV) [25][29].
- **Normalisation marker**: PMMoV (pepper mild mottle virus), crAssphage, HF183, Lachno3: faecal-load markers used to divide the target concentration [25].
- **Occupancy model**: hierarchical model of site occupancy, sample-level occurrence and replicate-level detection probability [16][17].
- **Negation**: USFWS term for invalidating a range of samples because a control amplified [10].
- **CRM, CF, cp/cp vs m/m, RSDr/RSDR, trueness**: GMO vocabulary; see 1.4 [50][54][55].

## 3. Discoveries (ranked by impact × feasibility × fit)

### D1. "Assay LOD/LOQ passport": Klymus-2020 curve-fitting from raw plates, with the model-choice robustness grid and a recorded, re-runnable definition

1. **Persona and situation.** An eDNA assay developer (USGS/USFWS/Canadian ministry lab, university lab, consultancy) validating a new species assay or re-verifying a published one in their lab; also a wastewater or food-microbiology lab establishing LOD/LOQ for a target. Frequency: once per assay per lab, repeated at reagent/instrument changes (Klymus: re-determine on transfer) [1]; for the Whitney lab this sits in front of ~10,000 samples a year [9].
2. **Evidence of the pain.** P1–P3 above: "rarely report" [1]; 30% of assays Level 1 and LOD as the Level 4 gate [6]; model choice alone moves LOD up to 8.72-fold [1]; outliers flagged but handled manually [1][2]; standard degradation discovered only by eye [1]. The only implementation is a provisional 5-input R script that expects a hand-built CSV of Cq/SQ and has an open issue since January 2025 [2][4]. Cost (estimate): a failed or ambiguous LOD panel costs a plate of 20–96 replicates per standard (Klymus design) and a week; a wrongly high LOD inflates replicate requirements for every downstream sample.
3. **What exists today and why insufficient.** qPCR_LOD_Calc: Cq-only, no raw curves, no record of outlier decisions or precision-threshold choice, single model chosen by AIC, R/`drc` dependency (drc is distributed under GPL on CRAN; verify), provisional status [2]. edna-validation.com: manual checklist, no computation, all rights reserved [7]. Vendor software: standard curves only, no 95% detection modelling.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: RDML (or vendor export) of one or more LOD plates, with wells annotated as standards of nominal concentration (copies/reaction), negatives flagged by the engine's own amplification call (not by the instrument's "Undetermined"), plus NTCs. Deterministic method, per Klymus 2020 and Forootan 2017 [1][14]: (a) binary detection per standard from qPilot's per-well amplification call; (b) fit the family of log-dose sigmoid models (Weibull II, asymptotic regression, log-logistic, Michaelis–Menten, Weibull I with 2–4 parameters) by maximum likelihood (scipy/statsmodels, BSD), select by AIC/lack-of-fit, report the LOD at p = 0.95 with bootstrap CI, and the effective LOD for n = 1…12 replicates via p = 1 − 0.05^(1/n); (c) LOQ from the CV-versus-concentration model (exponential decay / polynomial) with the CV formula from E and SD(Cq), threshold default 35% but exposed; LOQ ≥ LOD enforced; (d) discrete-threshold LOD/LOQ computed alongside for comparison; (e) outlier candidates (Cq outside 90–110% of the standard's median) presented as a Decision Card with the LOD/LOQ under both choices; (f) a "standard stock drift" check: compare the plate's efficiency and intercept with the assay's previous passports and flag TE/tRNA-type degradation patterns (low-copy standards systematically late) [1]. Robustness: rerun under all solvable model functions × outlier in/out × CV threshold 25/30/35% and report the LOD spread (Klymus observed 1.07–8.72-fold) as the passport's robustness statement. Record: the Klymus reporting minimum (range and replicates per concentration, approach, criteria and definitions) written as engine facts [1], plus GBIF `pcr_primer_lod` / `pcr_primer_loq` fields [20]. MCP: `qpcr_lod(assay, plates)` returning LOD/LOQ/effective LOD with CIs and the robustness spread.
5. **Feasibility.** Public-domain validation data: USGS release 10.5066/P9AKHU1R with raw multi-lab Cq for 36 assays and the script's outputs for regression tests [5]; the paper's Table 1 gives expected modelled and discrete values per assay [1]. Libraries: scipy/statsmodels (BSD). Formats: RDML already supported. Effort: 2–3 weeks for the engine and tests; the hardest part is matching the paper's model-selection behaviour closely enough to pass a regression test on Comparison-Data.csv, and defining "positive" from raw curves consistently with the labs' instrument calls (Klymus used instrument calls; qPilot should report both).
6. **Why qPilot.** Raw curves let the engine make the positive/negative call itself and detect late, low-plateau curves rather than trusting "Undetermined"; the robustness engine turns the model-choice problem into a reported spread; Decision Cards make outlier removal and precision-threshold choices explicit and logged, which is exactly the reporting Klymus asks for; the record is the assay passport that the "lab memory" roadmap item wants.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Risk: `drc`'s exact fits may be hard to reproduce bit-for-bit; eDNA labs run many plates on Bio-Rad CFX and ABI instruments whose exports need the vendor-capture roadmap. The user base per lab is small (validation happens once per assay), so the value is in correctness and the record, not in daily throughput. Confidence: high that the pain is real and the method is specified; medium-high on adoption.

### D2. Detection-call and control-negation engine for eDNA surveys, with occupancy-ready export

1. **Persona and situation.** The eDNA processing lead and QA/QC specialist at an agency or consultancy lab, calling hundreds of samples per week against a QAPP; the resource manager who receives the calls. The USFWS runs this flow for ~10,000 samples a year with field blanks, hood controls, extraction negatives and 8 PCR replicates per sample on species plates [9][10].
2. **Evidence of the pain.** The definition of a positive detection must be negotiated with managers [8]; threshold rationales "rarely reported" [13]; amplified controls handled subjectively, and only ~6% of targeted studies per year even report NTC amplification [11]; negation rules are long prose in a 61-page QAPP ("samples 12–32 will be negated") [10]; Klymus: positives below LOD must not be censored, but must be interpreted with the effective LOD [1]; Thalinger: interpretation of a result depends on the assay's validation level and on field negative controls [6]. Cost (estimate): a mis-negated range can mean re-sampling a river reach; a false positive for invasive carp triggers agency response.
3. **What exists today.** SOP prose + Excel; ROC thresholds by a few groups [13]; occupancy packages that expect a hand-built detection matrix [16][17]; no tool connects the plate, the controls, the call rule and the record.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: the study's plates (RDML) with well roles (sample, field blank, hood control, extraction negative, NTC, positive control, IPC) and a "rule pack" (versioned YAML) encoding the lab's QAPP: positive-call rule (k of n replicates; Cq ≤ 40; optional ROC-derived Cq threshold with the ROC computed from known-positive/negative samples per Serrao [13]); control rules (field-blank range negation between adjacent clean blanks; hood-control batch negation; extraction-negative batch negation; positive-control minimums 1/8 and 1/2 per USFWS [10]); inhibition rule from IPC shift (ΔCq thresholds such as EPA's 3 Ct SPC / 1.5 Ct IAC or Pecson's 1.0 [29][37]) with "dilute and rerun" as the recommended option; multi-assay confirmation and resample-to-confirm logic (Sepulveda) [11]. Deterministic outputs: per-sample call (positive / negative / negated / inhibited / below-LOD positive), per-site summary with the effective LOD for the replicate count actually run [1], and the Thalinger interpretation sentence for the assay's level [6]. Decision Cards: every negation and every below-LOD positive is a card with evidence (the control's curve, the affected sample range) because these flip conclusions; autonomy level must be "human decides" for negations. Export: detection matrices in the site × sample × replicate layout consumed by eDNAoccupancy/msocc (CSV) and a Darwin Core archive with the DNA-derived data extension (occurrenceStatus Present/Absent with pcr_primer_lod/loq, quantificationCycle, threshold and baseline fields) for GBIF/OBIS publication [19][20]. MCP: `qpcr_call(study, rule_pack)`; an agent can ask "which sites are positive under rule pack v3 and how many calls change under v2".
5. **Feasibility.** Rule packs are small; the USFWS QAPP is public and precise enough to encode [10]; GBIF extension XML is public (CC BY-SA guide) [19][20]. Validation data: the Klymus release has standards only [5]; field datasets with controls and raw curves are scarce (a March 2026 Zenodo record for a European flat oyster assay contains environmental Cq values and the LOD/LOQ standard data [23]); the team will likely need a partner lab's de-identified plates. Occupancy model inside qPilot: optional later (PyMC/NumPyro are Apache-2.0); first ship the export. Effort: 3–4 weeks for rules, cards and exports. Hardest part: well-role capture (which wells are blanks, which range of samples they bracket) from vendor plate layouts; this is where the plate-design and vendor-capture roadmap items pay off.
6. **Why qPilot.** The decision space is explicit and conclusion-flipping; the robustness engine answers "how many of the reasonable call rules agree that this site is positive"; the hash-chained record is the defensible audit trail agencies want when a detection is challenged; the domain-agnostic core (roles, rules, cards) carries directly to wastewater and food QC.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Agencies may not run third-party software on monitoring data without a validation exercise; rule packs must never silently change a call (the escalation rule covers this). Confidence: medium-high on pain; medium on near-term adoption by agencies, higher for consultancies and university labs.

### D3. Composite (master) standard curve and absolute-quantification engine with published acceptance criteria and standard-matrix checks

1. **Persona and situation.** Wastewater surveillance labs (weekly runs for months), EPA recreational-water labs (beach season, Methods 1609.1/1696.1/Draft C), microbial-ecology labs quantifying 16S or functional genes, veterinary/plant-pathogen labs running absolute loads. Frequency: every plate; the composite curve is rebuilt whenever a new standard lot or run is added.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** P8, P9, P11, P12: per-plate curves required [29]; Bayesian master curves abandoned for an Excel WLR workbook with intercept/slope windows and ANCOVA [38]; EPA 1696.1's precise Table 2 criteria and LLOQ definition exist only in a PDF and a lab spreadsheet [36]; EMMI: a quarter of papers report slopes/R² [30]; 7 log10 inter-lab spread in LOD/recovery [29]; standards amplify unlike samples in 5/6 16S assays [44]; dilution error of 4–9% biases standard-curve efficiency [42]; WES removed from CRAN [32].
3. **What exists today.** Vendor software: one curve per plate, no pooling, no acceptance windows; EPA Excel workbook (Method C only); WinBUGS code (2008) [39]; R packages for Cq-level fitting; nothing from raw curves with per-well efficiency.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: all plates of a programme (RDML), standards annotated with lot and nominal copies, unknowns with sample volumes/concentration factors. Deterministic methods: (a) per-run weighted linear regression (weights ∝ 1/variance per standard, per Lane 2020) and a composite curve pooled across runs after an ANCOVA homogeneity test of slopes/intercepts (Lane 2020) [38], with an optional hierarchical model (run-level random slopes/intercepts, fit by REML in statsmodels, or Bayesian in PyMC/NumPyro) reproducing Sivaganesan's master curve [39]; (b) acceptance screens as switchable "method cards": EPA 1696.1 (R² ≥ 0.98, E 0.90–1.10, LLOQ = 95% prediction upper limit at the lowest standard, NTC/MB undetermined, IAC SD ≤ 1.16 Cq, SPC SD ≤ 0.62 Cq) [36]; Draft Method C (intercept 36.66–39.25, slope −3.23 to −3.74, ANCOVA) [38]; ENGL (slope −3.1 to −3.6, R² ≥ 0.98, ≥5 runs, trueness ±25%, RSDr ≤25%) [50]; (c) per-well efficiency from the amplification curve (LinRegPCR, already in qPilot) compared between standards and unknowns as a standard-matrix mismatch test (Gray/Hubert, Ruijter) with the one-point-calibration estimate shown as an alternative [42][44]; (d) unit chain: copies/reaction → copies per volume or mass with the CDC/NWSS conversion (template volume, extraction factor, concentration factor), recovery correction and normalisation marker ratios [24][25]; (e) non-detect policies (1 Ct below lowest standard; half-LOD; LLOQ-censoring) as explicit choices [24][29]. Robustness grid: per-run vs composite vs hierarchical × weighting × standard-point exclusion × efficiency source × non-detect policy → distribution of each sample's copies/L and of the trend slope. Decision Cards: drop a standard point (allowed in the USFWS QAPP but conclusion-relevant) [10]; accept a run whose curve fails a window; declare standard-lot drift; dilute an inhibited sample. Record: the full acceptance table per run and the composite-curve lineage (which runs, which lots). MCP: `qpcr_quantify(study, method_card)`.
5. **Feasibility.** Public data: EPA Method C multi-lab Ct data (Aw 2019; xlsx; EPA Science Hub licence, terms to be checked) [40] and Lane 2020 standard-curve data [41]; USGS multi-lab standards [5]; NIST RGTM 10169 is no longer distributed [34], so value assignment of standards is by the lab. Libraries: statsmodels (BSD), scipy, optional PyMC/NumPyro (Apache-2.0). Effort: 3–5 weeks including the three method cards. Hardest part: validating the hierarchical/WLR implementation against Lane's published intercept/slope comparisons and getting unit metadata (volumes, factors) into the record without a LIMS.
6. **Why qPilot.** It is the only candidate tool that already computes per-well efficiency from raw curves, so the standard-vs-sample efficiency check and the one-point-calibration alternative are nearly free; multi-plate studies are the native unit; the robustness engine answers the regulator's question "would the trend change under another reasonable calibration"; the record satisfies EPA/ENGL "document the verification" expectations (research use).
7. **Risks and confidence.** WBE is moving to dPCR (WastewaterSCAN reports PMMoV-adjusted dPCR-style outputs; platform not verified) and the NWSS public dataset was archived in 2025 [27][28]; the EPA beach methods and microbial ecology are steadier. Composite curves can hide real run failures if acceptance windows are loose; keep windows visible. Confidence: high on method and data, medium on which user segment adopts first.

### D4. NWSS/PHES-ODM-ready wastewater run packet: from plates to a submission with normalisation, trend and QC fields

1. **Persona and situation.** A state or academic WBE lab submitting weekly to a health department or to CDC's DCIPHER, or to a PHES-ODM instance in Canada/EU; a programme epidemiologist reading the trend.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** The NWSS minimum data set and ~100-field dictionary (v5.2.3) include quantification method, recovery efficiency, inhibition measurement, LOD and normalisation marker [24]; CDC's own trend method prescribes half-LOD substitution, log10 regression on ≥3 points and optional WLS [24]; labs convert copies/reaction to copies/volume by hand [24]; PHES-ODM is adopted in 23 countries but QA/QC parts are still "being developed" and the project is in maintenance mode [31]; WES was archived [32]; Pecson documents 36 different SOPs and 7 log10 LOD spread [29].
3. **What exists today.** DCIPHER CSV templates; PHES-ODM Excel templates and SQL schemas (CC BY/CC BY-SA, licence inconsistent) [31]; commercial providers' pipelines (Verily, Biobot) closed; wwinference downstream [33].
4. **The concrete feature.** Built on D3: a "run packet" exporter that maps engine facts to NWSS dictionary fields (target concentration, units, below-LOD flags, LOD per sample, inhibition detected/adjusted, recovery percent, human-faecal marker concentration and name, flow and population inputs) and to PHES-ODM `measures` long format; computes flow-population and marker normalisation and the CDC trend classification (percent daily change, sustained vs short-term) with the non-detect policy and regression weights as recorded choices [24]. Decision Cards: inhibition dilution, recovery exclusion (<0.01%, Pecson) [29], marker-vs-flow normalisation when population shifts. MCP: an agent assembles the weekly packet and lists the open cards for the human.
5. **Feasibility.** Field lists come from CDC's public dictionary (download link on the reporting page) and PHES-ODM CSV dictionaries [24][31]; no raw-curve public WBE dataset was found (gap), so validation would use a partner lab. Effort: 2–3 weeks on top of D3. Hardest part: keeping up with dictionary versions and the dPCR share of inputs.
6. **Why qPilot.** The decision log is the natural home for the "inhibition_adjust / rec_eff" provenance fields; agents can run the weekly packet; local-first suits health-department IT.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Programme funding and platform shifts (see D3); CDC templates may change; many WBE labs are dPCR-only. Confidence: medium.

### D5. GM% module to ENGL/ISO 21570 rules with conversion factors, expanded uncertainty and the compliance decision

1. **Persona and situation.** GMO method developers and NRL/EURL researchers running or verifying event-specific qPCR, food-science labs comparing qPCR and dPCR, students learning the EU framework; routine official control is out of scope (ISO 17025 environments).
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Collaborative-trial raw data are collected on an EURL-designed Excel sheet; one of 12 labs could not fill it; results computed "over not rounded data" create inconsistencies [55]; conversion from cp/cp to m/m needs per-CRM factors with uncertainty propagation [54]; MU must be estimated top-down and the decision made on C − U [53]; verification must be documented in the quality system [52].
3. **What exists today.** Instrument software for curves; Excel for GM%, CF and MU; GMOMETHODS for method lookup (228 methods) [56]; no open tool.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: plates with event and taxon assays, calibration samples with nominal GM% and DNA mass, 1C genome value (e.g., maize 2.73 pg) [55]. Deterministic: two standard curves → copies → GM% = event/taxon × 100 [55]; ENGL acceptance (slope, R², dynamic range, trueness, RSDr, LOQ/LOD, robustness) as a method card [50]; dPCR inhibition dilution test (within 25%) when dPCR inputs exist [51]; cp/cp → m/m via the published CF table with u_comb = √(u_m² + u_CF²) [54]; expanded uncertainty U = k·u_c with k = 2 given the recommended replication, reported as C ± U [53]; compliance statement against 0.9% / 0.1% on C − U [53][58]; ISO 5725-2 Cochran/Grubbs outlier screening for multi-lab datasets as Decision Cards [55]. Record: the WG-MV verification file structure (design, criteria, test records, fitness statement) [52]. MCP: `qpcr_gm_percent(study, event, crm)`.
5. **Feasibility.** All formulas and criteria are in CC BY 4.0 JRC documents [50]–[55]; the CF table is published (CF-CRM-values.pdf) [57]; validation reports give lab-level GM% but I did not find downloadable raw Cq data (gap); CRMs cost money; ISO 21570/21569 texts are paid [47]. Effort: 2 weeks for the maths and cards. Hardest part: test data.
6. **Why qPilot.** Dual-curve ratio with uncertainty is a small extension of D3; the record matches the regulatory habit of documented verification; robustness over calibration choices maps to MU.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Small research user base; official labs will not switch; EU specificity. Confidence: medium on usefulness, high on feasibility.

### D6. FAIR export and assay-transfer verification: auto-filled Thalinger checklist items, GBIF Darwin Core archive and a "same assay, new lab" comparison card

1. **Persona and situation.** The assay developer publishing a new assay (Environmental DNA journal, agency report), the lab adopting a published assay, and a data manager publishing detections to GBIF/OBIS.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** 20–76% of 122 validation variables are reported; details "are often spread across different sections … or ambiguously displayed" [6]; LOD must be re-quantified on transfer [1]; ENGL requires documented verification with specific replicate designs [52]; WOAH requires ≥3 labs and ≥20 samples for reproducibility [59]; GBIF's qPCR fields exist but are hand-filled [19][20].
3. **What exists today.** edna-validation.com manual entry; GBIF IPT manual mapping; Excel.
4. **The concrete feature.** From the record, emit (a) the machine-checkable subset of Thalinger's checklist and of Klymus's three reporting items: primer/probe sequences, PCR conditions, extract volume in PCR, number of technical replicates, LOD/LOQ with method and criteria, IPC used, NTC results, standard template type (gBlock/plasmid/genomic) [1][6]; items that can never be machine-checked (in silico database coverage, field sampling details, Sanger confirmation) listed as "human must attest"; (b) a Darwin Core archive with the DNA-derived data extension (fields in 1.1) [19][20]; (c) a transfer-verification card comparing the new lab's LOD/efficiency/intercept with the published passport (from D1) using the Klymus discrete and modelled LODs with CIs, and the WG-MV replicate design for quantitative assays [1][52].
5. **Feasibility.** Thalinger's checklist and data are on Figshare (10.6084/m9.figshare.12184860.v1) [6]; GBIF extension XML public [20]; effort 1–2 weeks after D1. Hardest part: deciding and documenting which of the 122 variables are machine-checkable from RDML.
6. **Why qPilot.** The record already contains the facts; the "no naked numbers" rule binds every checklist entry to an engine fact.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Checklists alone are "not a discovery" per the brief; the value here is the specific 122-variable/GBIF mapping and the transfer card. Confidence: medium.

### Which 2–3 domains qPilot should serve first
1. **Targeted eDNA** (D1, D2, D6): public-domain reference implementation and data to validate against, a clearly stated reporting standard, agencies with QAPPs, a consultancy sector, and raw-curve/decision/record fit. Highest.
2. **Environmental-microbiology absolute quantification** (D3, later D4): EPA methods supply numeric acceptance criteria and public multi-lab Ct datasets; the same engine serves WBE, beach monitoring and 16S load; dPCR drift is the main risk.
3. **GMO** (D5): fully specified, small, but a small research user base; do after D3.

## 5. Open questions and what I could not verify

- **Forum-level pain evidence** (ResearchGate, Reddit, eDNA mailing lists): not collected because the shared web-search budget was exhausted early in this session; the pain inventory rests on systematic literature surveys (Thalinger, Sepulveda, EMMI) and agency SOPs. A follow-up session should search for practitioner complaints about qPCR_LOD_Calc, IPC interpretation and NWSS submissions.
- **Lesperance et al. 2021 (Environmental DNA, LOD/LOQ statistical model, cited by the USGS toolbox)** and **Langlois et al. 2021 (edn3.164, often called the eDNA "MIQE")**: Wiley blocked both the fetcher and the browser; contents not read. Also unread: Goldberg et al. 2016, Hunter et al. 2017, Burian et al. 2021 (replicate thresholds). I found no paper titled "MIQE 2.0 for eDNA".
- **UK great crested newt scheme specifics** (12 replicates, positive rule, proficiency test operator, number of ponds per year): only the Thalinger summary was readable; Natural England/Defra pages did not yield the protocol.
- **Inter-laboratory ring trials for eDNA** beyond the Klymus 7-lab LOD comparison: not located.
- **Market size** for environmental DNA or wastewater testing: no reliable primary source found; vendor pages give client counts, not revenue.
- **Pecson 2021 results beyond the abstract** (per-method-group LODs): the page text was truncated at 30k characters; abstract numbers are used.
- **Ahmed et al. 2022 (minimising RT-PCR errors in WBE)** and **Kantor et al. 2021**: publisher blocked.
- **Standard-curve drift over time in WBE** (Dutch medRxiv preprint found by search): not readable; claim omitted from the findings.
- **WastewaterSCAN and Verily platform (dPCR vs qPCR)**: not verified; PMMoV adjustment confirmed only from the site label.
- **NWSS laboratory count and site count**: CDC's current site does not state them; the public concentration dataset (211k rows) is archived as of September 2025, meaning the data stream changed; reason not verified.
- **ISO 16140-2 statistical protocol and allergen PCR standards (EN 15634 series)**: not read; allergen testing is therefore not covered.
- **drc licence, MDPI article licence, mare package licence, EPA Science Hub terms**: to be checked before any code or data reuse.
- **Whether eDNA labs can export raw amplification data at scale**: Whitney Lab instruments and export formats not identified; this determines whether D1–D2 can start from raw curves or must accept Cq tables as a fallback.
