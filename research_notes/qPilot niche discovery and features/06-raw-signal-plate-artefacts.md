# 06 — Between the optics and the Cq: raw-signal processing, plate-position and instrument artefacts, and how to detect and trend them

Researcher note, 17 October 2026. Scope: what happens to a fluorescence reading between the camera and the Cq value in the four main vendor pipelines (Thermo/Applied Biosystems QuantStudio, Bio-Rad CFX, Roche LightCycler 480, Qiagen Rotor-Gene); which intermediate data are exportable; the evidence base for plate-position and instrument effects on Cq; existing computational detectors; and concrete product opportunities for qPilot. Method: 20 web searches (the shared search budget ran out after that), ~60 direct fetches of manuals, papers, package pages and datasets, text extraction of 17 vendor/paper PDFs with pypdf, and two attempts to render ResearchGate threads in the built-in browser (both returned empty pages, so forum quotes come from search-result excerpts and are marked as such). Everything stated below has a source; where a number or claim could not be verified it is listed in section 6, not asserted.

Terms used throughout: **raw fluorescence** = the per-well, per-cycle intensity the camera/detector reports in one optical filter or channel; **passive reference** = a dye (usually ROX) that does not change during PCR and is used to divide out well-to-well optical/volume differences; **Rn** = reporter signal divided by passive-reference signal (Thermo); **ΔRn** = Rn minus the fitted baseline (Thermo); **RFU** = relative fluorescence units (Bio-Rad); **Cq/Ct/Cp** = the cycle at which the curve crosses a threshold or reaches a feature (second-derivative maximum on Roche); **N0** = starting target quantity estimated from the curve (LinRegPCR); **RDML** = the XML exchange format qPilot already reads; **react** = RDML's element for one well; **adp** = RDML's per-cycle amplification data point (cycle, temperature, fluorescence).

## 1. How this corner of the niche works

### 1.1 People and roles
- **Bench scientist / student** (gene expression, pathogen load): runs 96- or 384-well plates, exports from the vendor desktop software, pastes into Excel or an analysis tool, looks at amplification plots to decide which wells to drop. Vendor manuals tell them to inspect the multicomponent and raw-data plots for "spikes, dips, and/or sudden changes" one well at a time [1].
- **Core-facility manager**: owns several instruments of different makes, runs the vendor's calibration and verification plates on a schedule, and is asked by clients "why did my run fail?". The vendor tools answer only "pass/fail" per instrument, with no cross-instrument or cross-run trend view [2][3][14].
- **Service engineer / QA**: performs IQ/OQ (installation/operational qualification), thermal verification, lamp replacement; frequency is sector-dependent: "every 6 months" (GMP), "annually at minimum" (CAP/CLIA), annually for research [25].
- **Software/analysis person** (this is qPilot's user too): needs to know whether an exported curve is raw or already baseline-subtracted/normalised before applying LinRegPCR; today this is learned the hard way on forums [12].

### 1.2 The signal chain, step by step (what each vendor does)

**Thermo Fisher / Applied Biosystems (QuantStudio family).** Excitation by a halogen lamp (QuantStudio 6/7 Flex) or LEDs (QuantStudio 3/5); emission through a set of filters; a camera image per filter per cycle. The pipeline, in Thermo's own training material, is "Raw data (Fluorescence) → Multicomponent (Fluorescence) → Rn → Ct": "The multicomponenting algorithm incorporates pure dye and background calibrations", then "Normalized reporter (Rn) = Reporter dye / passive reference", and "Passive reference dye normalization accounts for non-PCR fluctuations in fluorescence" [4]. The same slide lists the "Common sources of fluorescent variation" that ROX is meant to cancel: "Water vapor reflux, Condensation, Air bubbles, Evaporation, Refluxing, Optical warping" [4]. The calibrations that feed this are stored per instrument and copied into every .eds file: "Each experiment (.eds) file stores the calibration data from the QuantStudio 3 or 5 Instrument on which it was run", and the desktop software allows overriding with another instrument's calibration data [1]. The four to five calibrations and their purposes (QuantStudio 6/7 Flex maintenance guide): ROI calibration — "The software generates a calibration image for each filter to account for minor differences in the optical path" (every 6 months); background calibration — "The software uses the background calibration data during subsequent experiments to remove background fluorescence from the experiment data" (monthly; also used "to check for contamination"); uniformity calibration — "generates data that allows the software to compensate for the physical effects of the instrument filters" (every 6 months); dye calibration — "the software determines the contribution of the fluorescent dyes used in each reaction by comparing the raw spectra to the pure spectra calibration data ... Because the age and use of instrument components can affect spectra readings, you must perform a dye calibration at least every 6 months"; normalization calibration (every 6 months) [3]. Saving an ROI calibration "will invalidate other existing calibrations except HRM Calibrations" [3]. On QuantStudio 3/5 the recommended ROI/uniformity interval is "Every two years (recommended)", background "as needed to check for contamination", RNase P verification "After installing or moving the instrument" and "As needed thereafter" [2]. Baseline and threshold: "The Baseline Threshold algorithm ... subtracts a baseline component and sets a fluorescence threshold in the exponential region"; an alternative "relative threshold (Crt) algorithm" exists [1]. What is exportable: the Raw Data Plot "displays the raw fluorescence signal (not normalized) for each optical filter during each cycle" and the Multicomponent Plot "displays the complete spectral contribution of each dye over the duration of the PCR run" [1]; export formats are "QuantStudio format .txt/.xls/.xlsx" and "RDML format (for Standard Curve, Relative Standard Curve, Comparative CT, and Melt Curve experiments)" [1]; the export sheets documented by Thermo include Results, Amplification Data, Multicomponent Data and Sample Setup [6][S1]. A third-party guide warns that the exported Ct values are "already ROX-normalized (derived from ΔRn, not raw Rn)" [6]. A core-facility guide gives the practical ROX ranges: "A high ROX signal will typically appears around 1mio fluorescence units, while a 'low rox' signal will show up around 100'000 units. If your mastermix does not contain rox at all, the signal will be flat at 0 unit. If the concentration of rox in your mastermix is too high, you can still save the analysis by unchecking the 'rox passive dye' option during data analysis" [5]. Usage counters exist on the instrument: "Instrument Statistics: Displays instrument usage information: Block Cycle Count and LED Life", and a "Maintenance Summary.pdf" calibration status report and a calibration history report can be downloaded [2].

**Bio-Rad (CFX96/CFX384/CFX Opus, CFX Manager / CFX Maestro).** No passive reference; per-fluorophore RFU per cycle. The software "automatically subtracts the baseline from well data" and offers "two modes for quantification cycle (Cq) determination: regression and single threshold" [9]. The baseline settings seen in third-party kit protocols are "Baseline Settings = Baseline Subtracted Curve Fit" and "Baseline Settings = Apply Fluorescence Drift Correction" [10]; a genotyping guide describes drift correction as optional and to be enabled "only if clusters are misassigned" (Settings > Baseline Setting > Apply Fluorescence Drift Correction) [11]. Export: right-click the data table to export "as a text, XML, Microsoft Excel, or HTML file" [9], or use Custom Export with chosen columns such as Well, Fluorophore, Target Name, Content, Cq, Sample Name, End RFU [10]. Users report that the exported amplification data are baseline-corrected by default and that "No Baseline Subtraction" must be chosen in settings before export to get data LinRegPCR can use (forum report, from search excerpt) [12]. Bio-Rad's own PrimePCR manual states the evaporation rule of thumb: "Seals must fully enclose the PCR plate to prevent evaporation from the wells during thermal cycling. Evaporation is identified by analyzing technical repeats of data — specifically, if a well near the edge of the plate yields variable data, evaporation has likely occurred" [13]. Bio-Rad's "well factors" (an optical per-well correction collected at run start) are referenced by users and vendor material but I could not fetch a Bio-Rad document defining them within this session (see section 6). Verification: the CFX Qualification Plate "contains a five-point standard curve, two unknown groups, and no template controls (NTCs) for validating the optics and thermal block" with pass/fail criteria of "2-fold discrimination with 99.7% confidence level and a single product melt peak" (cat. 184-5098 96-well, 184-5099 384-well) [14][15]; Bio-Rad IQ/OQ services add a "wet chemistry validation of system performance ... using the CFX Qualification Plate" [16]. CFX Maestro is listed as RDML 1.1 compliant [58].

**Roche LightCycler 480.** Per-channel fluorescence with filter combinations (e.g., "465-510" for SYBR) [19]. Multi-dye assays require **colour compensation** (a correction for spectral overlap between channels, computed from single-dye calibration runs): "Please apply color compensation for your individual kit. Consult the kit manual in order to know which dyes are used in the kit and need to be taken into account for color compensation" [17]; for single-dye quantification a kit guide instructs the opposite: "Make sure to turn the colour compensation off and to select the High Sensitivity algorithm" under "Abs Quant/2nd Derivative Max", and "you must always recalculate" after changing settings [18]. Cq is either the second-derivative maximum (Cp) or a "Fit Point" threshold with a user-set "Noise Band" [19]; the two differ systematically: on an LC480, fit-point Cq values were 1.2 (±0.5) cycles lower than second-derivative values and the corresponding efficiencies were 104.4% vs 137.3% [32]. Export: "Right click on the table to export the data including Cp values (in .txt format); graph for raw fluorescent value" [19]; endpoint fluorescence export is documented separately [17]. The LC480 is not on rdml.org's compliant-instrument list (LightCycler 96 is) [58].

**Qiagen Rotor-Gene Q.** Rotary (air-heated, tubes spin past a single optical path), hence vendor uniformity spec of ±0.02 °C and the claim that rotation "removes the edge effect" [23]. Background handling is per tube: "Dynamic tube. Leave this option selected. It determines the average background of each sample before amplification commences. Note that for some data sets, background fluorescence is not consistent during the cycles before amplification begins, and in these cases, it may be necessary to deselect dynamic tube normalization"; "Slope Correct: Select this option when the background fluorescence shows a gradual increase or decrease over several cycles. Noise slope correction improves the data when raw data backgrounds are observed to slope upward or downward before the take-off point"; an "NTC Threshold" via "Outlier Removal" at "5-15%" is recommended [20]. The 2002 Corbett manual describes dynamic-tube normalisation as using the second derivative of each trace to find the start of amplification and averaging background from cycle 1 to that cycle (search excerpt) [21]. Rotor-Gene Q is listed as RDML 1.1 compliant [58].

The 2009 RDML paper requires "A Cq value should be provided for each reaction well in the experiment" and says data elements may hold "quantification (and possibly raw) data" [58]. Compliant instruments listed: CFX Maestro (1.1), StepOne, QuantStudio 12K Flex, ViiA 7, LightCycler 96 (1.1), Rotor-Gene Q (1.1), MyGo (1.2); compliant third-party software: Clarida's RDML viewer (1.3), Primer3Plus (1.3), RDMLdb (1.2), LinRegPCR (1.2), RDML R package (1.1), a Mathematica package (1.2) [58]. ### 1.5 Money and time
Thermo list prices (USD, Thermo catalogue page): RNase P verification plates $1,136 (96-well 0.2 mL), $1,178 (Fast 96), $1,508 (384-well); ROI and Background plates $369–374; spectral calibration plates $426–696 each or 10-dye kits $1,304–1,392; calibration install kits $3,100–3,180 [7]; the UK product page shows GBP 876 for the 96-well RNase P plate and says to verify "every six months" [8]. Each verification plate is single-use. Bio-Rad's Qualification Plate price is behind a login [15]. Service verification frequencies: GMP "every 6 months", clinical "annually at minimum", research annually [25]. A failed plate or an undetected position effect costs a re-run (reagents, sample, a day); estimates in section 3 are labelled as such.

### 1.6 What "good" looks like and what goes wrong
Good: passive reference flat across the run, reporter flat then rising, no amplification in NTCs [1]; technical replicates within ~0.5 Cq (vendor troubleshooting treats "Cq difference >0.5" between technical replicates as a problem) [56]; a verification plate that distinguishes 5,000 from 10,000 copies at 99.7% confidence [2][8]. Goes wrong: edge-well evaporation (vendor rule of thumb [13], sealer vendor: "differential evaporation from wells can lead to large standard deviations in the number of quantification cycles" [30]); bubbles ("bubbles affect all dyes" in a well; cause baseline drift) [56]; wrong ROX setting [5]; baseline-subtracted exports fed to tools that expect raw data [12]; thermal non-uniformity (a 96-replicate plate on an ABI 7900HT had Ct SD 1.91 versus 0.34 on a CFX96) [23]; Cq offsets of 4–5 cycles between instruments for the same samples [32]; calibrations lapsing while the instrument ages ("the age and use of instrument components can affect spectra readings") [3].

### 1.7 Key papers, sites, communities
Ruijter 2013 benchmark of curve-analysis methods and its public datasets [37][38]; Ruijter 2015 factor correction for between-plate variation [36]; Ruiz-Villalba 2021 "Use and misuse of Cq" [35]; Rogers-Broadway 2015 instrument comparison [23]; Dellière 2019 cross-platform comparison [32]; Evans 2021 and Rhoads 2021 on inter-laboratory Cq [33][34]; Bar 2003 / Tichopad 2010 / Sisti 2010 kinetic-outlier detection [40–43]; Spiess 2008 sigmoid fitting [44]; PCRedux and chipPCR (Rödiger, Burdukiewicz, Spiess) [45][46]; Brintz 2025 and the CC0 165k-curve set [47]; Mao 2023 and Wang 2026 on cross-source degradation [48][49]; Lachmann 2016 on spatial bias in multiwell plates [51]; rdml.org [58]; gene-quantification.de (hosts several of the PDFs); Westgard QC [54]; Biosearch/LGC troubleshooting blog [56]; ResearchGate Q&A threads [12][28][57].

## 2. Key questions answered (per the researcher brief)

### KQ1. Vendor pipelines and which intermediate data are exportable

#### Takeaway
All four vendors transform the camera signal through several undocumented-in-detail steps (spectral unmixing or colour compensation, passive-reference division, per-tube background estimation, baseline fitting/curve fitting, drift correction, threshold or derivative methods). Raw per-filter/per-channel data are viewable everywhere and exportable on QuantStudio (Raw Data / Multicomponent), Bio-Rad (if baseline subtraction is switched off before export) and LC480 (text export of fluorescence), but the default exports are processed, and RDML exports typically carry only the reporter channel.

#### Cited Findings
- QuantStudio: "The Raw Data Plot screen displays the raw fluorescence signal (not normalized) for each optical filter during each cycle of the real-time PCR"; "The Multicomponent Plot displays the complete spectral contribution of each dye over the duration of the PCR run"; use it to "Confirm that the signal from the passive reference dye remains unchanged throughout the run" and "Review reporter dye signal for spikes, dips, and/or sudden changes" — [QuantStudio D&A desktop UG](https://www.ffclrp.usp.br/divulgacao/emu/real_time/manuais/QuantStudioDesign_Analysis_Desktop_Software_UG.pdf)
- QuantStudio export formats: "QuantStudio format .txt/.xls/.xlsx" and "RDML format (for Standard Curve, Relative Standard Curve, Comparative CT, and Melt Curve experiments)"; "Each experiment (.eds) file stores the calibration data from the QuantStudio 3 or 5 Instrument on which it was run" and calibration data from another instrument can override it — [QuantStudio D&A desktop UG](https://www.ffclrp.usp.br/divulgacao/emu/real_time/manuais/QuantStudioDesign_Analysis_Desktop_Software_UG.pdf)
- QuantStudio pipeline: "Raw data → Multicomponent → Rn → Ct"; "The multicomponenting algorithm incorporates pure dye and background calibrations"; "Normalized reporter (Rn) = Reporter dye / passive reference"; "Common sources of fluorescent variation: Water vapor reflux, Condensation, Air bubbles, Evaporation, Refluxing, Optical warping" — [Stanford QS6 Pro orientation slides](https://orthofacilities.su.domains/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Stanford-QS6Pro-Orientation-0222021.pdf)
- QuantStudio thresholds: "The Baseline Threshold algorithm ... subtracts a baseline component and sets a fluorescence threshold in the exponential region"; alternative "relative threshold (Crt) algorithm"; for end-point reads "ΔRn = Rn (post-PCR read) – Rn (pre-PCR read), where Rn = normalized readings" — [QuantStudio D&A desktop UG](https://www.ffclrp.usp.br/divulgacao/emu/real_time/manuais/QuantStudioDesign_Analysis_Desktop_Software_UG.pdf)
- Exported Ct values are "already ROX-normalized (derived from ΔRn, not raw Rn)"; export sheets Results, Amplification Data, Multicomponent Data ("the raw fluorescence from each dye channel per cycle"), Melt Curve Data, Sample Setup; .eds is "the native project file" to keep "as your raw archive" — [VoilaPCR blog](https://voilapcr.com/blog/how-to-export-and-analyze-quantstudio-qpcr-data) (secondary source)
- ROX levels and misconfiguration: "A high ROX signal will typically appears around 1mio fluorescence units, while a 'low rox' signal will show up around 100'000 units ... - Qiagen: dynamic tube ("determines the average background of each sample before amplification commences ... it may be necessary to deselect"), slope correct ("when the background fluorescence shows a gradual increase or decrease over several cycles"), NTC threshold "5-15%", export via "File > Save As… > Excel Analysis Sheet" — [BDR Rotor-Gene Q manual 2022](https://www.bdrdiagnostics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Rotor-Gene-Q-Manual-ENG-2022_Rev01.pdf); dynamic tube uses the second derivative to find the start point and averages background from cycle 1 to that cycle (search excerpt) — [Corbett Rotor-Gene software manual 2002](https://documents.cdn.ifixit.com/RsASNtE4gJ4VhLFv.pdf)
- RDML compliance lists: instruments CFX Maestro (1.1), StepOne, QuantStudio 12K Flex, ViiA 7, LightCycler 96 (1.1), Rotor-Gene Q (1.1), MyGo (1.2); software Clarida RDML viewer (1.3), Primer3Plus (1.3), RDMLdb (1.2), LinRegPCR (1.2), RDML R package (1.1) — [rdml.org instruments](https://rdml.org/instruments.html), [rdml.org software](https://rdml.org/software.html)
- RDML 2009 design: "A Cq value should be provided for each reaction well in the experiment"; data elements may hold "quantification (and possibly raw) data" — [Lefever et al. 2009, NAR](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkp056)
- Vendor verification criteria: Thermo RNase P plate "must demonstrate the ability to distinguish between 5,000 and 10,000 genomic equivalents with a 99.7% confidence level", verify "every six months", GBP 876 — [Thermo product 4432382](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/4432382); the pass rule is [(CT_A) − 3(σCT_A)] > [(CT_B) + 3(σCT_B)] and "The software automatically adjusts the threshold and omits a defined number of wells from the unknown populations to meet the installation specifications" — [QuantStudio 3/5 Install, Use, Maintenance Guide](https://www.ffclrp.usp.br/divulgacao/emu/real_time/manuais/QuantStudio3_5_InstallUseMaint_UG.pdf); Bio-Rad plate: "five-point standard curve, two unknown groups, and no template controls (NTCs) for validating the optics and thermal block", "2-fold discrimination with 99.7% confidence level and a single product melt peak" — [Bio-Rad Bulletin 6323](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin%206323.pdf)
- Contamination diagnosis by plate rotation: "Remove the plate from the instrument, rotate the plate 180°, then perform the calibration again"; identical failed positions → "The sample block is contaminated"; reversed → "The plate is contaminated"; "Common contaminants include ink residue from permanent pens, powder from disposable gloves, and dust" — [QuantStudio 3/5 guide](https://www.ffclrp.usp.br/divulgacao/emu/real_time/manuais/QuantStudio3_5_InstallUseMaint_UG.pdf)
- Usage counters: "Instrument Statistics: Displays instrument usage information: Block Cycle Count and LED Life"; downloadable "Maintenance Summary.pdf" calibration status report and calibration history report — [QuantStudio 3/5 guide](https://www.ffclrp.usp.br/divulgacao/emu/real_time/manuais/QuantStudio3_5_InstallUseMaint_UG.pdf)

#### Inferences
- A per-well, per-cycle, multi-channel raw record (reporter + passive reference + other filters) exists on every QuantStudio and can be exported; qPilot's existing RDML path loses the passive-reference channel, so the bubble/evaporation detectors in D6 need the vendor export path (QuantStudio Multicomponent/Raw Data, Bio-Rad per-fluorophore RFU) rather than RDML alone.
- "Raw" is ambiguous per vendor: QuantStudio "Amplification Data" is ROX-normalised and (ΔRn) baseline-subtracted; Bio-Rad default exports are baseline-subtracted; Rotor-Gene exports are dynamic-tube normalised unless disabled. A deterministic "what processing has this curve had?" detector (D1) is both feasible and needed, because LinRegPCR's baseline estimation assumes unprocessed data (Spiess 2008 likewise used data "not processed (i.e. baseline corrected)").
- Vendor verification tools hide position information: the RNase P check auto-omits outlier wells and reports pass/fail; the same plate re-analysed per well is a free uniformity map (D5).

#### Gaps
- The exact algorithm behind Bio-Rad's "Baseline Subtracted Curve Fit", "Fluorescence Drift Correction" and "well factors" could not be read from a Bio-Rad document (the CFX Maestro/Manager user guides exceed the 10 MB fetch limit; the 2006 Applications Guide does not mention well factors).
- Whether QuantStudio RDML exports contain raw per-filter data or Rn/ΔRn was not verified.
- QuantStudio flag names (e.g., BADROX, SPIKE, NOISE, OFFSCALE) were not found in the fetched UG text; the UG refers to the in-software Help for flag definitions.
- LC480 RDML export support and the exact format of its per-cycle text export were not verified.

### KQ2. Evidence for spatial (plate-position) and instrument effects on Cq; inter-instrument differences

#### Takeaway
Measured plate-wide replicate spreads range from ~0.3 Cq SD on modern block instruments to ~1.9 Cq SD on an older ABI 7900HT; vendors and service firms consistently blame edges (evaporation, heat loss) and block uniformity (±0.4–0.5 °C typical specs), and the vendor remedy is "look at edge technical replicates". Instrument-to-instrument offsets of 4–5 cycles and inter-laboratory spreads of 6–14 cycles for the same material are documented; nothing in research software quantifies plate position effects routinely.

#### Cited Findings
- Vendor uniformity specs: ABI Prism 7900HT ±0.5 °C; Bio-Rad CFX96 ±0.4 °C "well-to-well within 10 sec of reaching 90°C"; Qiagen Rotor-Gene Q ±0.02 °C; BJS xxpress ±0.3 °C — [Rogers-Broadway & Karteris 2015, Exp Ther Med](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4578049/)
- Measured 96-replicate Ct spread/SD per instrument: CFX96 spread 1.315, SD 0.34; xxpress 1.2/0.29; ABI Prism 7900HT 4.526/1.91; Rotor-Gene Q 1.319/0.43; block-based systems are "vulnerable to greater heat losses on the edges and surfaces"; rotation through heated air "removes the edge effect" — [Rogers-Broadway & Karteris 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4578049/)
- "Manufacturers typically specify ΔTmax of ±0.5 °C or less at set point"; "A 1–2 °C block error can produce Ct shifts of 1–3 cycles"; thermal verification "every 6 months" (GMP), "annually at minimum" (CAP/CLIA) — [Femto Scientific thermal verification page](https://www.femtoscientific.com/thermal-cycler-pcr-rtpcr-calibration-service-repair-thermal-verification) (service vendor; the Ct-shift figure is uncited)
- Vendor demonstration of uniformity: 96 replicate reactions (125 pg lambda DNA, SYBR) with Cq values "subtracted from the average value of 96 replicates" and plotted by well position; no SD given — [GEN / Thermo Fisher PikoReal article](https://www.genengnews.com/resources/minimizing-variation-in-qpcr-workflows/)
- QIAquant vendor claim: "Low variation of Cq values (SD < 0.2) and Tm of the melt curves (SD < 0.1) demonstrate temperature uniformity across the block" (search excerpt) — [QIAquant overview](https://franklinyoung.com/content/Related-Document/QIAquant_overview.pdf)
- Bio-Rad on evaporation/edges: "if a well near the edge of the plate yields variable data, evaporation has likely occurred"; troubleshooting row "Wells along edge of PCR plate are varying between technical repeats → Evaporation of PCR reactions → Seal plate better ...; use a heat sealer for best results" — [Bio-Rad PrimePCR manual](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/global/english/primePCR/LIT10026370.pdf)
- Sealer vendor: "differential evaporation from wells can lead to large standard deviations in the number of quantification cycles (Cq)"; a user review reports manual sealing led to evaporation at corner and edge wells (search excerpt) — [SelectScience PX1 page](https://www.selectscience.net/product/px1-pcr-plate-sealer)
- Edge-effect mechanisms in plate assays (not qPCR-specific): temperature gradients ("outer wells ... begin warming faster"), evaporation ("the dominant mechanism in assays with incubation periods exceeding 30 minutes"), alignment error; "even a 1–2°C differential" alters kinetics; detection by heat-map review and border-vs-interior Wilcoxon/t-test; recommends reserving outer rows/columns for controls — [Lab Manager](https://www.labmanager.com/edge-effects-in-microplate-readers-causes-troubleshooting-and-how-to-prevent-assay-errors-35310)
- A patent example (non-qPCR) measured "the wells in the periphery losing about 6 times more liquid volume than the ones in the center" (search excerpt) — [US patent 8846332](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8846332)
- Practitioner view (search excerpt): "Normally there is no effect on ct for well position ... the 'edge effect' may happen if the seal or cap-strip cannot seal the plate properly. Severe evaporation may happen to the wells on the outer area of the plate"; older cyclers may show thermal gradients; run controls across the plate — [ResearchGate: Is there an effect of well position on ct in qPCR?](https://www.researchgate.net/post/Is-there-an-effect-of-well-position-on-ct-in-qPCR)
- Thermo lists "Condensation", "Air bubbles", "Evaporation", "Water vapor reflux" and "Optical warping" as sources of fluorescence variation that passive-reference normalisation addresses — [Stanford QS6 Pro slides](https://orthofacilities.su.domains/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Stanford-QS6Pro-Orientation-0222021.pdf)
- Cross-platform offsets for the same samples: mean Cq (whole nucleic acid, high load) Rotor-Gene Q 22.6, QuantStudio 7 Flex 22.6, LC480 23.2, ABI 7500 27.4 (p < 0.0001); DNA: 27.7 / 29.2 / 33.3 / 33.5; master-mix median ΔCq 2.6 and 2.9; efficiency did not differ among platforms (p = 0.14); "intrinsic properties of the thermocycler, including detector sensitivity and settings, may significantly modify qPCR results"; "variability ... due to master mix and thermocycler prevents the application of a consensual threshold" — [Dellière et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7151141/)
- Inter-laboratory: calibration intercepts differed by more than 6 cycles between laboratories (~100-fold); "The interlaboratory variation for a given Cq value was >1000 fold in copies/mL (99% CI)"; causes "RNA extraction, assay performance, and instrument and quantification threshold settings"; between-lab slope/intercept differences "responsible for almost 90% of the variability seen" — [Evans et al. 2022, Clin Chem](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/68/1/153/6385233)
- CAP survey of >700 labs on one material: median Ct "varied by as much as 14 cycles" across methods; up to 3.0 cycles between targets on one instrument; up to 12.0 cycles for one target on one method across labs — [Rhoads et al. 2021, CID](https://academic.oup.com/cid/article/72/10/e685/5891762)
- Why Cq is not comparable: machines set thresholds automatically, often at "10 standard deviations (SD) above the mean ground phase fluorescence values"; "the lowest thresholds result in significantly higher Cq variation"; a Cq by itself "is meaningless"; "the reporting of Cq values must be discouraged"; dividing Cq values to correct between-plate variation is wrong — [Ruiz-Villalba, Ruijter, van den Hoff 2021, Life](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8229287/)
- Between-plate variation example: "6-times difference between runs 2 and 5"; between-plate variation "is multiplicative"; calibrator error "propagated to all samples in the plate" — [Ruijter et al. 2015, BDQ](https://www.gene-quantification.de/ruijter-et-al-variation-multi-plate-pcr-bdq-2015.pdf)
- Instrument ageing acknowledged by the vendor: "the age and use of instrument components can affect spectra readings"; halogen lamp replacement triggers "all calibrations and an RNase P instrument verification run" — [QuantStudio 6/7 Flex guide 4489821](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/4489821.pdf)

#### Inferences
- On a well-maintained modern block the pure position effect is small (SD ≈ 0.3 Cq in a 96-replicate test, which also includes pipetting); on older or drifting blocks it is large (SD ≈ 1.9 Cq on the 7900HT). A lab cannot know which regime it is in without a position-resolved analysis of its own plates; qPilot sees every plate and can compute this for free from replicate residuals.
- Evaporation produces a spatially structured, late-cycle signature (edge wells, concentrated reagents, changed volume); thermal gradients produce a smooth row/column gradient in Cq; alignment/contamination produces irregular single-well artefacts. These have different spatial statistics (global Moran's I vs local clusters vs isolated outliers), so a spatial layer can give the scientist a cause-shaped hint, not just an outlier flag.
- Inter-instrument offsets of several cycles mean any multi-instrument study needs an explicit between-run factor (Ruijter 2015) and a design with overlapping samples; qPilot's robustness engine is the natural place to show how much the conclusion depends on that correction.

#### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed qPCR-specific study quantifying edge-well Cq shift as a function of plate type, seal type and volume was found within the search budget (evidence is vendor rules of thumb, a plate-reader article and a patent example).
- "Kogoj 2022" (named in the brief) could not be located; the one DOI I tested resolved to an unrelated paper, so it is not cited.
- No source was found on optical well-to-well crosstalk specifications or on condensation effects beyond the Thermo training slide.

### KQ3. Existing computational detectors (sigmoid-fit residuals, feature libraries, ML classifiers, spatial statistics) and their validation

#### Takeaway
Deterministic, published detectors exist for kinetic outliers (efficiency Z-test, Mahalanobis distance on fit parameters; SOD sensitivity 0.93–1.00 vs KOD 0.30–0.76 on inhibitor-spiked LC480 data), for per-curve features (PCRedux, MIT, ~90 descriptors incl. hook, baseline slope, plateau SD, autocorrelation) and for spatial bias on plates (Moran's I with adjacency weights; 96% of 1,894 384-well plates showed bias in one large study). ML classifiers reach 0.996 accuracy within-source (Brintz 2025) but degrade across sources (Mao 2023: +20.9% after domain adaptation; Wang 2026: cross-platform MAE 2.62 cycles). None of these tools is position-aware or trends across runs; licences are MIT (PCRedux), GPL (qpcR, chipPCR), CC0/CC BY (Brintz data).

#### Cited Findings
- KOD (Bar 2003) as implemented in qpcR: each sample's efficiency "is removed from its replicate group and tested against the remaining efficiencies with a Z-test", P < 0.05 flags an outlier; uni2 tests sigmoidal structure ("the difference between the first and second derivative maxima ... must be less than 10 cycles"); multi1/multi2 (Tichopad 2010) and multi3 (Sisti 2010) use robust Mahalanobis distance with χ² p-values — [qpcR KOD documentation](https://rdrr.io/cran/qpcR/man/KOD.html); qpcR licence "GPL-2 | GPL-3", v1.4-2 (2025-06-10) — [CRAN qpcR](https://cran.r-project.org/package=qpcR)
- Tichopad 2010 amplification compatibility test: two fitted parameters "related to amplification efficiency" → Z-score against "a predefined reference set of reactions, typically calibration reactions"; "The test identified 44 of the set of 75 inhibited reactions as deviant. All NTC samples were also identified as outliers"; "at least 10 reference measurements should be available for every assay"; implemented in the commercial Kineret software (Labonnet) — [Tichopad et al. 2010, Methods](https://www.gene-quantification.de/Tichopad-et-al-The-ongoing-evolution-of-qPCR-Methods-2010.pdf)
- Sisti 2010 shape-based outlier detection (SOD): 5-parameter Richards fit; parameters F_max, slope at inflection m, inflection y-coordinate Y_f; Mahalanobis distance vs χ²(3), threshold 7.81 (α = 0.05); reference set = 72 standard-curve runs on a LightCycler 480 with SYBR; sensitivity/specificity SOD vs KOD: tannic acid 0.93/1.00 vs 0.30/0.94; IgG 1.00/0.94 vs 0.76/0.96; quercitin 1.00/0.98 vs 0.50/0.98; SOD "has been developed for the chemistry Sybr Green" — [Sisti et al. 2010, BMC Bioinformatics](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-11-186)
- Spiess 2008 five-parameter log-logistic model: "five-parameter models resulted in a significantly better fit" (F-test p 10⁻³–10⁻¹⁶); "absolutely symmetric qPCR data seldom (in our observations never) occur"; R² "should not be reported or trusted solely for demonstrating the validity of a fit"; raw data used "were not processed (i.e. baseline corrected)" — [Spiess, Feig, Ritz 2008](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-9-221)
- PCRedux v1.2-1 (2025-11-19), licence "MIT + file LICENSE"; features include cpD1, cpD2, eff, sliwin, slope_bg/intercept_bg, sd_bg, sd_plateau ("standard deviation over the last five cycles"), head2tail_ratio, hookreg_hook, autocorrelation, polyarea, peaks_ratio, amptester_* tests, segmented-regression breakpoints, window MAD/median ratios — [PCRedux CRAN reference manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/PCRedux/refman/PCRedux.html); "computes 90 boolean and numerical descriptors" (search excerpt of the JOSS paper) — [JOSS 4407](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.04407)
- PCRedux labelled data: all listed curve sets "were classified in interactive, semi-blinded sessions" with chipPCR's humanrater(), "at least three times"; the htPCR set (8,858 curves, Fluidigm Biomark HD, 95×96 grid) "was classified eight times in total" by one user and "different (subjective) class mappings" resulted; classes negative/ambiguous/positive; decision CSVs cover RAS002/RAS003 (CFX96, RDML), batsch, boggy, C126EG595 (Chromo4), C127EGHP (iQ5), competimer, dil4reps94 (CFX384), guescini, karlen (ABI 7700), lievens (ABI 7300), vermeulen (LC480), VIMCFX96_60, kbqPCR (CFX96), HCU32_aggR (VideoScan), lc96_bACTXY (LC96), stepone_std (StepOne); classifiers compared with mlr (ranger, ksvm, lda, gbm, multinom, glmnet) — [PCRedux supplement PDF](https://github.com/PCRuniversum/PCRedux-supplements/blob/master/PCRedux.pdf)
- chipPCR v1.0-2, licence "GPL-3": normalising/baselining, start/end detection, smoothers, false-positive detection, Cq by derivative maxima — [CRAN chipPCR](https://cran.r-project.org/package=chipPCR)
- Brintz 2025: 165,214 TaqMan Array Card curves (MAL-ED 56,539; Niger 108,675); labels by "two experts in qPCR curve analysis"; inputs "only the normalized fluorescence values from each of the 40 cycles" (FAM/VIC divided by ROX, then Fnorm = Fstand − (Fmin − 1)); internal test (n = 33,043) accuracy 0.996, sensitivity 0.997, specificity 0.993, Ct MAE 0.590; external EQA (1,472) accuracy 0.997, Ct MAE 0.611; misclassified curves mostly "exponential growth at high cycle counts"; data "CC0 1.0 Public domain dedication"; code and data at GitHub/Zenodo — [Brintz et al. 2025, Gates Open Res (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11756513/); repository licence shown as CC0-1.0 with CSVs qpcr_training_data.csv, raw_training.csv, dye_target_corrected.csv, niger_final.csv and EQA xlsx — [GitHub bbrintz/qPCR-amp-call](https://github.com/bbrintz/qPCR-amp-call); the Zenodo software record (v1.0.0, 67.8 MB zip) is labelled CC BY 4.0 — [Zenodo 13798821](https://zenodo.org/doi/10.5281/zenodo.13798820)
- Mao 2023 (IEEE JBHI 27(6):3093–3103): amplification-curve classification "faces ... distribution discrepancies between different data sources"; T-CDAN domain adaptation from synthetic DNA to clinical isolates; 198 isolates; 93.1% curve-level and 97.0% sample-level accuracy, gains of 20.9% and 4.9% — [PubMed 37028376](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37028376/)
- Wang 2026 (medRxiv, not peer reviewed): 41,770 curves, two platforms (SLAN-96S, Gentier 96R), 45 features; within-platform XGBoost Ct MAE 0.0419; applied cross-platform without recalibration MAE 2.62 cycles with 24.58% of samples |ΔCt| > 3, attributed to "a systematic offset, probably from optical differences"; large deviations linked in expert review to "baseline drift, non-specific amplification, or low signal-to-noise"; data not public ("commercial confidentiality") — [medRxiv 2026.06.11.26355491](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.11.26355491v1.full)
- Baseline modelling with controls (NIST): subtract a scaled non-template/extraction-control signal chosen to minimise background noise; "can yield a decade improvement in sensitivity relative to standard approaches" — [Patrone et al. 2020, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05466)
- Spatial bias on plates: Moran's I "measures the correlation of signal amongst spatial neighbors" with adjacency weights t_ij = 1 for adjacent wells; components with autocorrelation ≥ 0.5 flagged; spatial bias found in "1825 out of 1894 (96%)" 384-well plates, accounting for 22% of variance; correction improved reproducibility for 85% of samples — [Lachmann et al. 2016, Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/32/13/1959/1742942)
- Moran's I and Local Moran's I (LISA) applied to a 96-well potency assay found "systematic gradients along the rows" and specific wells with significant local clustering (search excerpt; page fetch was refused) — [MDPI Evaluation of Plate Homogeneity](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4125/6/4/66)
- HTS plate-effect methods exist and are citable: B-score (Brideau et al. 2003, J Biomol Screen, PMID 14711389, DOI 10.1177/1087057103258285), Malo et al. 2006 Nat Biotechnol (PMID 16465162, DOI 10.1038/nbt1186), Caraus et al. 2015 Brief Bioinform review of systematic bias (PMID 25750417, DOI 10.1093/bib/bbv004) — identifiers confirmed via [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1177/1087057103258285%20OR%20DOI:10.1038/nbt1186%20OR%20DOI:10.1093/bib/bbv004&resultType=lite&format=json) (full texts not fetched)
- Vendor troubleshooting table: "Baseline drift" ← "Degradation of the probe, Bubble in well, Reduction of azo bonds in BHQ quencher"; checks: "look at the baseline without baseline subtraction", "check all dyes in each well (bubbles affect all dyes)"; "Jagged signal throughout" ← weak signal, "mechanical error", "Contact equipment technician"; technical replicates "Cq difference >0.5" treated as a problem — [Biosearch/LGC qPCR troubleshooting](https://blog.biosearchtech.com/thebiosearchtechblog/bid/63622/qpcr-troubleshooting)
- Forum (search excerpts): wells whose fluorescence starts high and rises erratically attributed to "poor seal/evaporation, a bubble in the well, or something on the outside of the well"; another reply blames "instrument detector faults" and advises repeating before excluding — [ResearchGate: Strange qPCR amplification plot curves](https://www.researchgate.net/post/Strange_qPCR_amplification_plot_curves_Can_I_omit_wells_that_have_unusual_RFU_values_What_causes_such_a_pattern_in_the_RFU_output)

#### Inferences
- Everything needed for a deterministic plate-QC layer already exists as published maths: kinetic outliers (Bar 2003 Z-test; Sisti 2010 Mahalanobis), curve features (PCRedux definitions, MIT — reimplementable in Python), spatial autocorrelation (Moran's I with permutation p-values; local Moran for cluster localisation), row/column effects (median polish / B-score). The novelty for qPilot is the combination (residuals of LinRegPCR outputs within replicate groups × plate coordinates × multi-channel raw signatures), the evidence card, and the robustness rerun.
- ML curve classifiers are trustworthy only within the training source; a versioned, test-gated model trained on Brintz CC0 data is acceptable as an advisory module, but not as the arbiter, and never for cross-platform Ct prediction (Wang 2026's 2.62-cycle MAE shows why).
- The two R feature packages are GPL (qpcR, chipPCR); PCRedux is MIT but depends on qpcR/chipPCR for fitting. A clean-room Python reimplementation of the needed features from the papers (Spiess 2008 model; Ruijter window-of-linearity already in rdmlpython; PCRedux descriptor definitions) keeps qPilot Apache-2.0.

#### Gaps
- No published detector with validation metrics for bubbles, evaporation or condensation signatures in qPCR curves was found; all vendor guidance is qualitative.
- PCRedux's reported classifier accuracies could not be extracted from the supplement text within budget.
- Lachmann 2016 provides no software/licence on its page; the method must be reimplemented (straightforward).

### KQ4. What a lab could learn by trending its own runs; what qualification kits do today; costs

#### Takeaway
Clinical molecular labs trend control Cq with Levey-Jennings charts and Westgard rules (sold as features in clinical software such as UgenTec FastFinder); research labs have no equivalent, while vendors require semi-annual calibration precisely because components age, charge ~$1,100–1,500 per verification plate, and report pass/fail per instrument with no cross-run or cross-instrument view.

- Thermo calibration cadence (QuantStudio 6/7 Flex): ROI, background, uniformity, dye, normalization "Semi-annually (every 6 months)"; background "monthly"; RNase P "As needed"; lamp replacement "As needed" followed by "all calibrations and an RNase P instrument verification run"; "the age and use of instrument components can affect spectra readings" — [4489821](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/4489821.pdf); QuantStudio 3/5: ROI/Uniformity "Every two years (recommended)", background "as needed to check for contamination", RNase P after installation/moving and "As needed"; instrument reports "Block Cycle Count and LED Life"; downloadable "Maintenance Summary.pdf" and calibration history report; e-mail calibration reminders (search excerpt) — [QuantStudio 3/5 guide](https://www.ffclrp.usp.br/divulgacao/emu/real_time/manuais/QuantStudio3_5_InstallUseMaint_UG.pdf)
- Thermo prices (USD list): RNase P plates $1,136 / $1,178 / $1,508; ROI & background plates $369–374; spectral plates $426–696, 10-dye kits $1,304–1,392; calibration install kits $3,100–3,180; "Always consult your instrument user guide for the recommended calibration/verification schedule" — [Thermo calibration & verification page](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/pcr/real-time-pcr/reagents-kits/other/calibration-verification.html); GBP 876, "every six months", "single-use plate" — [product 4432382](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/4432382)

- Service verification frequencies and standards: GMP "every 6 months", CAP/CLIA "annually at minimum", research annually; "CAP and CLIA require documented calibration of PCR instruments"; ISO/IEC 17025 certificates — [Femto Scientific](https://www.femtoscientific.com/thermal-cycler-pcr-rtpcr-calibration-service-repair-thermal-verification)

#### Inferences
- A lab that already writes every plate into qPilot's Analysis Record has, for free, a time series per instrument of: NTC amplification rate, positive-control/inter-plate-calibrator N0 and Cq, per-assay efficiency, baseline level and slope distributions, plateau heights, passive-reference level (when vendor exports are captured), replicate SD, and plate-position indices. Levey-Jennings/Westgard rules are deterministic and published; applying them per instrument × assay × control is a few hundred lines of code plus the identification of controls.
- Verification plates are run at most twice a year per instrument; ordinary plates with technical replicates are run weekly. Trending ordinary plates gives a far denser signal than the vendor's semi-annual pass/fail.

#### Gaps
- No pricing for Thermo or Bio-Rad IQ/OQ services or the CFX Qualification Plate was found.
- I found no research-oriented tool (open source or commercial) that trends qPCR controls across runs; absence of evidence, not proof of absence.

### KQ5. Public datasets with raw per-cycle fluorescence usable to validate detectors; licences

#### Takeaway

## 4. Discoveries (ranked by impact × feasibility × fit)

### D1. "Is this curve raw?" — a signal-provenance guard that detects vendor processing before LinRegPCR runs

1. **Persona and situation.** A postdoc exports a CFX Maestro run (default settings) or a QuantStudio "Amplification Data" sheet and loads it into qPilot (or converts it to RDML). Happens on the first use of every new instrument/software version and whenever a core changes export settings; estimate: once per lab per month, and silently every week where nobody noticed.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** CFX exports are baseline-corrected by default; the fix ("No Baseline Subtraction" before export) is forum knowledge [12]. QuantStudio exports Ct "already ROX-normalized (derived from ΔRn, not raw Rn)" [6]; kit protocols lock "Baseline Subtracted Curve Fit" and "Apply Fluorescence Drift Correction" [10]; Rotor-Gene applies dynamic-tube normalisation and slope correction unless unchecked [20]. LinRegPCR-type analysis estimates its own baseline from raw data; the Ruijter 2015 pipeline states analysis "starts with raw fluorescence values which can either be processed by the software of the qPCR system ... or be exported as raw data" [36], and sigmoid-fit work explicitly uses data "not processed (i.e. baseline corrected)" [44]. Cost: wrong baselines → wrong efficiencies and N0 for every well with no error message (estimate: a full study re-analysed, or worse, published).
3. **What exists today.** Nothing. rdmlpython, LinRegPCR, qpcR, PCRedux accept whatever numbers they are given. Vendor manuals describe the settings but not their footprint in the exported numbers.
4. **The feature.** Input: the per-cycle fluorescence matrix (RDML `adp` or vendor sheet) plus declared source (vendor, software, export sheet name). Deterministic tests, each producing an engine fact with a confidence: (a) ground-phase offset test — raw curves have a positive offset (RFU hundreds–thousands on CFX; Rn around 0.1–10 on QuantStudio; "intercept between 0.09 and 0.40 RFU" even on Biomark [45]), while baseline-subtracted curves have early-cycle mean ≈ 0 with sign-symmetric noise and negative values; (b) subtraction footprint — early-cycle values that are exactly linear or near-constant at 0 (curve-fit subtraction) versus natural noise; (c) scale test — Rn-like scale vs RFU scale vs normalised 0–1 (Brintz-style); (d) ΔRn vs Rn by sheet name when the QuantStudio export header is present; (e) plate-wide consistency — a mixture of raw and subtracted wells indicates a per-well override. Output: a provenance fact ("signal: baseline-subtracted, vendor curve fit; passive-reference normalised: yes/unknown") written to the Analysis Record; if the engine's assumptions are violated, a Decision Card: "These curves were baseline-subtracted by CFX Maestro before export. Options: re-export with 'No Baseline Subtraction' (instructions per vendor), or analyse with the vendor baseline accepted (efficiency from the plate dilution series only), or stop." Agent call: `qpcr_signal_provenance(study)` returns the facts; `qpcr_plan` can refuse to compute per-well efficiencies on subtracted data.
5. **Feasibility.** Days: 3–5 for the detector and acceptance tests; test data: `ruijter` ds_94_4 raw (CC BY 4.0) and the same curves after synthetic baseline subtraction/curve-fit subtraction/ROX division; PCRedux/qpcR sets cover LC 1.0, LC480, ABI 7700/7300, CFX96/384, Chromo4, iQ5, Biomark (GPL — use for tests only). Hardest part: assays with genuinely tiny baselines (some probe chemistries) and vendor curve-fit subtraction that leaves smooth non-zero residuals; keep thresholds conservative and report "unknown" rather than guess.
6. **Why qPilot.** qPilot's whole promise is "same open method on raw curves"; a wrong input silently breaks that promise. The guard is a fact in the record (re-runnable, chain-verifiable), a Decision Card (impact-ranked), and an MCP-callable check an agent must pass before `qpcr_study`.
7. **Risks.** False alarms annoy; mitigate with per-vendor presets (the roadmap's vendor capture) and a "declare and move on" option. Confidence: high that the pain is real and the detector is buildable; medium on false-positive rate until tested on more vendor exports.

### D2. Plate Residual Map — a position-aware QC layer with spatial statistics and cause-shaped hints

1. **Persona and situation.** A PhD student runs 384-well plates weekly with manual adhesive seals on a six-year-old block; a core manager runs client plates on four instruments of three makes. Both are asked "were any wells bad?" and today answer by eye.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Vendor rule: edge-well variability = evaporation [13]; sealer vendor: evaporation → large Cq SD [30]; measured 96-replicate SD of 1.91 Cq on an ABI 7900HT vs 0.34 on a CFX96 [23]; uniformity specs ±0.4–0.5 °C [23]; service firm: 1–2 °C block error → 1–3 Ct shift (uncited vendor claim) [25]; plate-reader literature: 96% of 1,894 384-well plates showed spatial bias worth 22% of variance [51]; practitioner advice: "run controls across the plate" [28]. Cost estimate: one re-run per affected plate (reagents ~€100–300 plus a day), or an undetected gradient that becomes a "difference between groups" when groups are pipetted by column.
3. **What exists today.** Vendor software: per-well flags, no spatial statistics, no trend. HTS tools (B-score, Moran's I in Lachmann 2016 [51], LISA in the MDPI study [52]) exist for screening plates, not for qPCR; none consumes RDML or LinRegPCR outputs. qpcR KOD/SOD detect kinetic outliers but are position-blind and GPL/R.
4. **The feature.** Input: RDML (react id → row/column via `pcrFormat`), LinRegPCR per-well outputs already in qPilot (N0, efficiency, baseline start/stop, plateau, fit residuals, Cq), sample/target layout. Steps: (i) within-replicate-group residual r = log N0 − group median (biology removed; also per-well efficiency and plateau residuals); (ii) global spatial autocorrelation: Moran's I with rook/queen adjacency and a permutation null that shuffles residuals within replicate groups (so layout structure is respected) [51]; (iii) local Moran (LISA) to localise clusters; (iv) row/column effects via median polish (B-score family, Brideau 2003) and a border-vs-interior Wilcoxon test [27]; (v) edge index = median residual of outer rows/columns vs interior; (vi) kinetic outliers by Bar 2003 efficiency Z-test and a Sisti-2010-style Mahalanobis distance on fit parameters (reimplemented, with SYBR-only caveat [43]). Output: a residual heatmap with p-values; engine facts "plate 3: Moran's I = 0.31 (p < 0.001), edge index +0.42 Cq-equivalent, 7 wells LISA-high in rows O–P"; cause-shaped hint by signature (smooth gradient → thermal; outer ring late-cycle drift → evaporation/seal; isolated wells → bubble/contamination; see D6). Decision Card only when the robustness engine shows a conclusion could flip (rerun with: edge wells excluded; plate row/column effect modelled as a fixed effect; nothing changed). Record: heatmap data, statistics, the chosen option and reason. MCP: `qpcr_plate_qc(study, plate)`.
5. **Feasibility.** 2–3 weeks. Methods are published and permissively reimplementable (Moran's I, permutation tests, median polish, Wilcoxon; numpy/scipy). Validation data: `ruijter` ds_380 (380 technical replicates with well IDs, CC BY 4.0 [38]) for null/real position effects; htPCR 95×96 grid for scale [39]; synthetic injection of gradients/edge rings into real plates for power curves (publish as the acceptance test). Hardest part: confounding between layout and position (treatment by column); solved by group-wise residuals and layout-respecting permutations, and by refusing to attribute when the design is confounded.
6. **Why qPilot.** It already has per-well raw-curve facts for every plate, the replicate structure, the robustness engine to quantify "does it matter", Decision Cards to avoid approval fatigue, and a domain-agnostic plate model (the same layer serves ELISA/plate-reader assays later).
7. **Risks.** Over-flagging small plates (few replicates per group → weak tests): require minimum group sizes and show "insufficient replicates" honestly. Confidence: high on feasibility and fit; medium on how often research plates show actionable effects (the 7900HT-class instruments are ageing out; modern blocks are tighter).

### D3. Instrument Passport — Levey-Jennings/Westgard trending of controls, NTCs, efficiency, baseline and uniformity per instrument across the lab's own records

1. **Persona and situation.** A core-facility manager with 3–6 instruments of mixed make; a lab head whose QuantStudio is five years old. Weekly: "is instrument B drifting?"; semi-annually: "is it worth the $1,500 plate and the service visit?"
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Vendors require dye calibration every 6 months "because the age and use of instrument components can affect spectra readings" [3]; lamp replacement resets everything [3]; instruments count "Block Cycle Count and LED Life" but only show it on the touchscreen [2]; verification plates cost $1,136–1,508 each and are single-use [7][8]; clinical labs trend controls with Levey-Jennings/Westgard (sold by UgenTec) [55] but research labs have nothing; inter-instrument offsets of 4–5 cycles exist [32]. Cost estimate: an undetected drift invalidates weeks of plates; a needless service visit costs a day plus fees.
3. **What exists today.** Vendor calibration reminders and history reports per instrument [2]; verification plates (pass/fail) [2][14]; service IQ/OQ contracts [16][25]; clinical software with L-J charts (not for research, closed) [55].
4. **The feature.** Input: all Analysis Records in the workspace; per run: instrument identity (RDML run metadata or vendor export header; user-confirmed mapping), date, block type, sample types (NTC, positive control, inter-plate calibrator, standards — from RDML sample types and the lab profile naming rules), per-assay efficiency, per-well baseline level/slope, plateau, replicate SD, D2 plate indices, passive-reference level (when vendor multicomponent data were captured). Deterministic charts: per instrument × assay × control material, Levey-Jennings with limits from ≥20 points [54], Westgard rules 1-3s, 2-2s, R-4s (within-run), 4-1s, 10x [54]; NTC amplification rate as a p-chart; efficiency and baseline-slope as EWMA with a change-point test; calibration/verification events overlaid (user-entered or imported from the vendor's Maintenance Summary PDF/report). Output: an "instrument passport" page per instrument (facts with ids), alerts as Decision Cards only when a rule fires on a control that matters for an open study ("Instrument B: positive control for GAPDH violated 2-2s since 3 Oct; last dye calibration 7 months ago; options: proceed with a plate factor (D4), re-run on instrument A, schedule calibration"). Record: every alert and its disposition. MCP: `qpcr_instrument_status(instrument)` for an agent deciding where to run a plate.
5. **Feasibility.** 3–4 weeks. Westgard/L-J are public, deterministic; charts are plain. Hardest part: reliably identifying the same control material across runs and the instrument identity in files (vendor capture roadmap helps; a lab profile with naming rules is required; start with user-tagged controls).
6. **Why qPilot.** Only a tool that stores every plate as a re-runnable record with per-well facts can trend without extra work from the scientist; the escalation rule and Decision Cards keep alerts rare; the domain-agnostic core makes the same passport work for a plate reader.
7. **Risks.** Small labs may not have 20 control points per assay for months (show "learning limits"); false alarms if controls change lot (lab memory/assay passports already on the roadmap can carry lot ids). Confidence: high on need in cores (vendor cadence and prices show the stakes); medium on adoption in single-instrument labs.

### D4. Between-run and between-instrument offset estimation by factor correction (Ruijter 2015), wired into the robustness grid

1. **Persona and situation.** Any study spread over more than one plate or instrument (most studies); cores pooling client plates run on different machines.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** "6-times difference between runs 2 and 5" in a routine 6-plate experiment; between-plate variation is multiplicative; calibrator-sample error "propagated to all samples in the plate" [36]; platform offsets of 4–5 cycles [32]; "dividing Cq values to correct between-plate variation is wrong" [35].
3. **What exists today.** Calibrator samples (needs every calibrator to succeed on every plate; propagates error) [36]; Factor-qPCR (standalone program; licence not found); qbase+ inter-run calibration (commercial); nothing in LinRegPCR/rdmlpython.
4. **The feature.** Implement the published model Y_nj = F_n × (Y_mean + C_j + error): per pair of runs, geometric mean of ratios over all overlapping conditions (target × group); fill missing pairs by the nested geometric-mean substitution (Eq. 3) and warn "when more than 2 rounds of substitution are required ... run another plate" [36]; run factor = geometric mean of the completed ratio-matrix column (Eq. 4); corrected N0 = N0 / F_n (Eq. 5); efficiency-corrected Cq via E_mean (Eqs. 7–12) for export. Add what the paper does not: a bootstrap over overlapping pairs for a confidence interval on each F_n; express log(F_n)/log(E) as a Cq-equivalent instrument/plate offset; a design check ("plates 5 and 6 share no conditions") before the run (ties to `qpcr plan`); a robustness-grid dimension {no correction, calibrator correction, factor correction}. Decision Card when the correction changes a conclusion. Record: the ratio matrix, factors, CIs, the choice. MCP: `qpcr_run_factors(study)`.
5. **Feasibility.** 1–2 weeks; all equations are in the paper (reimplement under Apache-2.0; do not reuse Factor-qPCR code). Validation: Ruijter's worked 6-plate example in the paper; synthetic plate factors applied to `ruijter` data (CC BY 4.0).
6. **Why qPilot.** Multi-plate, whole-study analysis is qPilot's core; the robustness engine is exactly where "how much does the between-plate correction matter" belongs; the record keeps the factor and reason.
7. **Risks.** When plates coincide with biological groups, the factor absorbs biology — the design check must refuse to correct confounded designs and say so. Confidence: high (published method, clear maths, strong fit).

### D5. Verification-plate re-analysis — turn the $1,200 RNase P / CFX Qualification plate into a position-resolved uniformity map and a trend

1. **Persona and situation.** Cores and QA-minded labs that run Thermo RNase P plates (recommended every six months [8]) or Bio-Rad Qualification plates for preventive maintenance [14].
2. **Evidence of the pain.** The vendor result is pass/fail; Thermo's software "automatically adjusts the threshold and omits a defined number of wells from the unknown populations to meet the installation specifications" [2]; Bio-Rad gives "1-click pass/fail" [14]. Position information (which wells were omitted, whether failures cluster at edges or one optical region) is discarded although the plate layout is fixed and known [2][14].
3. **What exists today.** Vendor report PDF; service IQ/OQ reports [16][25].
4. **The feature.** Import the verification run (QuantStudio export/RDML; CFX export); apply the known layout (Thermo: STD 1,250–20,000 copies, Unknown A 5,000, Unknown B 10,000, NTC [2]; Bio-Rad: 5-point standard curve, two unknown groups, NTCs [14]); recompute the vendor criterion exactly ([(CT_A) − 3σ_A] > [(CT_B) + 3σ_B] [2]; 2-fold at 99.7% [14]) with and without omitting wells; produce per-well efficiency, N0 residual and plateau maps, D2 spatial statistics, standard-curve slope/intercept; store as an instrument-passport event (D3) and trend intercept, slope, replicate SD, edge index and Moran's I across the plates run over years. Decision Card: "Verification passed with 3 omitted wells, all in column 24; edge index +0.6 Cq. Options: accept; schedule uniformity calibration; investigate seal/block." MCP: `qpcr_verification_report(run)`.
5. **Feasibility.** About one week on top of D2/D3 (parsing vendor verification exports is the main work). Hardest: obtaining the verification run data in a form with per-cycle curves (the .eds export) and covering plate variants (96 standard/fast, 384).
6. **Why qPilot.** Raw-curve engine + plate model + record + trend; immediately useful to cores and a credible "we take instruments seriously" story.
7. **Risks.** Thermo's own analysis uses its calibration data inside the .eds; qPilot's numbers will differ slightly from the vendor's; present both. Confidence: medium–high.

### D6. Well Signal Integrity — deterministic multi-channel artefact signatures (bubble/step, spike, baseline drift, late-cycle evaporation drift, passive-reference anomaly) with evidence cards

1. **Persona and situation.** Bench scientists reviewing a plate before analysis; agents deciding whether a well is trustworthy.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Thermo instructs users to inspect every well for "spikes, dips, and/or sudden changes" and to confirm the passive reference "remains unchanged throughout the run" [1]; bubbles cause baseline drift and "affect all dyes" [56]; Thermo lists condensation, bubbles, evaporation and optical warping as fluorescence-variation sources [4]; Rotor-Gene has a "Slope Correct" because backgrounds "slope upward or downward before the take-off point" [20]; wrong ROX settings are a recurring support issue [5]; forum users cannot tell bubble from seal failure from detector fault [57]. Cost estimate: minutes per plate for careful users, inconsistent exclusions for everyone else.
3. **What exists today.** Vendor per-well flags (definitions in in-software Help; not trended, not explained); PCRedux features such as slope_bg, sd_plateau, autocorrelation, hookreg (MIT, R) [45]; no published detector with validation metrics for bubbles/evaporation (gap).
4. **The feature.** Input: multi-channel per-cycle data where available (QuantStudio Multicomponent/Raw Data sheets, CFX per-fluorophore RFU, LC480 per-channel export) — captured through the vendor-import path, since RDML carries only the reporter per target. Deterministic signatures, each with an evidence trace and a published or vendor rationale: (a) step/bubble — a first-difference outlier (> k × MAD) at the same cycle in ≥ 2 channels including the passive reference [1][56]; (b) spike/dip — single-cycle outlier in one channel [1]; (c) ground-phase drift — slope of cycles 3–12 relative to noise (Rotor-Gene's slope-correct rationale [20], Biosearch's probe-degradation rationale [56]); (d) late-cycle drift — plateau slope over the last five cycles (PCRedux sd_plateau/tailreg idea [45]) and passive-reference trend, reported together with D2's edge index to suggest evaporation; (e) passive-reference anomaly — ROX level far from the plate median or ≈ 0 (EPFL ranges [5]); (f) hook — late decline after plateau (PCRedux hookreg definition [45]). Output: per-well integrity facts; Decision Card only when the well changes a conclusion (robustness rerun with/without the well); the card shows the multi-channel trace. Record and MCP as usual.
5. **Feasibility.** 2–3 weeks after vendor capture of multi-channel exports; acceptance tests by synthetic injection into real curves (CC BY data) plus an opt-in labelled commons. Hardest: no public ground truth; thresholds must be conservative and reported as "signature present", never as a diagnosis.
6. **Why qPilot.** The per-well evidence and robustness rerun turn an eyeballed exclusion into a recorded, reasoned, re-runnable decision; agents get a trust signal per well.
7. **Risks.** Dependence on vendor multi-channel exports (RDML-only users get a reduced set: spike, drift, hook on the reporter); risk of over-engineering before D1–D3. Confidence: medium.

Ranking rationale: D1 (days, prevents silent wrong answers), D2 (weeks, broad pain, strong fit), D3 (weeks, high value for cores), D4 (weeks, published method, multi-plate studies are the norm), D5 (week, narrower persona), D6 (depends on vendor capture and lacks ground truth).
