## 1. How this corner of the niche works

### 1.0 Narrative (the analysis half of a qPCR study, in one read)

**What the data are.** A qPCR run produces, for every well on a plate (96 or 384 wells), a fluorescence reading per cycle (typically 40–45 cycles): an "amplification curve". The instrument software subtracts a baseline (the early-cycle fluorescence before product is visible), applies a threshold, and reports the fractional cycle at which the curve crosses it: the Cq (also called Ct or Cp). Cq is lower when more target was present at the start. MIQE 2.0 (the 2025 revision of the field's reporting standard) lists the analysis steps that silently change a Cq: how baseline is subtracted, whether and how a threshold is used, whether thresholds are shared across runs or set per run, and whether smoothing is applied [3]. The same document says plainly that Cq, ΔCq and ΔΔCq values are "intermediate data" and that "statistical tests on Cq values should be avoided because these values are not efficiency-corrected and are not normalized" [3].

**Efficiency.** Each cycle multiplies the target by a factor E between 1 and 2 ("PCR efficiency"; 2 = 100%). Converting a Cq into a starting quantity N0 requires E: N0 = Nq / E^Cq where Nq is the fluorescence at the threshold [11]. There are two schools for estimating E: (a) a dilution series ("standard curve": slope of Cq against log input), used by vendor software, most labs and core facilities (the UC Davis core's QA/QC guidance computes E = 10^(−1/slope) from a standard curve [77]); (b) per-well estimation from the log-linear part of each curve, the LinRegPCR family (Ruijter group, Amsterdam), which qPilot already uses via rdmlpython. The two schools disagree in public: Svec et al. (Kubista/Pfaffl groups) show the standard-curve estimate has a 95% CI as wide as 42.5% when built from single replicates, shrinking to 3.3% with 15 replicates, and recommend one careful standard curve per assay with 3–4 replicates per point, never one per run [14]; Ruijter and van den Hoff argue dilution-series efficiencies are biased by pipetting (actual steps were 8- to 9.9-fold instead of 10 in a 69-gene reanalysis) and "circular", and recommend the per-assay mean of per-well efficiencies with a common threshold [12]. MIQE 2.0 takes the Ruijter position for standardised lab experiments ("the mean efficiency extracted from individual curves, using a common quantification threshold ... provides the most sensitive and precise efficiency-corrected target quantification") and allows per-reaction efficiency for inhibited clinical samples [3].

**Normalisation.** Gene-expression studies divide the target quantity by the quantity of one or more "reference genes" assumed stable across conditions. The classic 2^−ΔΔCq shortcut (Livak & Schmittgen 2001) assumes E = 2 for all assays; MIQE 2.0 now says "we strongly recommend against relying on the ΔΔCq approach in quantitative analysis" and prescribes fold difference = mean(GOI/RG)treated / mean(GOI/RG)control computed on efficiency-corrected per-sample quantities, with statistics done "with the normalized data per sample before averaging per group" [3]. Reference-gene choice is validated with geNorm (pairwise stability M, pairwise-variation V with the 0.15 cut-off, "minimal use of the three most stable" genes) [17], NormFinder (model-based intra- and inter-group variation) [18], BestKeeper, the comparative ΔCt method, or the RefFinder web tool that averages the four rankings geometrically [21]. The important catch: RefFinder accepts raw Cq only and ignores efficiency, and when efficiency is ignored geNorm rankings changed in 71% (20/28) of comparisons and NormFinder rankings in 50% (14/28) in De Spiegelaere's test [19]; Sundaram et al. call RefFinder's rank-averaging "not scientifically sound" [20].

**Multi-plate studies.** A study rarely fits on one plate. Plates differ by a near-constant multiplicative factor (Ruijter shows a 6-fold difference between two runs of the same samples) [11]. The two correction methods are inter-run calibrators (IRCs: identical samples repeated on every plate; qBase/qbase+ [16]) and Factor-qPCR (run factors from the geometric mean of all overlapping samples between plates, no dedicated calibrators) [11]. With a single IRC, Hellemans et al. found the error increased by about 70% (ratio 1.68) and with three IRCs by about 40% [16]. Kubista's group instead recommends a single precise efficiency plus a robust inter-plate calibrator "measured at least in triplicate" [14].

**Non-detects.** Wells with no crossing within the run are "undetermined". They are not missing at random: McCall et al. show the probability of a non-detect rises as expression falls (P from 2.6×10⁻⁶ to <2×10⁻¹⁶ across datasets), so substituting Cq = 40 or dropping the well both bias results; in one example Cq = 40 substitution turned observed ΔΔCq values of −0.16 and −1.54 into an average of about −2.5 [24]. The principled alternatives are censored-likelihood models (Pipelers et al., Vandesompele group [28]), EM/multiple imputation (McCall/Sherina, the Bioconductor package `nondetects` [25][27]) or count models where a failed amplification is a zero (Matz 2013 [22]).

**Statistics.** The state of the art is a linear (mixed) model on log-scale quantities per biological sample, with plate and sample as random effects and efficiency uncertainty propagated (Steibel 2009 [30]; Yuan 2006 [31]; Bilgrau 2016 [15]; Hampton 2025 [29]); Bayesian hierarchical versions exist (Matz 2013, R package MCMC.qpcr [22][23]). The state of practice is a t-test on 2^−ΔΔCq fold changes of technical triplicates with a single unvalidated reference gene and 100% efficiency assumed (Bustin & Nolan 2017 [6]; Bustin & Wittwer 2026 [2]).

**Who the "best groups" are and what they actually do.** Ruijter/van den Hoff (Amsterdam UMC): LinRegPCR per-well baseline and efficiency, per-assay mean efficiency, common threshold one cycle below the mean second-derivative maximum, Factor-qPCR for plates, N0 in fluorescence units converted to copies with a single replicated standard (six replicates give "99% precision") [11][12]. Vandesompele/Hellemans (Ghent/Biogazelle): qBase framework — efficiency-corrected relative quantities, geometric-mean normalisation factor, IRC calibration, delta-method error propagation [16]; geNorm [17]; censored regression for non-detects [28]; global-mean normalisation when many targets are measured [70]. Kubista/TATAA (Prague/Gothenburg): nested-ANOVA design (sampling noise dominates for tissue and single cells; RT noise matters for blood) [33], pilot-based power with powerNest [34], LOD/LOQ methods [72], one precise standard curve plus inter-plate calibrators, GenEx software (feature claims could not be verified from the vendor page, see gaps). Bustin/Nolan/Wittwer: MIQE 2.0 [3], audits [2][6], and the 2025 "two-fold is not enough" study giving Cq-dependent minimum credible fold changes [9]. Pfaffl (Munich): efficiency-corrected ratio and REST randomisation test (closed-source Windows tool; code availability not stated) [61].

**Money and time (estimates).** No price data were found for GenEx or qbase+ in this session; the qbase+ product domain qbaseplus.com showed a "domain may be for sale" placeholder when fetched on 17 Oct 2026 and biogazelle.com/qbaseplus returned 404 (status unverified; another researcher covers competitors) [62]. Time: the 2013 benchmark took the original authors of eleven methods to run each method on the same data [1]; Brintz et al. report that manual post-run curve review of a 384-well TaqMan Array Card (up to 768 amplifications) is "time-consuming and subjective" [59].

**What goes wrong (the audited facts).** Bustin & Wittwer's 2026 Clinical Chemistry audit of 355 full-text articles found PCR-efficiency reporting fell from 13% (2019) to 1% (2024), reference-gene validation from 13% to 5%, RNA-integrity reporting from 22% to 11%; MIQE-citing 2024 papers did better (40%, 47%, 31%) "but still omitted essential details"; unknown or uncorrected efficiency "can skew fold-change calculations by 20% to 40% or more" [2]. Dijkstra et al. could adequately assess the validity of only 3% (6/179) of colorectal-cancer qPCR papers [7]. A 2013 Nature-group review found "none of ten papers" reported the essential technical information and all used a discredited analysis method; a 2017 repeat on twenty papers "paints essentially the same picture" [6].

**Key sites and communities.** gene-quantification.de (Pfaffl's method and literature portal) [61]; rdml.org and RDML-Tools/web LinRegPCR (gear-genomics.com) [55][56]; medischebiologie.nl/files (LinRegPCR, Factor-qPCR, the 2013 benchmark data) [57]; Bioconductor (HTqPCR, NormqPCR, ddCt, nondetects, unifiedWMWqPCR) [47][48][49][27][50]; CRAN (qpcR, chipPCR, PCRedux, RDML, MCMC.qpcr) [42][43][44][45][23].

### 1.1 Key question: curve-analysis methods and their comparisons

**Takeaway.** Eleven-plus published methods turn a raw curve into Cq and efficiency; the only developer-run benchmark (Ruijter 2013) found "large differences between methods" on technical performance but "most methods perform relatively well on the biomarker data"; later tests show threshold-based Cq is systematically biased by well position on common instruments, while derivative/sigmoid methods are not. Almost all reference implementations are GPL, closed or unmaintained; only rdmlpython (MIT) is permissive.

**Cited findings.**
- Ruijter et al. 2013 (Methods 59:32–46; 13 authors including the developers of LinRegPCR, PCR-Miner, qpcR/5PSM, MAK2, LRE, Cy0/SOD, FPK-PCR and the qBase group) built a benchmark on the Vermeulen 2009 neuroblastoma biomarker dataset plus dilution and technical experiments, assessing bias, precision and resolution; abstract: "large differences exist between methods when considering the technical performance experiments" and "most methods perform relatively well on the biomarker data"; data and per-method results were posted at qPCRDataMethods.hfrc.nl (now redirecting to medischebiologie.nl/files, which lists "qPCR data with Methods paper") [1][57]. The paper is not open access and the per-method numbers could not be read in this session.
- Ruijter 2009 (LinRegPCR algorithm paper): baseline errors "are directly reflected in the observed PCR efficiency values" and the N0 error is "about an order of magnitude larger" than the efficiency error; independent baseline errors of up to 2% made an observed expression ratio vary from 0.7 to 9.5; a 30% baseline error moved Ct only ~0.3 cycles (so Cq looks fine while N0 is wrong); per-amplicon mean efficiency recommended; using one common efficiency for two amplicons biased the ratio (P < 0.0001) [10].
- Ruijter & van den Hoff 2025 (open-access review): LinRegPCR iterative baseline, four-cycle window with lowest CV of efficiencies, E = 10^slope per reaction, mean per assay; threshold "one cycle below the mean fluorescence at the second derivative maximum"; F0 = F_C / E^Cq; a single standard in six replicates converts to copies with 99% precision; dilution series not recommended (labour, cost, pipetting bias; actual dilution steps 8–9.9-fold) [12].
- Karlen 2007: sigmoidal and exponential curve-fitting methods "performed poorly"; efficiency depended mainly on the amplicon; per-amplicon averaged efficiency performed best; ΔCt was most robust but systematically biased; to detect ~2-fold changes with 50% biological CV you need about 4 biological samples (97 for a 10% confidence range) [32].
- Spiess 2016 (Sci Rep): technical replicates on Bio-Rad CFX96/CFX384 and ABI StepOne show fluorescence periodicity by well position (period ~12 on 96-well, ~24 on 384-well blocks) attributed to optics; fixed-threshold Cq inherits the periodicity while SDM, Cy0, Miner and 5PSM do not; calibration curves built from periodic Ct gave efficiencies 1.79–2.19 and copy numbers 28–86 at Ct 30; authors call fixed-threshold quantitation "highly questionable" and say workers "should either use scale-insensitive Cqs or normalize their growth curves" [36].
- Cy0 (Guescini 2008): Richards-equation fit; equal to Ct/Cp/SCF under optimal conditions but "significantly more accurate and precise" under inhibition; the web tool at cy0method.org requires subscription/login and states no licence [37].
- FPK-PCR (Lievens 2012): whole-curve variable-efficiency model with per-cycle efficiencies for QC; R code, licence not stated in the record [38].
- LRE Analyzer (Rutledge 2011): Java, GNU GPL, absolute quantification via optical calibration factor, "±15–30%" accuracy [39].
- qpcR (Spiess; 5-parameter sigmoid, MAK2, etc.): CRAN, GPL-2 | GPL-3, v1.4-2 (2025-06-10), depends on minpack.lm [42]. chipPCR (baseline, FDM/SDM Cq, smoothing): GPL-3, last release 2021 [43]. PCRedux (curve features for ML): MIT, v1.2-1 (2025-11-19) [44]. pcrEfficiency web/R: GPL v2 [61].
- Chainy (Mallona 2017): web tool computing efficiencies "using kinetic methods", reference-gene checks, normalisation and group tests; "open source and freely available at maplab.cat/chainy" — the site refused connections on 17 Oct 2026 [41].
- Zhang 2021 (CqMAN, modified Gompertz fit) compared seven methods (LinRegPCR, DART, FPLM, FPK-PCR, 5PSM, PCR-Miner, Cy0) on the Ruijter datasets: Friedman test found no significant overall differences; Cy0 had the best relative error on the biomarker set, LinRegPCR the best resolution on the MYCN set; web-only, patents pending [40].
- ML Cq calling: Brintz 2025 trained XGBoost on 165,214 TaqMan Array Card curves with expert calls as gold standard; accuracy 0.996, Ct MAE 0.59 cycles; beat 14 of 17 scientists on calls but only 4 of 17 on Ct error; trained only on stool-metagenome TAC data; data CC0 [59].
- MIQE 2.0 Table 1 (data analysis) requires reporting "Method of baseline correction and Cq determination", "qPCR analysis program (source, version)" and "PCR efficiency estimation and method for its determination", with a footnote that "efficiency-corrected target quantity calculation is necessary" [3].

**Inferences.**
- For a robustness engine, the curve-analysis "universe" should include at least: baseline method (LinRegPCR iterative vs instrument trendline), Cq method (common threshold at SDM−1 vs per-run threshold vs SDM vs Cy0-type fit), and efficiency model (per-well, per-assay mean, per-plate mean, dilution series, assumed 2.0). Spiess 2016 and Ruijter 2009 give direct evidence that these choices move N0 and ratios by factors, not percent.
- The "ML Cq caller" literature is about amplification yes/no and Ct for arrayed diagnostics, not about efficiency-corrected quantities; it is a QC layer, not a replacement for curve analysis.
- A permissive Python implementation of SDM/FDM, Cy0 (Richards fit), 5PSM and per-window efficiency is a few hundred lines with numpy/scipy (curve_fit) each; the papers are public and the maths small. The GPL status of qpcR/chipPCR/LRE Analyzer only forbids copying code, not re-deriving methods.

**Gaps.**
- I could not read the per-method bias/precision/resolution tables of Ruijter 2013 (paywalled); the benchmark data appear to be on medischebiologie.nl/files but terms of use are not stated [57].
- "Cq-free" and Bayesian curve-fitting methods and 2023–2026 methods other than CqMAN/Brintz were not findable without search; the 2026 title "A Deep Learning Approach to Quantitative PCR that Learns from Ground Truth" appeared only in a search-result listing and could not be fetched.

### 1.2 Key question: efficiency handling

**Takeaway.** Efficiency is the largest single analytical lever: ignoring it or ignoring its uncertainty inflates fold changes by tens of percent and inflates false positives; the two camps (one precise standard curve per assay vs per-well/per-assay LinRegPCR) agree that per-run minimal standard curves are the worst option. Efficiency uncertainty should be propagated into every interval and p-value (delta method), which no mainstream tool does.

**Cited findings.**
- Svec 2015: with one replicate per concentration the 95% CI of E was 42.5% (Hprt) and 32.0% (H2afz), falling to 3.3% and 3.1% with 15 replicates; efficiency "varies significantly across different instruments"; recommendations: one robust standard curve with at least 3–4 replicates per concentration, 5 dilution steps (6 concentrations), ≥5 µl transfer volumes, do "not ... perform separate estimates of E in each experiment run", correct runs with an inter-plate calibrator "measured at least in triplicate" [14].
- Bilgrau 2016: plugging in a point estimate of efficiency gave a false-positive rate of 0.073 at nominal 0.05 (bootstrap 0.083) versus 0.053 with their delta-method variance adjustment (2000 simulated datasets, 6 samples per group, 6 dilution steps, target/reference efficiencies 0.80/0.95); in a reanalysed three-dilution Arabidopsis example the p-value moved from 5.35×10⁻⁴ to 0.313; model Cq = μ_ij + A_js + γ_i·x_k + ε with a random sample effect, fitted with lme4; "always accounting for the uncertainty of the AE" is recommended [15].
- Bustin & Wittwer 2026: "at a ΔCq of 4, fold-change estimates vary from 11.3 (92% efficiency) to 23.0 (110%) — a 40% deviation in either direction from the ideal 16-fold value" [2]; Bustin 2025: at ΔCq 6 the range is 50.1 to 85.8; "measure efficiency when ΔCq ≥ 3 and whenever small fold changes (<2×) are interpreted" [9].
- MIQE 2.0: standard curves should have ≥3 technical replicates per dilution step over 4–5 orders of magnitude, with confidence intervals for the efficiency estimate; slopes below −3.32 (E > 100%) usually indicate dilution error; inhibitors raise standard-curve efficiency artificially and lower per-well efficiency; one cycle should be subtracted when converting Cq to cDNA quantities for dye chemistries [3].
- Ruijter 2015 gives the formulas for pooling per-well efficiencies across plates into a per-target mean with a standard error (weighted by reactions per plate; residual sums of squares) and for an efficiency-corrected Cq = −log(N0)/log(E_mean) with threshold 1, so downstream software that only accepts Cq can still be used [11].
- Hellemans 2007 (qBase) propagates the standard error of the slope into E and then into normalised relative quantities with a delta method; "previously ignored efficiency error is now included" [16].
- Bustin & Nolan 2017: "most researchers very rarely measure efficiency and assume it to be 100%", "still the case today" [6]; audits: efficiency reported in 1% of 2024 general papers, 40% of MIQE-citing papers [2]; 24–30% of 461 Arabian-region papers [8].

**Inferences.**
- qPilot already has per-well efficiencies; the missing piece is the variance of the per-assay mean efficiency and its propagation into log-quantity differences (delta method as in Bilgrau/Hellemans). This is a day of work with numpy and turns every fold-change CI from optimistic to honest.
- The "efficiency model" is a legitimate axis of the multiverse with five reasonable universes; the evidence above shows conclusions flip along it (Arabidopsis example), so it belongs in the robustness grid and on a Decision Card.
- A tool can and should refuse to call a fold change below ~1.4 "significant" when the efficiency model is "assumed 2.0" and ΔCq ≥ 3 (Bustin 2025 rule), while still showing the number.

**Gaps.** Pfaffl 2001 (efficiency-corrected ratio) could not be fetched; its formula is restated in Hellemans 2007 [16]. No fetched source quantifies how often per-run minimal standard curves are used in practice.

### 1.3 Key question: normalisation

**Takeaway.** The field's own standard now rejects ΔΔCq; the correct object is the efficiency-corrected per-sample ratio to the geometric mean of validated reference genes, with the number of references chosen by geNorm V (<0.15) or NormFinder's inter-group term, and with rank-averaging tools (RefFinder) treated as a warning sign rather than a method. Normalisation is a log-ratio (compositional) operation; "stable reference" is an assumption that can be tested, not a fact.

**Cited findings.**
- geNorm: single-gene normalisation error of 3.0-fold at the 75th percentile and 6.4-fold at the 90th, "sporadic errors above 20-fold"; M = mean pairwise log-ratio SD; V cut-off 0.15; "minimal use of the three most stable internal control genes"; assumes control genes are not co-regulated [17].
- NormFinder (Andersen 2004): model estimates overall variation and "the variation between sample subgroups", giving a direct estimate of the systematic error a candidate would introduce; "less sensitive to coregulation" than pairwise approaches [18].
- RefFinder (Xie 2023): geometric mean of rank weights from geNorm, NormFinder, BestKeeper, ΔCt; raw-Cq input; PHP source downloadable; 377 citations [21]. De Spiegelaere 2015: RefFinder "has no field for assay efficiency"; ignoring efficiency changed geNorm rankings in 71% and NormFinder rankings in 50% of comparisons; BestKeeper-in-RefFinder ranks by SD rather than by correlation with the index, "producing a substantially different order"; four assays deviated >10% from 100% efficiency; "treat RefFinder as a complementary tool" [19]. Sundaram 2019: geNorm favoured co-regulated Gapdh/Tbp (r = 0.937) with CVs ~59%; rank averaging "not scientifically sound"; recommended CV filter (>50% out) then NormFinder, top two genes [20].
- MIQE 2.0: justify "the selection and number of reference genes"; ΔCq with mean Cq over references loses absolute information; use geometric mean of reference quantities so no single reference dominates; fold difference = mean(GOI/RG)treated / mean(GOI/RG)control; "we discourage the use of the ΔΔCq approach"; checklist items "Method of validation of reference genes" and "Description of normalization method" [3].
- Hellemans 2007 thresholds from their data: reference panels acceptable at mean CV < ~25% and M < ~0.5 (homogeneous samples), up to ~50% and 1 (heterogeneous) [16].
- Global mean normalisation (Mestdagh 2009) for many-target miRNA panels "outperforms the current normalization strategy" [70]; Huggett 2005 lists normalisation to sample size, total RNA or reference genes and notes these are "frequently applied without appropriate validation" [71].
- Compositional view (Quinn 2019): dividing by a reference gene is an additive log-ratio (alr); it is a normalisation only "if the reference is assumed to have fixed absolute abundance across samples", otherwise the output is relative to the chosen reference [69].
- Hampton 2025 (preprint): when treatment shifts the reference gene by one SD, ΔΔCt false-positive rates were 14.9% (ρ = 0), 32.9% (ρ = 0.5) and 93.2% (ρ = 0.9); ANCOVA with the reference as a covariate gave 5.0/12.7/75.5%; ΔCt (no reference) stayed at 4–5%; authors conclude only ΔCt is valid when the reference moves [29].
- Dijkstra 2014: most colorectal-cancer papers used single, unvalidated reference genes; "incorrect normalisation is a widespread problem" [7]; Abdel Nour 2014: normalisation/reference-gene criterion met by 3% of 461 papers [8].

**Inferences.**
- qPilot's existing geNorm M+V, reverse-normalisation guard and "results under every normaliser set" already cover the main axis. What is missing and evidence-backed: (i) stability algorithms must be run on efficiency-corrected quantities (De Spiegelaere), which qPilot can do natively because it has per-well efficiencies; (ii) a NormFinder reimplementation from the 2004 model (the official code forbids redistribution per the brief) adds the inter-group term that geNorm lacks; (iii) when the two algorithms disagree in their top set, that disagreement is itself a Decision Card with per-gene consequences, not something to average away; (iv) an "unnormalised ΔCt-only" universe (Hampton) is a legitimate fallback when the reverse-normalisation guard fires.
- Spike-ins / total RNA / cell-number normalisation: MIQE 2.0 lists "source and amount of spike-in nucleic acids" as a reportable item; no fetched source gives a quantitative comparison of spike-in vs reference-gene normalisation for RT-qPCR (gap).

**Gaps.** BestKeeper and the ΔCt method papers were not fetched (described through De Spiegelaere/Sundaram). No study quantifying how often reference-gene ratios shift between groups in published datasets was found.

### 1.4 Key question: inter-run calibration, hierarchical models, non-detects, replicates, multiple testing

**Takeaway.** The right model for a multi-plate study is a linear model on log-scale efficiency-corrected per-sample quantities with plate (and sample/RT) as random or factor effects, non-detects as right-censored observations at an assay LOD, efficiency uncertainty propagated, and inference per biological unit; Bayesian versions add prediction intervals naturally. No permissive-licence implementation of this combination exists; the pieces exist in GPL R packages and papers.

**Cited findings.**
- Between-run variation is multiplicative ("parallel lines per run" on a log plot); calibrator-based correction is biased by failed calibrators and artificially removes residual error from calibrator samples; Factor-qPCR derives run factors as geometric means of all overlapping-condition ratios, substitutes missing overlaps by nested geometric means, and recommends "run another plate" if more than two rounds of substitution are needed; balanced overlap designs avoid losing condition effects; export to RDML [11]. Hellemans: single IRC → mean error increase 1.68 (max 10.98); two IRCs 1.37–1.49; three 1.40 (max 5.28); IRC stability M computed like geNorm [16].
- Mixed models: Steibel 2009 — general linear mixed model on CT values of target and control genes "more powerful" than the comparative CT method with identical fold-change estimates in plasmode simulations [30]; Yuan 2006 — regression/ANCOVA on Ct gave ΔΔCt −0.685 (SE 0.119) versus t-test SE 0.130, CI for fold change obtained by transforming the ΔΔCt CI endpoints, minimum of 2 replicates at 3 concentrations [31]; Bilgrau 2016 — lme4 model with random sample effect and delta-method variance [15]; Hampton 2025 — lm(target ~ donor + treatment + reference), power 72–76% vs 48–53% for ΔΔCT at ρ = 0 [29].
- Bayesian: Matz 2013 — Poisson-lognormal GLMM on counts derived from Cq and efficiency; random effects for template loading, grouping factor, gene×sample and gene×technical replicate; a failed amplification is a zero count and "informative"; control genes optional as priors allowing ~1.2-fold drift; all Bayesian models "had higher power than the original pairwise t-tests"; implemented in MCMC.qpcr (GPL-3, v1.2.4, 2025-11-08, wraps MCMCglmm) [22][23]. Sherina 2019 — fully Bayesian imputation for non-random missing qPCR data, software not stated [26].
- Prediction intervals: MIQE 2.0 — report both confidence intervals (slope/efficiency) and prediction intervals ("the correct measure for the precision of the reported target quantity") for standard-curve quantification; Cq values "should be converted into efficiency-corrected target quantities and reported with prediction intervals, along with detection limits and dynamic ranges" [3].
- Non-detects: McCall 2014 (MNAR evidence, Cq = 40 substitution creates spurious group differences; `nondetects` package) [24]; Sherina 2020 (multiple imputation because single imputation "underestimates residual variance, which can make inference anti-conservative") [25]; Pipelers 2017 (right-censoring at LOD, maximum likelihood, one-step normalisation and testing; censoring tolerance before a type II error: LOD-substitution 0.69, MNV+1 0.79, KNN 0.34, UCNR 0.90; code GPL-3) [28]; NormqPCR reports fold change as "+" or "−" when a transcript is undetermined in only one condition and warns that imputation "may inflate reference-gene stability values" [48]; MIQE 2.0: LOD cannot be below 3 copies; LLOQ ~10 copies ≈ Cq 35 at 90% efficiency (LOD at 37), 42/45 at 70%; a positive reaction in a validated qualitative assay "should be reported, irrespective of the Cq value"; negative control amplification requires unknowns to precede it by ≥5 cycles [3]; Forootan 2017 gives LOD/LOQ procedures adapted from regulatory statistics [72].
- Replicates: Lazic 2018 — of 200 animal studies, 22% had genuine replication, 46% pseudoreplication, 32% unclear; reduce to one value per experimental unit [35]; Tichopad 2009 — nested ANOVA: sampling noise dominates for solid tissue and single cells, RT and qPCR noise matter for blood and cultures; replicate at the noisiest level [33]; Kitchen 2010 — pilot variance components → power/optimal design within cost (powerNest) [34]; Bustin 2025 — ≥3 technical replicates, 6 replicates to detect 1.5-fold at SD 0.5 Cq with 80% power, ≥5 replicates above Cq 30 because Poisson sampling dominates [9]; MIQE 2.0 — "consistent reporting of technical replicates"; arithmetic mean of Cq is the geometric mean of quantities; replicate description and repeatability are checklist items [3].
- Fold change vs absolute copies: Ruijter 2021 — efficiency correction required; a reaction with specific amplification is positive regardless of Cq; single undiluted calibrator for absolute quantities; proposed MIQE items on how efficiency and quantities were determined [13]; MIQE 2.0 adopts absolute copies via a replicated single standard [3].
- Multiple testing and effect sizes: HTqPCR provides "parametric or non-parametric significance testing" across many targets and depends on limma [47]; unifiedWMWqPCR offers a modified Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney test for qPCR [50]; no fetched qPCR-specific source discusses false-discovery control explicitly (gap). Equivalence testing: no qPCR application found (gap).

**Inferences.**
- Plate as a random effect and Factor-qPCR are the same idea in two dialects: a multiplicative run factor is an additive plate intercept on the log scale. A mixed model estimates it jointly with the treatment effect and its uncertainty; Factor-qPCR estimates it first and then pretends it is known. The engine should run both and show whether they agree (they will, in balanced designs; in unbalanced designs the mixed model is the safer default).
- Censoring at an assay-specific LOD is implementable in a few dozen lines with scipy (normal log-likelihood with Φ terms for censored points), which is exactly Pipelers' UCNR; the GPL only covers their R scripts.
- Prediction intervals for per-sample quantities fall out of the same model (residual + random-effect variance); MIQE 2.0 asks for them, nobody reports them, and a Cq-input tool cannot produce them honestly because it lacks the efficiency and replicate variances.

**Gaps.** No paper using brms/Stan/PyMC specifically for qPCR was found in this session (MCMC.qpcr uses MCMCglmm; Sherina 2019's software unstated). Pseudoreplication prevalence specifically in qPCR papers is not quantified in any fetched source (Lazic's 46% is across animal studies).

### 1.5 Key question: malpractice evidence

**Takeaway.** Four audits spanning 2013–2026 agree: efficiency is almost never measured, reference genes are almost never validated, MIQE citation does not guarantee compliance, and the dominant analysis (2^−ΔΔCq on technical replicates, no uncertainty) is the one the standard now explicitly discourages. Statistical-error-specific audits (t-tests on ratios, SEM of n = 3 wells) were not found as separate studies; the evidence is embedded in the audits' qualitative findings and in MIQE 2.0's prescriptions.

**Cited findings.**
- Bustin & Wittwer 2026 (Clin Chem, hvaf176): 355 articles from 2019 and 2024 (3×85 random "RT-qPCR"/"MIQE" cohorts + 2×50 targeted); RNA integrity 22%→11% (P = 0.039); reference-gene validation 13%→5%; PCR efficiency 13%→1% (P = 0.004); MIQE-citing 2024: 31%/47%/40%; PMC "PCR efficiency" mentions fell from 3% (2007) to <1% (2025); MIQE citation peaked ~5% in 2014–2016; even reference-gene-titled papers reported all three parameters in only 56%, efficiency-titled papers in 24%; "reviewers seldom assess methodological adequacy, and journals rarely require compliance documentation"; recommends "integration of MIQE-derived modules within electronic laboratory notebooks or data repositories" [2].
- Bustin 2025 IJMS editorial: "fold-changes of 1.2 to 1.5 are reported as biologically meaningful, even at low expression, without measurement uncertainty or technical variance"; genes declared regulated "when confidence intervals span significance thresholds"; MIQE compliance "patchy, and in many cases, entirely superficial" [4].
- Bustin & Wittwer 2025 (Methods): "Flawed qPCR studies routinely lead to the publication of unreliable or exaggerated fold-changes"; common failures: unvalidated reference genes, uncertainty around efficiency measurement, inadequate reporting of Cq variation; "Although web-based tools now exist to guide proper qPCR analysis, uptake remains limited" [5].
- Bustin & Nolan 2017: a replication study of MYC down-regulation found 6%/8% of control in the original vs 14%/12% in the replicate — "a twofold difference", while "changes in RNA levels reported by many publications using RT-qPCR data tend to be in that range, typically between 1.5- and fivefold"; "fold changes less than three- to fivefold are likely to be unreliable"; master-mix choice alone changed ΔCq for the same cDNA (their Figure 2); "just under 60% of respondents posited that poor analysis was an important contributor to irreproducible research" [6].
- Dijkstra 2014: 179 colorectal-cancer qPCR papers (2006–2013), validity adequately assessable in 3% [7]. Abdel Nour 2014: 461 papers, median 6/15 MIQE criteria, 2% cited MIQE, IF ≥ 5 journals less compliant [8].
- MIQE 2.0's own list of analysis-stage variability: "background subtraction algorithms, calculations of Cq values, quantification thresholds, outlier detection and exclusion, handling of missing values, diagnostic cutoffs ..., the use of standard curves, and choice of reference genes ... can systematically affect reported outcomes" [3].
- Pabinger 2014 tool survey: of 27 open tools only three read RDML, nine offer statistics, five propagate error, most output bar plots (authors recommend box plots) [60].

**Inferences.**
- The malpractice is not exotic: it is the default path of vendor software plus Excel. A tool that makes the correct path the default and the shortcut path a visible, logged deviation addresses the audited gaps directly.
- Reviewer behaviour ("seldom assess methodological adequacy") means an automated, record-backed statistics statement would be read by reviewers who otherwise check nothing — that is where a "reviewer-critic" output earns its keep.

**Gaps.** No retraction database could be searched (Retraction Watch and search engines unavailable); no forum threads could be fetched. A dedicated audit of statistical tests used in qPCR papers was not found.

### 1.7 Key question: a "methods paper" for a robustness/multiverse approach and validation datasets

**Takeaway.** No multiverse or specification-curve analysis of qPCR was found (the one targeted search returned nothing on qPCR); the methodology is mature in psychology/ecology/neuroimaging and the "many analysts" studies show the problem is general. Public datasets with permissive terms exist for the technical layer (Ruijter 2013 technical sets, CC BY 4.0 via the `ruijter` R package; Brintz 2025, CC0) and for non-detect/efficiency statistics (UCNR, effadj, nondetects example data), but the biomarker benchmark's terms are unstated.

**Cited findings.**
- Steegen 2016: perform "a multiverse analysis" over all reasonable data-processing choices rather than one analysis; shows how much results depend on arbitrary choices and which choices matter [64]. Simonsohn 2020: three steps — enumerate theoretically justified, statistically valid, non-redundant specifications; display graphically; joint inference across specifications [65]. Boba: DSL + visualiser, BSD-3 [68].
- Many-analysts evidence: 70 teams, 9 hypotheses, one fMRI dataset, substantial variation in conclusions [66]; 174 teams/246 analysts in ecology, effects ranging across zero, about a third of Eucalyptus effects crossing significance in one direction or the other [67].
- Validation data: `ruijter` R package (Ramiro Magno) — ds_94_4 (MYCN, four 10-fold dilutions, 384 curves), ds_380 (replicates), ds_competimer (efficiency modulation), CC BY 4.0 [58]; "qPCR data with Methods paper" on medischebiologie.nl/files (terms not stated) [57]; Brintz 2025 — 165,214 curves with expert calls, CC0 on GitHub (Zenodo metadata says CC BY 4.0; 67.8 MB archive) [59]; Bilgrau's effadj repository (data, R, knitr; article data under CC0 per the article) [15][78]; UCNR case-study data (GPL-3 repo) [28]; nondetects package ships the three McCall datasets (GPL-3) [24][27]; Ruijter & van den Hoff 2025 include an Excel qPCR simulator (File S1) [12]; MCMC.qpcr includes coral and fibroblast example data (GPL-3 package) [22][23].
- Vermeulen 2009 (Lancet Oncology; the 2013 benchmark's biomarker set) could not be located via Europe PMC's DOI lookup in this session; its reuse terms are unknown (gap).

**Inferences.** A method paper is viable (Section 3, D6) because it combines three things no one has published together: a raw-curve-to-conclusion specification space with evidence-weighted axes, a joint-inference summary tuned to qPCR (sign-and-CI agreement rather than p-value vote-counting), and a re-analysis of public datasets showing how many published "significant" genes survive. The technical benchmark data permit a bias/precision/resolution table per universe, in the style of Ruijter 2013.

**Gaps.** No prior qPCR multiverse to compare against; no licence statement for the 2013 biomarker data; GEO RT-qPCR series could not be enumerated without search.

## 3. Discoveries

### 3.0 Design derivation: what a tool should sweep, model and refuse (evidence-backed)

**Sweep (each axis has fetched evidence that it changes conclusions):**
1. Baseline method: LinRegPCR iterative per-reaction vs instrument early-cycle trendline — Ruijter 2009 ratio 0.7–9.5 under 2% baseline error [10].
2. Cq method / threshold: common threshold at SDM−1 cycle (LinRegPCR) vs per-run threshold vs SDM vs Cy0-type fit — Spiess 2016 periodicity, E 1.79–2.19 from periodic Ct [36]; MIQE 2.0 names "whether thresholds are consistent across runs" as a source of discordance [3].
3. Efficiency model: per-well; per-assay mean (LinRegPCR default); per-plate mean (Ruijter 2015 Eq. 7); dilution-series; assumed 2.0 — Bilgrau p 5×10⁻⁴ → 0.31 [15]; Bustin 11.3–23.0 at ΔCq 4 [2].
4. Efficiency-uncertainty handling: plug-in vs delta-method propagation — FPR 0.073 vs 0.053 [15].
5. Reference set and stability algorithm input: geNorm vs NormFinder; efficiency-corrected vs raw-Cq input — 71%/50% ranking changes [19]; plus the "no reference, ΔCt-only" universe when the reference moves [29]. (qPilot already sweeps reference subsets.)
6. Plate model: none; Factor-qPCR run factors; IRC calibration; plate random intercept — 6-fold run differences [11]; IRC error inflation [16].
7. Non-detect rule: censor at assay LOD; EM/multiple imputation; substitute LOD; substitute Cq 40; drop — McCall [24]; Pipelers censoring-tolerance 0.90 vs 0.34–0.79 [28].
8. Replicate aggregation: per-well modelling vs mean per sample (geometric) vs arithmetic mean of linear quantities — MIQE 2.0: arithmetic mean of Cq equals geometric mean of quantities [3].
9. Test: linear model on log quantities vs t-test on ΔCq vs Wilcoxon vs randomisation (REST) — Yuan 2006 SE 0.119 vs 0.130 [31]; Matz 2013 higher power [22].
10. Outlier rule for technical replicates (kinetic outlier detection) — already a qPilot roadmap item; not re-researched here.

**Model (the default engine):** log-scale efficiency-corrected per-well quantity y = log N0 with N0 = Nq/E^Cq; fixed effects for condition × target; random intercepts for plate and biological sample (and RT replicate when present); right-censoring at the assay's LOD quantity; efficiency uncertainty propagated by the delta method from the per-assay mean E and its SE (Ruijter 2015 Eq. 9–11 [11]; Bilgrau [15]); inference per biological unit; prediction intervals for per-sample quantities; fold difference as MIQE 2.0 defines it [3]. Bayesian variant (optional, versioned module) for small n and non-detect-heavy data, following Matz's Poisson-lognormal structure [22].

**Refuse (or downgrade to "cannot conclude", always with the number still visible and a logged reason):**
- Any p-value or "significant" label computed on Cq, ΔCq or ΔΔCq without efficiency correction (MIQE 2.0 [3]).
- Any group test where the number of biological units per group is < 2, or where wells of one cDNA are the only replication (Lazic [35]; MIQE 2.0 per-sample statistics [3]).
- Fold-change claims below the Cq-dependent credible floor without demonstrated precision: < 2× at Cq < 25, < 3× at Cq 25–30, < 4× at Cq > 30 (Bustin 2025 [9]) and any claim when the bootstrap/model CI includes 1.
- Interpreting small (< 2×) changes or ΔCq ≥ 3 differences under an "assumed 100%" efficiency universe (Bustin 2025 [9]).
- Non-detect substitution with a fixed Cq when > ~30% of a group is censored without a sensitivity analysis (Pipelers' censoring tolerance [28]).
- Inter-run correction when overlap between plates requires more than two rounds of substitution ("run another plate" [11]).
- Any conclusion that differs across the reference-set universes when a reference-gene pair's ratio shifts between groups (already a qPilot guard; Hampton's 93% false-positive rate quantifies why [29]).

### D1. Efficiency-uncertainty-aware inference and the efficiency-model sweep (rank 2 of 8)

1. **Persona and situation.** Any scientist comparing two groups where the target and reference assays differ in efficiency (most assays: four of eleven assays deviated > 10% in De Spiegelaere's panel [19]); daily, every analysis.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** 1% of 2024 papers report efficiency [2]; fold changes vary 11.3–23.0 across 92–110% efficiency at ΔCq 4 [2]; ignoring efficiency uncertainty gave FPR 0.073 at nominal 0.05 and flipped a p-value from 5.35×10⁻⁴ to 0.313 in a three-dilution example [15]; Svec: one-replicate standard curves carry 32–42.5% CIs [14]. Cost: false positives and unreproducible fold changes ("twofold difference" between original and replicate MYC study [6]).
3. **What exists.** qbase+ propagates slope error (closed, status uncertain) [16][62]; LinRegPCR reports per-well E and per-assay mean without carrying its SE into group tests [12]; vendor software and all Cq-input tools assume E or use a per-run standard curve that Svec explicitly discourages [14]; Bilgrau's delta-method code is an unlicensed knitr repo [78].
4. **The concrete feature.** Inputs: qPilot's per-well E and Cq from rdmlpython (RDML), plus Nq (common threshold). Engine: (a) per-assay mean E with SE pooled over plates by Ruijter 2015 Eq. 7–11 [11]; (b) per-well log quantity y = log Nq − Cq·log E with variance from the delta method, Var(y) ≈ (Cq/E)²·Var(E) + Var(Cq-residual); (c) group contrasts on y with the combined variance (Bilgrau EC&VA1 [15]); (d) a five-universe sweep over the efficiency model {per-well, per-assay mean, per-plate mean, dilution-series if standards exist, assumed 2.0}, with the delta-method CI in each. Decision Card: "Efficiency model" with per-gene consequence table (fold change, CI, p under each universe), recommendation = per-assay mean with propagation (MIQE 2.0 [3]) unless inhibition flags suggest per-well (MIQE 2.0 clinical clause [3]). Record: E_mean, SE(E), method, universe results, chosen universe and reason. MCP: `qpcr_robustness(axis="efficiency_model")` returns the table; `qpcr_decide` logs the choice.
5. **Feasibility.** numpy/scipy only; formulas are in open papers [11][15][16]; effort 3–5 days including tests against Bilgrau's published numbers (FPR 0.073/0.053 reproduced by simulation) and Svec's CI widths. Hardest part: deciding the variance of Cq residuals when technical replicates are absent (fall back to the assay's pooled residual).
6. **Why qPilot.** Only a raw-curve tool has per-well E and its spread; the robustness grid and Decision Cards already exist; the Analysis Record makes "which efficiency universe" auditable.
7. **Risks.** Delta method is first-order; at very low copy numbers Poisson noise dominates (MIQE 2.0 [3]) and a bootstrap (Bustin 2025 [9]) may be needed — include it as a second estimator. Confidence: high (methods published and numerically checkable).

### D2. The study-level model: plate-aware, censored, log-scale mixed model with prediction intervals (rank 1 of 8)

1. **Persona and situation.** The PhD student or core analyst with 4–12 plates, 6–40 biological samples, 3–8 targets, some "undetermined" wells; once per study, and again for every reviewer revision.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Between-run factors up to 6× [11]; single-IRC error inflation ~70% [16]; non-detects MNAR and Cq = 40 substitution creating spurious effects [24]; single imputation anti-conservative [25]; pseudoreplication 46% [35]; MIQE 2.0 asks for prediction intervals and per-sample statistics that no mainstream tool produces [3]; Pabinger: only five of 27 tools propagate error [60].
3. **What exists.** Factor-qPCR (Windows binary, licence unstated) [57]; qbase+ IRCs (closed) [16]; MCMC.qpcr (GPL-3, R, MCMCglmm; Bayesian, handles zeros) [23]; nondetects (GPL-3) [27]; UCNR (GPL-3 R scripts) [28]; lme4-based ad hoc scripts (Bilgrau [15]; Hampton [29]). None combine plates, censoring, efficiency propagation and prediction intervals; none are permissive or callable.
4. **The concrete feature.** Input: the per-well table qPilot already builds (sample id, biological unit, group/covariates, target, plate, Cq, E, Nq, flags, "undetermined"), plus per-assay LOD quantity (from NTC/negative-control Cq and MIQE 2.0's ≥ 5-cycle rule, or from a dilution series via Forootan's procedure when present [3][72]). Model: y_ijkp = log N0 as in D1; y = μ + α_target + β_group×target + u_sample + v_plate + ε, with u, v ~ Normal; undetermined wells enter the likelihood as P(y < y_LOD) = Φ((y_LOD − mean)/σ) (right-censored Cq = left-censored log quantity), as in UCNR [28]; variance of E added by the delta method (D1). Estimation: maximum likelihood with scipy.optimize over the marginal likelihood (random effects integrated by Gauss–Hermite or Laplace; for balanced designs statsmodels MixedLM can be used for the uncensored case and a censored EM step added), deterministic seeds, versioned. Outputs: per-target group contrasts (log fold difference, CI, p), per-sample normalised quantities with prediction intervals (random-effect variance + residual), plate factors with SE (to compare against Factor-qPCR), ICC per level (sample/plate/residual) feeding `qpilot plan`. Sweep axes (Section 3.0, items 6–9): plate model {none, Factor-qPCR, IRC, random intercept}, non-detect rule {censor, EM impute, substitute LOD, substitute 40, drop}, test {LMM, t on ΔCq, Wilcoxon}. Refusals: biological n < 2; > 2 substitution rounds in Factor-qPCR; conclusions that flip between "censor" and "drop". Decision Cards: "Plate correction", "Undetermined wells", "What counts as n". Record: model spec, variance components, censoring points, convergence diagnostics, universe table. MCP: `qpcr_study(model="lmm_censored")`, `qpcr_robustness(axis="plate_model"|"nondetect_rule")`.
5. **Feasibility.** numpy, scipy, statsmodels (all BSD-3). Published reference results to test against: Ruijter 2015's between-run ratio matrix and factors (Fig. 2 values 1.186, 0.514, 1.305, 0.652, 2.672, 0.722) [11]; Hellemans Table 2 error inflation [16]; Pipelers' censoring-tolerance ordering [28]; Hampton's simulated FPRs [29]; Bilgrau's FPR [15]. Effort: 3–4 weeks for a first validated version (censored LMM is the hard part; Laplace approximation over two crossed random effects is the hardest numerical piece); a Bayesian variant via PyMC (Apache-2.0) adds 1–2 weeks and gives prediction intervals natively but must be a separate versioned module to keep the core deterministic.
6. **Why qPilot.** It already has whole-study per-well data, plate identity, the robustness grid and the record; the model is just the right "engine fact" generator. Cq-input competitors cannot propagate efficiency or build prediction intervals; GPL R packages cannot be embedded.
7. **Risks.** Over-complex for 1-plate, 3-vs-3 studies — the engine should degrade gracefully to the simple model and say so. Convergence failures in sparse designs must be surfaced, never hidden. Confidence: high on need, medium on effort estimate.

### D3. The "claim floor": Cq-range-aware minimum credible fold change with replicate-level bootstrap (rank 3 of 8)

1. **Persona and situation.** Every biologist about to write "X was upregulated 1.6-fold (p < 0.05)"; PIs reviewing figures; reviewers.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** "Fold-changes of 1.2 to 1.5 are reported as biologically meaningful ... without measurement uncertainty" [4]; "fold changes less than three- to fivefold are likely to be unreliable" [6]; empirical 95% CI widths of 2.2–4.0 (Cq < 25), 1.4–2.7 (Cq 25–30) and 0.71–14.74 around a 4.56 estimate above Cq 30; recommended floors 2×/3×/4× by Cq range; ≥ 5 replicates above Cq 30 [9]; LLOQ ≈ 10 copies ≈ Cq 35 at 90% E [3].
3. **What exists.** Bustin's paper gives rules and a bootstrap, no software; GraphPad gives p-values on anything; qPilot's GRADE-style confidence level exists but is not tied to a Cq-dependent floor.
4. **The concrete feature.** For each target: compute mean Cq per group and the floor from the Bustin table; bootstrap fold change from replicate-level (biological-unit-level) quantities 10,000× with efficiency drawn from N(E_mean, SE) (combining D1); produce the 95% CI; label the claim "below credible floor" when |log FC| < log(floor) or CI includes 1; add an equivalence-style statement ("the data rule out changes larger than Y×") computed from the CI (TOST logic; Lakens 2017 is the standard reference but was not fetched — treat as an inference). Decision Card only when the label would change the study's headline. Record: floor used, CI, bootstrap seed, replicate counts per group. `qpilot plan` tie-in: required replicates to detect 1.5× at SD 0.5 Cq with 80% power = 6 [9]. MCP: `qpcr_study` returns `claim_floor` and `ci` per gene; the AI layer may not state a fold change without them (extends "no naked numbers").
5. **Feasibility.** 2–4 days; numpy only; acceptance test: reproduce Bustin's modelled 11.3/16.0/23.0 at ΔCq 4 and the CI width bands on their published replicate design [9].
6. **Why qPilot.** The "no naked numbers" and confidence-level machinery exist; the floor is a fact id the AI must cite.
7. **Risks.** Bustin's thresholds are "context-specific and should be determined empirically for each assay" [9] — present them as defaults the lab can override (lab rule pack), never as hard refusals of the number itself. Confidence: high.

### D4. Curve-analysis multiverse: baseline, Cq-method and threshold universes from the raw curves (rank 4 of 8)

1. **Persona and situation.** Labs mixing instruments (CFX, QuantStudio, LightCycler) or re-analysing old runs; cores asked "why does your Cq differ from ours"; anyone on a CFX96/384 or StepOne block.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Well-position periodicity in threshold Cq on CFX96/384 and StepOne; efficiencies 1.79–2.19 and copies 28–86 at Ct 30 from periodic Ct; SDM/Cy0/5PSM immune [36]; baseline error → N0 error "an order of magnitude larger" than the E error [10]; MIQE 2.0 lists baseline, threshold, per-run threshold and smoothing as reportable choices [3]; the 2013 benchmark found "large differences between methods" on technical data [1].
3. **What exists.** One method per tool: LinRegPCR (common threshold), vendor software (per-run auto-threshold), Cy0 (subscription web) [37], qpcR (GPL) [42], chipPCR SDM/FDM (GPL-3) [43]. No tool runs several and reports agreement.
4. **The concrete feature.** From the baseline-corrected curves already produced by rdmlpython, implement in numpy/scipy: FDM and SDM via 5-point-stencil derivatives on smoothed curves (chipPCR describes the method [43]); Cy0 from a Richards fit (five parameters, scipy.optimize.curve_fit, tangent at the inflection as in Guescini 2008 [37]); a 5-parameter log-logistic/sigmoid Cq (Spiess 2008 model as cited in MIQE 2.0 [3]); per-run vs common threshold. Also a well-position periodicity check (autocorrelation of plateau fluorescence across well index; Spiess's test [36]) that raises a Decision Card recommending scale-insensitive Cq universes when periodicity is detected. Sweep: Cq method × threshold policy; report per-gene whether the log fold difference's sign and CI agree. Record: per-well Cq under each method, periodicity statistic, chosen universe. MCP: `qpcr_robustness(axis="cq_method")`.
5. **Feasibility.** 2–3 weeks including validation on the `ruijter` CC BY 4.0 dilution sets (bias/precision/resolution per method as in Ruijter 2013) [58] and the Brintz CC0 curves for amplification-call agreement [59]. Hardest part: robust Richards/sigmoid fits on noisy or late curves (need bounded fits and failure flags).
6. **Why qPilot.** Only possible with raw curves; every Cq-input competitor (Clarida, AnnealIQ, Auto-qPCR, SATqPCR) is structurally unable to do it; it validates publicly against the RDML benchmark the team already uses.
7. **Risks.** Reimplementations must be tested against the originals' published outputs, not just each other; Cy0's reference tool is behind a login. Confidence: medium-high.

### D5. Replicate-structure guard and variance components ("what is your n?") (rank 5 of 8)

1. **Persona and situation.** Students and small labs designing 3 wells × 1 cDNA × 2 groups; core managers asked to "do the stats".
2. **Evidence of the pain.** 46% pseudoreplication, 32% unclear [35]; MIQE 2.0 requires statistics per sample before group averaging and "description of replicates" [3]; Tichopad: sampling noise dominates for tissue and single cells, RT noise for blood — replicate at the noisy level [33]; Kitchen: pilot variance components → power and cost-optimal design [34].
3. **What exists.** powerNest (Kubista group; availability/licence not verified); GraphPad takes any n; qPilot `plan` exists without variance components from the lab's own data.
4. **The concrete feature.** From RDML sample annotations (and the lab profile), infer the hierarchy well ⊂ cDNA ⊂ RNA extraction ⊂ biological unit ⊂ group; when the hierarchy is ambiguous, raise a Decision Card ("Are wells A1–A3 one cDNA or three animals?") with the consequence (n per group and whether a p-value is allowed). Estimate variance components at each present level from the D2 model (ICCs) and write them into the record as the lab's "noise passport" per assay; `qpilot plan` then uses them (replicate allocation rule: spend replicates where the component is largest [33]). Refuse group tests with < 2 biological units per group; downgrade to descriptive output with a logged reason.
5. **Feasibility.** 1 week on top of D2; the inference logic is metadata handling; RDML's sample/annotation elements already carry what is needed when the user fills them (the hard part is that they often do not — hence the card).
6. **Why qPilot.** Decision Cards with impact ranking are the right UI for "this choice changes whether you have a p-value at all"; the record keeps the declared design for the methods text.
7. **Risks.** Over-prompting if layouts are messy — rank the card by impact and batch questions per plate. Confidence: high on need, medium on metadata availability.

### D6. The methods paper: "A specification-curve (multiverse) framework for qPCR, from raw curves to conclusions", with a validation plan (rank 6 of 8; it is a deliverable, not a feature)

1. **Persona and situation.** The qPilot team (publishable credibility), reviewers and editors (a citable justification for demanding robustness), method-minded cores.
2. **Evidence.** No qPCR multiverse exists (search found none; Section 1.7); many-analysts variability is documented in neuroimaging (70 teams [66]) and ecology (174 teams, ~a third of effects crossing significance either way [67]); specification-curve methodology is published [64][65]; Pabinger's survey shows the tool landscape lacks statistics and error propagation [60]; the audits show the practice gap [2][6][7][8].
3. **What exists.** Boba (BSD-3) as a generic multiverse runner/visualiser [68]; Ruijter 2013 as the only method benchmark [1].
4. **The concrete deliverable.** Paper structure: (i) define the specification space (Section 3.0 axes) with a "reasonableness tier" per universe (tier 1 = MIQE 2.0-recommended, tier 2 = published alternative, tier 3 = common-but-discouraged such as assumed 100% E and Cq = 40 substitution), so the summary can be reported for tier-1 universes and for all; (ii) joint-inference summary tailored to qPCR: for each target, the fraction of universes whose CI excludes 1 with the same sign, the median and range of log fold difference, and the single most influential axis (variance decomposition of the estimate across axes), avoiding p-value vote counting (the known weakness of specification curves); (iii) validation on (a) technical data with known truth: `ruijter` dilution and replicate sets (CC BY 4.0) for bias/precision/resolution per universe [58]; (b) simulated studies from a generative model with plates, censoring and efficiency drift (Ruijter & van den Hoff's simulator as a starting point [12]) to measure CI coverage with and without efficiency propagation (reproducing Bilgrau's 0.073 vs 0.053 [15]); (c) re-analysis of public datasets with reported conclusions (nondetects' three datasets [24], UCNR case studies [28], MCMC.qpcr examples [22], Hampton's donor data [29], the 2013 biomarker set if terms allow [57]) reporting how many published "significant" targets are robust across tier-1 universes; (d) amplification-call agreement with expert calls on Brintz's 165k curves [59]. Deposit: code (Apache-2.0), RDML inputs, REPLICATE.json, hash-chained records.
5. **Feasibility.** 6–10 weeks of writing and compute on top of D1–D4; venues: BMC Bioinformatics, Bioinformatics (application note), Methods, NAR Methods; preprint on bioRxiv. Hardest part: obtaining re-analysable raw curves for published studies (most deposit Cq only — which is itself a finding the paper can report).
6. **Why qPilot.** The robustness engine is the method; the record is the reproducibility artefact; the RDML benchmark validation already exists.
7. **Risks.** Reviewers from the Ruijter/Vandesompele schools will scrutinise the curve-analysis reimplementations; validate against their published numbers and invite them to co-review. Confidence: medium-high that it is publishable; medium on timeline.

### D7. Reference-gene decisions on efficiency-corrected inputs with NormFinder-from-the-model and an "algorithms disagree" card (rank 7 of 8)

1. **Persona and situation.** Plant, veterinary, microbiology and cancer labs validating reference genes (the one qPCR sub-genre whose papers still report methods: 84% efficiency reporting in reference-gene-titled papers [2]); every new tissue/condition.
2. **Evidence.** RefFinder's raw-Cq input and rank averaging [21][19][20]; geNorm's co-regulation blind spot [17][20]; NormFinder's inter-group term [18]; 95% of general papers do not validate [2].
3. **What exists.** RefFinder (PHP, raw Cq) [21]; geNorm in qbase+ (closed); NormqPCR (LGPL-3) has both algorithms [48]; NormFinder official code forbids redistribution (brief).
4. **The concrete feature.** Run geNorm (already in qPilot) and a NormFinder reimplementation from Andersen 2004's variance model [18] on efficiency-corrected log quantities (from D1/D2), plus a co-regulation screen (pairwise correlation of candidate references with each other and with targets, flagging the Gapdh/Tbp-type pairs [20]) and NormFinder's inter-group variation as the principled "ratio shifts between groups" statistic alongside qPilot's existing guard. When the top-k sets disagree, raise one Decision Card with the per-target consequences under each set (qPilot's existing "every normaliser set" results make this cheap). Offer global-mean normalisation as a universe when ≥ ~10 targets are measured [70]. Record: stability tables, inputs (efficiency-corrected flag), chosen set and reason.
5. **Feasibility.** 1 week; numpy; validation against De Spiegelaere's published rankings (FS1/CIS panels) [19] and Sundaram's tables [20].
6. **Why qPilot.** Per-well efficiencies make "efficiency-corrected inputs" automatic; the sweep already exists.
7. **Risks.** NormFinder's model has implementation subtleties (group variance estimation with small n > 8 recommended [19]); document the deviation from the official Excel add-in. Confidence: medium (overlaps roadmap "NormFinder reimplementation"; the new part is the inputs, the disagreement card and the co-regulation screen).

### D8. A machine-derived MIQE 2.0 "Data analysis" statement and statistics paragraph from the record, with the items that can never be auto-filled made explicit (rank 8 of 8)

1. **Persona and situation.** Authors at submission; reviewers; journals that embed the checklist (Clinical Chemistry does [3]).
2. **Evidence.** MIQE 2.0 Table 1 Section 5 lists: raw data via RDES/RDML; identity of standards; baseline and Cq method; program and version; controls; negative-control frequency and Cq; examples of results; efficiency estimation method; target-quantity method ("efficiency-corrected ... necessary"); replicates; repeatability; reproducibility; LOD; dynamic range; reference-gene validation; normalisation; statistical methods; significance level and power [3]. Audits show these are the least reported items [2]. Bustin & Wittwer call for MIQE modules "within electronic laboratory notebooks or data repositories" [2].
3. **What exists.** Clarida's MIQE score and methods text (Cq-input, closed); self-reported checklists.
4. **The concrete feature.** From the Analysis Record, generate the Section-5 rows with values and fact ids: machine-derivable rows = raw-data format and hash, baseline/Cq method and version (rdmlpython + qPilot module versions), efficiency method and per-assay E ± SE, target-quantity method, replicate description and counts per level (D5), repeatability (within-plate CV of log quantities), reproducibility (plate variance component from D2), LOD (if derivable from controls/dilutions), dynamic range (if standards present), reference-gene validation (D7), normalisation formula, statistical model and test, significance level, post-hoc power or MDE from `plan`. Rows that can never be auto-filled and must be marked "author to supply": identity and quantification of standards, positive/negative control identities, specificity evidence, examples of positive/negative results, sample/RT/reagent lot details. Output: a methods paragraph bound to fact ids (extends the stretch item "templated methods paragraph") and the editable checklist table.
5. **Feasibility.** 1 week once D1–D5 exist; the hard part is honesty about "not derivable" rows.
6. **Why qPilot.** The record already holds versions, hashes and decisions; "no naked numbers" guarantees every value in the paragraph has a fact id.
7. **Risks.** Overlaps the roadmap headline "auto-filled MIQE 2.0 checklist" and another researcher's scope; the specific value here is the derivable/non-derivable split for Section 5. Confidence: medium.
