# Brief for researchers: qPilot niche discovery (read this fully before you search anything)

Date: Saturday 17 October 2026. You are one of ~17 research agents working in parallel for a two-developer team building **qPilot**, an open-source qPCR analysis tool. The team lead's instruction, in their own words:

> "Look across all areas of molecular biology and qPCR and see what we can integrate to make it an excellent product that will make the people who use this tool actually efficient. The use case must be very strong. All I care about is making the lives of the people in this area better. Learn about this niche, form an understanding of how it works, then formulate what would make it much better. Don't come up with basic stuff. It has to have good use cases, something that will really help the scientists in this niche. This has to be like a discovery. Go through every corner of the internet."

Your job is NOT to summarise qPCR for a general audience. It is to (1) build a precise working understanding of one corner of this niche (the people, their week, their vocabulary, standards, tools, money, time, failure modes), and (2) surface specific, evidence-backed opportunities that qPilot could build that would materially change a working scientist's day. Think like a product researcher embedded in a lab for a month, with the internet as your lab. The product horizon is the next 6–18 months, not just this weekend (the team is at a hackathon this weekend; the hackathon scope is already frozen and is NOT your concern).

## What qPilot is today (so you do not re-propose it)

One line: qPilot takes every plate of a qPCR study, analyses them all with the same open published method (LinRegPCR per-well baseline and efficiency, via the MIT-licensed rdmlpython), shows the scientist only the judgment calls that could change a conclusion (impact-ranked "Decision Cards", each with evidence, options, per-gene consequences, a recommendation, and a logged reason), and reports how many of the reasonable ways of analysing the same plates agree with the answer (a "robustness engine" that reruns the study under every reasonable combination of choices). Everything is written into an Analysis Record (RDML with results + a hash-chained decision log + versions + checksums; byte-for-byte re-runnable, chain-verifiable) and callable by an AI agent through an MCP server. Open source (Apache-2.0), local-first, runs on a laptop, research use only.

**Already built or being built (do not propose):** whole-study raw-curve analysis from RDML; reference-gene stability (geNorm M + V) with a reverse-normalisation guard (does any reference-gene pair's ratio shift between groups) and results under every normaliser set; robustness grid (reference subsets × group cut-offs × empty-well rules); impact-ranked Decision Cards; autonomy levels (Teach me / Suggest / Decide-and-tell-me) with an escalation rule (conclusion-flipping choices are always human); "no naked numbers" (every number in AI prose is bound to an engine fact id); explain-any-number; Analysis Record with hash-chained decision log; on-disk shared workspace with locks so a human and an agent can work on the same study; MCP server (qpcr_study, qpcr_robustness, qpcr_decide, qpcr_plan); any model (Anthropic or local OpenAI-compatible) used only for explanations and "ask your data"; a model harness that generates ~50 questions with engine-known answers and scores any model (correct / invented numbers / safety / latency); public validation against the RDML consortium benchmark; one-page Gradio UI; GRADE-style per-gene Study Confidence level with named reasons; `qpilot plan` (power / minimum detectable effect before the study); security hardening (hardened XML, path policy, offline test, SBOM, threat model); visible autonomy policy; closed-loop contract (actor = human/agent/system; record root hash); cost and drop-in honesty sections; classroom worksheet with hidden answer key; Excel/CSV export.

**Stretch items already named:** agent-requests inbox (agent decisions wait for human approval); Hugging Face Space demo; templated methods paragraph and RO-Crate in the record; Bio-Rad RDML preset; agent-task harness; REPLICATE.json; `records summarise` and `run --batch` with lab profile; Prism .pzfx export; MCP manifest hash; parallel plates; `records compare`.

**Roadmap headlines already named** (you may go here ONLY if you get far deeper and more concrete than the headline, with evidence, data, exact methods and formats): vendor capture (QuantStudio TXT importer, Thermo CLI export, CFX/.pcrd and .eds readers, GUI-export automation "GhostDriver"); kinetic replicate outliers (Bar 2003); censored non-detects; mixed model with plate effects; melt-peak checks; NormFinder reimplementation; auto-filled MIQE 2.0 checklist; methods text from the decision log; reviewer-critic agent; plate layouts pushed into vendor software; prior evidence on cards (HRT Atlas, BioMCP); multi-user roles/sign-off/LIMS push; fine-tunable local curve-quality classifier (Brintz 2025 CC0 165k-curve set); plate design assistant (randomised blocks, inter-plate calibrators); lab memory (assay passports per primer pair, drift alerts by reagent lot, lab rule packs); plugin ecosystem with method cards; opt-in open commons of records and a cross-lab reference-gene atlas; beyond qPCR (image quantification, ELISA, western blot).

**Competitors already known:** Clarida (clarida.bio; geNorm/MIQE authors; Cq input; MIQE score; methods text; paid €4,800–9,600/yr lab tier) is the incumbent; AnnealIQ (chat over Ct values, closed beta); qbase+; GenEx; vendor desktop software (QuantStudio Design & Analysis, CFX Maestro, LightCycler); LinRegPCR web / RDML-Tools; many Ct-input academic tools (Auto-qPCR, SATqPCR, qPCRtools, qLOOK, PlateVision, ProntoPCR, lazyQ, qDATA, MCTOT); clinical tools (UgenTec FastFinder, PCR.AI) are out of scope as competitors but their practices are of interest.

## Fixed constraints of the product (do not fight these; design within them)
- Open source, Apache-2.0. Dependencies must be permissive (MIT/BSD/Apache). No GPL, AGPL or EUPL code in the distribution (note: Primer3 and primer3-py are GPL; NormFinder's code forbids redistribution; qslib is EUPL). If a key method lives only in a GPL tool, say so and find or propose a permissive route (reimplement from the paper, call as a separate process, or an alternative).
- Local-first: runs on a laptop, works offline, nothing leaves the machine by default. Cloud is optional, never required.
- Deterministic engine: no LLM in the maths. Models only explain, answer questions over engine facts, or draft text that is checked against the engine. Any ML model that affects numbers must be a validated, versioned method module with a public acceptance test.
- The scientist decides where it matters; the tool never silently changes a conclusion. Decisions are impact-ranked to avoid approval fatigue.
- Research use only: not clinical, not IVD, not 21 CFR Part 11. But practices from regulated QC are welcome if adapted for research.
- Everything is recorded, re-runnable and callable by an AI agent (MCP). The core (decision space, cards, facts, record, store) is domain-agnostic; qPCR is the first plugin, so features that generalise to other plate-based assays are valuable.

## What counts as a discovery (the bar)
A discovery is a specific thing a working scientist, core-facility manager, PI, student or lab-automation engineer would recognise as "yes, THAT is where I lose hours, money or confidence, and nothing does that for me today." Each discovery must have all seven parts:
1. **Persona and situation:** who, in which kind of lab, doing what, how often.
2. **Evidence of the pain:** forum threads, papers, surveys, GitHub issues, vendor docs, talks, job adverts, grant text. Quote briefly and cite. Estimate frequency and cost (time, money, wrong conclusions) where you can; label estimates as estimates.
3. **What exists today** for it (commercial, open source, vendor, Excel habit) and precisely why it is insufficient (closed, single-plate, manual, unmaintained, wrong method, expensive, no API, nobody trusts it).
4. **The concrete feature or integration.** Not "AI-powered X". Say what data goes in (file, format, fields), what the deterministic method is (with the paper), what decision the scientist makes, what goes into the record, what an agent can call.
5. **Feasibility:** public datasets with licences, libraries with licences, standards and schemas, formats, published methods with DOIs, rough effort (days or weeks), the hardest part.
6. **Why qPilot specifically:** what in its architecture makes this natural (raw curves + robustness + decisions + record + MCP + domain-agnostic core).
7. **Risks** and what would make it a bad idea; your confidence (high / medium / low) and why.

Not a discovery on its own: "add a MIQE checklist", "add a chatbot", "support more file formats", "better plots", "add NormFinder", generic "integrate with LIMS", "use machine learning". If you touch one of those, you must reach a level of specificity and evidence that makes it new (for example the exact LIMS objects and API calls, or the exact MIQE 2.0 items that are machine-checkable from an RDML file and which can never be).

Think about efficiency in the scientist's whole week, not only the analysis minute: the run that has to be repeated, the primer pair that never worked, the plate that was pipetted against the wrong layout, the reference gene chosen by habit, the figure redrawn three times for the reviewer, the thesis chapter whose data cannot be found, the core facility that cannot tell a client why their run failed, the agent that cannot tell whether a result is trustworthy.

## Research method expectations
- Go deep and wide: at least 30 distinct sources, across at least 5 source types: peer-reviewed papers (PubMed/PMC, bioRxiv, journals), standards bodies (MIQE 2.0, dMIQE, RDML, ISO 20395, Allotrope, SiLA), vendor documentation and user guides, community forums (ResearchGate Q&A, Reddit r/labrats r/Biochemistry r/bioinformatics r/molecularbiology, Biostars, protocols.io, Bio-Rad and Thermo Fisher community pages, Biology Stack Exchange, X/Twitter, LinkedIn, Bluesky), GitHub / Bioconductor / PyPI / CRAN (issues and stars are pain and adoption signals), conference programmes (qPCR & dPCR Congress, GQ / gene-quantification.de, ABRF, SLAS, AMP, ESHG, ISBER), product pages and price lists, YouTube tutorials, course materials, job adverts, patents.
- Prefer primary and recent (2022–2026) material; include older foundational work where it defines a method.
- Keep digging: when a search returns the obvious, reformulate with practitioner vocabulary (the words a bench scientist types into Google at 7 pm when a run failed). Search for complaints ("doesn't work", "annoying", "hours", "wish", "how do you", "is there a tool"), not only for products.
- Record disagreements and uncertainty honestly. Distinguish "widely reported" from "one forum post". Never invent a number or a citation; if you cannot verify, say so.
- You have a long time budget and nobody is waiting on you minute by minute. Do not stop early. Aim for 25–45 minutes of real searching and reading. Use WebSearch (use mode "extended" for niche or recent facts) and WebFetch; use the built-in browser only if a page needs rendering.
