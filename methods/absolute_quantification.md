# Method card: absolute quantification (standard curves, LOD, LOQ, unknowns' copies)

**What it answers.** How much template was in each reaction, in the unit the standards were declared in (copies when the file or a person says so), with a 95 % prediction interval, read from a dilution series of known quantity measured with the samples. Relative quantification (the rest of the engine) compares groups; this says "about 2,600 copies per reaction (2,500 to 2,700)" and whether that number can be trusted: inside the standards, above the limit of quantification, above the limit of detection. It never changes a fold change, a call or a Decision Card.

**References.** Forootan A, Sjöback R, Björkman J, Sjögreen B, Linz L, Kubista M. Methods to determine limit of detection and limit of quantification in quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR). *Biomol Detect Quantif* 2017;12:1–6 (LOD as the lowest concentration detected with 95 % probability, by the empirical rate or a logistic fit; LOQ from the CV of the measured concentrations, computed on the log scale because qPCR quantities are log-normal; 35 % as the example threshold). Bustin SA et al. The MIQE guidelines. *Clin Chem* 2009;55:611–622 (standard curve with slope, intercept, efficiency and R²; LOD; linear dynamic range; efficiency 90–110 %, R² > 0.98 as common acceptance) and MIQE 2.0, *Clin Chem* 2025;71:634–651. Miller JN, Miller JC. *Statistics and Chemometrics for Analytical Chemistry*, section 5.6 (inverse prediction from a calibration line; the test reproduces its Example 5.6.1). Thermo Fisher Scientific, *DNA and RNA molecular weights and conversions* (molar masses for ng → copies).

**Which wells.** The engine's per-well table with the confirmed design applied (well roles, the samples' types, exclusions; targets left out are gone). A well a person excluded is left out of everything. A well is **detected** when it has the engine's Cq and LinRegPCR called it amplified (or gave it a quantity), under the study's primary amplification rule (a Cq cut-off applies) and a person's well calls. qPilot never drops a standard level or a well itself: a level that is outside the linear range is left out by a person (`curve.exclude_well`, with a reason), and the curve is fitted again.

**The standards' quantities.** In this order: the quantity a person gave on the Design page (`design.dilution_series`: a top quantity and a dilution step, written per standard sample), the file's declared quantity (RDML `<quantity>`, with its unit: `cop` is copies), a sample name `STD_<number>`. Names that only number the levels (`Std 1` … `Std 5`, as QuantStudio Design & Analysis exports them) are never read as quantities: the assay is listed as "standards without a quantity" until a person gives them. Every curve says where its quantities came from (`design`, `file`, `name`).

**The curve** (per assay and plate; `quantify`).

| Quantity | Rule |
|---|---|
| Fit | ordinary least squares of Cq on log10 quantity over the detected standard wells (every replicate a point), at least 3 distinct levels (as G-STD and the efficiency axis) |
| Slope, intercept, R², residual SD | from the fit; equal to `scipy.stats.linregress` |
| Efficiency | E = 10^(−1/slope) − 1 (1.00 = 100 %); 95 % interval from the slope's t interval, t(0.975, n − 2) |
| No curve | the Cq does not fall with the quantity; or the slope is flatter than −1 cycle per ten-fold (an efficiency above 900 %: the assay does not measure the standards' template, e.g. an internal positive control spiked at one level) |
| Warnings (shown, not acted on) | efficiency outside 90–110 %; R² below 0.98 (MIQE; the same thresholds as the Run Gate's G-STD) |
| Residuals, read-back | each standard's Cq minus the line; each standard's quantity read back from its own Cq |
| Prediction band (chart) | Cq of one new well at x: ŷ ± t·s·√(1 + 1/n + (x − x̄)²/Sxx) |
| Pooled | when one assay's standards are on several plates with the same series (the same set of quantities), a curve over all of them is fitted too and named "pooled"; it is only read by plates without standards for that assay |

**LOD** (limit of detection). The lowest standard level detected in at least 95 % of its wells, every higher level too (a level below a failing level does not count). When every level passed, the LOD is "at or below the lowest standard" and said so (a dilution series that never fails cannot locate it). With 3 wells per level, 95 % means every well. When three or more levels are measured and at least one is partly detected, a logistic regression of detection (yes/no per well) on log10 quantity, fitted by maximum likelihood (Newton–Raphson), gives the **modelled** LOD: the quantity detected with 95 % probability (Forootan et al. 2017). It is shown beside the empirical LOD; it is not fitted when detection jumps from none to all between two levels (the likelihood has no maximum), and it says when it lies below the lowest standard (extrapolated). Flags use the empirical LOD.

**LOQ** (limit of quantification). The lowest level, at or above the LOD, whose read-back quantities have a coefficient of variation of at most 35 %, every higher level too; a level needs two detected wells for a CV. The CV is log-normal: CV = √(exp(SD_ln²) − 1), SD_ln the SD of the natural log of the read-back quantities (the same as SD(Cq) × ln(1 + E)), as Forootan et al. recommend; the arithmetic CV of back-transformed values is biased for log-normal data. 35 % is a lab-editable default (`AbsoluteSettings.loq_cv`), not a law.

**Unknowns.** One estimate per biological unit and assay (the samples of a unit and their technical replicates are averaged, as the engine averages log2 N0; never counted as n). From the mean Cq ȳ0 of its k detected wells on one curve: x0 = (ȳ0 − b)/m and s_x0 = (s/|m|)·√(1/k + 1/n + (ȳ0 − ȳ)²/(m²·Sxx)); the 95 % prediction interval is x0 ± t(0.975, n − 2)·s_x0 on log10 quantity, given back as quantities (an asymmetric interval). A unit whose wells were read from several curves gets the mean of the per-curve log10 estimates and √(Σ s_i²)/N, with the smallest degrees of freedom. A unit on a plate without its own curve reads the pooled curve (else the assay's only curve) and is flagged. Flags, in plain words: *above the top standard* / *below the lowest standard* (extrapolated), *below the limit of quantification*, *below the limit of detection*, *not detected in any well* (no number), *some wells were not detected* (the number is from the detected ones), *read from another plate's curve*, *wells on several plates*.

**Per µL and per ng.** When a person records what one reaction held (`choice.reaction_input`: µL of template, ng of input; absent by default, so no design hash changes), each quantity is also given per µL of template (quantity ÷ µL) and per ng of input (quantity ÷ ng). It is recorded with a reason in the session's decision record, after the blind stages (it never re-opens them); the study is analysed again with it, every number unchanged.

**ng → copies** (preparing standards). copies = ng × 10⁻⁹ × 6.02214076 × 10²³ ÷ molar mass, molar mass (g/mol): dsDNA bp × 617.96 + 36.04; ssDNA nt × 308.97 + 18.02; ssRNA nt × 320.5 + 159.0. 1 ng of a 3,000 bp plasmid is 3.25 × 10⁸ copies. The Design page's standards editor shows it beside the top quantity; `qpilot copies --ng 1 --length 3000 --step 10 --levels 6` prints the series.

**A study that only quantifies (`design.purpose` quantify; `qpilot/qpcr/quantify_study.py`).** A run that compares no groups (a viral load, wastewater surveillance, a construct's copy number, a library) says so on the Design page: its purpose is *quantify samples*, a person's design item (the rules propose it only when the file declares the standards' quantities and no sample sheet names groups; `D-PURPOSE` never decides it for a person, so it is never in "Accept all consistent"). It needs no comparison and no reference gene (`D-NGUARD` does not apply), and it needs standards with a quantity (at least three levels) instead. Its result is this module's: every unit's class and quantity. **What is reported for a unit** is its class, in this order: *not detected*, *no curve*, *below the LOD*, *below the LOQ*, *below the lowest standard* and *above the top standard* (extrapolated), *quantified*; for a class that reports a number, also the number. **Choices tested** (the robustness idea, for quantities): which wells count as detected (LinRegPCR's own call, Cq ≤ 40, 38, 35: the amplification rule and the Cq cut-off) and the LOQ line (CV ≤ 35 %, the default, or 25 %); every combination is quantified (`grid.csv`). Each choice is a Decision Card (`quantity.amp_call`, `quantity.loq_cv`) that waits for a person only when another option, at the planned other choice, changes what is reported for at least one unit: its class, or its number by more than 2-fold (a lab-editable default, the relative engine's 2-fold line); otherwise it is decided automatically, and an agent still cannot change it when some other setting would (`flips_anywhere`). The option a person chooses is the analysis the Quantities view, the report and the exports use. Run Gate runs every rule; its consequence test asks whether leaving the flagged wells out changes what is reported for a unit (standards count: a standard moves the curve), and G-STD reads the person's quantities. The relative outputs (contrasts, robustness, grades, geNorm) are empty; the Evidence Ledger, the audit, Trace, Claims, Prism and the planner, which compare groups, say so instead.

**"Detected only in" (relative quantification).** Not this module, but the same question: a gene measured in both groups and detected in none of one group's units has no fold change (a ratio to nothing). The engine withholds it with the words "detected only in ⟨group⟩ (k of n biological units); not detected in any of the m units of ⟨other⟩", gives the detected group's mean ΔCq (Cq of the gene minus the mean Cq of the control genes, per unit), and no cell of the choice grid compares a group whose values were all filled in by the cap (`qpilot/qpcr/space.py`, `tests/engine/test_detected_only.py`).

**Not claimed.** No weighted regression, no outlier rejection, no inter-run calibration beyond pooling one series, no digital-PCR-style Poisson correction, no correction for the efficiency of the reverse transcription (copies of cDNA, not of RNA), and the LOD and LOQ are those of the standards' matrix, which may differ from the samples'.
