# 04 — Pre-analytics and QC gates for RT-qPCR: sample quality, reverse transcription, inhibition, gDNA, controls

Research notes for the qPilot team. Date: 17 October 2026. Scope: which pre-analytical factors actually move Cq values (with effect sizes), how labs measure them (instruments, exports, formats, parsers, licences), how rarely they reach the analysis, what MIQE 2.0 asks for and which items are machine-checkable, and concrete product discoveries.

Method note: ~40 sources were read, mostly primary (papers, standards, schema files, parser source code, vendor product pages, instrument test-data files). Several pages could not be opened by the tooling (Reddit blocked at tool level; ResearchGate, Agilent technical overviews and Benchling connector guides returned 403; Sigma protocol pages timed out; PubMed cookie wall), so where a fact comes only from a search-engine summary of such a page it is labelled "(search summary, page not opened)". Full texts of the key PDFs (MIQE 2.0, MIQE 2009, Bustin & Wittwer 2026, Bustin 2014, Nolan 2006, Fleige 2006, Ho-Pun-Cheung 2009, the 2025 NTC preprint) were downloaded and text-extracted locally. The shared WebSearch budget was exhausted mid-way, so the second half of the work used direct fetches of known URLs plus local extraction. Citations are [n] into the numbered source list in section 7.

Terminology for developers (used throughout): Cq = quantification cycle (older names Ct, Cp). RIN = RNA Integrity Number (Agilent Bioanalyzer, 1–10); RINe = RIN equivalent (Agilent TapeStation); RQN = RNA Quality Number (Agilent Fragment Analyzer/ProSize); RQI = RNA Quality Indicator (Bio-Rad Experion). DV200 = percentage of RNA fragments longer than 200 nucleotides (used for FFPE = formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue). 28S/18S = ratio of the two ribosomal RNA peaks. A260/A280 and A260/A230 = UV absorbance purity ratios (NanoDrop-type instruments). RT = reverse transcription (RNA → cDNA). No-RT (also −RT, RT(−), NRT) = control in which the reverse transcriptase is omitted, to reveal genomic DNA (gDNA). NTC = no-template control. NAC = no-amplification control. SPUD = a universal inhibition-detection qPCR assay (potato sequence). IAC/IPC = internal amplification/positive control. KOD = kinetic outlier detection (efficiency-based). SOD = shape-based outlier detection. ValidPrime = a gDNA-correction method/assay (TATAA). RDML = Real-time PCR Data Markup Language (XML standard qPilot already reads).

## 1. How this corner of the niche works

### 1.1 Roles and where the pre-analytical facts live

- **Bench scientist (PhD student / postdoc / technician)** collects tissue or cells, stabilises them, extracts RNA (column kit or TRIzol-type), measures concentration (NanoDrop and/or Qubit), sometimes sends samples to a core for integrity (TapeStation / Bioanalyzer / Fragment Analyzer), treats with DNase (or not), reverse-transcribes a fixed mass of RNA into cDNA, dilutes the cDNA, and runs plates. The person who does the extraction is usually the same person who later analyses the plate, but the QC numbers live in a PDF from the core, a NanoDrop CSV on a shared drive, or a lab notebook, and are rarely joined to the Cq table.
- **Core facility manager / genomics core staff** run the electrophoresis instruments for many labs, return a PDF report per run (and can export XML/CSV), and are asked "why did my run fail?" without seeing the client's qPCR data.
- **PI / lab head** signs off conclusions and is the person who answers reviewers' MIQE questions ("what was the RIN?", "did you run no-RT controls?").
- **Lab automation / LIMS engineer (enterprise, pharma, large cores)** builds connectors so that NanoDrop, Qubit, TapeStation and Lunatic files flow into an ELN/LIMS; Benchling publishes configuration guides for exactly these instruments and open-sourced the parsers (allotropy, MIT) [43][44][49].

### 1.2 The pre-analytical workflow, step by step, and what each step does to Cq

1. **Sampling and stabilisation.** RNA degrades in vivo and ex vivo; MIQE 2.0 asks for "sampling procedure (including time to storage)" and storage temperature/duration/aliquots [1]. Left at room temperature, PBMC RNA fell from RIN 9.3 (0 h) to 7.9 (12 h), 6.4 (24 h) and 3.78 (84 h) [31].
2. **Extraction.** Phenol, detergents, bile salts, heparin, haemoglobin, humic acids, polysaccharides and IgG can co-purify and inhibit RT and/or PCR; different extraction methods "can selectively enrich or deplete nucleic acid fragments of specific sizes" [1][11][13]. In Nolan et al.'s test, trace phenol from one operator's extraction shifted the SPUD Cq and lowered efficiency while NanoDrop and A260/A280 showed nothing wrong [11].
3. **Quantification.** UV absorbance (NanoDrop, Lunatic) versus dye fluorometry (Qubit). NanoDrop reads higher than Qubit; for partially degraded FFPE DNA only Qubit was reproducible and consistent with qPCR, and the authors recommend NanoDrop followed by Qubit [33]; but Qubit readings depend on salt (>1 mM NaCl needed) and were not always the most accurate measure of PCR-available template [34]. MIQE 2.0: fluorometry "lacks information on contamination and integrity"; combine dye staining with electrophoresis; 5 ng RNA ≈ 170–500 transcriptome equivalents is generally enough for most mammalian transcripts [1].
4. **Integrity.** Microfluidic electrophoresis gives RIN/RINe/RQN/DV200/28S-18S. Effect sizes on Cq are in section 2 (KQ1): roughly 0.1–1.6 cycles of Cq per RIN unit depending on gene, tissue and amplicon length [24]; expression-ratio error up to ~100% at RIN ≈ 5 relative to RIN 10 [25]. The 28S/18S ratio has CV 32 ± 18% and did not correlate with qPCR performance [24].
5. **Purity / inhibition.** A260/A230 flags organics; it does not detect inhibition. Only 6% of researchers tested their samples for inhibitors in Bustin's 2005 survey [11][37]; SPUD or dilution series are the recommended checks [4][11]; inhibition is assay-specific, so a clean SPUD does not guarantee a clean target assay [12].
6. **DNase treatment and gDNA.** Residual gDNA inflates signals for assays that amplify genomic sequence (intronless genes, pseudogenes such as GAPDH and ACTB). Checked with no-RT controls (one per sample at first extraction; thereafter desirable, not essential, per MIQE 2009/2.0) or with a gDNA-specific assay plus gDNA standard (ValidPrime) [1][4][22][55].
7. **Reverse transcription.** The single largest technical variance source: 2–3-fold apparent expression differences between RT replicates of the same RNA are typical, up to ~7-fold in Bustin's data, and dependent on enzyme, priming, RNA input and RNA quality [5][6]. Carry-over of reverse transcriptase into PCR inhibits it and inflates apparent efficiencies above 100% in dilution series [8].
8. **cDNA dilution and plate run.** Dilution reduces RT-reagent carry-over but increases variability for rare transcripts [8]; a 1:5 lower RNA input raised the RT-replicate Cq range from 0.65–1.45 to 2.48–4.14 cycles [5].
9. **Controls on the plate.** NTC per assay per plate; no-RT; positive control; inter-run calibrator; standards; optional spike (SPUD / Xeno / TATAA Universal RNA Spike / ERCC). MIQE 2.0 rule: a sample is positive only if its Cq precedes the negative control's by ≥5 cycles; in-sample internal controls should be added at 3–10× LOD [1].
10. **Analysis.** Almost none of the above reaches the analysis software. RDML 1.3 has fields for it (sample `type` incl. `nrt`/`ntc`/`nac`/`pos`/`opt`/`std`, `cdnaSynthesisMethod` with enzyme/primingMethod/dnaseTreatment, `templateQuantity`, `quantity`, `interRunCalibrator`, free `annotation` property/value pairs, and per-reaction `excl`/`note`) [45], but vendor exports rarely populate them and analysis tools do not use them.

### 1.4 Standards

- **MIQE 2009** (Clin Chem 55:611) checklist, Sample/Extraction/RT sections, E = essential, D = desirable (verbatim from Table 1) [4]: Sample: description E; volume/mass processed D; micro/macrodissection E; processing procedure E; if frozen, how and how quickly E; if fixed, with what and how quickly E; storage conditions and duration E. Nucleic acid extraction: procedure/instrumentation E; kit and modifications E; details of DNase or RNase treatment E; contamination assessment (DNA or RNA) E; nucleic acid quantification E; instrument and method E; purity (A260/A280) D; yield D; RNA integrity method/instrument E; RIN/RQI or Cq of 3′ and 5′ transcripts E; electrophoresis traces D; inhibition testing (Cq dilutions, spike, or other) E. Reverse transcription: complete reaction conditions E; amount of RNA and reaction volume E; priming oligonucleotide (if GSP) and concentration E; reverse transcriptase and concentration E; temperature and time E; manufacturer and catalogue numbers D; Cqs with and without reverse transcription D (footnote: essential when first extracting RNA; once validated DNA-free, desirable); storage conditions of cDNA D. The text adds: A260/A280 alone "is not sufficient" for quantitative work; provide gel evidence at least, better RIN/RQI or a 3′:5′ assay (threshold ratio "approximately 0.2–5"); check inhibition "by dilution of the sample (preferably) or use of a universal inhibition assay such as SPUD"; because PCRs are differentially susceptible, "routinely use dilutions" [4].
- **MIQE 2.0** (Clin Chem 2025;71:634–651) replaces E/D with a Yes/No + "where in the article / why omitted" table [1]. Section 2 SAMPLE PREPARATION items (verbatim): detailed description of sample types and numbers; sampling procedure (including time to storage); sample aliquoting, storage conditions and duration; description of extraction method including amount of sample processed; source and amount of spike-in nucleic acids added; volume of elution buffer used to elute/resuspend nucleic acids; number of extraction replicates; extraction blanks and percent yield included; method to evaluate quality and quantity of nucleic acids; storage conditions: temperature, concentration, duration, buffer, aliquots; clear description of dilution steps used to prepare working template solution; template modification (digestion, sonication, pre-amplification, DNAse treatment etc.); purification after modification. Section 3 REVERSE TRANSCRIPTION: cDNA priming method and primer concentration; one- or two-step protocol (include reaction details for two-step); amount of RNA used per reaction; detailed reaction components and conditions; estimated copies measured with and without addition of RT (footnote e: no-RT essential when first extracting RNA, desirable afterwards); manufacturer of reagents, catalog number, and lot number; storage of cDNA. Section 5 DATA ANALYSIS includes: storage and submission of raw fluorescence data using RDES or RDML; details of positive and negative controls; frequency and Cq of negative controls; examples of positive and negative results; PCR efficiency estimation and method; method of baseline correction and Cq determination; qPCR analysis program (source, version); description of replicates; repeatability; reproducibility; LOD; dynamic range; method of validation of reference genes; normalization method; statistical methods; significance level and statistical power; specificity [1].
- **RDML 1.3** (MIT-licensed schema) sample-level fields listed in 1.2 above; `primingMethod` enumeration: `oligo-dt`, `random`, `target-specific`, `oligo-dt and random`, `other`; `nucleotide`: DNA, genomic DNA, cDNA, RNA; `quantity/unit`: cop, fold, dil, ng, nMol, other; sample `type`: unkn, ntc, nac, std, ntp, nrt, pos, opt (with optional `targetId`); per-reaction `data` has `excl` (reasons, semicolon-separated), `note`, `corrF`, `corrP`, `corrCq`, `meltTemp`, `endPt`, `bgFluor`, `quantFluor`, raw `adp`/`mdp` points; target has `amplificationEfficiency(SE)`, `detectionLimit`, `sequences` (primers, probes, amplicon) [45].
- **Allotrope Simple Model (ASM)** JSON is what Benchling's allotropy emits for NanoDrop/Qubit/TapeStation/Lunatic and also for QuantStudio, CFX Maestro and QIAcuity files; note that its TapeStation output carries RINe only as a generic "calculated data" item, not a named field [44].

### 1.5 Money and time (estimates are labelled)

- Qubit RNA HS kit: £103 for 100 assays (≈ £1/sample reagent) [54]. TATAA Universal RNA Spike I: €296.89 (250 rxn, SYBR) to €934.10 (1000 rxn, HEX probe), i.e. ≈ €0.8–1.2 per reaction [51]. Thermo Xeno RNA spike: £748 for 5 × 200 µL at 10,000 copies/µL, used at 10 µL per sample (≈ £7.5 per sample) [50]. ValidPrime kits €225.82–657.07 (250/1000 rxn) [52]. ERCC spike-in mix (92 transcripts): £1,251.65 per 10 µL mix (aimed at NGS/arrays) [53]. TapeStation/Bioanalyzer per-sample consumable prices could not be retrieved (Agilent store 403; core price list refused connection); order-of-magnitude "a few € per sample" is my estimate, not a sourced figure.
- Cost of not checking: Bustin & Wittwer's modelling shows uncorrected efficiency can distort fold changes by 20–40% [2]; RNA degradation alone introduces up to ~100% error in expression ratios at RIN ≈ 5 [25]; RT variability alone produces 2–3-fold apparent expression differences [5]. Each is larger than the 1.2–1.5-fold changes commonly reported as meaningful [3].

### 1.6 Where decisions are made, what "good" looks like, what goes wrong

Decisions (today made by habit, rarely recorded): accept/reject a sample by RIN (common cut-offs 5, 7, 8 — see KQ1); whether to DNase-treat (Bio-Rad warns it may lose material when input is limited [63]); RNA input to RT (constant mass across samples is the rule [6]); cDNA dilution factor; whether to include a late-amplifying sample whose Cq is close to the NTC; whether a no-RT signal 6 cycles later "matters"; whether to drop a plate whose NTC amplified.

"Good" looks like: RIN ≥ 8 and amplicons ≤ 200 bp (Fleige's quadrant IV) [24]; RT replicates with ΔCq range < 1 cycle [5]; SPUD/spike ΔCq ≤ 1 [11][23]; no-RT ≥ 5–7 cycles later than +RT (≤ ~3% of signal) [55]; NTC clean or late with a dimer-type melt peak ≥ 3 °C below product [55][57]; equal RNA input, same priming and enzyme for all samples [6].

What goes wrong (evidence in section 3): QC numbers never reach the analysis; groups differ in RIN and the "biology" is degradation (Gallego Romero's caveat [31]); a reference gene is more degradation-sensitive than the target [24][40]; a 205% "efficiency" standard curve is taken as fine because R² = 1.0 [62]; a same-Tm NTC peak is dismissed as primer dimer [62]; SPUD is clean but the target assay is inhibited [12]; RT replicates are never run, so a 3-fold change is reported from one cDNA synthesis [5][35].

## 2. Key questions answered (researcher format: takeaway / cited findings / inferences / gaps)

### KQ1. RNA QC: RIN, RQN, DV200, purity ratios, Qubit vs NanoDrop; instruments, exports, parsers; effect sizes on Cq

#### Takeaway
Degradation moves Cq linearly with RIN at roughly 0.1–1.6 cycles per RIN unit depending on gene, tissue and amplicon length, and moves expression ratios by up to ~2-fold at RIN ≈ 5; normalisation and efficiency correction reduce but do not remove the effect. Instruments export XML/CSV with well-documented fields and two MIT-licensed parsers exist, but the data almost never reach the qPCR analysis.

#### Cited findings
- Fleige et al. 2006: 135 bovine RNA samples (11 tissues/cell types), artificially degraded, RIN 1.1–9.5, measured in triplicate (n = 405). Ct regressed on RIN: mean R² > 0.81, P < 0.001 in almost all datasets; slopes ranged from −0.04 to −1.6 cycles per RIN unit (e.g. lymph node β-actin −0.794, IL-1β −0.674; corpus luteum 18S −1.568; granulosa 18S −0.114). RIN's effect on single-run PCR efficiency was minor (most slopes |< 0.02| per RIN unit); efficiency depended mainly on tissue ("tissue-matrix effect"). 28S/18S ratio had CV 32 ± 18% and no correlation with qPCR performance. Both ΔΔCt and efficiency-corrected models remained RIN-sensitive after normalising to 28S; β-actin at RIN 8 vs RIN 2 differed four-fold after normalisation. Amplicons 66–976 bp: best repeatability with RIN > 5 (better > 8) and amplicon < 200 bp; recommend RIN > 5 and ≤ 200 bp as minimal requirement, ideal amplicon 80–150 bp, never > 400 bp — [Fleige 2006](https://gene-quantification.de/fleige-et-al-rna-integrity-2006.pdf) [24]
- Ho-Pun-Cheung et al. 2009: nine genes in three cell lines, thermal hydrolysis RIN 10 → 4.7. Relative expression ratio vs RIN was linear (R² 0.86–1.00), mean slope 0.086 ± 0.025 ratio units per RIN unit; maximum errors 104% (5 ≤ RIN < 6), 92% (6–7), 75% (7–8), 47% (RIN ≥ 8); minimum ratio 0.48 (RPLP0, RIN 4.7 → 108% error). A consensus correction, ratio = 0.08 × RIN + 0.19, RIN-normalised ratio = measured + measured × (1 − y)/y, cut maximum error to < 25% and mean error to 8.4 ± 6.6%. In 112 rectal biopsies 73.2% had RIN 5–7; correction changed values by 1.52-fold on average (1.13–2.10) and reversed the ranking of some samples. Requires random hexamer priming and amplicons < 200 bp — [Ho-Pun-Cheung 2009](https://gene-quantification.de/ho-pun-cheung-rin-algorithm-2009.pdf) [25]
- Vermeulen et al. 2011: 740 neuroblastoma tumours; parameters 18S/28S (S), RQI (R), HPRT1 5′–3′ ΔCq (D), HPRT1 3′ Cq (C), Alu Cq (A), normalisation factor (N). Reference-gene variation was 3.5 in the 50 most degraded vs 1.5 in the 50 most intact samples; 18 of 24 gene × parameter combinations significant; D and N had the largest impact; the worst-quality samples had 6.9% lower classifier sensitivity; in multivariate regression only D (OR 4.65) and N (OR 17.86) predicted misclassification; "normalization did not fully remove the effect"; no universal cut-off — set study-specific cut-offs in a pilot (they used D < 3.54, the 75th percentile). The 5′/3′ assay: two HPRT1 assays (efficiency 88.6% and 94.9%), anchored oligo-dT priming. SPUD ΔCq > 1 flagged 8/740 (1.1%) — [Vermeulen 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3089491) [23]
- Jahn et al. 2008 (bacterial RNA, Dickeya dadantii): "RIN values below 7.0 resulted in high variation and loss of statistical significance"; with RIN > 7 the reference-gene SD was 0.67 and a relative expression ratio of 2.4 was significant (p = 0.0011), while below RIN 7 the same comparison gave 0.23 (p = 0.35) — [Jahn 2008](https://gene-quantification.de/jahn-bak-rna-extraction-2008.pdf) [26]
- Padhi et al. 2018 (rat): 3′:5′ assay on Pgk1, amplicons 856 bp apart, 3′:5′ ratio = 2^(Cq5′ − Cq3′); ratio 8.0 (ΔCq 3) ≈ RIN 5.0, ratio ≈ 4.0 ≈ RIN 7.0; classification agreed with RIN in 96/99 samples; caution that heat degradation is random whereas enzymatic (post-mortem) degradation is not — [Padhi 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6006387/) [27]
- Die et al. 2011: the 3′:5′ assay's "practical applications are unexploited primarily because of the absence of an experimental threshold"; they propose placing the 5′ amplicon a fixed distance from the 3′ end so a cut-off can be defined (abstract only) — [Die 2011](https://helvia.uco.es/handle/10396/27133) [28]
- Antonov et al. 2005: strand breaks reduce the number of molecules carrying an intact amplicon; Ct shifts in degraded RNA can be offset by ΔCt against the mean of several control genes, enabling FFPE measurements — [Antonov 2005, abstract](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/15951835) [29]
- Cronin et al. 2004 (FFPE breast cancer, 1985–2001 blocks): mean raw Cq 35.3 in the oldest vs 31.0 in the newest blocks; FFPE vs paired frozen RNA offset ≈ 2 Cq ("~75% loss of intact amplicon template"); genes whose normalised values drifted with block age had amplicons of 104 ± 15 b versus 78 ± 11 b for the rest; redesign to 70–85 b removed the bias; amplicons "preferably limited to less than 100 bases" — [Cronin 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1602211/) [30]
- Gallego Romero et al. 2014 (RNA-seq, but the only quantitative covariate test found): RIN fell 9.3 → 7.9 → 6.4 → ~5.1 → 3.78 over 0–84 h at room temperature; 71% of genes differentially expressed at 84 h; faster decay for longer CDS/3′UTR and higher GC; a GLM with RIN as covariate cut 0 h vs 84 h DE genes from 9,998 to 42; regressing RIN out first, to 0; the correction only works "if RIN scores are not associated with the effect of interest" — [Gallego Romero 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4071332/) [31]
- Strand et al. 2007: in 24 frozen breast tumours, RIN agreed with manual trace evaluation in 23/24 versus 21/24 for 28S/18S; RIN preferred because objective; RIN ≥ 6 vs < 6 changed the top differentially expressed genes — [Strand 2007](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/17519006) [32]
- Bustin et al. 2014: samples with RIN 10 had median RT-replicate ΔCq ranges of 2.2 and 2.4 versus 2.8 and 3.4 for RIN 8 and 9.5; a RIN 4.9 preparation gave higher Cqs and wider ranges (median 3.70 vs 2.42) and lost the between-gene correlation (Pearson r 0.208 vs 0.715); "a universal RIN value provides no definitive information about the integrity of individual mRNA species" — [Bustin 2014](https://gene-quantification.com/bustin-et-al-rt-variability-clin-chem-2014.pdf) [5]
- Simbolo et al. 2013: NanoDrop gave higher concentrations than Qubit; for FFPE DNA "only Qubit was reproducible and consistent with qPCR"; recommend NanoDrop (contamination) followed by Qubit (quantity) — [Simbolo 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3675123/) [33]; Nakayama et al. 2016: Qubit values did not scale with dilution in low-salt water (>1 mM NaCl needed) and "Qubit is not always the most accurate method for quantifying PCR-available DNA" — [Nakayama 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4777359/) [34]
- MIQE 2.0: fluorometry "lacks information on contamination and integrity"; combine dye staining with electrophoresis; RNA is stable at −80 °C for months and ≥ 5 years in LN2 vapour; avoid freeze–thaw; dilute samples < 1 ng/µL can adsorb to tube walls — [MIQE 2.0](https://www.multid.se/publications/MIQE-ver2.pdf) [1]
- DV200 bands used for FFPE sequencing input: > 70% high, 50–70% medium, 30–50% low, < 30% too degraded; an Illumina poster used ΔCq ≤ 5 plus DV200 ≥ 20 for > 95% QC success (search summary; Agilent and Illumina pages not opened) [48][65]
- Formats/parsers: TapeStation XML carries `RINe`, `Ratio28S18SHeight`, `Ratio28S18SArea`, `Concentration`, peaks and regions per `Sample`, plus `RINeVersion`, instrument serial, software version and `ScreenTapeID` — [allotropy test XML](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Benchling-Open-Source/allotropy/main/tests/parsers/agilent_tapestation_analysis/testdata/agilent_tapestation_analysis_example_01.xml) [44]; Bioanalyzer XML carries `DARRIN/Channel/RIN` and base64 float32 `ProcessedSignal` — [bioanalyzeR source](https://rdrr.io/github/jwfoley/bioanalyzeR/src/R/bioanalyzer.R) [43]; ProSize "Quality Table.csv" carries `RQN`, `28S/18S`, `% RNA Contamination` — [bioanalyzeR prosize.R](https://rdrr.io/github/jwfoley/bioanalyzeR/src/R/prosize.R) [43]; NanoDrop One, Qubit Flex and Lunatic CSV headers are reproduced in section 1.3 — [allotropy test data](https://github.com/Benchling-Open-Source/allotropy) [44]. bioanalyzeR is MIT (52 stars, 459 commits) and computes DV200 from the electropherogram [43]; allotropy is MIT (93 stars) and has no Bioanalyzer or ProSize parser [44].

#### Inferences
- A per-gene "expected Cq shift from degradation" is estimable but wide: ~0.1–1.6 cycles per RIN unit; the study's own reference-gene Cq vs RIN slope is a better estimator than literature values (Fleige's and Vermeulen's analyses are exactly that regression).
- RIN thresholds in use (5 minimal, 7 practical, 8 optimal) are compatible across sources; a product should expose the threshold as a decision, not hard-code it.
- Because all the export formats carry a stable per-sample identity only as free text, the join to RDML samples is the engineering risk, not the parsing.

#### Gaps
- No paper found that quantifies RINe (TapeStation) vs RIN (Bioanalyzer) agreement (Agilent technical overview 5990-9613EN returned 403).
- No sourced per-sample price for TapeStation/Bioanalyzer runs.
- Perez-Novo 2005 (RNA quality vs reference-gene stability) abstract not retrievable; cited only via Bustin 2014 and Fleige 2006.
- LUNA cell-counter export format not found.

### KQ2. The RT step as the biggest variance source: kits, priming, efficiency, dilution, spikes, practice

#### Takeaway
RT reproducibility is enzyme-, sample-, concentration- and assay-dependent; replicate RT reactions of the same RNA differ by 0.4–1.7 cycles (up to 3 cycles for a poor enzyme, 2.5–4 cycles at 1:5 lower input), producing 2–3-fold apparent expression differences; the remedy (RT replicates, constant input, same priming/enzyme, exogenous RNA spike) is cheap, standardised and almost never done or reported.

#### Cited findings
- Bustin et al. 2014: ten RT replicates with six commercial RTases gave GAPDH ΔCq ranges (max − min) of 0.4–1.74 cycles versus 0.07–0.16 for qPCR/pipetting replicates; across five mRNAs the least variable enzyme (Readyscript) spanned 0.34–1.74 and the most variable (Vilo) 0.86–3.05; with 50 ng/µL RNA the RT-dependent range was 0.65–1.45 and with a 1:5 dilution 2.48–4.14; heterogeneity of variance among samples P = 0.005; relative fold changes between RT replicates: 31% < 2-fold, 44% 2–3-fold, 25% > 3-fold (max ≈ 7-fold); recommended enzyme-selection workflow: pick an RTase with ΔCq < 1 across 4 targets, r² > 0.9 between co-amplified targets, and dilution ΔCq ≈ 3.2 per 1:10; "variability can be up to 100-fold" citing Ståhlberg et al. 2004b; RT in duplicate or triplicate "remains unheeded" — [Bustin 2014](https://gene-quantification.com/bustin-et-al-rt-variability-clin-chem-2014.pdf) [5]
- Ståhlberg et al. 2004: five genes, random hexamer vs oligo(dT) vs gene-specific priming; "most experimental variation came from the reverse transcription step"; each gene responded differently to priming; yield depended on total RNA concentration; comparable results require the same priming, conditions and RNA input, and at least duplicate samples from the RT step — [Ståhlberg 2004, abstract](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/14726469) [6]
- Curry et al. 2002 (via gene-quantification.de): MMLV RT conversion efficiency ≈ 20%, falling to 6% for rare templates — [gene-quantification.de RT page](https://www.gene-quantification.de/reverse-transcription1.html) [42]
- Schwaber et al. 2019: RT efficiency measured with synthetic standards and dPCR varied by transcript (combined average 120% for EPOR, 55% for GCSFR); replicate CV < 12% at 10 fg and 1 fg input but 12–35% at 100 ag; recommend per-target synthetic standards at ≥ 3 concentrations; spike-ins such as ERCC give a global efficiency but cannot estimate gene-specific efficiency — [Schwaber 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6374950/) [7]
- Suslov & Steindler 2005: unpurified SuperScript III reactions inhibited PCR (mean inhibitory percentage 75% at the most concentrated point), giving apparent efficiencies of 2.03–2.29 (> 100%) versus 1.82–1.85 after purification; six of 14 primer pairs gave ΔCt < 0.6 per 2-fold dilution and six gave negative ΔCt; template was ≈ 5% v/v of RT reaction in the PCR; dilution reduces inhibition but adds Cq variability for rare transcripts — [Suslov 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1298932/) [8]
- Tichopad et al. 2009: nested design 3 animals × 3 samples × 3 RT × 3 qPCR; in cell culture and blood "RT and qPCR noise contributed substantially"; recommend sample replicates for tissue/culture/single cells and RT replicates for blood; budget-optimal allocation; Kitchen et al. 2010 generalise this with the powerNest tool — [Tichopad 2009](https://www.labgenexp.eu/publications/design-and-optimization-of-reverse-transcription-quantitative-pcr) [9], [Kitchen 2010](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/20109551) [10]
- MIQE 2.0: RT yield and specificity "have a major and generally disregarded effect"; "RT may not be a linear process"; "RT yield also varies among targets"; keep RNA input, priming, enzyme and conditions constant; in research labs cDNA is commonly diluted after RT "to mitigate interference from RT reagents" and "dilution can influence quantitative results" — [MIQE 2.0](https://www.multid.se/publications/MIQE-ver2.pdf) [1]
- Ho-Pun-Cheung 2009 used 0.3 pg of A. thaliana CAB mRNA spiked into every RT; CAB was recovered within a 1.5-fold range across samples, taken as evidence of no RT/PCR inhibition — [Ho-Pun-Cheung 2009](https://gene-quantification.de/ho-pun-cheung-rin-algorithm-2009.pdf) [25]
- Products: TATAA Universal RNA Spike I — synthetic ~1000-base capped, poly-A RNA with no homology to known organisms, assay on a 300-base 3′ region (SYBR/FAM/HEX), 250 or 1000 reactions, €296.89–934.10; interpretation: higher Cq than the water control = inhibition, equal Cq = 100% extraction yield — [TATAA](https://webshop.tataa.com/products/tataa-universal-rna-spike-i) [51]. Thermo TaqMan Universal RNA Spike In/RT (Xeno) control — 10,000 copies/µL, 10 µL added at isolation, assay Ac00010014_a1/Pa00010014_a1, £748 per 5 × 200 µL, RUO — [Thermo A39179](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/A39179) [50]. ERCC Mix 1 — 92 polyadenylated transcripts 250–2,000 nt from NIST-traceable plasmids, £1,251.65, positioned for NGS/arrays — [Thermo 4456740](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/4456740) [53]
- Practice: Bustin & Nolan's 2017 audit of 20 Nature-publisher papers found "No information" for RT replicates and RT conditions in essentially every row — [Bustin & Nolan 2017](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/28796277) [35]; the 2026 audit did not even score RT replicates, only RNA integrity, sequences, reference-gene validation and efficiency [2].

#### Inferences
- RT batch is a plausible hidden covariate of the same magnitude as the biology (2–3-fold); when all treated samples are reverse-transcribed on one day and controls on another, the "effect" can be the RT batch. No analysis tool tests this.
- The RT-replicate ΔCq < 1 criterion [5] and the 3.2-cycle dilution-linearity criterion are directly checkable from Cq tables when replicates are annotated.

#### Gaps
- No published variance-component numbers (percent of variance from RT vs sampling vs qPCR) could be read; Tichopad 2009's full text (cas.cz mirror) refused connection.
- No survey of how many labs run RT duplicates; only the audits above (reporting, not practice).
- Thermo gives no expected Cq or ΔCq rule for the Xeno control.

### KQ3. Inhibition: SPUD, internal controls, dilution series, kinetic signatures, computational detection, prone sample types

#### Takeaway
Inhibition is common in FFPE, blood, soil, stool and plant extracts, is invisible to A260 ratios, is assay-specific (so a universal spike can be clean while the target is inhibited), and leaves measurable signatures in the raw curve: a delayed Cq with reduced per-well efficiency (polymerase inhibitors), a delayed Cq with normal slope (IgG-type binding), or a lowered plateau with normal Cq (dye quenching). Published, testable detectors exist (KOD 2003, Kontanis 2006, Sisti's SOD 2010, Tichopad's ACT 2010); the shape-based ones are far more sensitive than efficiency-only tests.

#### Cited findings
- Nolan et al. 2006 (SPUD): 101-bp potato phyB amplicon (no homology to other organisms), ~20,000 copies spiked, TaqMan probe, giving a reference Ct of 23–24 in water; "determination of inhibition by Ct shift is valid only when [runs] are made as a single experiment and with constant threshold settings"; trace phenol shifted Ct and reduced efficiency although NanoDrop and A260/A280 "gave no indication"; in FFPE RNA, Ct shifts > 1 occurred "in up to 30% of samples" (5 of 30 significant); a survey found "only 6% of researchers test their nucleic acid samples for the presence of inhibitors"; reference-gene normalisation "assumes that the two assays are inhibited to the same degree" — [Nolan 2006](https://www.soihub.org/modules/Data_Quality_Modules/DQ_refs/qPCR_refs/Nolan_2006.pdf) [11]
- Vermeulen 2011 used SPUD ΔCq > 1 as the inhibition flag (8/740 samples) [23]
- Huggett et al. 2008: DNA extracts from 19 urines raised the mtLSU133 Ct by > 0.5 cycles in 15/19 but SPUD in only 1/19; at 10% unextracted urine one assay (IS1081) was unaffected while mtLSU133 was completely inhibited; 4 mM EDTA inhibited everything; susceptibility ranking SPUD < PjHSP70a < mtLSU133; conclusion: a control must be "inhibition-compatible" with the target; GC content/Tm were only tentatively linked — [Huggett 2008](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC2564953) [12]
- Sidstedt, Rådström & Hedman 2020 review: inhibitors by matrix — blood (haemoglobin, haematin, IgG, EDTA, heparin, lactoferrin), soil (humic/fulvic acid), faeces (bile salts, phytic acid, bilirubin), plus collagen, cellulose, phenol, DTT; mechanisms — polymerase inhibition (humic acid, haemoglobin, bile salts, phenols), DNA binding (IgG, collagen, bilirubin), fluorescence quenching (humic acid, haemoglobin, DTT); curve signatures — IgG: delayed Cq with slopes unaffected; humic acid and haemoglobin: decreased efficiency, then complete inhibition; quenching: lowered end-point fluorescence although product is present; AmpliTaq Gold/Taq fully inhibited by 0.004% v/v blood while rTth/Tfl amplified with 20%; purification recovers only 10–80% of DNA; hydrolysis probes were not quenched, dsDNA dyes were; IACs "highly recommended in diagnostics"; kinetic outlier detection "can complement" them — [Sidstedt 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072044) [13]
- Sidstedt et al. 2015: 500 ng humic acid quenched nearly all EvaGreen fluorescence without preventing amplicon production ("lowered amplification plots"); 1000 ng gave complete inhibition — [Sidstedt 2015, abstract](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/26170001) [14]
- MIQE 2.0: in the standard-curve method "diluting the sample also dilutes the inhibitor … resulting in artificially higher calculated efficiencies"; "efficiency values derived from individual amplification curves tend to be lower when inhibitors are present or when mismatches occur near the 3′ end of the primers"; "comparison to the efficiency of control targets, or analysis of Cq values from diluted samples, can be used to identify inhibited samples"; for clinical samples with variable inhibition "it may be more appropriate to derive PCR efficiency from individual reactions"; comparing positive-control and sample curves on a log[fluorescence] axis "permits the detection of potential inhibitors" — [MIQE 2.0](https://www.multid.se/publications/MIQE-ver2.pdf) [1]
- Bio-Rad applications guide: inhibitors make standard-curve efficiency "appear to increase" because the most concentrated points carry the most inhibitor; target 90–105% — [Bio-Rad Bulletin 5279](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf) [63]
- 2025 preprint (boar DNA assay, QuantStudio 3): a standard curve with slope −2.06, R² = 1.0 and "efficiency 205.15%" was interpreted as inhibitors in the concentrated standards or pipetting error — [Asrori et al. 2025, bioRxiv, CC BY 4.0](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.07.669038v1) [62]
- Bar et al. 2003 (KOD): each sample's curve-derived efficiency is tested against the mean of a training set; validated on dNTP-inhibited reactions and on cDNA with aberrant two-gene ratios; detects outliers differing 1.3–1.9-fold in quantity at P = 0.05 — [Bar 2003, abstract](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/12930979) [15]
- Kontanis & Reed 2006: sample-specific efficiency over a four-cycle window starting at the threshold cycle; reliably detected 0.4 ng tannic acid in a 25-µL reaction using prediction boundaries — [Kontanis 2006, abstract](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/16882221) [16]
- Sisti et al. 2010 (SOD): fit a 5-parameter Richards function; fingerprint = plateau Fmax, slope at the inflection point, and the inflection's y-coordinate; Mahalanobis distance to the standard-curve reference set, χ²(3) > 7.81 (α = 0.05); reference set of 72 standard reactions; shape parameters were not correlated with input amount (R² ≤ 0.033). Sensitivity/specificity, KOD vs SOD: tannic acid 0.30/0.94 vs 0.93/1.00; IgG 0.76/0.96 vs 1.00/0.94; quercetin 0.50/0.98 vs 1.00/0.98 (SOD more sensitive, p < 0.001); SYBR Green only; some false positives from the 95% CI definition — [Sisti 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2873533/) [17]
- Tichopad et al. 2010 (ACT): two efficiency-related parameters fitted to the most dynamic phase are Z-transformed against a reference set of calibration reactions; outperformed the univariate efficiency test; "the quality of the reference set determines the test's performance" — [Tichopad 2010, abstract](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/20109549) [18]
- Ruijter et al. 2009 (LinRegPCR authors): individual window-of-linearity efficiencies are normally distributed around the amplicon mean; they attribute residual per-well variation to statistical error and use one mean efficiency per amplicon; the paper gives no procedure for flagging inhibited wells; baseline errors propagate into efficiency — [Ruijter 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2665230/) [20]
- Svec et al. 2015: standard-curve efficiency uncertainty is up to 42.5% (95% CI) with single replicates and falls to ~2–3% with 3–4 replicates; efficiency is instrument-specific — [Svec 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4822216/) [19]
- qpcR (R, CRAN) implements KOD variants uni1 (Bar), uni2 (own), multi1/multi2 (Tichopad 2010), multi3 (Sisti) and is GPL-2 | GPL-3 — [qpcR refman](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/qpcR/refman/qpcR.html) [21]
- Practitioner rules: spike-in delayed vs control-alone = inhibition; dilute 1:5 or 1:10; efficiency < 90% suggests inhibition, > 110% suggests dimers/pipetting — [Azure Biosystems guide](https://azurebiosystems.com/qpcr-troubleshooting-workflow/) [57]; Promega: dilute 1:10 or 1:100 and include an internal amplification control — [Promega](https://www.promegaconnections.com/5-questions-to-ask-when-your-rt-qpcr-isnt-working/) [58]
- Bustin & Nolan 2017: "most publications do not even mention sample purity, as measured by the absence of inhibition"; a general inhibition assay "is probably not sufficient" because PCRs differ in susceptibility — [Bustin & Nolan 2017](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/28796277) [35]

#### Inferences
- The curve signatures map onto features qPilot already computes or can compute cheaply from `adp` data: per-well efficiency (LinRegPCR), plateau height, slope at inflection, asymmetry. A deterministic SOD/KOD implementation from the papers avoids GPL qpcR.
- Because inhibition is assay-specific, the right unit of reporting is per sample × per assay, with the spike result as supporting evidence only.
- The "efficiency > 110% with R² ≈ 1" standard-curve pattern is a specific, checkable inhibitor signature that current software treats as success.

#### Gaps
- No public labelled dataset of inhibited qPCR curves with raw fluorescence was found (Sisti's data availability not confirmed); the RDML benchmark used by qPilot contains no deliberate inhibition.
- SOD validated on SYBR only; behaviour with hydrolysis probes (no quenching) is untested in the literature read.
- Kontanis & Reed's full method (window definition details) is behind a paywall; only the abstract was read.

### KQ4. gDNA contamination and NTC amplification: no-RT controls, intron-spanning assays, DNase, per-gene gDNA estimation, dimer vs contamination

#### Takeaway
The gDNA share of a signal is a simple per-gene arithmetic from no-RT wells (fraction ≈ E^−ΔCq), with widely used thresholds (≥ 5–7 cycles ≈ ≤ 3% signal; ≥ 10 cycles ≈ 0.1%); ValidPrime generalises it to a correction with grades (< 3% ignore, 3–60% correct, > 60% uncorrectable) and MIQE 2.0 now asks for "estimated copies with and without RT". NTC signal is separated into dimer vs contamination by melt Tm; MIQE 2.0's 5-cycle rule and "frequency and Cq of negative controls" item give the acceptance logic.

#### Cited findings
- MIQE 2.0: no-RT control "checks for the presence of contaminating genomic DNA"; pure gDNA can be run to see whether primers amplify genomic targets; "an alternative is an assay that amplifies non-transcribed genomic DNA … combined with a control measurement on a genomic DNA standard, RT-qPCR data can be corrected for genomic DNA background"; footnote e: no-RT essential at first extraction, then desirable; pseudogenes "usually lack introns, and residual genomic DNA" must be considered at assay design; negative-control rule: a sample is positive only if its Cq precedes the negative control's by ≥ 5 cycles; NTC artifacts are "usually distinguishable from true products by analyzing the melting curves"; in diagnostics a probe signal in the NTC means the run "should not be reported"; checklist item "Frequency and Cq of negative controls" — [MIQE 2.0](https://www.multid.se/publications/MIQE-ver2.pdf) [1]
- Laurell et al. 2012 (ValidPrime): a gDNA-specific assay (VPA) targeting a non-transcribed locus measures gDNA in each RT(+) sample; the GOI's gDNA sensitivity is calibrated on a gDNA reference sample (ΔCq GOI − VPA); the RNA-derived quantity is the total minus the gDNA-derived quantity; grades A+ (assay does not amplify gDNA), A (< 3% gDNA, no correction), B (3–25%), C (25–60%, corrected if the assay passes a 0.3-SD ΔCq criterion), F (> 60%, "correction is not recommended", output HIGHDNA); 26 of 30 candidate VPAs amplified gDNA at 90–110%; LOD 3.2–3.7 copies; ValidPrime-estimated gDNA matched RT(−) measurements (ratio 1.20 ± 0.29); validated with 50–5,000 spiked haploid copies over 17–19 assays and 81 macrophage RNA preparations; replaces m RT(−) reactions plus m × n qPCRs with m + n + 1 controls (> 95% fewer controls on a 96.96 array); software gh-validprime "free for academic use", also in GenEx 5.3; two authors are TATAA employees — [Laurell 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3326333/) [22]; kits for human/mouse/rat/vertebrate, €225.82–657.07 — [TATAA ValidPrime](https://webshop.tataa.com/products/validprime%C2%AE) [52]; a patent application by the same group is listed on Justia (search summary, page not opened) [22]
- Bitesize Bio controls guide: −RT Ct should be ≥ 5–7 cycles above +RT; < 5 cycles means gDNA contributes > ~3%; always judge relative to the target's Ct; in routine work run one −RT per extraction batch; never skip −RT for GAPDH/β-actin-type pseudogene targets (intron-spanning primers still amplify processed pseudogenes); NTC: late Ct (> 36–38) with a melt peak ≥ 3 °C below the target = dimer, target-Tm peak or reproducible early signal = contamination; IPC ± 0.5–1 Ct; positive control ± 1 cycle — [Bitesize Bio](https://bitesizebio.com/37844/control-qrt-rtpcr/) [55]
- Azure guide: NTC typically Cq > 38; dimers melt 65–75 °C vs amplicons 78–88 °C; replicate Tm spread > 2–3 °C is suspicious — [Azure](https://azurebiosystems.com/qpcr-troubleshooting-workflow/) [57]
- ResearchGate threads (titles/snippets from search; pages returned 403): "Amplification in reverse transcription negative controls (no RT enzyme)", "Is amplification in no RT control normal in qPCR?", "Why is there amplification of only one target in the no RT control": answerers quote 10–15 cycles later as negligible (≈ 0.09% at 10 cycles, "roughly 1000-fold"), advise longer/higher DNase, exon-junction primers and checking melt curves — [59][60][61]
- 2025 preprint: NTC Cq 25.97 and non-target (cow) Cq 26.18 versus positive 18.47 with a single melt peak at the same Tm → "minute DNA contamination rather than … primer-dimer formation" — [Asrori 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.07.669038v1) [62]
- Bio-Rad guide: DNase treatment "may be inadvisable" with limited material; design primers across splice junctions instead — [Bio-Rad](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf) [63]
- MIQE 2009: "contamination assessment (DNA or RNA)" and "details of DNase or RNase treatment" are essential; "Cqs with and without reverse transcription" desirable with the same footnote — [MIQE 2009](https://www.gene-quantification.de/miqe-bustin-et-al-clin-chem-2009.pdf) [4]
- RDML 1.3 types every reaction's sample as `ntc`, `nac`, `nrt`, `pos`, `std`, `opt` or `unkn` and records `dnaseTreatment` as a boolean — [RDML schema](https://github.com/RDML-consortium/rdml-schema) [45]

#### Inferences
- With no-RT wells typed in RDML, "estimated copies with and without RT" is fully machine-computable per gene; the efficiency-corrected fraction and a per-gene verdict (ignore / correct / exclude) follow ValidPrime's grade bands.
- Melt-based dimer/contamination classification needs a per-assay product Tm from the positive wells of the same run (MIQE 2.0 notes Tm depends on mix, ramp and instrument calibration), not a fixed number.

#### Gaps
- ValidPrime IP status could not be verified (patent application listing seen only in a search result); implementing the published subtraction for research use seems low-risk but should be checked.
- Intron-spanning status of an assay cannot be derived from RDML alone (it holds sequences, not genomic coordinates); an offline check needs a local transcript/genome index or a human flag.
- No source quantifying how often NTCs amplify across runs in research labs.

### KQ5. MIQE 2.0 pre-analytic items: which are machine-checkable from RDML + QC exports, which need a human; current compliance

#### Takeaway
About a third of the pre-analytical items in MIQE 2.0 Sections 2–3 and most of Section 5 can be filled or checked automatically from an RDML file plus instrument QC exports; sampling, storage, extraction and reagent-lot items can only come from a short human form (or an ELN). Reporting of the checkable items is poor and falling: RNA integrity 22% → 11% (2019 → 2024), reference-gene validation 13% → 5%, efficiency 13% → 1% [2].

#### Cited findings
- Bustin & Wittwer 2026 (Clin Chem, doi 10.1093/clinchem/hvaf176): manual evaluation of 355 full-text articles (three cohorts of 85 plus two targeted cohorts of 50); scoring: RNA integrity counted only if assessed electrophoretically ("studies reporting only A260/280 ratios were scored 0"); results: RNA integrity 22% (19/85) in 2019 → 11% (9/85) in 2024 (P = 0.039); reference-gene validation 13% → 5%; PCR efficiency 13% → 1% (P = 0.004); 2024 MIQE-citing papers: 31%, 47%, 40%; 2024 "reference gene" cohort: RNA integrity 60%, efficiency 84%, sequences 98%, all three 56%; "PCR efficiency" cohort: RNA integrity 40%, reference-gene validation 38%, all three 24%; PMC keyword retrieval: "RNA integrity" in 4% of RT-qPCR papers (2007) → 7% (2025), "PCR efficiency" 3% → < 1%; MIQE citation peaked ~5% in 2014–2016; Asia 62% of 2024 output but Europe 44% of MIQE-citing papers; recommendation: journals should "reinstate concise, method-specific checklists", and "integration of MIQE-derived modules within electronic laboratory notebooks or data repositories" — [Bustin & Wittwer 2026](https://www.gene-quantification.de/bustin-wittwer-rt-qpcr-reporting-standards-clinical-chemistry-2026.pdf) [2]
- Bustin 2013 (Nat Methods): two surveys of > 1,700 qPCR publications found essential technical information often not reported; MIQE-citing papers markedly better — [Bustin 2013](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/24173381) [36]
- Bustin & Nolan 2017: of 20 recent Nature-publisher papers, almost all gave "No information" on RT replicates, RT conditions and PCR efficiency; a few gave RIN ≥ 5, RIN 9–10 or RIN ≥ 7 as their only quality statement; "transparency of reporting is negatively correlated with the impact factor" — [Bustin & Nolan 2017](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/28796277) [35]
- Bustin 2025 editorial: nucleic acid quality and integrity "are not properly assessed", efficiencies "assumed, not measured", compliance "patchy, and in many cases, entirely superficial"; MIQE > 17,000 citations; fold changes of 1.2–1.5 reported as meaningful — [Bustin 2025, IJMS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12154065/) [3]
- MIQE 2.0 Table 1 items (verbatim lists in section 1.4) and the RDML 1.3 fields (section 1.2) — [1][45]
- Biosearch summary of MIQE 2.0: "the reporting checklist has been streamlined"; stronger advice on "multiple negative controls and exogenous spike-ins"; raw data should be made available for re-analysis — [Biosearch blog](https://blog.biosearchtech.com/miqe-2.0-for-qpcr) [64]

- A tool that fills Sections 2–3 from QC exports and typed wells would, for its users, directly lift the three parameters whose reporting is collapsing.

#### Gaps
- No audit measures how often no-RT or NTC results are reported; the 2026 study scored only five parameters.
- A search-engine summary attributed "RNA integrity in 7–10% of studies; accession numbers 11%/32%" to the MIQE 2.0 paper; these figures are not in the paper's text and are not used here.

### KQ6. Which covariates (RIN, RT batch, extraction batch, plate) should enter a model; adjust vs exclude

#### Takeaway
Evidence supports treating RIN (or a study-derived integrity index) as a continuous covariate and RT/extraction batches as random effects when they are not confounded with the groups, and excluding or down-weighting samples beyond a study-specific integrity cut-off set in a pilot; where RIN tracks the group variable no adjustment can rescue the comparison, which is itself a finding the tool should surface.

#### Cited findings
- Gallego Romero 2014: RIN as a GLM covariate removed ~99.6% of degradation-driven differences; regressing RIN out first removed them entirely but altered between-individual differences (overlap with top variable genes 72% vs 43%); works only when RIN is not associated with the effect of interest — [Gallego Romero 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4071332/) [31]
- Ho-Pun-Cheung 2009: a RIN-based multiplicative correction derived from a consensus degradation profile reduced mean error to 8.4%; valid only for their protocol (random hexamers, amplicons < 200 bp); in biopsies the correction changed EGFR ratios 1.13–2.10-fold and reversed some rankings — [Ho-Pun-Cheung 2009](https://gene-quantification.de/ho-pun-cheung-rin-algorithm-2009.pdf) [25]
- Vermeulen 2011: normalisation did not fully remove quality effects; quality cut-offs should be study-specific from a pilot; the 5′–3′ ΔCq and the normalisation factor were the parameters with predictive value — [Vermeulen 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3089491) [23]
- Fleige 2006: normalisation to a degradation-insensitive reference gene plus efficiency correction reduces but does not abolish the RIN effect; reference genes differ in degradation sensitivity (28S least) — [Fleige 2006](https://gene-quantification.de/fleige-et-al-rna-integrity-2006.pdf) [24]; Antonov 2005 and Cronin 2004: ΔCt against the mean of several short-amplicon reference genes offsets fragmentation in FFPE — [29][30]
- Bustin & Nolan 2017: comparisons are valid "if they are done using samples of comparable RNA integrity"; RIN > 5 "good", > 8 "perfect", but degradation "affects transcripts in different ways", so samples differing in degradation "are likely to show differences where there are none" — [Bustin & Nolan 2017](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/28796277) [35]
- Tichopad 2009 / Kitchen 2010: nested ANOVA over subject / sample / RT / qPCR gives the variance share of each level and the budget-optimal replicate allocation — [9][10]
- Bustin 2014: RT-replicate variance differs significantly between samples (P = 0.005) and tracks RNA integrity — [5]
- Svec 2015: efficiency is instrument-specific — plate/instrument is a legitimate blocking factor — [19]
- MIQE 2.0 asks for "statistical methods used", "choice of significance level and calculation of statistical power", "repeatability" and "reproducibility" — [1]

#### Inferences
- A model of normalised expression with fixed group effect, continuous integrity covariate, and random RT-batch/extraction-batch/plate effects is defensible from the literature; but the first computation should be a confounding check (groups × batches crosstab; RIN by group), because the covariate approach is invalid when RIN or batch is collinear with the group.
- "Adjust vs exclude" is a decision, not a rule: the robustness grid should carry both (threshold exclusion at RIN 5/7/8; covariate adjustment; no adjustment) and report whether the conclusion changes.

#### Gaps
- No qPCR-specific study was found that compares RIN-as-covariate against exclusion on real experiments; the quantitative evidence is RNA-seq (Gallego Romero) and artificial-degradation cell-line qPCR (Ho-Pun-Cheung, Fleige).
- No published mixed-model template with RT batch as a random effect for RT-qPCR was found.

### D1. Per-sample "fitness-for-analysis dossier" built from instrument QC exports and joined to the study's own curves

1. **Persona and situation.** A PhD student or core client who measured RNA on a NanoDrop/Qubit and had a TapeStation or Bioanalyzer run done by the core, now analysing 6–12 plates; a core manager asked "why did these three samples fail?". Happens every study; QC files are produced anyway and ignored.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** P1, P2, P9, P10. RNA integrity is reported in 11% of 2024 RT-qPCR papers [2]; Fleige: Cq moves 0.04–1.6 cycles per RIN unit and both ΔΔCt and efficiency-corrected ratios stay RIN-dependent [24]; Ho-Pun-Cheung: up to ~100% error at RIN 5 and 73% of biopsies at RIN 5–7 [25]; Vermeulen: quality predicted misclassification (OR 4.65 for the 5′–3′ ΔCq) and "normalization did not fully remove the effect" [23]; MIQE 2.0 asks for the method of quality/quantity evaluation, spike-ins, extraction blanks and yield [1]. Nolan: NanoDrop and A260/A280 missed phenol inhibition [11]; Simbolo: NanoDrop reads higher than Qubit, and only Qubit tracks PCR-available template in FFPE [33].
3. **What exists today.** Benchling/SLIMS connectors push these files into an ELN (paid, enterprise, no link to qPCR analysis) [49]; allotropy (MIT) parses NanoDrop One/Eight/8000, Qubit 4/Flex, TapeStation, Lunatic into ASM JSON but has no Bioanalyzer or ProSize parser and reports RINe only as a generic calculated value [44]; bioanalyzeR (R, MIT) reads Bioanalyzer XML, TapeStation XML+CSV and ProSize CSVs and computes DV200, but is R-only and not connected to any qPCR tool [43]; Clarida scores MIQE from Cq tables and a questionnaire (no QC files) [brief]. Nobody joins the QC file to the plate and asks "does quality explain my result?".
4. **The feature.** Inputs: TapeStation XML (+ optional "_Electropherogram.csv"), Bioanalyzer 2100 XML/.xml.gz, ProSize CSV bundle (Peak Table, Smear, Quality Table, Size Calibration, Electropherogram), NanoDrop One/Eight CSV, Qubit 4/Flex CSV/XLSX, Lunatic CSV/XLSX — field names as in section 1.3. Deterministic method: (a) parse into canonical `SampleQC` facts (concentration by UV and by dye, A260/280, A260/230, RIN/RINe/RQN, 28S/18S area and height, DV200 from the region table or computed from the electropherogram as in bioanalyzeR, instrument serial, software/RINe version, ScreenTape ID/expiry, alerts, run date), each with the source file hash; (b) join to RDML sample ids by exact/normalised name match with a human-confirmed mapping stored in the record (the `Comment`/`Sample Name` free text is the only key); (c) rule pack with published thresholds, all editable: RIN ≥ 5 minimum and ≥ 8 optimal [24][35], RIN < 7 "high-variance" warning [26], DV200 bands for FFPE [48], A260/230 < 1.8 organics warning and UV/dye concentration ratio > ~1.5 "overestimated input" warning [33][24], amplicon > 200 bp on RIN < 8 or > 100 bp on FFPE [24][30], oligo-dT priming on RIN < 7 [25]; (d) study-derived integrity model: regress each reference gene's Cq and the normalisation factor on RIN across samples (Fleige's and Vermeulen's regression), report slope ± CI as Cq per RIN unit, and test RIN by group (Gallego Romero's confounding caveat) — this becomes a Decision Card: "RIN explains 38% of reference-gene variance; treatment group mean RIN 6.1 vs control 8.4; options: exclude samples with RIN < 7 / add RIN as covariate / accept; consequence per gene: …"; (e) optional 3′/5′ integrity index from two assays on one gene (Vermeulen/Padhi formula 2^(Cq5′ − Cq3′), ratio 8 ≈ RIN 5, ≈ 4 ≈ RIN 7 [27]) for labs without an electrophoresis instrument. Decision: include / exclude / adjust per sample, logged with reason. Record: QC facts, mapping, rule-pack version, MIQE 2.0 Section 2 items "method to evaluate quality and quantity", "extraction blanks and percent yield" (from spike recovery, D5), "template modification (DNase)" auto-filled. Agent: `qpcr_sample_qc(study)` returns the dossier; `qpcr_fitness(sample_id)` returns verdict and reasons; `qpcr_import_qc(file)` performs the parse and proposes the mapping.
5. **Feasibility.** Formats fully documented by MIT test files and source (allotropy test data, bioanalyzeR source) [43][44]; Python parsers: TapeStation XML (ElementTree), Bioanalyzer XML (base64 little-endian float32 for signals), ProSize CSVs, three CSV dialects — ~1–2 weeks; DV200 from raw traces needs size calibration via ladder peaks (bioanalyzeR does it; reimplement from its logic, ~1 week); mapping UI 1 week; integrity regression and cards 1–2 weeks. Public test data: allotropy test files (MIT), bioanalyzeR demo data (MIT). Hardest part: name matching across systems and the absence of any public corpus pairing QC exports with RDML plates (gap — the team should create one with a core facility). TapeStation native files are encrypted, so the user must export XML; the tool should display the exact export menu path.
6. **Why qPilot.** It already holds the raw curves, per-well efficiencies, reference-gene stability and the robustness grid; "sample inclusion by integrity threshold" and "RIN as covariate" are two more grid axes; amplicon lengths come from RDML `sequences`; the record and MCP make the dossier queryable by an agent; the domain-agnostic core means the same dossier pattern serves ELISA/plate assays later.
7. **Risks and confidence.** RIN is rRNA-based and individual mRNAs degrade differently (Bustin 2014, Vermeulen), so per-gene impact estimates must be shown as intervals and the study-derived slope preferred over literature values; thresholds are tissue-specific (Fleige's tissue-matrix effect); over-exclusion reduces power; labs without electrophoresis get only purity/quantity facts. Confidence: high for the dossier and auto-fill; medium for the impact model.

### D2. Inhibition gate from curve kinetics plus spike-ins, with the dilution-series artefact detector

1. **Persona.** A researcher working with FFPE, blood, stool, soil, plant or any extract with a low 260/230; a diagnostics-adjacent lab running SPUD or a Xeno/TATAA spike; a core manager with a "no amplification" complaint.
2. **Evidence.** P5, P6. Only 6% of researchers test for inhibitors [11][37]; FFPE: up to 30% of samples inhibited [11]; inhibition is assay-specific (SPUD inhibited in 1/19 urine extracts vs mtLSU133 in 15/19) [12]; MIQE 2.0: curve-derived efficiency drops with inhibitors while standard-curve efficiency is "artificially higher" [1]; signatures by mechanism (delayed Cq + lower efficiency; delayed Cq + normal slope; lowered plateau) [13][14]; published detectors with numbers: KOD detects 1.3–1.9-fold outliers at P 0.05 [15]; SOD sensitivity 0.93–1.00 vs KOD 0.30–0.76 at equal specificity [17]; ACT multivariate outperforms univariate [18]; a 205% efficiency with R² = 1.0 was read as inhibition by its authors [62].
3. **What exists.** qpcR (R, GPL-2/3) implements KOD/SOD/ACT but is GPL and not a product [21]; vendor software reports slope/R² and nothing about inhibition; SPUD is a manual Excel comparison; diagnostics kits have IACs; LinRegPCR uses the amplicon-mean efficiency and gives no per-well flag [20].
4. **The feature.** Inputs: raw `adp` curves, LinRegPCR per-well efficiency and window, well types from RDML (`std`, `ntc`, `pos`, spike wells annotated as property `spike=SPUD|Xeno|TATAA`), optional dilution series. Methods (all deterministic, from the papers; reimplemented, not copied from qpcR): (i) KOD per amplicon — Z-test of a well's efficiency against the amplicon's reference set (standards or the robust centre of all wells), α = 0.05 [15]; (ii) SOD — fit a 5-parameter Richards function, fingerprint (plateau, slope at inflection, inflection y), Mahalanobis distance to the reference set, χ²(3) > 7.81 [17]; (iii) quenching flag — plateau markedly below the reference with normal Cq and slope (dye assays) [14]; (iv) spike rule — ΔCq(spike in sample − spike in water) > 1 cycle = inhibited, same run and threshold [11][23]; (v) standard-curve artefact — efficiency > 110% or slope shallower than −3.1 with residuals trending with concentration → "inhibitor dilution-out suspected; do not accept this efficiency" [1][63]; (vi) differential-susceptibility caveat attached to every spike-based verdict [12]. Output per sample × assay: inhibited / suspect / clean with p-values and the reference-set composition. Decision Card (impact-ranked): "3 samples in group B flagged by SOD for 2 of 5 assays; options: exclude wells, dilute 1:5 and rerun, keep with flag; per-gene consequence: …"; escalation when a group conclusion flips. Record: detector version, reference set, parameters. Agent: `qpcr_inhibition(study, method=SOD|KOD|ACT|spike)`.
5. **Feasibility.** Pure numpy/scipy; KOD 2–3 days, SOD (Richards fit per well, 96 × plates) 1–2 weeks, ACT 1 week; validation set is the hard part: no public labelled inhibited-curve dataset was found; propose building one with SPUD plus tannic acid, humic acid and IgG titrations (Sisti's concentrations: tannic acid 0.0125–0.1 mg/mL, IgG 0.5–2 mg/mL, quercetin 0.04 mg/mL [17]) on two instruments, SYBR and TaqMan, and publishing it (CC0) as the acceptance test; the Brintz 2025 curve set (roadmap) may provide negatives. Hardest: false positives at low copy where curve shape degrades anyway [19][39]; TaqMan assays lack dye quenching so (iii) must be chemistry-aware.
6. **Why qPilot.** Per-well efficiency and raw curves already exist; detectors are "validated, versioned method modules with a public acceptance test" exactly as the constraints require; robustness reruns with/without flagged wells; the record stores reference sets so verdicts are reproducible.
7. **Risks.** Per-well efficiency is noisy (Ruijter treats residuals as statistical error), so KOD alone under-detects — ship SOD/ACT as primary; a flag is evidence of a kinetic anomaly, not proof of an inhibitor (mispriming, 3′ mismatches also lower efficiency [1]); spikes clean ≠ target clean. Confidence: medium-high.

### D3. gDNA contribution per gene from no-RT wells, with ValidPrime-style correction and grades

1. **Persona.** Anyone targeting intronless genes or pseudogene-rich references (GAPDH, ACTB) [55]; labs that skip DNase because input is limited [63]; anyone staring at a no-RT well 6 cycles behind the sample.
2. **Evidence.** P7. MIQE 2.0: "estimated copies measured with and without addition of RT"; no-RT essential at first extraction; correction via a gDNA assay plus gDNA standard is endorsed [1]; ValidPrime grades and the 60% limit [22]; rule-of-thumb thresholds 5–7 and 10 cycles [55][59][60]; MIQE 2009 lists DNase details and contamination assessment as essential [4].
3. **What exists.** ValidPrime kits (€226–657) with GenEx 5.3 or gh-validprime (free for academic use, Qt, Google Code) [22][52]; otherwise mental arithmetic.
4. **The feature.** Inputs: wells typed `nrt` with the same sample id (RDML `type` with `targetId`), optional VPA wells and a gDNA standard (`std` with `nucleotide = genomic DNA`), LinRegPCR mean efficiency per amplicon. Method: per sample × gene, gDNA fraction f = E^−(Cq_nrt − Cq_rt) with the amplicon's efficiency; grade per ValidPrime bands (A < 3%, B 3–25%, C 25–60%, F > 60% uncorrectable) [22]; with VPA + gDNA standard, compute the GOI's gDNA sensitivity ΔCq(GOI − VPA on gDNA), estimate the gDNA-derived quantity in every RT(+) sample from the VPA Cq, subtract from the total quantity (RQ_RNA = RQ_total − RQ_gDNA), require the assay-quality criterion (ΔCq SD ≤ 0.3 on gDNA) [22]; propagate uncertainty into N0. Decision Card per gene: "gDNA contributes 4–31% in 9/48 samples for GAPDH; options: subtract (grade B/C), exclude affected samples, drop GAPDH as reference (geNorm rerun), accept"; the robustness grid adds "corrected vs uncorrected". Record: fractions, grades, correction parameters; auto-fills the MIQE 2.0 RT item. Agent: `qpcr_gdna(study)`. Design hook: `qpilot plan` places one no-RT per sample at first extraction, then one per extraction batch, or m + n + 1 ValidPrime-style controls instead of m × n no-RT reactions [22][55].
5. **Feasibility.** Arithmetic only; one week including cards; the hard part is layout capture — vendor exports rarely mark no-RT wells, so the importer/plan must type them (RDML `nrt`). Intron-spanning status cannot be derived offline from RDML sequences (no genomic coordinates): ask the human or allow an optional local transcript index. ValidPrime IP: a patent application by the authors is listed (search summary) — implement the published subtraction for research use but check before commercial packaging.
6. **Why qPilot.** Reference-gene choice, per-gene consequences and robustness are already native; a gDNA-inflated reference gene is exactly the kind of judgement call the cards exist for.
7. **Risks.** Equal efficiency assumed for RT(+) and no-RT amplification; contamination of the no-RT well itself; low-copy noise near Cq 35+ makes fractions unstable (MIQE 2.0: LLOQ ≈ 10 copies [1]). Confidence: high.

### D4. Negative-control ledger: dimer-vs-contamination classification and contamination trending across runs

1. **Persona.** The student whose NTC lit up at Cq 32 the night before a lab meeting; the lab manager who suspects a contaminated water bottle or primer lot; the core that runs the same assays for many clients and has no cross-run memory.
2. **Evidence.** P8. MIQE 2.0: NTC signal "could indicate reagent contamination or cross-contamination"; artefacts "usually distinguishable … by analyzing the melting curves"; sample positive only if ≥ 5 cycles before the negative control; checklist asks for "frequency and Cq of negative controls" and reagent lot numbers [1]. The 2025 preprint: NTC Cq 25.97 with the product's Tm — contamination, not dimer [62]. Practitioner rules: late NTC (> 36–38) with a melt peak ≥ 3 °C below product = dimer; product-Tm peak or reproducible earlier signal = contamination; dimers melt ~65–75 °C vs amplicons 78–88 °C; replicate Tm spread > 2–3 °C suspicious [55][57]. No-RT ledger: one −RT per extraction batch [55].
3. **What exists.** Vendor software shows the NTC curve; melt-peak calling exists in vendor tools and in qPilot's roadmap ("melt-peak checks"); no tool keeps a per-assay/per-lab ledger or control chart, and no tool computes the "frequency and Cq of negative controls" item.
4. **The feature.** Inputs: `ntc`/`nac`/`nrt` wells, `mdp` melt data, per-run product Tm derived from the positive/unknown wells of the same assay in the same run (MIQE 2.0: Tm depends on mix, ramp and calibration, so never a fixed literature number [1]), run date, instrument serial, reagent/primer lot annotations (RDML `annotation` property/value; the plan writes them). Per run: NTC Cq (or "no Cq"), ΔCq to the lowest unknown, melt class (dimer if peak ≤ Tm_product − 3 °C; contamination if |peak − Tm_product| ≤ ~2 °C; ambiguous otherwise; probe assays: Cq-only), and the 5-cycle rule applied to every unknown. Across runs (lab memory): per assay, NTC positivity rate, NTC Cq series, Tm-class series; a control chart (Shewhart limits on NTC Cq; run-length rule on positivity) raising "contamination weather" alerts; change-point alignment with reagent lot and primer lot annotations ("positivity rose after lot X"). Decision Cards: invalidate run / exclude samples within 5 cycles of NTC / accept dimer-type NTC; per-gene consequences. Record: ledger entries are facts with ids; the MIQE 2.0 item is rendered from them. Agent: `qpcr_controls_ledger(lab, assay)`.
5. **Feasibility.** Melt-peak calling (derivative, smoothing, peak picking) 1 week; ledger and chart 1 week; lot annotations rely on the plan/import capturing them. Test data: qPilot's own RDML corpus (NTC wells present in most RDML files); the preprint's numbers as a worked case [62]. Hardest: cross-instrument Tm comparability (solved by within-run reference Tm) and persuading users to record lots (make it a one-click "same as last run").
6. **Why qPilot.** It already has melt data, typed wells and a store that persists across studies ("lab memory" is on the roadmap); the ledger is the first concrete lab-memory object with a standard behind it.
7. **Risks.** Probe assays give no melt class; low-level dimers in late NTCs are common and should not block runs (false alarms → approval fatigue; mitigate by impact ranking: only NTCs within 5 cycles of an unknown that matters get a card). Confidence: high.

### D5. The RT step as a first-class covariate: capture, variance decomposition, confounding check, and RT-replicate planning

1. **Persona.** Any lab reporting fold changes < 3; the student who reverse-transcribed treated samples on Monday and controls on Thursday; the core processing 200 samples in batches; the lab head choosing an RT kit.
2. **Evidence.** P3, P4. RT variability 0.4–3 cycles between replicates, 2–3-fold ratios, worse at low input and low RIN; enzyme-selection criterion ΔCq < 1 and 3.2 cycles per 1:10 dilution [5]; same priming and RNA input required [6]; RT is "a major and generally disregarded effect", non-linear, target-dependent [1]; RT replicates recommended for blood, sample replicates elsewhere, with a budget-optimal allocation method [9][10]; RT-enzyme carry-over inflates efficiency [8]; audits: RT replicates and conditions "No information" in almost all papers [35]; MIQE 2.0 Section 3 items [1].
3. **What exists.** powerNest (Kitchen 2010; availability/licence not verified), GenEx (commercial) nested-ANOVA features; nothing in free qPCR tools; RDML has `cdnaSynthesisMethod` and `templateQuantity` fields that no importer populates [45].
4. **The feature.** (a) Capture: a one-screen "RT form" per study writing RDML `cdnaSynthesisMethod` (enzyme, primingMethod enum, dnaseTreatment, thermal conditions), `templateQuantity.conc/nucleotide`, and annotations `rt_batch`, `rt_date`, `rna_input_ng`, `cdna_dilution`, `extraction_batch`, `operator`, `reagent_lot`; importable from a sample-sheet CSV so cores can supply it. (b) Design checks (deterministic rules): RNA input constant across samples [6]; cDNA fraction per PCR ≤ ~5% v/v or dilution ≥ 1:5 [8] versus the low-input variance penalty at 1:5 [5] (show both); priming method vs integrity (oligo-dT on RIN < 7 warning) [25]. (c) If RT replicates exist (same `sample_source`, different `rt_batch`): nested variance decomposition sample / RT / PCR (nested ANOVA, Tichopad 2009; Kitchen 2010) reporting the RT share and the ΔCq range per enzyme against the Bustin benchmark (< 1 good; 0.4–1.7 typical) [5][9][10]. (d) Confounding check: crosstab of group × rt_batch × extraction_batch × plate; flag any batch nested within a group (Gallego Romero's caveat generalised) [31]. (e) Mixed model (roadmap item extended): random effects for plate, RT batch and extraction batch when not confounded; robustness grid axis "batch effects in/out". (f) `qpilot plan`: recommend RT duplicates/triplicates and the replicate allocation under a budget [9][10]. Decision Card: "RT batch explains 22% of variance in normalised expression; group A was reverse-transcribed in batch 1 only — confounded; options: re-RT a subset, include batch effect (not identifiable), report as limitation". Record and MIQE 2.0 Section 3 auto-fill (priming method, one/two-step, RNA per reaction, reagents with lot, cDNA storage from the form). Agent: `qpcr_rt_variance(study)`, `qpcr_confounding(study)`.
5. **Feasibility.** Nested ANOVA and mixed models: statsmodels (BSD) ~1–2 weeks; form and sample-sheet import 1 week; the hard part is metadata capture — nobody records RT batches today, so the UI must make it a 30-second task with "same as last time" defaults, and the importer must accept a plain CSV.
6. **Why qPilot.** The decision log, per-gene consequences and robustness grid turn a variance table into a decision; the plan command already exists; RDML fields exist; MCP lets a core's LIMS push the sample sheet.
7. **Risks.** Low value when no metadata is captured (the form must be frictionless); nested designs need replicates many labs will not run — then the deliverable is the confounding check and the "no RT replicates: fold changes below 3 are within RT noise [5]" caveat on the confidence level. Confidence: medium.

### D6. QC-ready plate design rules in `qpilot plan` plus the MIQE 2.0 Sections 2/3/5 auto-fill map

1. **Persona.** The person designing next week's plates; the first author filling the MIQE table at submission; the Clin Chem-style reviewer.
2. **Evidence.** P9, P11, P12. MIQE 2.0 Table 1 and its footnotes [1]; MIQE 2009 E/D items [4]; audits [2][35][36]; design rules with numbers: NTC per assay per plate, one −RT per extraction batch, positive control ± 1 cycle, IPC ± 0.5–1 Ct [55]; spike ΔCq > 1 rule [11][23]; amplicon ≤ 200 bp (RIN ≥ 5) and ideally 80–150 bp [24], < 100 bases for FFPE [30]; RT duplicates/triplicates [5][6]; dilution series to catch inhibition, "preferably" over SPUD [4]; inter-run calibrator flag in RDML [45]; in-sample internal control at 3–10× LOD [1].
3. **What exists.** Clarida's MIQE score (Cq input, questionnaire) [brief]; the checklist as an editable table [1]; plate-design assistants in vendor software (layouts only, no control logic).
4. **The feature.** `qpilot plan` gains a "QC-ready layout" option: given assays, samples, groups, sample type (fresh/frozen/FFPE/blood/plant) and whether a spike/SPUD/ValidPrime is available, it emits a plate map with typed wells (ntc per assay, nrt per extraction batch, pos, std dilution series per new assay, interRunCalibrator, spike-in-water control, VPA + gDNA standard if chosen) and writes the RDML sample types/annotations so D2–D5 are automatically checkable after the run; a rule check on assays (amplicon length vs sample type and expected RIN; priming vs integrity; pseudogene/intronless flag entered by the user). At the end of the study, a MIQE 2.0 renderer fills every machine-checkable item from engine facts and the two short human forms (Sample/Extraction form, RT form), marks the remaining items "not recorded", and exports the Yes/No table with locations — this is the "MIQE-derived module" Bustin & Wittwer call for [2]. Agent: `qpcr_plan(qc_ready=true)`, `qpcr_miqe(study)`.
5. **Feasibility.** Layout logic 1 week; renderer 1 week; the map in KQ5 is the specification. Hardest: not over-constraining plates (controls cost wells; impact-rank which controls matter for this study's conclusion).
6. **Why qPilot.** Plan, record, decision log and the domain-agnostic core already exist; the checklist renderer is a roadmap headline, and the item-by-item machine-checkability map above is the concrete specification that was missing.
7. **Risks.** Users may see mandated controls as nagging (keep every rule optional with its consequence stated); MIQE 2.0's editable supplementary table format may change. Confidence: high.

## 6. Open questions and what could not be verified

- Per-sample cost and turnaround of core electrophoresis QC (Agilent store and a core price list were unreachable); my "a few € per sample" is an estimate.
- RINe (TapeStation) vs RIN (Bioanalyzer) agreement figures (Agilent technical overview 403).
- Whether any public dataset pairs instrument QC exports with RDML plates (none found; needed for D1 tests).
- Whether ValidPrime's correction is patent-encumbered for software implementation (Justia listing seen only in a search result).
- Public availability of Sisti's inhibited-curve data and of powerNest (licence).
- Exact Thermo Xeno/TATAA expected Cq and recommended ΔCq rules (manuals not opened; TATAA page states only "higher Cq than control = inhibition").
- LUNA cell-counter export format (not in allotropy; not found).
- Reddit/ResearchGate threads could not be opened; thread titles and the 5–7/10-cycle rules of thumb are from search-result summaries and Bitesize Bio.
- The 2025 bioRxiv NTC preprint is not peer-reviewed.
- Forum-level evidence for "RIN values copied by hand into Excel" was not directly found; the inference rests on the existence of paid LIMS connectors and on audit data showing integrity is rarely reported.
- A search-engine summary attributed reporting rates ("7–10%", "11%/32%") to the MIQE 2.0 paper; the extracted text does not contain them, so they are excluded.
