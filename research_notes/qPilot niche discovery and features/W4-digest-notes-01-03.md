## A. How this corner works

RT-qPCR is the default way a wet lab measures mRNA of 3–20 target genes plus 1–3 reference genes across 6–60 biological samples (uncited in note; framed as "typically"). Volume: ~5,000 PMC papers/month mention qPCR in Methods (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41332908/); the author's own PubMed query gives 12,900 records for 2015, 16,827 for 2019, 19,510 for 2024 and 22,390 for 2025 (https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi?db=pubmed&term=(qPCR[tiab]+OR+RT-qPCR[tiab]+OR+%22real-time+PCR%22[tiab]+OR+%22quantitative+PCR%22[tiab]+OR+%22real-time+quantitative+PCR%22[tiab])+AND+2025[dp]&rettype=count). Actors: PhD students/postdocs (design, bench, Excel analysis, defend to PI), technicians ($18–36/h US adverts; https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Qpcr), core staff (run plates, often "does not provide data analysis services"; https://www.huck.psu.edu/core-facilities/genomics-facility/services), PIs (see only the bar chart), biotech R&D (gene expression is 31% of buyer applications; https://labmanager.com/surveys/are-you-in-the-market-for-qpcr-18095).

The week: design (estimate 0.5–2 days) → RNA extraction (<20 min column to ~1 h organic; https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/dna-rna-purification-analysis/rna-extraction/rna-applications/rna-extraction-for-real-time-pcr.html) → RT (40 min program; https://www.surgery.pitt.edu/sites/default/files/GSVLLabInternal/Info_handbook/SOPs/General/protocol%20for%20RNA%20extraction%20and%20qPCR_0.pdf) → plate setup (estimate 45–90 min/96-well) → "~2 hours" run (https://www.bowdish.ca/lab/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Updated-qPCR-Protocol-Aunika-Venables.pdf) → end-of-run check (estimate 10–30 min) → export .xlsx → Excel analysis (estimate 30 min–2 h per plate) → stats → figure → write-up. A single plate is a ~1.5-day loop (author inference).

Economics: QuantStudio 5 list $66,500 (https://www.fishersci.com/shop/products/quantstudio-5-real-time-pcr-system-96-well-0-2ml/A28139); service 10–15% of list/yr, optical repair $7,000–10,000, $85.78 per 96-well plate (https://unicornlifescience.com/pcr-equipment-cost-breakdown-labs-actually/); core runs $30–45 (https://pcrlab.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/research/research-services) to $56–100 (https://biomedcorefacilities.brown.edu/genomics-facility); a failed plate costs roughly $150–300 plus 3–5 person-hours (estimate). Market: instruments ~$1.2B in 2025 (https://straitsresearch.com/report/qpcr-instruments-market); whole real-time PCR ~$6.35B in 2024 (https://www.precedenceresearch.com/real-time-pcr-market). The 2026 audit of 432 papers: "impossible to determine whether >90% of qPCR results are reliable" (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.04.626769v1.full).

## C. Discoveries (note's ranking: impact × feasibility × fit, D1 first)

### D1. Run triage: "repeat, rescue or ignore" decided by consequence and cost, not by a 0.5-Ct rule
- **Persona:** PhD student/technician at 7 pm with a finished plate: NTC at Ct 36.4, two samples with 0.9 Ct replicate spread, one undetermined well. Every lab, several times a month (estimate).
- **Pain evidence:** Replicate rules conflict: 0.2–0.5 Ct (https://www.researchgate.net/post/Triplicate-for-samples-in-qPCR-analysis), "<1.5 difference. Omit outliers" (https://bridgeslab.sph.umich.edu/protocols/index.php/QPCR_Analysis_on_ThermoCloud), SD>0.5 (https://www.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/application-notes/pcr/an299-real-time-qpcr-guidelines-for-a-comparison-of-reagent-performance.pdf), "a spread of up to two cycles should be acceptable" to Cq 35 (https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/11/6/496). A notebook flagged 17 samples with >1 Ct gap and "called for a full rerun" (https://eagle.fish.washington.edu/trilobite/notebooks/jake/heareresearch.blogspot.com/search%3Fupdated-max=2015-08-14T18:58:00-07:00&max-results=7&reverse-paginate=true.html). Repeat ≈ $150–300 and 3–5 person-hours (estimate).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Vendor flags HIGHSD/NOAMP/EXPFAIL are "advisory"; qRAT drops above SD 0.3 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9297597/); VoilaPCR flags >0.5 Ct (closed, Cq-input, cloud); PlateVision heatmaps (GPL-3). None answers "does this well matter for any conclusion, and what exactly must I re-run?"
- **Feature:** Input: study RDML (raw curves, sample/target per well) + group design. Method per questionable well: (a) classify anomaly — NTC melt peak within ±1 °C of target Tm = contamination, 5–10 °C lower = primer-dimer; replicate deviation on LinRegPCR N0 with Poisson-aware tolerance CV ≈ 1/√N (33% at 10 copies; https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/11/6/496) and Bustin 2025 Cq tiers (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387016/); (b) consequence test — recompute every group comparison under keep / drop well / drop sample / subtract NTC background, report which conclusions change; (c) cost line — wells to repeat, reagent cost from a per-lab price profile, hours, cDNA volume vs recorded remaining volume (optional sample ledger). Decision card per gene: ignore / drop and record / repeat N wells / repeat plate. Record: anomaly class, evidence, options, choice, reason, hash. Agent: `qpcr_triage(study, plate)`, `qpcr_repeat_plan(card_id)`.
- **Feasibility:** rdmlpython (MIT, already in qPilot); melt-peak via negative first derivative; Grubbs/Dixon closed-form. Validate on Brintz 2025 CC0 curve set (brief) and RDML benchmark. Effort 2–3 weeks triage core + 1 week cost/ledger. Hardest: honest "no conclusion changes" when later plates aren't run yet.
- **Why qPilot:** Robustness engine pointed at one plate; card/record/MCP make the repeat decision auditable.
- **Risks / confidence:** False reassurance for systemic contamination (mitigate with D3 chart); labs may want a rule, not a model (keep a "lab rule pack"). **High** that pain is real and unserved; **medium** on adoption. Rank 1.

### D2. Cq provenance diff: where the vendor's baseline/threshold silently changes the answer
- **Persona:** Any analyst exporting Cq with auto baseline/threshold; reviewer who gets only Cq. Every run.
- **Pain evidence:** "the Ct values just shift by half a cycle and you never notice" (https://voilapcr.com/blog/how-to-export-and-analyze-quantstudio-qpcr-data); MIQE 2.0 names four analysis choices that move Cq (https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148); manufacturers' software "often yield substantially different results" (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41332908/); Ruijter 2013 compared 11 curve methods across 69 genes (http://www.gmo-qpcr-analysis.com/ruijter-methods-59-transcriptional-biomarkers-2013.pdf).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Manual threshold in vendor software; LinRegPCR as separate step needing vendor-specific switches ("no baseline subtraction" in Maestro; https://www.researchgate.net/post/Has_anyone_sucessfully_exported_raw_data_from_BioRad_qPCR_files_to_conduct_analysis_using_LinRegPCR_or_other_programs); qPilot computes N0 but doesn't compare with vendor Cq in the same file.
- **Feature:** Input RDML (vendor Cq next to fluorescence per reaction — note says verify element names against RDML 1.3 schema) or vendor xlsx Results joined to curves. Per well: vendor Cq, LinRegPCR Cq/N0, Cy0 (Guescini 2008 BMC Bioinformatics — note did not fetch, confirm licence-free reimplementation), fixed-threshold Cq. "Cq method" becomes a robustness-grid axis; card only when the axis flips a conclusion. Plus a baseline-window audit (vendor baseline end inside exponential phase). Record: per-well four-Cq table. Agent: `qpcr_cq_provenance(study)`.
- **Feasibility:** rdmlpython (MIT); Cy0 and fixed-threshold <200 lines; Ruijter 2013 dataset availability unknown. Effort 1–2 weeks. Hardest: reading vendor baseline params from xlsx-only exports.
- **Why qPilot:** Only open tool with raw curves plus robustness engine; natural "explain-any-number" target.
- **Risks / confidence:** Users may distrust disagreement with the instrument; present with MIQE 2.0 item pre-filled. **High**. Rank 2.

### D3. Lot and batch drift ledger: Levey-Jennings charts on IRC and NTC per assay, with lot fields in the record
- **Persona:** Lab running the same ~10 assays for months (thesis, biotech programme, core). Every reagent change (estimate: monthly).
- **Pain evidence:** Four master mixes on the same cDNA gave "around 10- to about 30-fold" differences; two mixes gave 2- to 5-fold fold-change differences (https://www.gene-quantification.de/bustin-nolan-miqe-2017.pdf); MIQE 2.0 requires lot numbers and verification "when changing batches" (https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148); UC Davis core compares every new enzyme lot (https://pcrlab.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk6571/files/inline-files/CoreFacility_QA-QCGuidance_2016.pdf); pooled ΔCq 1.5 between instruments (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387016/).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Paper logbooks at good cores; nothing in Excel; qbase/qRAT use calibrators only to scale plates; clinical Levey-Jennings/Westgard not in research software.
- **Feature:** Per-run optional fields `primer_lot[target]`, `mastermix_lot`, `rt_batch_id`, `rt_enzyme_lot`, `instrument_id`, `operator`, `plate_type` (form, CSV, or RDML `experimenter`/`documentation`; RDML has no formal lot field so qPilot's record extension carries them and exports a MIQE 2.0 lot table). Statistic: per assay, IRC efficiency-corrected N0, NTC Cq and median per-well efficiency per run; limits from first ≥10 runs; Westgard rules (1-2s warn, 1-3s/2-2s/R-4s flag); CUSUM change-point aligned to lot-change dates. Card: e.g. "primer lot P-0319 shifted IRC by −0.8 Cq (3.2 SD)" with options re-validate / apply lot offset (with robustness consequences) / exclude plates. Agent: `qpcr_lot_drift(lab_workspace, assay)`, `qpcr_lot_register(run, fields)`.
- **Feasibility:** Westgard/CUSUM public domain; ≥3 IRCs recommended by qBase (https://www.gene-quantification.de/qBase_manual_v1.0.1.pdf). Effort 2 weeks. Hardest: getting labs to type lot numbers (carry forward defaults; barcode/photo later).
- **Why qPilot:** Shared workspace + hash-chained record make a lab-level longitudinal store natural; per-well efficiency is a drift signal Cq-only tools can't see.
- **Risks / confidence:** Few labs run IRC every plate (fallback to NTC/reference-gene Cq, weaker). **Medium-high** (lot effects strong; lot recording weak). Rank 3.

### D4. Multi-plate assembly with layout-as-loaded verification and overlap-based between-run correction
- **Persona:** Any study >1 plate (most theses): 3–12 plates, different days/people.
- **Pain evidence:** "the 6-times difference between runs 2 and 5"; calibrator errors "propagate to every non-calibrator sample" (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214753515300012); "you can easily pipette the wrong solution into the wrong well" (https://bitesizebio.com/25261/control-your-error-how-to-minimize-pipetting-error-and-get-low-sds-in-qrt-pcr/); Maestro treats same-named biological replicates as technical (https://www.researchgate.net/topic/Maestro).
- **What exists / why it fails:** qbase/qRAT IPC scaling needs all calibrators to succeed; Factor-qPCR standalone, licence/maintenance unknown; tidyqpcr plans (R); PlateVision heatmaps (GPL-3).
- **Feature:** (a) ingest long-format plan (well, sample_id, target_id, prep_type, plate_id) per tidyqpcr (https://docs.ropensci.org/tidyqpcr/articles/platesetup_vignette.html) or vendor Sample Setup; (b) as-loaded checks: NTC no/late amplification, -RT ≥5 cycles later than +RT, per-sample reference-gene N0 consistent across plates within plate factor, replicate blocks agree, no systematic edge effects — each failure a card ("wells B3–B5 look like sample 7, not sample 8"); (c) Ruijter 2015 correction: geometric mean of ratios over shared conditions, nested geometric means for missing pairs (Eq. 3), run factor per plate (Eq. 4), divide N0 (Eq. 5), recommend another plate when >2 substitution rounds needed; (d) "correction method" (none/calibrator/overlap) as a robustness axis. Agent: `qpcr_plate_verify(study)`, `qpcr_between_run(study, method)`.
- **Feasibility:** Equations explicit in open-access paper; plan format public; heatmap ideas reimplementable without GPL code. Effort 3–4 weeks. Hardest: mis-loaded well vs real biological outlier — never auto-relabel.
- **Why qPilot:** Whole-study analysis from raw curves (needs N0, not Cq); record keeps plan and verification together.
- **Risks / confidence:** Labs with no overlap get only a warning (correct behaviour). **High** on method, **medium** on verification heuristics. Rank 4.

### D5. Claim guard: minimum reportable fold change from Cq level and replicate spread, with bootstrap intervals bound to the figure
- **Persona:** PI/student deciding whether "1.6-fold down, p=0.04" goes in the paper; reviewer. Every figure.
- **Pain evidence:** "Fold-changes of 1.2- or 1.5-fold are routinely reported as biologically meaningful" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12154065/); Bustin 2025: two-fold reliable only at Cq<25, ≥3-fold at Cq 25–30, ≥4-fold above Cq 30; 95% CI 0.71–14.74 for a 3× comparison above Cq 30; six technical replicates for 1.5-fold at SD 0.5 Cq (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387016/); 3% efficiency error can double a 30-cycle ratio (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41332908/).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Nothing in vendor software; Prism error bars on 2^-ΔΔCq; qPilot's GRADE-style confidence and `qpilot plan` lack the Cq-tier rule and technical-replicate bootstrap.
- **Feature:** Per gene/comparison: group Cq tier, technical-replicate SD on N0, 10,000-resample bootstrap of group ratio, compare observed fold change to tier threshold and bootstrap 95% interval; flag claims crossing 1 or below tier; write interval into figure legend and Study Confidence. Card only on failure: report as "not resolved at this depth" / add N technical replicates (computed at 80% power) / run dilution series. Agent: `qpcr_claim_check(study)`.
- **Feasibility:** numpy/scipy (BSD). Effort 1 week. Hardest: tiers are one group's proposal — ship as a named, versioned rule pack.
- **Why qPilot:** Per-well N0 and robustness engine exist; "no naked numbers" binds interval to figure and methods text.
- **Risks / confidence:** Authors dislike "unresolved"; frame as reviewer-proofing. **High** on need, **medium** on thresholds. Rank 5.

### D6. Machine-checkable MIQE 2.0: auto-fill what data can prove, state what it never can, emit editable Table 1
- **Persona:** Author at submission, reviewer, core manager. Every paper; 2 of 432 cite MIQE.
- **Pain evidence:** 432-paper audit: RNA integrity 7–10%, efficiency 5–8%, software unnamed 48–59%, M-values 0.4%, RDML 0%; authors ask for "automated tools" that "scan manuscripts before submission" (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.04.626769v1.full). Vendor-page trend numbers (13%→1% efficiency reporting 2019–2024) unverified (https://unicornlifescience.com/miqe-guidelines-qpcr-reporting-compliance/).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Static checklists; Clarida MIQE score (Cq input; paid); SciScore-style scanning for other guidelines.
- **Feature:** Classify Table 1 items: (A) provable from RDML + record, auto-filled with fact ids (raw data in RDML, baseline/Cq method, software version, NTC frequency/Cq, efficiency method, target quantity method, replicates, repeatability, dynamic range if series present, reference-gene validation via geNorm, normalisation, statistics, cycling parameters, instrument, primer sequences if present); (B) fillable only if typed (D3 lot fields, priming method, RNA amount, kit, storage); (C) never machine-checkable (sampling, optimisation, biological justification). Output: editable Table 1 with Yes/No, location, fact ids; methods paragraph companion. Agent: `qpcr_miqe(study)`.
- **Feasibility:** Checklist text in open-access paper (https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148); RDML 1.3 schema public. Effort 1–2 weeks. Hardest: class B capture UI (shared with D3).
- **Why qPilot:** Record already holds class-A facts with hashes; each row bound to a fact id.
- **Risks / confidence:** Journals don't enforce MIQE; reviewer-critic agent is the other consumer. **High** feasibility, **medium** demand. Rank 6.

### D7. Core-facility handoff bundle: client-readable "what happened to your run" page plus the record as deliverable
- **Persona:** Core manager and client PI/student. Every core run.
- **Pain evidence:** "the facility does not provide data analysis services" (https://www.huck.psu.edu/core-facilities/genomics-facility/services); UC Davis director "will interface with clients on all aspects... progress, problems, and recommended solutions", raw curves kept 5 years (https://pcrlab.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk6571/files/inline-files/CoreFacility_QA-QCGuidance_2016.pdf); commercial deliverable "raw Ct values, ddCt analysis, plus an Excel file" (https://arqgenetics.com/services/); prices $30–100 per run; EPFL robot-assembles plates (https://www.epfl.ch/research/facilities/gene-expression-core-facility/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/GECF-price-list-for-EPFL-users-November-2023.pdf).
- **What exists / why it fails:** iLab/booking, email, Excel; vendor cloud shares vendor files only; no auditable analysis or plain-language failure explanation.
- **Feature:** `core` profile in `run --batch`: takes client plate plan + instrument export, runs D1 triage and D4 verification with the core's rule pack, emits (i) one-page client report (controls passed, replicate quality, efficiency, wells to repeat and why, sentences bound to fact ids), (ii) Analysis Record as file of record, (iii) QA logbook line (instrument, operator, lots). Core staff approves via agent-requests inbox before release. Agent: `qpcr_core_report(run, client)`.
- **Feasibility:** Reuses D1/D3/D4; template a day's work; UC Davis QA/QC guidance is a ready spec. Effort 1–2 weeks after D1/D4.
- **Why qPilot:** Local-first (cores can't send client data to cloud), shared workspace with locks, autonomy levels.
- **Risks / confidence:** Cores fear liability — report "observations", not conclusions. **Medium** (no core survey reached). Rank 7.

## A. How this corner works

An assay is one primer pair (~18–24 nt) ± probe amplifying a 50–200 bp amplicon. Efficiency acceptance window is 90–110% (https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148 ; https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf, which gave 90–105%, R² > 0.980); Poisson limits put LLOQ at ~10 copies, Cq ≈ 35 at 90% efficiency (https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148). Life of an assay: design (hours to a day) in Primer-BLAST or a vendor tool, or copied from a paper/colleague; in-silico checks (minutes, often skipped); order; resuspend to 100 µM stock, 10 µM working aliquots at −20 °C (https://www.idtdna.com/page/support-and-education/decoded-plus/my-oligos-have-arrived-now-what/); validate (half a day to a week) with a 10-fold dilution series of ≥5 points × ≥3 replicates (https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148); use for months to years; re-order; loss when the student leaves; publication where MIQE 2.0 section 1 fields are mostly not reported.

Audit numbers: efficiency reported in 5%/8% of papers (genetics/plant), accession numbers 14%/31%, specificity 8%/13%, M-value 0.4%, 2 papers cite MIQE (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41073-026-00188-0); 2014 audit: median 40% compliance with 15 essential items, efficiency 30% (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0088266). Silent failures: a 3'-terminal mismatch shifts Ct 0.99–9.09 cycles depending on base pair (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2797725/); >80% of human primers overlap a documented variant, ~3 SNPs/primer (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5210624/); standard-curve efficiency uncertainty up to 42.5% (95% CI) with single replicates, 8.3% single wells vs 2.3% triplicates (https://www.gene-quantification.de/svec-et-al-pcr-efficiency-bdq-2015.pdf); choice of E method changed a telomere T/S ratio from 5.39 to 0.73 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8484782/). Thermo lot-to-lot spec <0.6 Ct (https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/4440888/faqs). Costs (estimate): one validation plate ≈ 18–24 wells + half a day; failed design = reorder cycle of days to a week; no citable oligo price found. Quartzy entry plan ~$159/month for five users (https://www.softwareadvice.com.au/software/150604/quartzy).

## C. Discoveries (note's ranking)

### D1. Assay Passport: per-primer-pair performance record computed from every RDML run
- **Persona:** Postdoc in a 10-person lab, 2–4 plates/week on CFX384 + QuantStudio, ~150 pairs in a spreadsheet half designed by departed people; core manager with 40 client assays. Every study start, re-order, failed plate.
- **Pain evidence:** Efficiency reported in 5–8% of papers (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41073-026-00188-0); standard-curve E uncertainty up to 42.5% (https://www.gene-quantification.de/svec-et-al-pcr-efficiency-bdq-2015.pdf); E-method alone changed a ratio 7-fold (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8484782/); Thermo lot-to-lot <0.6 Ct (https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/4440888/faqs). Cost: one repeated validation plate per suspicious assay per month per lab "is plausible" (estimate).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Vendor software reports E per plate and forgets it; Clarida/qbase+ take Cq input so can't compute per-well E or melt fingerprints; inventory tools store tubes.
- **Feature:** Input: every RDML. Identity = SHA-256 of (fwd, rev, probe) when `target/sequences` filled; else `commercialAssay{company, orderNumber}`; else target id + reconciliation card. Per well: LinRegPCR E and flags, Cq, melt points → Tm/width via rdmlpython MCA, sample type/quantity, NTC/no-RT roles, instrument id, run date. Registry side-file `assays.yaml` (manufacturer, lot, order number, resuspension date/buffer, concentration, freezer location, design tool/version, accession, amplicon coordinates — MIQE 2.0 section-1 fields; RDML has none of these). Write back `amplificationEfficiency`, `SE`, `meltingTemperature`, `detectionLimit`, `amplificationEfficiencyMethod` into RDML `target`. Methods: (a) per-run E = mean/SD of per-well window-of-linearity E; (b) Shewhart/EWMA charts segmented by lot/instrument/master-mix, limits learned from the assay's own first N runs; change-point at lot boundaries; (c) melt fingerprint per assay per instrument; (d) NTC behaviour and Cq gap vs MIQE ≥5-cycle rule, PrimePCR NTC Cq<35 → redesign (https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6262.pdf); (e) validation events: standard-curve E with CI from replicate resampling, LOD by probit 95%, LLOQ by precision; compare vs per-well E on same plate. Cards: e.g. "E for IL6 (lot B) averages 1.78 vs 1.91 for lot A over 11 plates — re-validate / retire / accept with note". Record: passport JSON appended; robustness axis "efficiency source = per-plate mean / passport prior / 100%". MCP: `qpcr_assay_passport(assay_hash)`, `qpcr_assay_history(assay_hash, since)`.
- **Feasibility:** rdmlpython MIT (https://github.com/RDML-consortium/rdmlpython); RDML schema MIT (https://github.com/RDML-consortium/rdml-schema). ≈2–3 dev-weeks v1 + 1 week for validation events/LOD. Hardest: identity across inconsistent names; thresholds without over-alerting; lot segmentation depends on one-time human entry.
- **Why qPilot:** Already has per-well E from raw curves, melt data, cards, hash-chained record, MCP; competitors would have to change input model.
- **Risks / confidence:** Cq-only vendor exports give no curves; alert fatigue; lot entry skipped. **High** for value and feasibility; **medium** that labs enter lot data. Rank 1.

### D2. In-silico pre-flight of existing assays against current genome resources (offline-first, permissive)
- **Persona:** Postdoc inheriting a spreadsheet; student designing at 7 pm; reviewer asking "which isoforms?". Every new/inherited assay; every annotation release.
- **Pain evidence:** >80% of human primers overlap a known variant (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5210624/); terminal-mismatch shifts 1–9 cycles (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2797725/); IDT: "Recheck previously used primers and probes, since genomic data keeps changing" (https://www.idtdna.com/page/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/832); Vogels: a degenerate mismatch cost the Charité RdRp set 6–10 Ct (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-020-0761-6).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Primer-BLAST manual, web, no batch, SNP check human-only (https://www.nlm.nih.gov/ncbi/workshops/2023-09_Primer-BLAST/interface.html); GETPrime/qPrimerDB precomputed catalogues not "your pair"; vendor pipelines only their assays; Geneious/Beacon closed.
- **Feature:** Input assay hash + sequences + species + pinned "pre-flight pack" version. Steps: (i) binding sites in pinned transcript set via seqkit `amplicon -m` (MIT; https://github.com/shenwei356/seqkit), re-scored with 3'-end-aware rules per Stadhouders/Lefever tables and NN duplex ΔG/Tm with mismatches (Biopython `Tm_NN`, https://biopython.org/docs/latest/api/Bio.SeqUtils.MeltingTemp.html, or TNTBLAST BSD-3, https://github.com/jgans/thermonucleotideBLAST); (ii) isoform coverage fraction, MANE Select included?; (iii) exon-junction status, predicted gDNA amplicon; (iv) genome-wide off-targets incl. processed pseudogenes (≤1 kb, ≤k mismatches outside last 5 nt); (v) SNP overlay with MAF via Ensembl REST `overlap/region?feature=variation` (https://rest.ensembl.org/documentation/info/overlap_region) or offline VCF, scored position × MAF × mismatch class into expected Cq bias; (vi) hairpin/homodimer ΔG (seqfold MIT; https://github.com/Lattice-Automation/seqfold); (vii) predicted amplicon Tm vs passport Tm. Cards: "Pair covers 3/7 isoforms"; "rs… MAF 0.21 at position −2: expect up to ~2 Cq underestimation — accept / annotate / redesign"; "Intron-less pseudogene GAPDHP1 amplifiable — no-RT control mandatory". Record: pack version, hits, rules. MCP: `qpcr_assay_preflight(assay_hash, pack_id)`.
- **Feasibility:** Ensembl data no restrictions, code Apache-2.0 (https://www.ensembl.org/info/about/legal/index.html), 55,000 req/h (https://github.com/Ensembl/ensembl-rest/wiki/Rate-Limits); NCBI public domain, 3 req/s (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/home/about/policies/); UCSC data free but isPcr/Blat need a licence (https://genome.ucsc.edu/license/) — avoid. Effort ≈3–5 weeks for human/mouse/rat packs. Hardest: laptop-speed genome-wide mismatch search (pre-index k-mers), 3'-mismatch scoring beyond published tables, pack size (few hundred MB/species).
- **Why qPilot:** Passport gives observed Tm/E to compare; robustness engine can exclude flagged genotypes; record pins pack version.
- **Risks / confidence:** Non-model species lack SNP data; commercial secret sequences only via context sequence; GPL contamination to avoid. **High** on need/data; **medium** on effort. Rank 2.

### D3. Lot-change gate: deterministic bridging study for research labs
- **Persona:** Lab manager receiving a resynthesised primer; PI cites MIQE 2.0 but nobody knows what "re-validate" means. Every re-order (monthly in an active lab).
- **Pain evidence:** MIQE 2.0 "revalidate assays when new batches of reagents, primers, or probes are introduced" (https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148); "Never work under the assumption that primers from different batches will work at the same efficiency" (https://www.aatbio.com/resources/faq-frequently-asked-questions/what-should-i-consider-when-i-set-up-a-pcr-efficiency-test-for-my-assay); CLSI EP26 practice, QC material missed 7.8% of real shifts (https://myadlm.org/cln/articles/2014/july/bench-matters ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0004563214520749).

MCP: `qpcr_lot_bridge(assay_hash, old_lot, new_lot)`.
- **Feasibility:** ~1–2 weeks on top of D1. Hardest: persuading users to spend 20 wells.
- **Why qPilot:** Per-well E, impact ranking, records, autonomy levels (agent proposes, human accepts).
- **Risks / confidence:** Lot-driven drift evidence is vendor/anecdotal, not peer-reviewed (Gap). **Medium**. Rank 3.

### D4. Melt-identity fingerprint and NTC-artefact model per assay (beyond "single peak")
- **Persona:** SYBR users on every plate; cores asked "why did this well fail?".
- **Pain evidence:** "a single peak at the correct Tm does not guarantee identification of the intended amplicon" (https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148); Bio-Rad: melt sensitivity "too limited to detect small fractions of nonspecific PCR products", so they sequenced every amplicon (https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6262.pdf); CASRAI: single peak "consistent with specificity, not proof of it" (https://www.casrai.org/guides/qpcr-melt-curve-analysis-peaks-primer-dimers).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Instrument software shows derivative peaks per plate; roadmap names "melt-peak checks" as a headline only; rdmlpython MCA gives peaks per well; a Ruijter-group melt manuscript exists (https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/ws/files/107164215/Ruiter_et_al_Melt_curve_resub.pdf).
- **Feature:** Per assay per instrument fingerprint of normalised −dF/dT (median curve, Tm, width, shoulder); per well Tm offset, width ratio, shape distance; classify matches / shifted / broad or extra peak / low-Tm only (dimer). NTC dimer modelled separately with Cq gap to lowest sample. Card: "3 wells deviate from IL6 fingerprint (ΔTm +1.4 °C, width +60%); excluding them changes group B mean by 0.3 ΔCq — exclude / keep / run a gel". D2's predicted Tm shown alongside. MCP: `qpcr_melt_identity(study)`.
- **Feasibility:** 2 weeks; melt data already parsed. Hardest: ramp-rate and dye effects (fingerprint per instrument/chemistry); avoiding over-exclusion.
- **Why qPilot:** Raw melt data + cross-run memory + robustness reruns + record; Cq-input tools can't do this.
- **Risks / confidence:** Probe assays have no melt data. **High** feasibility, **medium** on how often it changes conclusions. Rank 4.

## A. How this corner works

A multi-plate study passes through four representations of "which well holds what" — design (Excel template, "then print it", Sharpie on the printout; https://barricklab.org/twiki/bin/view/Lab/QPCR), pipetting (by hand or robot), instrument plate setup (import that "overwrites all existing settings"; https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0018200_QuantStDesgnAnalysisSW_v2_Desktop_UG.pdf), analysis layout — each re-typed, with its own naming rules and no shared identifier. At EPFL's core the user fills two Excel sheets ("DNA"/"PRIMER", ".xls, not .xlsx"), a Hamilton GUI in a Windows XP VMware VM builds the worklist, and "the robot outputs a .txt file" loaded into QuantStudio; a full 384-well assembly takes ~30 minutes, max 64 primer pairs × 192 samples, 10 µL (384) / 15 µL (96) reactions, 3 replicates recommended (https://www.epfl.ch/research/facilities/gene-expression-core-facility/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/qPCR-plate-setup-guide-with-Hamilton-MicroLab-Star-v5.03.pdf).

Three deterministic published design/correction methods exist: Hellemans 2007 (sample maximisation; three IRCs cut error increase from ~70% to ~40%; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1852402/), Ruijter 2015 (factor correction from all overlapping replicates; runs 2 and 5 differed ~6-fold; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4822202), Kitchen/Kubista/Tichopad 2010 (nested variance from a pilot; https://gene-quantification.de/bioinf-subpage3.html). Only qbase+ (commercial), qRAT (MIT; IPC only; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9297597/) and Factor-qPCR (closed) implement any; none checks a layout before pipetting. The only qPCR randomiser found is GPL-2 with 4 stars (https://github.com/crukci-bioinformatics/PlateLayout). Robots: Opentrons (one CSV runtime parameter, API 2.20; https://docs.opentrons.com/python-api/runtime-parameters/defining/), Tecan GWL, Hamilton CSV/Excel, PyLabRobot (MIT) abstracting STAR/Vantage/EVO/OT-2 (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyLabRobot/pylabrobot/main/README.md). Instruments accept flat text tables with different headers (Thermo TXT/CSV, CFX 30-column `.plrn` with 113-row structure, LC480 9-column TXT). LIMS: Benchling has barcoded plates/runs/result schemas; eLabFTW has experiments/items/extra_fields but no plate object. Position effects: outer cell-culture wells ~16–35% lower activity (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8024881/); qPCR-specific evidence thin. Costs: 20–60 min re-typing per plate (estimate); no instrument/robot/LIMS prices reachable; Opentrons claims only qualitative "hours" saved (https://opentrons.com/archives/resource/automating-pcr-prep-on-a-budget).

## C. Discoveries (note's ranking)

### D1. One plate-map object that compiles to the robot, the instrument and the analysis — with a round-trip check
- **Persona:** PhD student or core technician setting up a 2–8-plate SYBR/TaqMan study on QuantStudio/CFX/LightCycler, with or without Opentrons/Hamilton/Tecan, several times a month.
- **Pain evidence:** EPFL chain Excel → XP VM → `.txt` → QuantStudio (https://www.epfl.ch/research/facilities/gene-expression-core-facility/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/qPCR-plate-setup-guide-with-Hamilton-MicroLab-Star-v5.03.pdf); Thermo "Click OK to confirm that the plate setup is overwritten", "Each well can contain only one sample", files "checksum protected" (https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0018200_QuantStDesgnAnalysisSW_v2_Desktop_UG.pdf); names "must match ... exactly (including case)" (https://apps.thermofisher.com/apps/help/MAN0014303/GUID-6F7D8B38-680C-496A-968C-700658A8F05A.html). Cost 20–60 min re-typing per plate plus occasional study lost to a transposed row (estimate; no published rate).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Vendor editors (single instrument, no robot output); EPFL one-way `.txt`; Benchling input generators (commercial); wellmap (MIT) as a format with no writers (https://github.com/kalekundert/wellmap); GPL R tools.
- **Feature:** `qpilot plate design|export|verify`. In: design table (sample_id, group, batch factors, target_id, role, task {unknown, standard, ntc, nrt, pos, irc}, replicate n, quantity, reporter/quencher, format 96/384). Object: an RDML file with `pcrFormat`, one `react` per well (id = `number + letter*columns`; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RDML-consortium/rdmlpython/master/rdml.py), `sample/type`, `interRunCalibrator`, `quantity`, no `<data>`, plus sidecar `platemap.json` (source labware/well, volume, liquid class) and content hash. Writers: QuantStudio TXT/CSV with `* Block Type` headers and columns Well, Well Position, Sample Name, Task, Target Name, Reporter, Quencher; CFX per-fluorophore template and `.plrn` per Table 38 (https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/10000126764.pdf); LC480 9-column TXT (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jimrperkins/ReadqPCR/master/inst/exData/LC480_Example_SampleInfo.txt); Opentrons CSV + protocol skeleton; Tecan GWL (https://www.tecan.com/knowledge-portal/how-to-use-worklist-in-fluentcontrol); generic Hamilton CSV; EPFL Excel pair. Verify: `qpilot plate verify --design platemap.rdml --instrument export.txt` → well-by-well diff; mismatches become a card ("differs in 3 wells; trust design / trust instrument / exclude wells"). Record: design hash, emitted-file hashes, diff, resolution; plate barcode into CFX RunID / QuantStudio Experiment Name. MCP: `qpcr_plate_design`, `qpcr_plate_export(target=...)`, `qpcr_plate_verify`.
- **Feasibility:** All formats documented; RDML has the fields; no libraries beyond stdlib + rdmlpython. Effort ≈1–2 weeks (QuantStudio + LC480 + Opentrons CSV + Tecan GWL + verifier); CFX `.plrn` ≈3 days once vendor template CSVs from an installed copy are available. Hardest: one validation layer for vendor naming constraints; undocumented LC480 import variant.
- **Why qPilot:** Record is already RDML; design becomes the first hash-chain entry; layout conflicts as a robustness axis; agent prepares, human approves.
- **Risks / confidence:** Vendor format drift (golden-file tests); `.plrn` must be byte-exact. **High** for QuantStudio/Opentrons/Tecan writers and verifier; **medium** for CFX `.plrn` and LC480. Rank 1.

### D2. Design-time "can this study be analysed?" check (plate-design assistant made concrete)
- **Persona:** Scientist without a biostatistician planning 3+ plates; core manager reviewing a client's design before booking the robot.
- **Pain evidence:** Forum: "I would have some doubt in the convenience in combining data from different plates" (https://www.researchgate.net/post/What-is-the-best-way-to-combine-biological-replicates-from-different-qPCR-plates); qRAT: "it is necessary to include one common sample in all runs/plates" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9297597/); Hellemans 70%→40% with 3 IRCs (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1852402/); Ruijter "when more than 2 rounds of substitution are required, the design of the experiment is clearly incomplete" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4822202); Kitchen: replicate numbers "often uninformed and unreliable" (https://gene-quantification.de/bioinf-subpage3.html).
- **What exists / why it fails:** qbase+ warns only at analysis time (paid); qRAT requires IPCs but doesn't design; PlateLayout randomises without qPCR controls (GPL-2); powerNest (2010) unmaintained; vendor editors have no notion of calibrators or overlap.
- **Feature:** Allocation: sample maximisation first; when overflow, either ≥3 IRC samples on every plate with the same set (Hellemans) or balanced complete overlap for factor correction (Ruijter), user's choice. Controls: NTC per target per plate, NRT per sample set per plate (MIQE 2.0; https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), off corners. Randomisation: pseudo-block reimplemented from PlateLayout's description (diagonal traversal, row/column block shuffles, Σ1/distance score, many iterations), technical replicates non-adjacent (tidyqpcr idea), batch factors balanced across plates. Analysability facts computed before pipetting: overlap-graph connectivity and substitution rounds; IRC count and sameness; NTC/NRT presence; `qpilot plan` power fed by pilot variance if a previous study exists; edge-well exposure. Design-time card ranks fixes ("add 2 IRCs to plates 2–4" / "move target X to plate 1" / "run one more plate") with per-gene consequence. MCP: `qpcr_plate_design`.
- **Feasibility:** Pure combinatorics + published formulas; no datasets needed. Effort ≈2 weeks incl. synthetic tests. Hardest: default when users refuse IRCs and overlap — say plainly plates won't be comparable (qbase "assumes no inter-run difference").
- **Why qPilot:** `qpilot plan` and cards exist; impact ranking avoids nagging; design drives D3's correction choice.
- **Risks / confidence:** Over-constraining (keep "check my layout" mode); edge evidence thin → soft rule. **High** for analysability checks and allocation; **medium** for randomisation benefit. Rank 2.

### D3. Inter-plate correction as a robustness axis: Ruijter factor correction and Hellemans IRC side by side, on N0 from raw curves
- **Persona:** Anyone with ≥2 plates for the same target; reviewer asking "how did you combine plates?".
- **Pain evidence:** ~6-fold run difference and critique of calibrators (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4822202); "some methods are erroneously based on dividing the Cq values on a plate by a factor per plate" (https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/11/6/496, snippet); qRAT scales Cq not N0 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9297597/); Factor-qPCR exists only as closed Excel/RDML program (https://www.econferences.de/removal-of-between-plate-variation-in-qpcr-with-factor-correction-completion-of-the-analysis-pipeline-supported-by-rdml/).
- **What exists / why it fails:** qbase+ (paid, IRC); qRAT (IPC on Cq); Factor-qPCR (closed, 2015); roadmap's "mixed model with plate effects" is statistical, not the published deterministic corrections.
- **Feature:** Validated module `plate_correction` with three options in the robustness grid: none; IRC (geometric mean of IRC NRQs per gene per run, SE propagated, refuse if IRC set differs between runs; Hellemans); factor correction (N0 = Nq/E^Cq with qPilot's per-well E; pairwise geometric-mean ratio matrix over shared conditions; nested substitution; plate factor = column geometric mean; divide; convert back with threshold 1 and reaction-weighted mean E; Ruijter Eqs. 3–5, 8, 12). Facts: plate factors with CIs, substitution rounds, calibrator stability M, agreement count across options. Card only when options disagree. Agent: `qpcr_robustness` gains the axis automatically.
- **Feasibility:** Equations fully specified in the open-access paper; no new dependency. Effort ≈1 week + acceptance test on Ruijter's chicken-heart dataset (hfrc.nl availability unverified) or synthetic data. Hardest: defining "condition" overlap robustly from user metadata.
- **Why qPilot:** Per-well E from raw curves is what factor correction needs; D2 guarantees computability; everything lands in the record.
- **Risks / confidence:** Users may expect a mixed model — keep it as a later axis. **High**. Rank 3.

### D4. Opentrons / PyLabRobot closed loop: emit, push, run (human start), pull as-pipetted log into the record
- **Persona:** Lab-automation engineer or automation-curious lab with OT-2/Flex (or Hamilton STAR/Vantage, Tecan EVO via PyLabRobot), weekly qPCR plates.
- **Pain evidence:** Opentrons ships CSV-driven qRT-PCR prep protocols but the plate map is hand-written (https://opentrons.com/applications/rt-pcr-protocol); "Only one CSV parameter is allowed per protocol", "CSV parameters have no default value" (https://docs.opentrons.com/python-api/runtime-parameters/defining/); parse "blocks protocol analysis until a CSV is selected" (https://docs.opentrons.com/python-api/runtime-parameters/using-values/); PyLabRobot is MIT (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyLabRobot/pylabrobot/main/LICENSE); robotools, only Python GWL writer found, is AGPL (https://github.com/JuBiotech/robotools).
- **What exists / why it fails:** Opentrons Protocol Designer/Library (no instrument file); PyLabRobot (no qPCR layer); vendor VENUS/FluentControl; Benchling automation runs (commercial).
- **Feature:** `qpilot robot opentrons` writes (a) protocol `.py` (API ≥2.20) with one `add_csv_file` parameter, `run()` reading source/destination/volume for super-mastermix and template steps; (b) the CSV from D1. Optional `--push` uploads protocol + CSV over HTTP API and creates the run but never sends "play" — technician starts after deck check (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Opentrons/opentrons/edge/robot-server/README.rst). `qpilot robot pull` downloads the run record (commands, timestamps, errors), stores hash and per-well "as-pipetted" table; aborted/skipped commands become empty-well facts. `qpilot robot pylabrobot` emits a PyLabRobot script against a user JSON deck; `qpilot robot tecan` writes GWL directly. MCP: `qpcr_robot_export`, `qpcr_robot_pull`; autonomy policy forbids `play`.
- **Feasibility:** Opentrons protocol docs complete; HTTP paths (`POST /protocols`, `/dataFiles`, `/runs`, `/runs/{id}/actions`) are from the author's own knowledge and must be confirmed on a robot's `/redoc` (https://socket.dev/pypi/package/labmcp-opentrons); PyLabRobot Flex support unverified. Effort: Opentrons ≈1–2 weeks with `opentrons_simulate`, Tecan GWL ≈2 days, PyLabRobot ≈2–3 weeks + hardware. Hardest: labware definitions and liquid classes per site; testing without hardware.
- **Why qPilot:** Closed-loop actor contract exists; robot run is an actor whose output is hashed; same object feeds instrument and analysis.
- **Risks / confidence:** Hardware diversity; liability (dry-run mode, volume/tip totals on a card, no auto-start); few academic qPilot users own robots. **Medium-high** Opentrons, **medium** PyLabRobot. Rank 4.

### D5. ELN/LIMS record push with exact objects: `.eln` RO-Crate for eLabFTW/RSpace, and Benchling plate-keyed results
- **Persona:** Labs on eLabFTW (European academia) or Benchling (industry, some cores); core managers needing a client report and chain of custody.
- **Pain evidence:** eLabFTW API has experiments, items, uploads, links, extra_fields, bookings, `.eln`/CSV import but no plate object (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/elabftw/elabftw/master/apidoc/v2/openapi.yaml); Benchling has barcoded plates/wells, runs, example qPCR result schema with `ct`, `ct_mean`, `quantity`, `batch_create` ≤25, `bulk_create` ≤60,000 (https://docs.benchling.com/docs/example-creating-results ; https://benchling.com/sdk-docs/1.11.0/benchling_sdk.benchling.html); Benchling qPCR connectors go through Allotrope ASM whose schemas are CC BY-NC (https://gitlab.com/allotrope-public/asm); cores keep paper logbook and e-mail.
- **What exists / why it fails:** Benchling file connectors (Cq-level, commercial); allotropy (MIT) for ASM conversion (https://github.com/Benchling-Open-Source/allotropy); "attach a PDF to the ELN".
- **Feature:** `.eln` export of the Analysis Record (RDML + decision log + versions + checksums) as RO-Crate zip per ELN Consortium spec (MIT; https://github.com/TheELNConsortium/TheELNFileFormat) for native import into eLabFTW, RSpace, Kadi4Mat, SampleDB. eLabFTW push via MIT client (https://github.com/elabftw/elabapi-python): `POST /experiments`, `POST /experiments/{id}/uploads`, `PATCH` with `metadatamerge` for decision-log/Study Confidence `extra_fields`, `items_links` to primer/sample items, optional `/events` booking, `canread`/`canwrite`. Benchling push: `bulk_get(barcodes=[plate barcode])`, then `POST /api/v3/result` per well against a user-defined schema (`sample_id`, `ct`, `n0`, `efficiency`, `study_confidence`), record as blob, or `assay_run` with output processor. Core report: client PDF/XLSX with as-run layout, per-plate QC, record hash; sample names replaceable by client `custom_id` (policy flag, inferred need).
- **Feasibility:** eLabFTW: free docker for tests, ≈1 week; `.eln` ≈3 days (reuses RO-Crate stretch item); Benchling ≈1–2 weeks, needs tenant + schema, SDK licence unverified (use raw HTTP). Do not vendor ASM schemas.
- **Why qPilot:** Record already hash-chained; ELN entry is the natural sign-off; MCP creates it as an approvals-inbox action.
- **Risks / confidence:** ELN diversity; AGPL of eLabFTW irrelevant for HTTP client but must be explained; Benchling test access. **High** for `.eln` + eLabFTW, **medium** Benchling. Rank 5.

### D6. Three-way "as designed / as pipetted / as run" reconciliation with well-level provenance
- **Persona:** Core managers, automating labs, the reviewer-critic agent.
- **Pain evidence:** EPFL: "Inspect the remaining volumes in the microtubes to detect putative inaccurate pipetting" and fill a logbook (https://www.epfl.ch/research/facilities/gene-expression-core-facility/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/qPCR-plate-setup-guide-with-Hamilton-MicroLab-Star-v5.03.pdf); CFX RunID "Typically populated with the plate barcode" (https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/2021-12/10000132623.pdf); MIQE 2.0 repeatability/reproducibility items.
- **What exists / why it fails:** Nothing joins robot log, instrument plate setup and analysis; Benchling automation runs closest (commercial).
- **Feature:** `provenance` block per well in the record schema: designed (D1), pipetted (D4 robot row or "manual"), run (instrument export/RDML react), analysed (fact ids). `records provenance` prints the chain and stage diffs; reviewer-critic can cite "well B7: designed NTC, instrument says UNKNOWN, excluded by decision #12". Barcodes carried through CFX RunID / QuantStudio Experiment Name.
- **Feasibility:** Schema and glue once D1/D4 exist; ≈1 week. Hardest: hand-typed layouts — chain starts at the instrument export.
- **Why qPilot:** Hash-chained record, actor model, MCP.
- **Risks / confidence:** Perceived as regulatory overkill unless shown only on disagreement. **Medium-high**. Rank 6.

**Smaller bets:** single task/control vocabulary with vendor mappings (UNKNOWN/NTC vs Unknown/NTC/NRT vs Target Negative vs STND/NTC/UNKN) — "a two-hour job"; row/column position-effect fact from replicate N0 (reported, never corrected); emit EPFL "DNA"/"PRIMER" Excel pair.
