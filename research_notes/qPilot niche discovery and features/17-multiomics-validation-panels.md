# 17 — qPCR in the multi-omics era: validation of sequencing results, reference genes from RNA-seq, concordance, hand-offs, and high-throughput panels

Researcher notes for qPilot (two-developer open-source qPCR analysis tool). Date: 17 October 2026.

Method note. The shared web-search budget for this run was exhausted before my first query, so every fact below comes from fetching primary pages directly by URL (journal full texts via PMC and Europe PMC, package pages on Bioconductor/CRAN/PyPI/GitHub, vendor product pages, HGNC, GEO via NCBI E-utilities). Community forums (Biostars, Reddit, ResearchGate) returned 403 or could not be queried, so forum evidence is explicitly absent and listed as a gap. Numbers are quoted as the sources state them; where a source is internally inconsistent I say so. Estimates of my own are labelled "estimate".

Terms used throughout (for the two developers): RNA-seq = sequencing-based measurement of all transcripts; a DEG table = the bioinformatician's output listing differentially expressed genes with a log2 fold change (log2FC) and an adjusted p-value; TPM/RPKM/FPKM = per-gene abundance units from RNA-seq; Cq (also Ct, Cp, Crt) = the PCR cycle at which fluorescence crosses a threshold, the raw qPCR readout; reference gene (housekeeping gene, endogenous control) = a gene assumed stable, used to normalise qPCR; geNorm M/V = stability measure and pairwise-variation statistic used to pick reference genes; ΔΔCq = the common relative-quantification formula; HT-qPCR = high-throughput qPCR on microfluidic chips (Biomark) or nanolitre through-hole plates (OpenArray) or pre-loaded 384-well cards (TaqMan Array Card); isoform = alternative transcript of the same gene; assay = a primer pair (and optionally a probe) that amplifies one target.

## 1. How this corner of the niche works

### 1.1 The people

- The **bench scientist** (PhD student or postdoc) who receives a DEG table from a sequencing core or a collaborator and has to "validate" it by qPCR before a paper or thesis chapter. They choose a handful of genes, choose reference genes, find primers, run one or two plates, compute ΔΔCq, draw a bar chart next to the RNA-seq bar chart, and write "RNA-seq results were confirmed by qRT-PCR". Everaert et al. describe RT-qPCR as "still considered the method of choice for validation of gene expression data" [S2].
- The **bioinformatician / core analyst** who delivers DEG tables, typically as spreadsheets. In 2014–2020, 32,841 of 166,139 open-access genomics articles had supplementary Excel files, and 11,117 of those contained gene-symbol lists [S9]; 30.9% of those articles contained gene-name errors introduced by spreadsheet auto-conversion [S9]. Cores advertise "raw data through to figure generation and publication" [S91]; I found no public statement of what a core delivers for the downstream qPCR step (gap).
- The **core-facility manager** running HT-qPCR (Standard BioTools Biomark X9 with 48.48/96.96/192.24 IFCs, Thermo QuantStudio 12K Flex with OpenArray and TaqMan Array Card blocks) or NanoString nCounter, for whom a "validation panel" is a service: 48–96 genes × 48–192 samples per chip [S46], up to four 3,072-reaction OpenArray plates per run [S48].
- The **PI and the reviewer** who decide whether validation is required. Coenye (2021, editorial in *Biofilm*): "Authors, reviewers and editors often struggle with this question" and argues RNA-seq is "robust enough to not always require validation by qPCR and/or other approaches" [S1]. I found no journal policy that requires qPCR validation of RNA-seq (gap; see Section 2, KQ1).

### 1.2 The workflow, step by step, as practised

1. **Sequencing and differential analysis.** The core returns per-gene results (Ensembl ID or symbol, log2FC, adjusted p, mean expression). Pipelines differ: in Rajkumar et al.'s mouse experiment, edgeR and Cuffdiff2 called 199 DEGs while DESeq2 and TSPM called 8 [S5].
2. **Decide whether to validate and what.** No numeric norm exists. Panel sizes in the papers I could read: 7 genes (Rachinger et al., SOX family and YAP/TAZ) [S4]; 18 genes × 8 chemicals (Wang et al.) [S6]; 115 genes with 16 biological replicates (Rajkumar et al.) [S5]; 843 TaqMan genes and 20,801 PrimePCR assays (SEQC consortium) [S3]; 18,080 genes (Everaert et al.) [S2]. Everaert note that earlier benchmarks used RT-qPCR for "just a few hundred genes" [S2].
3. **Choose reference genes.** Mostly by habit: in a 2015 systematic review of 128 vertebrate gene-expression papers, studies used a mean of 1.23 reference genes, only 13% used more than one, 93 of 128 (over 72%) used GAPDH, ACTB or 18S rRNA as the only normaliser, and only 15% (19/128) reported having tested a panel for stability [S35].
4. **Find assays.** PrimerBank (306,800 primers, "most known human and mouse genes", search by GenBank accession, NCBI Gene ID, PrimerBank ID or gene symbol, no batch search, last modified 2006) [S59]; GETPrime (gene-specific and transcript-specific primers for 13 species, Ensembl release 81, 1,175,874 primer pairs, REST API and batch download, up to 10 identifiers per web search) [S61][S63]; Bio-Rad PrimePCR ("Every PCR primer pair has been experimentally validated", sequences not disclosed, lookup tool and bulk search) [S64]; Thermo TaqMan ("over 2.8 million assays available across more than 30 species", designed to cover "the maximum number of transcript variants") [S50]. Primer design software (Primer3) is GPL and out of qPilot's licence scope (brief).
5. **Run.** 96/384-well plates for a handful of genes; HT panels for 48–96 genes. Biomark requires specific target pre-amplification (STA) before loading [S44]; OpenArray is 3,072 reactions per plate with ΔΔCt analysis in ExpressionSuite v1.3 [S48][S49].
6. **Analyse and compare.** ΔΔCq against the chosen references, then usually a Pearson correlation between qPCR and RNA-seq log2FC or a side-by-side bar chart. Giavarina's method-comparison tutorial: "The correlation between methods is always misleading and should not be used for assessing the method comparability" [S39].
7. **Report.** MIQE 2.0 (2025) now says "quantification cycle (Cq) values should be converted into efficiency-corrected target quantities" and reported with prediction intervals [S84]; Coenye recommends following MIQE and MINSEQE [S1].

### 1.3 Standards, formats and software that matter here

- **RDML** (the qPCR data standard qPilot already uses): the consortium's instrument list names CFX Maestro (1.1), StepOne, QuantStudio 12K Flex, ViiA 7, LightCycler 96 (1.1), Rotor-Gene Q (1.1) and MyGo (1.2); Fluidigm/Standard BioTools Biomark, OpenArray, TaqMan Array Cards and NanoString do not appear [S89]. The software list names Clarida's RDML viewer (1.3), Primer3Plus (1.3), RDMLdb (1.2), RTPrimerDB, LinRegPCR (1.2), the RDML R package (1.1) and a Mathematica package (1.2) [S90]. Pabinger et al.'s survey of qPCR tools: "most tools use their own file format", "so far only three applications are able to work with RDML data", and commercial systems are "black boxes" [S43].
- **Biomark (Standard BioTools, formerly Fluidigm).** IFC formats: Flex Six (six partitions, up to 144 reactions each, 12 samples × 12 assays), 48.48 (up to 2,304 reactions), 96.96 (up to 9,216 reactions, 96 samples × 96 assays), 192.24/24.192 (up to 4,608 reactions) [S46]. Analysis is in "Standard BioTools Real-Time PCR Analysis Software" for Windows 10 or 11 (user guide FLDM-01051) [S46][S47]. The CSV export, as read by Bioconductor's HTqPCR, has an 11-line header plus a column-header line and one row per reaction chamber (2,304 rows for a 48.48), with columns the package maps as `ID` (chamber position), `Name.1` (assay), `Value` (Ct) and `Call` ("Pass" → OK, "Fail" → Undetermined) [S41][S42].
- **OpenArray / QuantStudio 12K Flex (Thermo).** Interchangeable blocks: OpenArray plate, TaqMan Array card, 384-well, 96-well; "up to four 3,072-reaction QuantStudio 12K Flex OpenArray plates" per run; "up to 110,000 data points or more in an 8-hour day" [S48]. The OpenArray export columns HTqPCR expects are `ThroughHole.Outlier` (quality flag), `Assay.Assay.ID`, `Assay.Assay.Type`, `ThroughHole.Address`, `ThroughHole.Ct` [S42]. ExpressionSuite v1.3 (release notes dated 2019-12-02) does ΔΔCt, "endogenous control selection", box plots and signal-correlation plots [S49]. The QC metrics "AmpScore" and "Cq Confidence" that practitioners use for OpenArray/QuantStudio could not be verified from a vendor document in this run (Thermo's manual host returned 403) — gap.
- **NanoString nCounter** (adjacent, hybridisation-based, no amplification): "a direct digital readout of up to 800 mRNA targets" [S58]. Raw data are RCC files; the open QC tool NACHO (GPL-3, v2.0.8 published 2026-10-01) applies defaults of binding density 0.1–2.25 (MAX/FLEX) or 0.1–1.8 (SPRINT), field-of-view counted <75% flagged, positive-control linearity <0.95 flagged, limit of detection <2 flagged, positive-factor outside 0.25–4, housekeeping factor outside 0.091–11 [S51][S52]. Normalisation in the vendor's nSolver uses positive-control and housekeeping scaling; Bhattacharya et al. found "nSolver-normalized data was prone to residual unwanted technical variation when there were known technical biases" and that methods "were sensitive to the quality and the set of housekeeping genes" [S56].
- **HTqPCR** (Bioconductor, Artistic-2.0, v1.66.0, in Bioconductor 17 years, maintainer now Matthew McCall) remains the only general open tool that reads TLDA/SDS, OpenArray, BioMark, CFX, LightCycler and plain exports; it categorises Cq values as "OK", "Undetermined" (above `Ct.max`, default 35) or "Unreliable" (below `Ct.min` 10, failed flag, or outside a 90% replicate interval), assigns 40 to undetermined wells (`na.value`), and offers quantile, rank-invariant, ΔCt and geometric-mean normalisation plus t-test, Mann–Whitney and limma [S40][S41][S42].
- **Gene nomenclature.** HGNC data carry "No restrictions ... on access to, or use of, the data", are downloadable as TSV/JSON twice weekly, and have a REST API (`rest.genenames.org/fetch/symbol/…`, `search/prev_symbol/…`, `search/alias_symbol/…`, 10 requests/s) [S14][S15][S16]. HGNC renamed all approved symbols that spreadsheets auto-converted (decision taken in 2019), but "the original symbols still appear in the previous symbol field", so importing that field can reintroduce the error [S12].

### 1.4 Money and time

I could not retrieve assay price lists (PrimePCR's price list is a linked PDF; TaqMan pricing not fetched) — gap. Time: Biomark is advertised at "PCR data in as little as two hours" and "up to 46,080 datapoints per eight-hour shift" [S46]; OpenArray "as little as 20 minutes of hands-on time per run" [S48]. For a classic plate-based validation (10 genes, 2–3 references, 12–24 samples) the bench time is of the order of one to two days including cDNA synthesis and plate set-up — estimate, not sourced.

### 1.5 Where decisions are made (and where qPilot's Decision Cards fit)

Which genes to validate; which reference genes; whether a reference gene is itself regulated by the treatment (Pombo et al.: GAPDH was down-regulated during tomato immune response and "normalizing with it falsely inflated ARD2 expression" [S27]); the fold-change and direction thresholds that define "confirmed"; what to do with a discordant "story gene"; for HT panels, pre-amplification pass/fail and chip-level normalisation; for nCounter, which housekeeping set and how many unwanted-variation factors to remove [S56].

### 1.6 What "good" looks like

Pre-specified concordance definitions (Everaert: concordant = both methods agree on differential status at |log fold change| > 1; severely non-concordant = ΔFC > 2 or opposite direction [S2]); stratification by expression level (Wang et al.: for above-median genes, qPCR verified 94% of RNA-seq DEGs; below-median, 8 of 9 for RNA-seq but 1 of 6 for microarray [S6]); reference genes chosen from a candidate panel of more than ten, validated in the study's own samples (Chapman & Waldenström recommend "more than 10 candidates, preferably more than 20" [S35]); geNorm pairwise variation V < 0.15 as the stopping rule for the number of references [S36][S38]; efficiency-corrected quantities with prediction intervals per MIQE 2.0 [S84]; Bland–Altman bias and limits of agreement with limits "defined a priori" [S39].

### 1.7 What goes wrong (headline failure modes; details and frequencies in Section 3)

Excel corrupts gene lists (19.6% of papers with Excel gene lists in 2005–2015 [S8]; 30.9% in 2014–2020 [S9]; 19.5% of articles with gene lists in the May 2026 automated screen [S11]); a habitual reference gene is regulated by the treatment [S27][S38]; the validated gene is small, low-expressed or has few exons — the very genes most likely to be discordant [S2]; the assay and the RNA-seq signal come from different isoforms or an intron-located probe [S7]; a transcript is lost during library preparation and the qPCR on the library confirms the loss (SOX21) [S4]; stability tools rank candidates differently (geNorm ranking changed in 71% of cases depending on efficiency correction [S36]; geNorm favoured a co-regulated pair with CVs of 59% and 57% [S37]); pre-amplification beyond 18 cycles fails (57% success at 21 cycles, 46% at 24) [S44]; HT formats are proprietary and the only open reader is a 2009-era R package [S40][S43].

### 1.8 Key papers, sites and communities

Everaert 2017 (whole-transcriptome RT-qPCR benchmark) [S2]; SEQC 2014 [S3]; Coenye 2021 (the "is validation necessary?" editorial) [S1]; Chapman & Waldenström 2015 (reference-gene habit) [S35]; HRT Atlas (RNA-seq-mined housekeeping transcripts) [S18][S19]; Joshi 2022 and Wright Muelas 2019 (Gini coefficient) [S21][S33]; Hruz 2011 RefGenes [S24]; Ziemann 2016 and Abeysooriya 2021 plus the live dashboard (gene-name errors) [S8][S9][S11]; HGNC [S12–S16]; HTqPCR [S40–S42]; Korenková 2015 (pre-amplification) [S44]; NACHO and Bhattacharya 2021 (nCounter) [S51][S56]; gene-quantification.de reference-gene page [S65]; RDML [S89][S90]; GEO MAQC/SEQC qPCR sets [S72–S76].

## 2. Key questions answered

### KQ1. Prevalence: how often is RNA-seq validated by qPCR, what do reviewers ask, "ritual" critiques vs evidence it catches errors, typical panel sizes and sample sizes

#### Takeaway
No reliable published figure for the fraction of RNA-seq papers that include qPCR validation was found; the best-argued critique (Coenye 2021) says RNA-seq is usually robust enough that validation is optional, while benchmark data show 15–19% of genes are "non-concordant" between RNA-seq and qPCR but only about 1.8% severely so, concentrated in small, low-expressed genes — which is also where papers that found real problems (Rachinger 2021, Wang 2014) found them.

#### Cited Findings
- Coenye (editorial, *Biofilm* 2021): RNA-seq "robust enough to not always require validation by qPCR and/or other approaches"; "Authors, reviewers and editors often struggle with this question"; validating a random subset gives no guarantee ("obtaining concordant results for a random selection of genes is no guarantee" that other genes are right); validation is appropriate when a whole story rests on a few low-expressed genes or small differences; no survey of papers and no gene count given — [S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7823214/)
- Everaert et al. 2017: RT-qPCR data "for 18080 protein-coding genes" on MAQC A and B reference RNAs using Bio-Rad PrimePCR assays; non-concordant genes "ranged from 15.1% (Tophat-HTSeq) to 19.4% (Salmon)"; over 66% of non-concordant genes have ΔFC < 1 and 93% ΔFC < 2; severely non-concordant genes (ΔFC > 2 or opposite direction) 1.8% on average; discordant genes "were typically smaller, had fewer exons, and were lower expressed"; expression R² 0.798–0.845 and fold-change R² 0.927–0.934 across five workflows; RT-qPCR "still considered the method of choice for validation" — [S2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5431503/)
- SEQC 2014: 843 TaqMan assays (from MAQC-I) on samples A and B; 20,801 PrimePCR measurements on A, B, C, D with "at least 10 qPCR reactions per assay"; Spearman correlation with TaqMan 0.83 for RNA-seq vs 0.79 for microarrays; "Genes with large differential expression performed best" in titration consistency; for TaqMan vs PrimePCR, "Expression estimates vary considerably for individual genes, with some genes showing high expression in one platform but are not detected at all by the other"; all data under GEO GSE47792 — [S3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321899/)
- Wang et al. 2014 (rat liver, 27 chemicals): 18 cancer-related genes tested by TaqMan across 8 chemicals (65 chemical–gene pairs); qPCR verification 90% for RNA-seq vs 76% for microarray; above-median genes verified 94% (RNA-seq) and 88% (array); below-median 89% (8 of 9) vs 17% (1 of 6); fold-change R² with qPCR 0.97 (RNA-seq) vs 0.85 (array) with microarray ratio compression; DEG concordance between platforms 75% for above-median genes but 20–40% below median — [S6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4243706/)
- Rajkumar et al. 2015 (mouse Brd1+/−): 115 genes validated by high-throughput qPCR in 16 biological replicates (8 vs 8), all 8 DESeq2/TSPM DEGs plus 107 random from the 191 other DEGs; 7 reference genes (not named in main text); 60 replicated, 55 failed; edgeR sensitivity 76.67%, PPV 90.20%; Cuffdiff2 PPV 39.24% and 87% of false positives; DESeq2 sensitivity 1.67% with PPV 100%; Spearman of fold changes with qPCR 0.45–0.54; RMSD from qPCR log fold change 1.18 (DESeq2) to 2.50 (TSPM); validation criterion = same direction and qPCR fold change > 1.25 or < 0.80 — [S5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12864-015-1767-y)
- Rachinger et al. 2021: 7 genes by qRT-PCR with GAPDH as normaliser in melanoma lines; SOX21, SOX3 and SOX11 detected by microarray and qRT-PCR on cDNA but not by RNA-seq nor by qRT-PCR on the sequencing library, "indicating that SOX21 was lost during the sample preparation for RNA-seq"; recommends "validating the results of transcriptome analyses by using at least one additional method" — [S4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8430810/)
- Zhao et al. 2014 (activated T cells, RNA-seq vs Affymetrix): no qPCR validation performed; discordances traced to probe-set/isoform issues (two RORC probe sets could not distinguish RORγt from RORγ; a PRDM2 probe hitting only minor isoforms gave a 6.3-fold decrease; a PDE6D probe targeting an intron gave 5.7-fold up on array vs insignificant down by RNA-seq); about 30% of RNA-seq values were zero or below the 0.047 RPKM floor; within-group variance higher for low-expressed genes — [S7](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0078644)
- Bustin & Nolan 2017 (abstract): "the majority of published RT-qPCR data are likely to represent technical noise" — [S83](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28796277)
- Fang & Cui 2011 (abstract): for RNA-seq, "the relevant experimental design issues have rarely been discussed" — [S85](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21498551)
- MIQE 2.0 (2025, abstract): "quantification cycle (Cq) values should be converted into efficiency-corrected target quantities" reported with prediction intervals — [S84](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40272429)
- Udvardi, Czechowski & Scheible 2008, "Eleven golden rules of quantitative RT-PCR", a letter in *The Plant Cell* (record only; body not retrievable) — [S87](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/18664613)

#### Inferences
- Validation is a judgment call, not a rule: the strongest critique and the strongest "it caught something" cases agree that risk concentrates in low-expressed, short, few-exon genes and in sample-preparation artefacts. A tool that ranks a DEG list by *discordance risk* (expression quartile, gene length, exon count, multi-mapping fraction) would turn an argument with reviewers into a documented, evidence-based selection.
- The expected non-concordance rate under a good workflow (about 15–19%, 1.8% severe) is itself a usable prior: a validation panel can be sized to detect a materially worse rate, and a concordance report can state whether the observed rate is within the published benchmark range rather than reporting a bare correlation.
- Panel sizes range from 7 to 115 genes in the papers I could read, so any planner must handle both a two-plate manual study and a 96-assay chip.

#### Gaps
- No systematic count of RNA-seq papers with/without qPCR validation; no journal author-guideline text requiring it; no forum threads on reviewer demands (Biostars/Reddit unreachable in this run).
- Typical panel size: no survey; only the per-paper counts above.
- Fang & Cui 2011 and Udvardi 2008 full texts could not be fetched (not in PMC; publisher sites blocked).

### KQ2. Reference-gene selection from RNA-seq: methods, tools, licences, and evidence that data-derived references beat classic ones

#### Takeaway
Reference genes are still chosen by habit (87% single gene; 72% GAPDH/ACTB/18S), while every RNA-seq- or array-mined approach I could read (HRT Atlas, RefGenes, de Jonge, Pombo, Yim, Czechowski) found candidates more stable than GAPDH/ACTB in qPCR follow-up, with one counter-example (pig liver). The metrics are simple and deterministic (SD of log2 expression, max/mean fold, CV, Gini coefficient), but the published tools are either web-only, MATLAB/Ruby, academic-only, abandoned or GPL; nothing computes them from the lab's own count matrix inside a qPCR tool.

#### Cited Findings
- Chapman & Waldenström 2015: 128 vertebrate studies since 2010 used 1.23 reference genes on average; 13% (17/128) more than one; 93 of 128 used GAPDH, ACTB or 18S as the only normaliser; 15% (19/128) showed a panel had been tested; in 436 studies that did test panels, GAPDH was in 89% and ACTB in 86% of panels, each "less likely to be selected as most stable when more RGs were screened"; mean 9.53 candidates tested; recommend "more than 10 candidates, preferably more than 20"; authors describe a "fundamental disconnect" and attribute GAPDH/ACTB use to habit — [S35](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0141853)
- HRT Atlas (Hounkpe et al. 2021): 11,281 GTEx v7 samples (52 tissues/cell types) plus 2,111 ARCHS4 samples (human) and 507 mouse samples (14 tissues); housekeeping criteria on TMM-normalised RPKM: at least one protein-coding transcript above 1 RPKM in every tissue, SD of log2 RPKM below 1, (max log2 RPKM)/(mean log2 RPKM) below 2, RefSeq/CCDS transcripts only; candidate reference transcripts: SD log2 RPKM < 0.5 and mean RPKM ≥ 30 (filter can be disabled), isoforms of genes with pseudogenes excluded, ranked by a "Score Product" (geometric mean of ranks for mean, SD and fold change); 2,176 human HK genes / 2,158 HK transcripts; PBMC case study (7 samples): ENST00000253039 (EIF2S3) and ENST00000335508 (SF3B1) most stable, GAPDH ranked third and "not a suitable reference"; 31 human and 17 mouse primer pairs designed (75–200 bp amplicons, at least one pair spanning two exon junctions) and validated per MIQE; CSV/PDF downloads; article CC BY 4.0, database/code licence not stated in the article; about 1,801 genes from earlier lists absent, "about 98% of those failed the non-zero expression criterion" — [S18](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7778946/)
- HRT Atlas GitHub: MIT licence file; R/Shiny app with Dockerfile; 9 stars, 20 commits — [S19](https://github.com/bidossessih/HRT_Atlas); the web site itself is JavaScript-rendered and returned no text to a plain fetch — [S20](https://housekeeping.unicamp.br/)
- Joshi et al. 2022 "What are housekeeping genes?": Gini coefficient (GC) as the only metric, rank cut-offs (3,688 lowest-GC genes per dataset); coverage of the Eisenberg & Levanon 3,688-gene list 81.4% (HPA+GTEx) and 69.8% (NCI-60); GAPDH "has high Gini coefficient in most of the datasets"; housekeeping genes' median expression 22.1 TPM (HPA) and 24.7 TPM (GTEx), "housekeeping genes do not strictly have high expression values"; code in MATLAB (github.com/LewisLabUCSD/gene-gini-matlab); article CC BY 4.0 — [S21](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010295); repository is MATLAB, no licence stated, 0 stars — [S22](https://github.com/LewisLabUCSD/gene-gini-matlab)
- Wright Muelas et al. 2019: Gini coefficient on TPM across CCLE (934 lines), Klijn (622), HPA cell lines (56), GTEx ("53 different, normal human tissues") and others; filter GC ≤ 0.2 for cell lines (0.15 left "no or very few genes"); GAPDH GC 0.33, RSD 72.4%, "44 fold" variation in one dataset; ACTB GC 0.29; HNRNPK and PCBP1 stable in cell lines and by RT-qPCR; CHMP2A and VPS29 GC < 0.15 in tissues; GC vs RSD r² 0.938 (HPA) and 0.988 (RT-qPCR); computed with the R `ineq` package in a KNIME workflow; no software licence stated — [S33](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-54288-7)
- Eisenberg & Levanon list site: housekeeping genes "based on analysis of next-generation sequencing (RNA-seq) data", download `HK_genes.txt` and `HK_exons.xlsx`; 11 recommended "strongly and uniformly expressed" genes: C1orf43, CHMP2A, EMC7, GPI, PSMB2, PSMB4, RAB7A, REEP5, SNRPD3, VCP, VPS29; no licence statement — [S23](https://www.tau.ac.il/~elieis/HKG/)
- RefGenes (Hruz et al. 2011, Genevestigator): more than 40,000 MAS5-normalised Affymetrix arrays; finds probe sets with the lowest standard deviation of signal within a user-chosen expression band and sample set ("at least three independent studies comprising at least 60 arrays"); returns top 25; validation: mouse liver (16 samples) GAK and srp72 beat GAPDH/TUBB/ACTB/HPRT; Arabidopsis seedlings and shoot apex all candidates more stable than GAPDH/ACTB/UBQ10; human LCLs two of six candidates significantly better M values; cattle liver GAK/VPS4A best; pig liver (48 samples) "no extrapolated gene was significantly more stable than the controls"; top 20 stable genes within a 1.5-fold SD range vs more than 5-fold for 20 common reference genes; "freely available for academic users (upon registration)", commercial subscription otherwise — [S24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3072958/). The genevestigator.com domain did not resolve on 17 Oct 2026 (two attempts) — availability unverified.
- de Jonge et al. 2007: 13,629 human Affymetrix arrays; metrics CV (SD/mean) and maximum fold change (MFC = max/min), criteria low CV, MFC < 2, mean below max − 2 SD; top 15 are RPS13, RPL27, RPS20, RPL30, RPL13A, RPL9, SRP14, RPL24, RPL22, RPS29, RPS16, RPL4, RPL6, OAZ1, RPS12 (all CV < 4%); ACTB rank 57 (CV 4.88%, MFC 1.91), GAPDH rank 139 (CV 5.75%, MFC 6.37), B2M rank 176; experimental check was conventional PCR for pseudogene amplification only — [S25](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1976390/)
- Czechowski et al. 2005 (abstract): Affymetrix ATH1 data mined for stable Arabidopsis genes; "Specific and efficient primers were developed for 22 genes and tested on a diverse set of 20 cDNA samples" — [S26](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16166256)
- Pombo et al. 2017 (tomato leaf, 37 treatments/time points, about 3 replicates): coefficient of variation of RPKM across 110 values; 9 RNA-seq-selected candidates had VC 12.2–14.4% vs GAPDH 52.9%, EF1α 41.6%; Cq spread of new candidates 1.4–1.8 cycles vs GAPDH 5.4; geNorm M for ARD2/VIN3 0.092 vs GAPDH 0.204; NormFinder VIN3 0.013; V2/3 < 0.15 in every subset; GAPDH was down-regulated during ETI and "normalizing with it falsely inflated ARD2 expression" — [S27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5357963/)
- Yim et al. 2015 (soybean, 26 RNA-seq libraries): CoV < 0.2 (group 1) and < 0.24 (group 2) plus normality p-value filters; 136 genes passed, 7 taken to RT-qPCR against 8 classic genes; RefFinder top four in tissue assay: Bic-C2, F-box protein 2, ELF1b, Bic-C1; under salt/PEG stress Bic-C1, F-box protein 2, Bic-C2, VPS-like; ELF1b best classic but "only mediocre under stress" — [S28](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0136343)
- Carmona et al. 2017 (findRGs, AutoFlow/Ruby): reads-per-mapped-million; CV default maximum 10% (range 0–20%); minimum reads 0.00003 × smallest library (floor 10); in olive "no candidate is obtained for both pollen and pistil" at strict settings; prostate "No candidate RG is obtained in the most stringent conditions"; the most stringent Arabidopsis setting "returned almost only rubisco genes"; experimental test was end-point PCR on gels; licence not stated — [S29](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5568602/)
- ERgene (Zeng et al. 2020): inverts the internal-standard method, computes per-sample gene-to-gene ratios, ΔF across sample pairs, ranks genes by the variance of ΔF, returns top 20; "depth" controls which sample pairs are compared; 4.58 s vs 55 min for NormFinder on 2 samples × 2,000 genes; demonstrated on microarray probe IDs; paper says code available "for academic non-commercial research purposes" — [S30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7596506/); PyPI lists MIT, v1.2.8 (July 2021) — [S31](https://pypi.org/project/ERgene/); GitHub README gives no method description, 5 stars, MIT — [S32](https://github.com/Starlitnightly/ERgene)
- Single cell (Lin et al. 2019, scMerge): scSEGIndex averages scaled ranks of four features (mixing proportion, Gaussian variance, regularised zero proportion, F-statistic across cell types); 1,076 human and 916 mouse stably expressed genes; 62% of human SEGs overlap bulk housekeeping lists; "GAPDH and ACTB show clear bimodality"; GPL-3 — [S34](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6748759/)
- Tool disagreement: De Spiegelaere et al. 2015 — geNorm rankings differed in 71% (20/28) of cases with vs without efficiency correction (16 major), NormFinder 50% (14/28); RefFinder accepts only raw Cq and its arithmetic combination "has no scientific basis"; use geNorm V 0.15 as rule of thumb; NormFinder needs > 8 samples — [S36](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0122515). Sundaram et al. 2019 — geNorm top pair Gapdh/Tbp had r = 0.937 but CVs 59.1% and 57.2%; "averaging the ranks can lead to suboptimal assessment"; workflow: drop genes with CV > 50%, then NormFinder with group structure; BestKeeper's SD = 1 threshold "too lenient" — [S37](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6650036/)
- Kozera & Rapacz 2013: GAPDH differed about 14-fold between skeletal muscle and breast across 72 human tissues; a tuberculosis study normalised to GAPDH gave false IL-4 results; using one reference gene "may result in significant bias like three-fold in 25 % of the analyzed results"; choose candidates with Ct between 15 and 30 and similar to the target; "Best 3" rule (three reference genes, three programs, three replicates) — [S38](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3825189/)
- Tool landscape and licences: geNorm Excel applet "free download for academic use only", NormFinder Excel/R (no licence stated), BestKeeper, RefGenes, a web tool combining four methods — [S65](https://www.gene-quantification.de/hkg.html); RefFinder web tool (copy Ct values from Excel; no licence, no source) — [S66](https://www.ciidirsinaloa.com.mx/RefFinder-master/); NormqPCR Bioconductor LGPL-3, 15 years — [S67](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/NormqPCR.html); ctrlGene CRAN GPL-2|3, last published 2019 — [S68](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/ctrlGene/index.html); `eleven` (Python geNorm) BSD but last release 2014 and Python 2.7 — [S71](https://pypi.org/project/eleven/)

#### Inferences
- The stability metrics are a few lines of numpy each (SD of log2, max/mean log2, CV, Gini, group F-test). What is missing is the *integration*: compute them on the lab's own matrix, match the expression band of the targets, exclude genes with pseudogenes or with a group effect, cross-reference external atlases as prior evidence, and then confirm with qPCR geNorm M/V — qPilot already owns the last step.
- The RNA-seq pre-screen reduces the geNorm plate from "GAPDH, ACTB, 18S" to a candidate list that has already passed a stability filter on dozens of the lab's own samples, which addresses Chapman's "more than 10 candidates" without burning plate space on habit genes.
- Published tools are unusable as dependencies for an Apache-2.0 local tool (web-only, MATLAB, Ruby, academic-only, GPL/LGPL); reimplementation from the papers is the only route and is cheap.

#### Gaps
- Genevestigator/RefGenes current availability and pricing (domain did not resolve).
- HRT Atlas database licence (code is MIT; data terms not stated).
- No head-to-head study of "references derived from the lab's own RNA-seq" versus "atlas-derived references (HRT Atlas)" in qPCR; HRT Atlas authors say further qPCR validation is "still in progress".
- Czechowski 2005 full text (foundational) not retrievable; only abstract numbers used.

### KQ3. Concordance analysis: methods, known systematic biases, and honest reporting

#### Takeaway
The field reports concordance as a Pearson/Spearman correlation of log2 fold changes, which method-comparison statisticians call misleading; the better-specified studies use pre-declared direction and ΔFC thresholds, sensitivity/PPV against a reference, RMSD, and stratification by expression level, and they show the known biases: microarray ratio compression (RNA-seq does not compress), low-expression noise, isoform/probe-location mismatch, and library-preparation loss.

#### Cited Findings
- Giavarina 2015 (Bland–Altman tutorial): bias = mean of paired differences, limits of agreement ± 1.96 SD; "The correlation between methods is always misleading and should not be used for assessing the method comparability"; "Acceptable limits must be defined a priori, based on clinical necessity, biological considerations or other goals"; regress differences on means to detect proportional bias; log-transform non-normal differences; Passing–Bablok regression as a robust alternative — [S39](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4470095/)
- Everaert 2017 definitions and results (concordant/non-concordant/severe; 15.1–19.4%; 93% ΔFC < 2; 1.8% severe; predictors: smaller, fewer exons, lower expressed; multi-mapping relative risk 1.071–1.075; poor-quality reads 1.073–1.12; GC content and paralog count not predictive) — [S2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5431503/)
- Rajkumar 2015 metrics: sensitivity, specificity, PPV against qPCR; Spearman 0.453–0.541; RMSD 1.18–2.50 — [S5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12864-015-1767-y)
- Wang 2014: fold-change R² 0.97 (RNA-seq) vs 0.85 (microarray) with array ratio compression; concordance depends on treatment effect size (60% for nafenopin vs 25% for beta-naphthoflavone) and on expression (about 75% above median vs 20–40% below) — [S6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4243706/)
- SEQC 2014: TaqMan vs PrimePCR disagree for individual genes ("some genes showing high expression in one platform but are not detected at all by the other"); titration-order consistency as a platform-independent accuracy check ("Genes with large differential expression performed best") — [S3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321899/)
- Zhao 2014: isoform and probe-location artefacts (RORC isoforms; PRDM2 minor-isoform probe; PDE6D intron probe; mis-annotated probes reflecting EPB41); RNA-seq slopes 1.18–1.27 relative to array; dynamic range 2.6×10^5 vs 3.6×10^3 — [S7](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0078644)
- Rachinger 2021: transcript lost in library preparation; secondary structure proposed as a cause; GC content ruled out; matched amplicon lengths (196–286 bp) and GAPDH normaliser described as methods, not as causes — [S4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8430810/)
- Prokopec 2013 (abstract): NanoString nCounter and ABI OpenArray compared against qRT-PCR, 38 genes across 165 samples; "All three measurement technologies showed good concordance, but with divergent price/time/sensitivity trade-offs" — [S45](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23169800)
- mRNA vs protein (for "validating proteomics by qPCR"): Edfors et al. 2016 — per-sample Pearson correlation between TPM and protein copy number "from 0.39 in the kidney-derived HEK293 to 0.79 in the breast-derived cell line MCF7", median across samples 0.67, rising to 0.93 after applying a gene-specific RNA-to-protein conversion factor; RNA-to-protein ratios vary "from 200 for a transcription factor (MYBL2) to 220,000 for SERPINB1"; "it is important to follow up the RNA-based analysis with protein profiling" — [S81](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5081484/); Liu, Beyer & Aebersold 2016 (abstract): "transcript levels by themselves are not sufficient to predict protein levels in many scenarios" — [S82](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27104977)
- Single-cell qPCR guidance exists (Ståhlberg & Kubista 2018, abstract only) — [S86](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28756182)

#### Inferences
- An honest concordance report has at least: (1) pre-declared definitions (direction; |log2FC| threshold; ΔFC "severe" threshold), (2) counts of concordant / non-concordant / severe, compared with the Everaert benchmark range, (3) Bland–Altman bias and limits on log2FC with a proportional-bias check (slope), (4) stratification by RNA-seq expression quartile, (5) a per-gene list of discordant genes annotated with the known risk factors (low expression, short, few exons, multi-mapping, isoform coverage), and only then (6) a correlation coefficient because reviewers expect one.
- "Validating proteomics by qPCR" is not validation of protein levels: with gene-specific RNA-to-protein ratios spanning three orders of magnitude, qPCR can only confirm the transcript-level change; a tool should say so explicitly rather than let the prose claim protein confirmation.
- Because the discordance predictors are computable from the DEG table and annotation before any qPCR is run, they belong in the planner (KQ1) as much as in the report.

#### Gaps
- Lin's concordance correlation coefficient (the standard agreement statistic) was not fetched; cite from the literature before use.
- No study quantifying what fraction of qPCR–RNA-seq discordances are due to isoform mismatch specifically (Zhao's examples are array probes).
- Vogel & Marcotte 2012 and Liu 2016 full texts are paywalled; only abstract-level claims used.

### KQ4. Hand-offs between the bioinformatician's DEG table and the bench scientist's plate

#### Takeaway
The one hand-off failure with hard numbers is spreadsheet corruption of gene symbols (19.6% of papers with Excel gene lists in 2005–2015, 30.9% in 2014–2020, 19.5% of screened articles in May 2026, 39.7% of GEO gene-list files), compounded by HGNC's renaming (old symbols live on in the "previous symbol" field) and by the absence of batch, identifier-based primer lookup in the free primer databases; what cores actually deliver and whether they analyse qPCR could not be documented.

#### Cited Findings
- Ziemann, Eren & El-Osta 2016: 35,175 supplementary Excel files screened from 18 journals (2005–2015), 7,467 gene lists in 3,597 papers; errors confirmed in 987 files from 704 articles = 19.6% of papers with Excel gene lists; GEO: 228 of 574 gene-list files (39.7%); errors rising about 15% per year; SEPT2 → "2-Sep" (also "2006/09/02"), MARCH1 → "1-Mar", RIKEN 2310009E13 → 2.31E+13; highest rates (> 20%) at Nucleic Acids Research, Genome Biology, Nature Genetics, Genome Research, Genes & Development, Nature; impact-factor correlation rho 0.52 — [S8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-016-1044-7)

- HGNChelper (CRAN 0.8.15, Nov 2024): fixes human (HGNC) and mouse (MGI) symbols converted to dates, withdrawn symbols and aliases; licence GPL-2 or GPL-3 — [S17](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/HGNChelper/index.html)
- Primer lookup for a DEG list: PrimerBank search by GenBank accession, NCBI protein accession, NCBI Gene ID, PrimerBank ID, gene symbol or keyword; no batch search or bulk download mentioned; copyright 2006 Massachusetts General Hospital, no licence text — [S59](https://pga.mgh.harvard.edu/primerbank/); PrimerBank design rules and validation: 306,800 primers for 61,425 genes; mouse 26,855 pairs validated, 65.1% passed all criteria, 82.6% by gel, 6.5% "failed by QPCR due to no amplification"; primers "were not required to sit on exon boundaries" (only 311 pairs on exon–exon boundaries); 2,534 pairs hit two targets — [S60](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2808898/)
- GETPrime: gene-specific primers chosen to span the junction covering the most transcripts and iterated "until all transcripts are covered"; transcript-specific primers favour minimal coverage; BLAST specificity (E-value 100); pairs spanning UTRs discarded; 2011 validation: 50 of 51 testable pairs met efficiency 92–108% and R² > 0.99; accepts gene symbols and Ensembl gene or transcript IDs; output is an Excel file — [S62](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3173022/); 2.0: 13 species, Ensembl v81, 1,175,874 pairs (human 444,256, mouse 268,855), human coverage 98.3% of protein-coding genes, dbSNP v145 mapped — "over 80% of human primers overlap a documented variant", "no experimental validation results" in 2.0, up to 10 identifiers per web search, tab-separated downloads, RESTful API, no database licence stated — [S63](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5210624/); site last updated 2015-11-12 (Ensembl 81) — [S61](https://getprime.epfl.ch/)
- Bio-Rad PrimePCR: "Every PCR primer pair has been experimentally validated", a document "Meeting the MIQE Guidelines by Full Wet-Lab Validation", probes "designed for maximum specificity and transcript coverage", lookup tool and bulk search, primer sequences not disclosed, manuals behind login — [S64](https://www.bio-rad.com/en-us/product/primepcr-pcr-primers-assays-arrays?ID=M0HROA15)
- Thermo TaqMan assays: "over 2.8 million assays available across more than 30 species", designed to cover "the maximum number of transcript variants"; the search tool page (/order/genome-database/) returned 403 to a plain fetch — [S50](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/pcr/real-time-pcr/real-time-pcr-assays/taqman-gene-expression.html)
- Kozera & Rapacz on primer checks: "Check for splice variants, polymorphisms, and pseudogenes" — [S38](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3825189/)
- Pabinger 2014: "most tools use their own file format"; inputs "either manually or semi-automatically" prepared — [S43](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5129434/)
- Harvard Chan Bioinformatics Core page: analysis "from raw data through to figure generation and publication"; nothing on deliverable formats or qPCR follow-up — [S91](https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/bioinformatics/)

#### Inferences
- Roughly one in five DEG spreadsheets that reach a bench scientist carries at least one corrupted symbol (the published rates are for supplementary files, which are usually the same files). A deterministic "symbol gate" (date-pattern and float-pattern detection, HGNC previous/alias mapping, ambiguous-case Decision Card) at every import into qPilot is cheap and prevents a wrong primer being ordered.
- Identifier-based batch lookup exists only at GETPrime (REST, Ensembl IDs, unvalidated in 2.0) and the commercial vendors (sequences withheld). PrimerBank, the most-cited free resource, has no batch mode and places primers without regard to exon boundaries. A local tool can therefore offer: "here is the Ensembl ID, here are candidate assays from each source, here is which isoforms each covers" — but cannot verify commercial probe sequences.
- Over 80% of human GETPrime primers overlap a documented SNP; for labs with genotype data this is a checkable, deterministic failure mode (assay passports in the roadmap).

#### Gaps
- No documentation of what sequencing cores hand over (file formats, fields) or whether they run qPCR analysis in R; no forum evidence (Biostars/Reddit/ResearchGate unreachable).
- Thermo's identifier/batch search capabilities (Ensembl ID, list upload) unverified.
- MGI (mouse) nomenclature data terms not fetched.

### KQ5. High-throughput qPCR: formats, tools, pre-amplification QC, pains; NanoString as the adjacent platform

#### Takeaway
HT-qPCR data leave the instrument as vendor CSV/TXT exports that no standard covers (Biomark and OpenArray are absent from RDML's list), the only open multi-format reader is a 2009-era R package, pre-amplification is a documented failure point with quantitative QC rules (±1.5 ΔΔCq, 15–18 cycles, Cq 6–23), and nCounter's housekeeping-based normalisation leaves residual technical variation — all of which map onto qPilot's reference-stability, robustness and record machinery, but raw amplification curves may not be exportable from these platforms.

#### Inferences
- A Biomark or OpenArray "validation panel" is a Cq matrix of assays × samples with a Pass/Fail or outlier flag per reaction, plus pre-amplification context. qPilot can ingest it as a Cq-mode study (the engine's LinRegPCR path needs raw curves, which these exports do not carry — flag Study Confidence accordingly) and apply its existing reference-stability and robustness machinery across 96 assays, something neither vendor software nor HTqPCR does.
- Pre-amplification QC is fully deterministic from paired controls (ΔΔCq within ±1.5, expected Cq window, 18S exclusion) and is a natural Decision Card per assay.
- Platform scope creep is the risk: nCounter is a different measurement (hybridisation counts) and its normalisation question (which housekeeping set, how many RUV factors) is exactly qPilot's "results under every normaliser set", but RCC parsing and count-model statistics are new code.

#### Gaps
- Vendor-documented Biomark export column list and Ct-quality threshold (the user guide is behind a support portal); whether raw fluorescence per chamber is exportable.
- OpenArray/QuantStudio "AmpScore" and "Cq Confidence" definitions and recommended cut-offs (vendor manuals returned 403).
- RCC file layout specification from the vendor (only tool-level descriptions).
- Public Biomark/OpenArray datasets with matching RNA-seq (none identified without search).
- Forum evidence of HT-qPCR pains (unreachable).

- Vermeulen et al. 2009 (abstract): 59-gene signature "profiled in the largest neuroblastoma patient series (n=579) to date using real-time quantitative PCR", 20 ng RNA, 30 training, 313 test, 236 validation tumours — [S79](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/19515614); GSE16476 is not this dataset (it is an 88-tumour Affymetrix set) — [S92](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE16476)
- NCBI policy: "NCBI itself places no restrictions on the use or distribution of the data contained therein", but "some submitters of the original data (or the country of origin of such data) may claim patent, copyright" rights, and NCBI "cannot provide comment or unrestricted permission" — [S80](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/home/about/policies/)
- Reference-gene resources with explicit terms: HGNC "No restrictions" [S15]; HRT Atlas code MIT, data terms unstated [S18][S19]; Eisenberg list no terms [S23]; Joshi code no licence [S22]

#### Inferences
- For a public acceptance test of a concordance module, SEQC's PrimePCR (20,801 assays × A–D, SYBR, "MIQE validated") against the SEQC RNA-seq is the obvious fixture; it is Cq-level (no raw curves), so it tests the report, not the curve engine.
- Everaert's published concordance numbers (15–19% / 1.8%) are reproducible from these files and can serve as the benchmark the report compares against.

#### Gaps
- GEO accession and licence of the Vermeulen 2009 qPCR data; overlap with the 498 SEQC neuroblastomas.
- Any public Biomark/OpenArray dataset with paired RNA-seq.
- Datasets pairing single-cell RNA-seq with single-cell qPCR were not identified in this run.

## 4. Discoveries (ranked by impact × feasibility × fit)

### D1. Reference-gene candidates from the lab's own sequencing, feeding qPilot's geNorm step ("RefSeek")

1. **Persona and situation.** A bench scientist (or the core analyst on their behalf) has a count or TPM matrix from the same tissue, treatment and species as the planned qPCR study (typical: 6–40 samples). They are about to put GAPDH and ACTB on the plate because that is what the lab always does. This happens every time an RNA-seq project moves to qPCR, i.e. for a majority of RNA-seq projects (Section 2, KQ1 and KQ2).
2. **Evidence of the pain.** 87% of vertebrate studies used a single reference gene and 72% used GAPDH/ACTB/18S alone; only 15% tested a panel [S35]. In every RNA-seq- or array-mined study I could read, data-derived candidates were more stable than the classics in qPCR follow-up: mouse liver GAK/srp72 [S24]; tomato ARD2/VIN3 M = 0.092 vs GAPDH 0.204 with GAPDH regulated by the treatment [S27]; soybean Bic-C2/F-box over eight classics [S28]; PBMC EIF2S3/SF3B1 over GAPDH [S18]; genome-wide, ACTB ranked 57th and GAPDH 139th [S25]. Cost of getting it wrong: a flipped IL-4 conclusion in a tuberculosis study [S38]; "three-fold bias in 25% of results" with one reference gene [S38]. Cost of doing it properly today: a 10–20 candidate geNorm plate [S35] designed from a web atlas that may not match the lab's tissue/treatment.
3. **What exists today.** HRT Atlas (web, JS-only site, R/Shiny code MIT, data terms unstated; tissue-level, not treatment-level) [S18][S19][S20]; RefGenes/Genevestigator (microarray compendium, academic registration, domain unresolved in this run) [S24]; Joshi's Gini code (MATLAB, unlicensed) [S21][S22]; ERgene (MIT on PyPI, "academic non-commercial" in the paper, microarray-oriented, 5 stars) [S30][S31][S32]; findRGs (Ruby/AutoFlow, unlicensed) [S29]; scMerge (GPL-3, single-cell) [S34]. None reads the lab's own count matrix inside a qPCR tool, none matches candidates to the target genes' expression band, none carries the evidence into the geNorm decision and the record.
4. **The feature.** Input: a gene × sample matrix (CSV/TSV; raw counts from featureCounts/STAR, or TPM from salmon/kallisto via tximport — detect by integer-ness and column names), a sample sheet with the experimental groups, the list of target genes to be validated, and the species. Deterministic method, per gene: (a) expressed in all samples (HRT Atlas rule: ≥ 1 RPKM/TPM in every sample [S18]); (b) SD of log2(TPM+1) across samples < 0.5 (candidate) or < 1 (housekeeping) [S18]; (c) max/mean of log2 expression < 2 [S18]; (d) CV of linear expression [S27][S28][S25]; (e) Gini coefficient across samples [S21][S33]; (f) group-effect test across the experimental groups (one-way ANOVA F or Kruskal–Wallis on log2; Sundaram's reason for NormFinder-style group awareness [S37]) — a gene with a group effect is excluded regardless of its SD, which is the RNA-seq-level analogue of qPilot's reverse-normalisation guard; (g) expression-band matching: keep candidates whose mean log2 expression lies within ±2 of the targets' mean (Hruz [S24]; Kozera's Ct 15–30 rule [S38]); (h) exclusions: genes with known processed pseudogenes (HRT Atlas rule [S18]) when an annotation table is supplied, rRNA and mitochondrial genes; (i) co-regulation screen: pairwise correlation among the top candidates, flagging pairs with r > 0.9 so geNorm's known bias toward co-regulated pairs [S37][S36] is visible. Rank by a Score Product (geometric mean of the ranks of mean, SD, fold change [S18]) and present the top 12–20 as the geNorm plate, each with: all metrics, whether it appears in HRT Atlas / Eisenberg lists for that tissue (prior evidence, cards), and assay availability (PrimerBank ID by NCBI Gene ID [S59]; GETPrime via REST by Ensembl ID [S63]; HRT Atlas validated primer where the candidate is one of its 31/17 [S18]). The scientist decides which 8–20 go on the plate (Decision Card: "habit set GAPDH/ACTB/18S fails rule f in your data: GAPDH log2FC −0.9 between groups, p = 0.004"). After the qPCR run, qPilot's existing geNorm M + V and reverse-normalisation guard run on the candidates; the final card shows per-target consequences of "habit set" vs "data-derived set". Record: input matrix hash, parameters, every metric per gene, chosen set, reason. MCP: `qpcr_reference_candidates(matrix_path, samplesheet_path, targets, species, band=2.0)` returning the ranked table with fact ids.
5. **Feasibility.** numpy/pandas only; Gini and the rest are closed-form. Acceptance tests: reproduce Pombo's ranking from their RNA-seq (data in the paper's supplement [S27]) and HRT Atlas's PBMC case [S18]; use SEQC RNA-seq (GSE47792 [S74]) to confirm the classics' instability across A/B/C/D. Licences: HRT Atlas lists — data terms unstated (ask the authors or treat as citation-only prior evidence, not redistributed); Eisenberg list — no terms [S23]; HGNC — no restrictions [S15]. Effort: 1–2 weeks for the engine and cards; +1 week for assay-availability lookups (offline PrimerBank/GETPrime snapshots are not redistributable without clarification; use REST at the user's request, off by default to stay local-first). Hardest part: honest handling of normalisation units (counts need library-size normalisation before stability; TPM already is) and small n (< 4 samples per group makes rule f meaningless — say so on the card).
6. **Why qPilot.** It already owns geNorm M/V, the reverse-normalisation guard, impact-ranked cards, the record and MCP; this adds the upstream evidence so that the reference-gene decision is made once, from the lab's own data, and is reproducible from the record. The domain-agnostic core gains a "candidate-from-external-data" pattern reusable for other assays.
7. **Risks and confidence.** RNA-seq stability does not guarantee qPCR stability (pig liver counter-example [S24]; HRT Atlas's own validation "in progress" [S18]) — so the feature must always end in the qPCR geNorm step, never replace it. Small or batch-confounded RNA-seq data will produce confident-looking nonsense; rule f and an n-threshold mitigate. Confidence: high that the pain is real and the maths is sound; medium on how often labs have a matched matrix at hand (they usually do when they are "validating RNA-seq").

### D2. Concordance Report bound to facts (RNA-seq vs qPCR agreement with pre-declared definitions and robustness)

1. **Persona and situation.** The same scientist after the qPCR run, writing the "validation" paragraph and figure; the reviewer reading it; the PI deciding whether a discordant story gene kills the paper. Every validation study ends here.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** The standard figure is a correlation, which "is always misleading" for method comparison [S39]; rigorous studies instead use direction + fold thresholds, sensitivity/PPV, RMSD [S5] and ΔFC-based "severe" definitions [S2]; concordance is strongly expression-dependent (75% vs 20–40% [S6]) and differs by pipeline (edgeR PPV 90% vs Cuffdiff2 39% [S5]). Discordances have known causes the scientist rarely checks: low expression, short genes, few exons, multi-mapping [S2], isoform/probe location [S7], library-prep loss [S4]. "Validating" proteomics by qPCR confirms only transcripts [S81][S82].
3. **What exists today.** Excel/Prism scatter plots; HTqPCR produces qPCR statistics but no cross-platform report [S41]; no open tool implements Bland–Altman on log2FC with expression stratification for qPCR–RNA-seq.
4. **The feature.** Input: qPilot's per-gene relative quantities (log2, with CI from the engine) for the validated genes and the DEG table (CSV/TSV with Ensembl ID or HGNC-checked symbol, log2FC, baseMean/logCPM, padj; optionally several DEG tables from different pipelines). Definitions are declared *before* results are visible (stored in the plan; defaults from the literature: DE if |log2FC| > 1 [S2]; validated if same direction and qPCR fold change > 1.25 or < 0.80 [S5]; severe if ΔFC > 2 or opposite direction [S2]) and locked in the record. Deterministic outputs: (a) counts concordant / non-concordant / severe with exact binomial CIs, compared with the Everaert benchmark range 15–19% / 1.8% [S2]; (b) Bland–Altman on log2FC: bias, 95% limits of agreement, slope of differences on means (proportional bias = compression/expansion) [S39], and Passing–Bablok slope as the robust alternative [S39]; (c) stratification by RNA-seq expression quartile [S6] and by gene length/exon count when a GTF is supplied [S2]; (d) per-discordant-gene risk annotation (quartile, length, exons, isoform count, assay coverage from D4 if available); (e) robustness: all of (a)–(c) recomputed under every reasonable qPCR analysis choice qPilot already enumerates (reference set, outlier rule, empty-well rule) so the report states "concordance verdict unchanged in 23 of 24 analysis variants"; (f) if several DEG tables are given, which pipeline agrees best with qPCR (sensitivity/PPV as in [S5]); (g) a methods paragraph with every number bound to a fact id ("no naked numbers"); (h) an explicit sentence when the upstream data are proteomics: qPCR confirms transcript change only, with the RNA–protein ratio caveat [S81]. Decision Cards: "Story gene X is severely discordant; it is in the lowest expression quartile and has 2 exons — options: re-run with 3 more replicates (power from `qpilot plan`), check assay isoform coverage, accept and report". MCP: `qpcr_concordance(study_id, deg_table_path, definitions)`.
5. **Feasibility.** Pure numpy/scipy statistics; one to two weeks. Public acceptance test: SEQC PrimePCR (GPL18522, samples A–D [S76]) and TaqMan (GPL4097 [S73]) against SEQC RNA-seq (GSE47792 [S74]) reproducing Everaert's 15–19% / 1.8% [S2] — all GEO, "no restrictions" from NCBI [S80]. Hardest part: matching identifiers across vendor assay IDs, symbols and Ensembl IDs (use D3's gate) and deciding sensible defaults when the DEG table lacks expression columns.
6. **Why qPilot.** The robustness engine is what makes a concordance claim honest (the verdict is reported across analysis variants, not for one arbitrary pipeline); the record freezes the definitions before the data are seen; the fact ids stop the LLM-drafted paragraph from inventing an r value.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Reviewers may still ask for "the correlation" — provide it, labelled as association not agreement. Over-strict defaults could make honest studies look bad; defaults must cite their source and be editable with a logged reason. Confidence: high (pain and methods both well sourced).

### D3. Validation Study Planner from a DEG table (gene choice with evidence, symbol gate, panel power, plate request, hand-off bundle)

1. **Persona and situation.** PhD student or postdoc who has just received a DEG spreadsheet (often Excel) from a core or collaborator and must decide which 6–20 genes to validate, order primers, and design plates; the core analyst who wants to hand over something the bench can act on. Once per RNA-seq project; often repeated after reviews.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** About one in five gene-list spreadsheets contains corrupted symbols [S8][S9][S11]; HGNC's renamed symbols persist in the previous-symbol field [S12]; validating "a random selection of genes is no guarantee" [S1]; the genes most likely to be discordant are identifiable in advance (low expression, short, few exons, multi-mapping) [S2]; sample size for validation is rarely designed (Fang & Cui: design issues "rarely been discussed" [S85]); primer lookup is one gene at a time [S59][S61][S64]; a rigorous example design used 115 genes, 16 replicates and 7 references [S5] — an effort few labs can afford without guidance.
3. **What exists today.** Manual: Excel filters, PubMed, vendor lookup tools; HGNC checker and HGNChelper (GPL) fix symbols but are separate steps [S13][S17]; qPilot's `qpilot plan` does per-gene power but not panel composition.
4. **The feature.** Input: DEG table (CSV/TSV/XLSX; columns auto-detected: gene id, symbol, log2FC, p/padj, mean expression), optional count/TPM matrix and GTF, optional transcript-level quant (salmon `quant.sf`), species. Steps: (a) **Symbol gate** — detect date patterns (`^\d{1,2}-(Jan|Feb|Mar|Sep|Oct|Nov|Dec)$`, locale variants), float patterns (`\d\.\d+E\+\d+`), withdrawn/previous/alias symbols via the HGNC complete-set TSV shipped with qPilot (no restrictions [S15], updated Tue/Fri [S16]; MGI for mouse pending licence check); ambiguous cases (Mar-01 → MARC1 or MARCH1/MARCHF1 [S10]) become Decision Cards; Ensembl ID becomes the primary key; the mapping is logged. (b) **Panel composition with evidence** — the scientist marks "story genes"; the planner adds a stratified set: by expression quartile × fold-change bin × direction, plus "risk genes" (lowest quartile, < 2 exons, short) deliberately included so the report can say the risky stratum was tested [S2][S6]; each candidate shows its discordance-risk flags. (c) **Panel power** — two deterministic calculations: per-gene replicate numbers from the existing `qpilot plan` (minimum detectable effect from historical qPCR SD), and panel-level: number of genes needed so that a severe-discordance rate worse than a chosen tolerance would be observed with 95% probability (binomial; e.g. tolerance 10% → 29 genes — my illustration, not a sourced norm), shown against the Everaert prior of 1.8% [S2]. (d) **Reference genes** — call D1 if a matrix exists, otherwise propose a candidate plate from the atlas priors with a card saying "no lab data; this is a prior". (e) **Assays** — for each gene, list candidate assays with provenance (PrimerBank by NCBI Gene ID [S59]; GETPrime by Ensembl ID via REST, user-triggered [S63]; lab's own assay passports), and run D4's isoform coverage check where sequences are open. (f) **Plate request and hand-off bundle** — a `VALIDATION.json` + CSVs: locked definitions (for D2), gene list with Ensembl IDs/HGNC symbols/strata/risk flags, references, assays, replicate plan, plate layout request (for the existing plate-design assistant), and the hash of the DEG table; the same bundle is what a core can produce for the bench and what qPilot reads back after the run. MCP: `qpcr_plan_validation(deg_path, matrix_path=None, gtf_path=None, story_genes=[...], tolerance=0.1)`.
5. **Feasibility.** Symbol gate: HGNC TSV (~45 MB JSON/TSV; "no restrictions") — 2–3 days. Stratified sampling and binomial power: 2 days. Risk flags need a GTF (Ensembl GTF; terms not fetched in this run — verify) — 3 days. Assay lookups: PrimerBank has no API or bulk download [S59], so scraping is not acceptable; GETPrime has REST and tab-separated downloads but no stated licence [S63] — user-triggered online lookups only, off by default. Total 3–4 weeks including cards and bundle schema. Hardest part: species/annotation versions (DEG tables carry no genome build) and the ambiguous-symbol cases that need a human.
6. **Why qPilot.** The decision-space/card/record core already exists; the planner just adds a pre-study decision space (which genes, which strata, which assays) whose choices are logged and later reconciled by D2. The MCP surface lets a core's agent produce the bundle and the bench's agent consume it.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Risk of being perceived as "just a gene-name checker" if (b)–(f) are thin; the value is the evidence-tagged panel and the locked definitions. Online lookups conflict with local-first — keep them explicit and optional. Confidence: medium-high on pain (symbol corruption is the best-quantified pain in this whole area), medium on adoption of the bundle by cores (no evidence on core practice).

### D4. Isoform-aware assay coverage check ("does this assay measure what the sequencing measured?")

1. **Persona and situation.** The scientist choosing or re-using a primer pair for a validation gene; the lab keeping assay passports; the analyst explaining a discordant gene.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Discordances traced to isoforms and probe location: RORC isoforms indistinguishable by array probes, PRDM2 probe hitting minor isoforms, PDE6D probe in an intron [S7]; discordant genes are short with few exons [S2]; PrimerBank primers "were not required to sit on exon boundaries" and 2,534 pairs hit two targets [S60]; GETPrime finds that over 80% of human primers overlap a dbSNP variant and that for *Ubtf* no primers could distinguish every transcript [S62][S63]; vendors claim "maximum transcript coverage" but withhold sequences [S64][S50]; guidance says "Check for splice variants, polymorphisms, and pseudogenes" [S38].
3. **What exists today.** GETPrime (2015 Ensembl 81, web/REST, no validation in 2.0) [S61][S63]; Primer-BLAST (NCBI web); vendor transcript-coverage statements; nothing local that uses the lab's transcript-level quantification.
4. **The feature.** Input: primer sequences (forward/reverse; probe optional) from the assay passport, PrimerBank/GETPrime records or the lab's design; species transcript cDNA FASTA + GTF (Ensembl, user-downloaded); optional salmon/kallisto transcript TPM per sample. Deterministic method: exact (and 1-mismatch, 3′-anchored) matching of both primers to every transcript in both orientations; amplicon per transcript (size, exons spanned, whether an exon–exon junction is crossed → gDNA risk if not); list of transcripts covered vs expressed; "fraction of the gene's TPM covered by the assay" per group, and whether the RNA-seq log2FC computed on covered transcripts only differs from the gene-level log2FC by more than the D2 "severe" threshold; optional SNP overlap if the lab supplies a VCF. Output: a coverage fact set per assay, a card when coverage < 80% of expressed signal or when the covered-only log2FC disagrees in direction, and a passport entry. MCP: `qpcr_assay_coverage(assay_id | sequences, species, quant_dir=None)`.
5. **Feasibility.** String matching over ~200k transcripts is trivial (seconds with a k-mer index); no Primer3 (GPL) needed because nothing is designed, only checked. Ensembl FASTA/GTF are freely downloadable (terms to confirm). Test fixtures: GETPrime's *Ubtf* case [S62]; Zhao's RORC/PRDM2/PDE6D examples [S7]. Effort: 1–2 weeks. Hardest part: commercial assays — only amplicon location/"assay location" metadata is public, so coverage must be computed from vendor-stated transcript lists and marked "vendor-declared, not verified".
6. **Why qPilot.** Assay passports and "prior evidence on cards" are already on the roadmap; this gives them a deterministic, record-bound fact ("assay covers 3 of 5 expressed isoforms, 62% of signal") that D2 can cite when explaining a discordance.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Transcript annotations change between Ensembl releases; store the release in the fact. Labs without transcript-level quant get only the structural check. Confidence: medium (clear mechanism, case-level evidence rather than frequency data).

### D5. HT-qPCR panel plugin: Biomark / OpenArray / TaqMan Array Card import, pre-amplification QC, panel-level reference stability and robustness

1. **Persona and situation.** Core-facility manager or analyst running 48–96-gene validation panels on Biomark (96.96: 9,216 reactions [S46]) or OpenArray (3,072 reactions per plate, up to four per run [S48]) for many client projects a month; the client who receives a vendor CSV and a heat map.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** Exports are vendor-specific and outside RDML [S89][S43]; the only open multi-format reader is HTqPCR, a 2009 design still at the same feature set (Artistic-2.0; categories and five normalisations; limma) [S40][S41][S42]; vendor analysis is Windows-only with the user guide behind a support portal [S46][S47]; pre-amplification failures are quantified (4% of reactions fail the ±1.5 ΔΔCq rule at ≤ 18 cycles; 57%/46% success at 21/24 cycles; pre-amp variability 13% vs RT 23.6%) and the QC requires paired controls and a Cq window of 6–23 [S44]; reference-gene stability across a 96-assay chip is rarely assessed with group awareness (tool disagreements [S36][S37]).
3. **What exists today.** Standard BioTools Real-Time PCR Analysis Software (Ct, quality threshold, heat map, CSV) [S47]; ExpressionSuite ΔΔCt with endogenous-control selection, last release notes 2019 [S49]; HTqPCR in R [S40]; Python `qpcr` reads generic Ct tables (GPL-3) [S70]; Excel macros (practice; unsourced).
4. **The feature.** Importers (deterministic, versioned): Biomark CSV — skip the header block to the column-header line; columns ID (S01-A01 style chamber), sample name/type, assay name/type, Ct `Value`, `Call` Pass/Fail (names as mapped by HTqPCR [S42]; vendor-exact names to be confirmed from the FLDM-01051 guide); OpenArray/QuantStudio export — `ThroughHole.Address`, `Assay.Assay.ID`, `Assay.Assay.Type`, `ThroughHole.Ct`, `ThroughHole.Outlier` [S42] plus the newer QuantStudio TXT (Crt, AmpScore, Cq Conf — names to verify); TaqMan Array Card — SDS/QuantStudio RQ exports (the SDS header-then-`#` convention HTqPCR documents [S41]). Each becomes a Cq-mode qPilot study with the chip/plate as the "plate" object and the sample × assay layout recorded; Study Confidence is capped because there are no raw curves (unless raw fluorescence export is confirmed). Deterministic modules: (a) pre-amplification QC per assay from paired non-pre-amplified controls: ΔΔCq within ±1.5 → pass [S44]; expected-Cq window check (6–23 on Biomark [S44]); 18S/high-abundance warning (Cq below cycle count [S44]); (b) chip-level effects: per-IFC normalisation before splitting into samples, as HTqPCR recommends [S41], exposed as a robustness axis (with/without); (c) assay failure detection (an assay "Fail"/outlier in > x% of samples → card "drop assay"); (d) reference stability across all assays with geNorm M/V (existing) plus the group-effect screen from D1, and the reverse-normalisation guard; (e) robustness grid extended with normalisation method (ΔCq vs geometric mean vs quantile — the HTqPCR set [S41]) and category thresholds (Ct.max 35 vs 32); (f) D2's concordance report when a DEG table is attached. MCP: `qpcr_panel_import(path, platform)`, `qpcr_preamp_qc(study_id)`.
5. **Feasibility.** Example files: HTqPCR ships `BioMark_sample.csv`, `OpenArray_sample.csv`, SDS and CFX samples (Artistic-2.0 — usable as parser test fixtures with attribution; do not vendor the R code) [S41]. Public paired datasets: none identified in this run (gap). Effort: importers 1–2 weeks (plus vendor-format verification with a real core's files), pre-amp QC and panel cards 1–2 weeks, robustness extension 1 week. Hardest part: getting vendor-exact column names and the raw-curve export question answered without the user guide; and the Standard BioTools/Thermo formats changing with software builds (2022 build for Biomark X [S47]).
6. **Why qPilot.** The whole-study (multi-plate) model, reference stability under every normaliser set, robustness and the record are exactly what a 96×96 panel needs and what vendor software does not provide; the domain-agnostic core treats an IFC as another plate-based assay.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Without raw curves, qPilot's distinguishing LinRegPCR path does not apply — the plugin is Cq-mode and must say so. Format verification depends on access to a core. Confidence: medium (pain plausible and tooling gap verified; frequency of pain not quantified beyond pre-amp numbers).

### D6. nCounter as the adjacent "validation" platform: RCC import with QC thresholds and normalisation robustness (lower priority)

1. **Persona and situation.** Core or clinical-research lab using nCounter panels (up to 800 targets, FFPE-tolerant [S58]) as the orthogonal check on RNA-seq signatures; the analyst choosing housekeeping genes and normalisation.
2. **Evidence of the pain.** nSolver normalisation "prone to residual unwanted technical variation"; results "sensitive to the quality and the set of housekeeping genes"; in 70 samples with both platforms, agreement with RNA-seq rose from distance correlation 0.051 (nSolver) to 0.36 (RUVSeq-based) [S56]; QC flags have published default thresholds (binding density 0.1–2.25 or 0.1–1.8; FoV < 75; linearity < 0.95; LoD < 2) [S52].
3. **What exists today.** nSolver (vendor, manual now hosted by Bruker; not retrievable) [S56]; NACHO (GPL-3, active 2026) [S51]; NanoTube (GPL-3) [S54]; NanoStringNorm (archived 2021) [S55]; NanoNormIter (no licence) [S57]. All GPL or unlicensed, R-only, and none frames the housekeeping-set choice as a robustness question with a record.
4. **The feature.** RCC parser (sectioned text; spec to be obtained — tool descriptions only in this run), QC facts with the NACHO/vendor thresholds [S52], housekeeping-set stability (geNorm M on log counts; group-effect screen), normalisation variants (positive-control geometric mean; housekeeping geometric mean; RUVg-style factor removal with k = 1–3 [S56]) run as a robustness grid; cards for flagged lanes and for housekeeping genes with a group effect; concordance report (D2) against RNA-seq. Licence-clean reimplementation from the papers (RUV: Risso et al. method — not fetched in this run).
5. **Feasibility.** 2–3 weeks; no public RCC + RNA-seq pair identified (Bhattacharya's CBCS data access not checked). Hardest part: the RUV factor-analysis step and count-model statistics are new to qPilot.
6. **Why qPilot.** "Results under every normaliser set" is literally the open problem here; the domain-agnostic core was designed for this.
7. **Risks and confidence.** Platform ownership changed (the nanostring.com manual URL now redirects to brukerspatialbiology.com, observed during this run; the business change itself was not verified from a source); scope creep away from qPCR. Confidence: low-medium.
